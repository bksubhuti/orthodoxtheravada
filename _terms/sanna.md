### Grammatical Analysis
**Saññā**: [f.] perception; recognition; sign; memory. Formed by prefix *saṃ* (together) + root *ñā* (to know). The Mūla explains it by the verb: *sañjānātīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā saññāti vuccati*, "it perceives, therefore it is called perception"; and what does it perceive? "It perceives blue, yellow, red, white" (S-iii 455, Khajjanīya Sutta). The Atthasālinī: *nīlādibhedaṃ ārammaṇaṃ sañjānātīti saññā*, "it perceives an object distinguished as blue and so on, thus it is perception" (As 1165).

### Orthodox Definition
*Saññā* is the third of the five aggregates (*saññākkhandha*) and one of the seven mental factors common to every consciousness (*sabbacittasādhāraṇā*: contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty and attention, Abhidh-s 84). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines the perception aggregate of a given consciousness as "perception (*saññā*), the mode of perceiving (*sañjānanā*), the state of having perceived (*sañjānitattaṃ*)" (Dhs 455).

The *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (the Dhammasaṅgaṇī commentary) gives the full definition by characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: recognition (*sañjānana*) is its characteristic, its function is re-cognition (*paccābhiññāṇarasā*) (As 1165), and by way of its general nature its function is making a mark for later recognition (*punasañjānanapaccayanimittakaraṇarasā*), like a carpenter marking wood; it is manifested as a fixed idea formed from the sign as it was apprehended (*yathāgahitanimittavasena abhinivesakaraṇapaccupaṭṭhānā*), like the blind men who describe the elephant, or as not staying long (*aciraṭṭhānapaccupaṭṭhānā*), like lightning; and its proximate cause is the object as it appeared (*yathāupaṭṭhitavisayapadaṭṭhānā*), like the perception "men" that arose in the young deer seeing straw men (As 1167). The same commentary illustrates its operation with the carpenter marking a piece of wood, the man recognised by a black mole, and the royal treasurer who reads the name-label on each ornament (As 1166).

The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī distinguishes perception, consciousness and wisdom: perception only perceives by way of blue and so on and cannot penetrate the characteristics; consciousness also penetrates the characteristics but cannot lead to the path; wisdom does all three, as a child, a villager and a treasurer understand a coin (Abhidh-s-t 1059).

Perception can be perverted. The Vipallāsa Sutta (AN 4.49) names four perversions of perception, of mind and of view: taking the impermanent as permanent, the suffering as pleasant, the not-self as self, and the unattractive as attractive (A-iv 533).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Kiñca, bhikkhave, saññaṃ vadetha? Sañjānātīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ saññā ’ ti vuccati. Kiñca sañjānāti? Nīlampi sañjānāti, pītakampi sañjānāti, lohitakampi sañjānāti, odātampi sañjānāti.**  
> “And why, bhikkhus, do you call it perception? ‘It perceives,’ bhikkhus, therefore it is called perception. And what does it perceive? It perceives blue, it perceives yellow, it perceives red, it perceives white.  
>

> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, saññāvipallāsā cittavipallāsā diṭṭhivipallāsā. Katame cattāro? Anicce, bhikkhave, niccanti saññāvipallāso cittavipallāso diṭṭhivipallāso;**  
> “Monks, there are these four perversions of perception, perversions of mind, and perversions of view. What four? Taking the inconstant to be constant is a perversion of perception, a perversion of mind, and a perversion of view.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye saññākkhandho hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye saññā sañjānanā sañjānitattaṃ – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye saññākkhandho hoti.**  
> 61. What is the perception aggregate at that time? Whatever perception (saññā), mode of perceiving (sañjānanā), act of perceiving (sañjānitattaṃ), exists at that time—this is the perception aggregate at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§61) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=27&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjEuIEthdGFtbyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgc2HDscOxxIFra2hhbmRobyBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, characteristic and function)
> **Nīlādibhedaṃ ārammaṇaṃ sañjānātīti saññā. Sā sañjānanalakkhaṇā paccābhiññāṇarasā. Catubhūmikasaññā hi nosañjānanalakkhaṇā nāma natthi. Sabbā sañjānanalakkhaṇāva.**  
> It perceives an object distinguished as blue and so on, thus it is perception (saññā). This has the characteristic of recognition (sañjānanalakkhaṇā) and its function is re-cognition (paccābhiññāṇarasā). For there is no perception in the four planes that does not have the characteristic of recognition. All indeed have the characteristic of recognition.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=153&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TsSrbMSBZGliaGVkYeG5gyDEgXJhbW1h4bmHYeG5gyBzYcOxasSBbsSBdMSrdGkgc2HDscOxxIEu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the similes)
> **Tassā, vaḍḍhakissa dārumhi abhiññāṇaṃ katvā puna tena abhiññāṇena taṃ paccābhijānanakāle, purisassa kāḷatilakādiabhiññāṇaṃ sallakkhetvā puna tena abhiññāṇena asuko nāma esoti tassa paccābhijānanakāle, rañño piḷandhanagopakabhaṇḍāgārikassa tasmiṃ tasmiṃ piḷandhane nāmapaṇṇakaṃ bandhitvā ‘ asukaṃ piḷandhanaṃ nāma āharā ’ ti vutte dīpaṃ jāletvā ratanagabbhaṃ pavisitvā paṇṇaṃ vācetvā tassa tasseva piḷandhanassa āharaṇakāle ca pavatti veditabbā.**  
> Its operation should be understood: when a carpenter (vaḍḍhaki) makes a mark on a piece of wood and then later re-recognizes it by that mark; when one marks a sign such as a black mole on a person and then later re-recognizes that person by that sign, thinking, ‘this is so-and-so’; or when a king’s treasurer (bhaṇḍāgārika) who guards the jewelry ties a name-label to each respective adornment, and when told, ‘Bring the adornment called such-and-such,’ he lights a lamp, enters the jeweled room (ratanagabbhaṃ), reads the label, and brings that very adornment.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=154&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGFzc8SBLCB2YeG4jeG4jWhha2lzc2EgZMSBcnVtaGkgYWJoacOxw7HEgeG5h2HhuYMga2F0dsSBIHB1bmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, manifestation and proximate cause)
> **Aparo nayo – sabbasaṅgāhikavasena hi sañjānanalakkhaṇā saññā. Punasañjānanapaccayanimittakaraṇarasā, dāruādīsu tacchakādayo viya. Yathāgahitanimittavasena abhinivesakaraṇapaccupaṭṭhānā, hatthidassakaandhā viya. Ārammaṇe anogāḷhavuttitāya aciraṭṭhānapaccupaṭṭhānā vā, vijju viya. Yathāupaṭṭhitavisayapadaṭṭhānā, tiṇapurisakesu migapotakānaṃ ‘ purisā ’ ti uppannasaññā viya.**  
> Another method: Indeed, perception (saññā) has the characteristic of recognition by way of comprehensive inclusion. Its function is making a mark for subsequent recognition, like carpenters and so on with wood and so on. Its manifestation is making a fixed idea by way of an apprehended sign, like blind men describing an elephant. Or, its manifestation is not remaining for long due to its not deeply pervading the object, like lightning. Its proximate cause is the object that appeared as it appeared, like the perception ‘these are men’ that arose in young deer seeing straw men.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=154&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBhcm8gbmF5byDigJMgc2FiYmFzYeG5hWfEgWhpa2F2YXNlbmEgaGkgc2HDsWrEgW5hbmFsYWtraGHhuYfEgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Pakārena jānāti aniccādivasena avabujjhatīti paññā, sā eva yathāsabhāvāvabodhane ādhipaccayogato indriyanti paññindriyaṃ. Atha saññāviññāṇapaññānaṃ kiṃ nānākaraṇanti? Saññā tāva nīlādivasena sañjānanamattaṃ karoti, lakkhaṇappaṭivedhaṃ kātuṃ na sakkoti. Viññāṇaṃ lakkhaṇappaṭivedhampi sādheti, ussakkitvā pana maggaṃ pāpetuṃ na sakkoti. Paññā pana tividhampi karoti, bālagāmikaheraññikānaṃ kahāpaṇāvabodhanamettha nidassananti.**  
> It is called wisdom (paññā) because one knows in a special way or understands by way of impermanence and so on; that very factor is a faculty because it exercises predominance in penetrating things according to their intrinsic nature, thus it is the wisdom faculty (paññindriyaṃ). Now, what is the distinction between perception, consciousness, and wisdom? First, perception merely performs the act of perceiving by means of color and so on; it is not able to perform the penetration of characteristics. Consciousness also accomplishes the penetration of characteristics; but it is not able, by endeavoring, to lead to the path. Wisdom, however, performs all three; in this matter, the understanding of a coin by a child, a villager, and a money-changer is the illustration.  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=112&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UGFrxIFyZW5hIGrEgW7EgXRpIGFuaWNjxIFkaXZhc2VuYSBhdmFidWpqaGF0xKt0aSBwYcOxw7HEgSwgc8SB)
