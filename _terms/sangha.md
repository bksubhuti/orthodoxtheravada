### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṅgha**: [m.] assembly; community; association; multitude. From the prefix *saṃ* (together) + root *han* (to strike, to kill). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī-abhinavaṭīkā explains it in two ways: the Saṅgha is *saṃhato ghaṭito*, "joined together" by noble view and virtue held in common (*ariyena diṭṭhisīlasāmaññena*), the community of the eight noble individuals; or it is *saṅgho* because they have "well killed" (*sammadeva ghātitattā*) the torment of defilements through the various paths and fruits (Kkh-nt 694).

### Orthodox Definition
The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī says that although the word *saṅgho* in general denotes a group of individuals, it is of two kinds (Kkh 1235):

1. *Sammuti-saṅgha* (the Saṅgha of designation, the conventional Saṅgha): "in general a group of bhikkhus". This is the Saṅgha meant for the legal acts of the Vinaya. The Mahāvagga lists five kinds of Order of bhikkhus by number: a fourfold, fivefold, tenfold, twentyfold Order, and an Order of more than twenty (Vin-iii 2344, §388). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī says that with a group of four every *saṅghakamma* is proper except higher ordination, invitation (*pavāraṇā*) and reinstatement (*abbhāna*); with a group of five, all except ordination (in the central provinces) and reinstatement; with a group of ten, all except reinstatement; with a group of twenty or more, no Saṅgha-act is improper (Kkh 1235).
2. *Dakkhiṇeyya-saṅgha* (the Saṅgha of those worthy of offerings, also called the *Ariya-saṅgha*): "the group of eight noble individuals" (*aṭṭha ariyapuggalasamūho*). The Mūla describes it in the formula of recollection of the Saṅgha: "the four pairs of men, the eight individuals: this Saṅgha of the Blessed One's disciples is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world" (*cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā … anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassa*, A-ix 223). The Itivuttaka commentary defines the disciples' Saṅgha in the same way as "the group of the eight noble individuals, joined by common view and virtue" (It-a 1391).

The Ratana Sutta, which recites the jewels one by one, says of the Saṅgha: "this too is a precious jewel in the Saṅgha" (*idampi saṅghe ratanaṃ paṇītaṃ*, Khp 109).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **“Saṅgho” tiiminā pana padena kiñcāpi avisesato puggalasamūho vutto, atha kho so dakkhiṇeyyasaṅgho, sammutisaṅgho cāti duvidho hoti. Tattha dakkhiṇeyyasaṅghoti aṭṭha ariyapuggalasamūho vuccati. Sammutisaṅghoti avisesena bhikkhusamūho, so idha adhippeto.**  
> By the word saṅgho, although a group of individuals is spoken of in general, it is twofold: the Saṅgha of those worthy of offerings and the Saṅgha of designation. Therein, the Saṅgha of those worthy of offerings is called the group of eight noble individuals. The Saṅgha of designation is a group of bhikkhus in general, and that is what is intended here.  
>  
> — *Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī* (Dvemātikā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=85&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNh4bmFZ2hvIOKAmeKAmSB0aWltaW7EgSBwYW5hIHBhZGVuYQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī, the groups by number)
> **So panesa kammavasena pañcavidho (mahāva.388) hoti – catuvaggo pañcavaggo dasavaggo vīsativaggo atirekavīsativaggoti. Tattha catuvaggena ṭhapetvā upasampadapavāraṇaabbhānāni sabbaṃ saṅghakammaṃ kātuṃ vaṭṭati. Pañcavaggena ṭhapetvā majjhimesu janapadesu upasampadañca abbhānakammañca sabbaṃ saṅghakammaṃ kātuṃ vaṭṭati. Dasavaggena abbhānakammamattaṃ ṭhapetvā sabbaṃ saṅghakammaṃ kātuṃ vaṭṭati. Vīsativaggena na kiñci saṅghakammaṃ kātuṃ na vaṭṭati, tathā atirekavīsativaggena .**  
> It is fivefold according to the legal procedure: the group of four, the group of five, the group of ten, the group of twenty, and the group of more than twenty. Therein, with a group of four, it is proper to perform all Saṅgha-acts except for higher ordination, invitation, and reinstatement. With a group of five, it is proper to perform all Saṅgha-acts in the central provinces, except for higher ordination and reinstatement. With a group of ten, it is proper to perform all Saṅgha-acts except for reinstatement. With a group of twenty, nothing is improper to perform as a Saṅgha-act, and the same applies to more than twenty.  
>  
> — *Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī* (Dvemātikā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=85&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=U28gcGFuZXNhIGthbW1hdmFzZW5hIHBhw7FjYXZpZGhvIChtYWjEgXZhLjM4OCkgaG90aQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, Mahāvagga)
> **Pañca saṅghā – catuvaggo bhikkhusaṅgho pañcavaggo bhikkhusaṅgho, dasavaggo bhikkhusaṅgho, vīsativaggo bhikkhusaṅgho, atirekavīsativaggo bhikkhusaṅgho. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ catuvaggo bhikkhusaṅgho, ṭhapetvā tīṇi kammāni – upasampadaṃ pavāraṇaṃ abbhānaṃ, dhammena samaggo sabbakammesu kammappatto. Tatra, bhikkhave, yvāyaṃ pañcavaggo bhikkhusaṅgho, ṭhapetvā dve kammāni – majjhimesu janapadesu upasampadaṃ abbhānaṃ, dhammena samaggo sabbakammesu kammappatto.**  
> There are these five (kinds of) Orders: a fourfold Order of monks, a fivefold Order of monks, a tenfold Order of monks, a twentyfold Order of monks, an Order of monks of more than twentyfold. Herein, monks, that which is a fourfold Order of monks, excepting three (formal) acts—ordination, invitation, rehabilitation—it is entitled (to take part) in all (formal) acts, if by rule, if it is complete. Herein, monks, that which is a fivefold Order of monks, excepting two (formal) acts—ordination in the middle districts, rehabilitation—it is entitled (to take part) in all (formal) acts, if by rule, if it is complete.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Mahāvagga, §388) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=439&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mzg4LiBQYcOxY2Egc2HhuYVnaMSBIOKAkyBjYXR1dmFnZ28gYmhpa2todXNh4bmFZ2hv)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **‘ suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho; yadidaṃ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā ’ ti.**  
> ‘The Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is practising the good way, practising the straight way, practising the true way, practising the correct way. That is to say, the four pairs of men, the eight types of mature persons—this Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, and is the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.’  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 9.27, Paṭhamavera Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_09&page_number=206&name=navakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCYIHN1cHBh4bmtaXBhbm5vIGJoYWdhdmF0byBzxIF2YWthc2HhuYVnaG8gdWp1cHBh4bmtaXBhbm5vIGJoYWdhdmF0bw%3D%3D)

> This precious jewel is the Saṅgha. By this asseveration of the truth may there be happiness.
