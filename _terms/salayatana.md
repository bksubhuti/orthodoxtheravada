### Grammatical Analysis
**Saḷāyatana**: [nt.] The six sense bases. Formed by *saḷ* (six) + *[āyatana](/glossary/ayatana/)* (sense base/gateway). Specifically designates the six internal sensory apparatuses when operating within the chain of Dependent Origination.

### Orthodox Definition
In the twelve-fold chain of Dependent Origination (*[paṭiccasamuppāda](/glossary/paticcasamuppada/)*), *saḷāyatana* constitutes the fifth link, conditioned directly by mentality-materiality (*[nāmarūpa](/glossary/namarupa/)-paccayā saḷāyatanaṃ*) and acting as the direct cause for contact (*saḷāyatana-paccayā phasso*).

While the term *[āyatana](/glossary/ayatana/)* broadly covers both internal and external elements of the grid, *saḷāyatana* focuses strictly on the development and deployment of the **six internal subjective functional fields** (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind) inside a living being. 

The *Sammohavinodanī* (the Vibhaṅga commentary) gives the characteristics of the six sense bases: "the characteristic of actuating (*āyatanalakkhaṇaṃ*), the function of seeing and so on, manifested as the state of physical base and door, with name-and-form as proximate cause" (Vibh-a 617:6). In its explanation of "the six sense bases conditioned by name-and-form" it shows how resultant *nāma* and *rūpa*, at rebirth-linking and during the course of life, are conditions (by conascence, mutuality, support, dissociation, presence, non-disappearance and so on) for the sixth, the mind base, and for the five physical bases (Vibh-a 836:1, 839:1, 842:1, 847:1, 850:1). In the Abhidhamma method the Vibhaṅga itself reads "*nāmarūpapaccayā chaṭṭhāyatanaṃ*" (Vibh 935:1) as well as *saḷāyatanaṃ* (Vibh 905:1).

### Quote
> **“Katamañca, bhikkhave, saḷāyatanaṃ? Cakkhāyatanaṃ, sotāyatanaṃ, ghānāyatanaṃ, jivhāyatanaṃ, kāyāyatanaṃ, manāyatanaṃ – idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, saḷāyatanaṃ.**  
> ‘‘And what, monks, are the six sense bases? The eye sense base, the ear sense base, the nose sense base, the tongue sense base, the body sense base, the mind sense base—this, monks, is called the six sense bases.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 12.2, Vibhaṅga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=245&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtYcOxY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2HhuLfEgXlhdGFuYeG5gz8%3D)
