### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhamma**: [m.; nt.] the Dhamma; a state; a phenomenon; cause; quality. The Aṭṭhasālinī derives it from the verb "to bear": "they are called *dhammā* because they bear their own nature (*attano pana sabhāvaṃ dhārentīti dhammā*); or because they are borne by conditions, or are borne according to their true nature (*dhāriyanti vā paccayehi, dhārīyanti vā yathāsabhāvato*)" (As 846:9-10).

### Orthodox Definition
The Aṭṭhasālinī says that the word *dhamma* is seen in the senses of scriptural text (*pariyatti*), cause (*hetu*), quality (*guṇa*), and being without a self or living entity (*nissattanijjīvatā*) (As 842:1): as scriptural text in "he masters the Dhamma, the discourse, the song" (*dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇāti suttaṃ geyyaṃ*, As 842:2); as cause in "knowledge of the cause is the discrimination of Dhamma" (*hetumhi ñāṇaṃ dhammapaṭisambhidā*, As 842:3, Vibh 720); as quality in "Dhamma and non-Dhamma do not bring the same result; non-Dhamma leads to hell, Dhamma brings one to a good destination" (As 843:1-845:1); and as being without self or living entity in "at that time there are *dhammā*" and "he dwells contemplating *dhammā* in *dhammā*" (*dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati*, As 845:2). In the context of the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, "it applies only in the sense of being without a self or living entity" (As 845:3).

In the Mūla, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī's mātikā (Dhs 4) begins with the Tikamātikā, the schedule of triads, whose first item is "1.(Ka) *kusalā dhammā*", wholesome states (Dhs 5:1, 6:1), and the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta gives the fourth foundation of mindfulness as contemplating *dhammā* in *dhammā* (D-ii 1345:2).

> **Tikamātikā 1.(Ka) kusalā dhammā.**  
> 1. The Schedule of Triads 1.(Ka) Wholesome mental phenomena.

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ.**  
> Which four? Here, monks, a bhikkhu dwells contemplating the nature of the body in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of feelings in feelings, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of the mind in the mind, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of things in things, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, D-ii 1341) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=231&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtZSBjYXR0xIFybz8%3D)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī)
> **Dhammasaddo panāyaṃ pariyattihetuguṇanissattanijjīvatādīsu dissati. Ayañhi “dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇāti suttaṃ geyyan” tiādīsu (a. ni.4.102) pariyattiyaṃ dissati. “Hetumhi ñāṇaṃ dhammapaṭisambhidā” tiādīsu (vibha.720) hetumhi. “Na hi dhammo adhammo ca, ubho samavipākino; Adhammo nirayaṃ neti, dhammo pāpeti suggatin” ti.(theragā.304; jā.1.15.386)– Ādīsu guṇe. “Tasmiṃ kho pana samaye dhammā honti”(dha. sa.121),“dhammesu dhammānupassī viharatī” tiādīsu (dī. ni.2.373) nissattanijjīvatāyaṃ. Svāyamidhāpi nissattanijjīvatāyameva vaṭṭati.**  
> But this word Dhamma is seen in the senses of theoretical study, cause, quality, being without a self or living entity (nissattanijjīvatā), and so on. Indeed, this is seen in the sense of theoretical study in passages like, “masters the Dhamma, the discourse, the song,” and so on. In passages like, “knowledge of the cause is discrimination of mental phenomena,” and so on, it is seen in the sense of cause. “Indeed, the Dhamma and unrighteousness do not both lead to the same results; unrighteousness leads to hell, the Dhamma causes one to reach a pleasant destination.” In such passages, it is seen in the sense of quality. In passages like, “But at that time, mental phenomena exist,” and, “contemplating the nature of things in things,” and so on, it is seen in the sense of being without a self or living entity (nissattanijjīvatā). Here, it applies only in the sense of being without a self or living entity (nissattanijjīvatā).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As, Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, As 842-845) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=81&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RGhhbW1hc2FkZG8gcGFuxIF5YeG5gyBwYXJpeWF0dGloZXR1Z3XhuYdhbmlzc2F0dGFuaWpqxKt2YXTEgWTEq3N1IGRpc3NhdGku)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī (the meaning of dhamma))
> **Attano pana sabhāvaṃ dhārentīti dhammā. Dhāriyanti vā paccayehi, dhārīyanti vā yathāsabhāvatoti dhammā.**  
> But because they bear their own true nature, they are called dhammā. Or, they are called dhammā because they are borne by conditions, or because they are understood according to their true nature.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 846) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=81&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXR0YW5vIHBhbmEgc2FiaMSBdmHhuYMgZGjEgXJlbnTEq3RpIGRoYW1txIEu)
