### Grammatical Analysis
**Kusala**: [nt.; adj.] wholesome; skillful; blameless; intelligent; kammically wise. The Aṭṭhasālinī explains it as *kucchitānaṃ salanādīhi atthehi* ("in the senses of shaking off the contemptible [kucchita] and so on", As 942:1); a later handbook adds *kusānaṃ lavanena* (cutting like *kusa* grass; Abhidh-av 38:1). The word is used in the senses of health (*ārogya*), blamelessness (*anavajja*), skill (*cheka*) and happy result (*sukhavipāka*) (As 841:2).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Theravāda [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/), *kusala* is the definitive overarching classification for any state of consciousness (*[citta](/glossary/citta-base/)*) or mental factor (*[cetasika](/glossary/cetasika/)*) that is ethically wholesome, spiritually blameless (*anavajja*), and productive of pleasant results or happiness (*sukha-vipāka*).

The *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* gives three senses in which a dhamma is *kusala* (As 942:2):
1. *Ārogyaṭṭhena*: in the sense of health; free from the sickness and disease of defilements (As 942:3).
2. *Anavajjaṭṭhena*: in the sense of blamelessness; free from the fault, blemish and distress of defilements (As 942:4).
3. *Kosallasambhūtaṭṭhena*: in the sense of being born of skill.

Its characteristic is blameless, happy result (*anavajjasukhavipākalakkhaṇaṃ*), its function is the destruction of the unwholesome (*akusalaviddhaṃsanarasaṃ*), its manifestation is cleansing (*vodānapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ*) and its proximate cause is wise attention (*yonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānaṃ*) (As 944:1).

Wholesome states are generated exclusively by the three wholesome roots (*[kusala-mūla](/glossary/kusala-mula/)*): non-greed (*[alobha](/glossary/alobha/)*), non-hatred (*[adosa](/glossary/adosa/)*), and non-delusion (*[amoha](/glossary/amoha/)*). They span the sensual, fine-material, immaterial, and supramundane planes.

### Quote
> **Kusalanti kucchitānaṃ salanādīhi atthehi kusalaṃ. Apica ārogyaṭṭhena anavajjaṭṭhena kosallasambhūtaṭṭhena ca kusalaṃ.**  
> Wholesome (kusala) means wholesome in the sense of shaking off (salana) contemptible things (kucchita), and so on. Furthermore, it is wholesome in the sense of being healthy (ārogya), blameless (anavajja), and produced by skill (kosalla).  
>
