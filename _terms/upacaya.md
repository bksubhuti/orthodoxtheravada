### Grammatical Analysis
**Upacaya**: [m.] Integration; accumulation; initial arising. From prefix *upa* (towards) + root *ci* (to heap up, pile, build). The Atthasālinī glosses *ācayo* as *nibbatti* (production) and *upacayo* as *vaḍḍhi* (increase) (As 2119:2-4).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Abhidhamma, *Upacaya* ("growth of form", *rūpassa upacayo*) is the first of the four characteristics of matter (*lakkhaṇarūpa*): *upacaya, santati, jaratā, aniccatā* (Dhs 641-644). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it: *yo āyatanānaṃ ācayo, so rūpassa upacayo*, "that which is the setting up (growth) of the sense bases is the growth of form" (Dhs 1371:2); and the next entry says *yo rūpassa upacayo, sā rūpassa santati* (Dhs 1372:2).

The Atthasālinī explains: *ācayo* is production (*nibbatti*); the growth of the sense bases as they are repeatedly produced is *upacaya*, "increase" (*vaḍḍhi*); the subsequent period of occurring is *santati*, "continuous occurring" (*pavatti*) (As 2119:1-6). By meaning both are only a designation for birth-form (*jātirūpa*): the two terms are given because of the diversity of mode and for the sake of those to be trained, and so the exposition says "that which is the growth of form is the continuity of form" (As 2120:6-7).

The Visuddhimagga gives its four-part definition: *ācayalakkhaṇo rūpassa upacayo*, "growth of form has the characteristic of setting up; its function is to make material instances emerge from the past; it is manifested as a presenting, or as a state of completion; its proximate cause is grown materiality" (Vism-ii 352:1). It records the commentary simile: the time when water emerges in a hole dug on a river bank is *ācaya* (production), the time when it fills the hole is *upacaya* (increase), and the time when it overflows is *santati* (occurrence) (Vism-ii 352:6). Thus the first arising of material instances is *ācaya*, the arising of others subsequent to them is *upacaya*, and the arising of still others again and again is *santati* (Vism-ii 353:3-5). See [Santati](/glossary/santati/).

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa upacayo? Yo āyatanānaṃ ācayo, so rūpassa upacayo – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa upacayo. 642. Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa santati? Yo rūpassa upacayo, sā rūpassa santati – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa santati.**  
> 641. Which form is the growth of form? That which is the growth of the sense bases – that is the growth of form – this is that growth of form. 642. Which form is the continuity of form? That which is the growth of form – that is the continuity of form – this is that continuity of form.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs 641-642, Rūpakaṇḍa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=169&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjQxLiBLYXRhbWHhuYMgdGHhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgcsWrcGFzc2EgdXBhY2F5bz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Ācayalakkhaṇo rūpassa upacayo , pubbantato rūpānaṃ ummujjāpanaraso, niyyātanapaccupaṭṭhāno, paripuṇṇabhāvapaccupaṭṭhāno vā, upacitarūpapadaṭṭhāno. Pavattilakkhaṇā rūpassa santati , anuppabandhanarasā, anupacchedapaccupaṭṭhānā, anuppabandhakarūpapadaṭṭhānā.**  
> Growth of form (rūpassa upacayo) has the characteristic of setting up. Its function is to make material instances emerge from the past. It is manifested as a presenting, or it is manifested as a state of completion. Its proximate cause is grown materiality. Continuity of form (rūpassa santati) has the characteristic of occurrence. Its function is linking. It is manifested as non-interruption. Its proximate cause is materiality that is to be linked.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 352, Upacaya and Santati) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=79&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDQ0LiDEgGNheWFsYWtraGHhuYdvIHLFq3Bhc3NhIHVwYWNheW8gLCBwdWJiYW50YXRv)
