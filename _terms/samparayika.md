### Grammatical Analysis
**Samparāyika**: [adj.] Belonging to the next life; pertaining to the future state. Derived from *samparāya* (the other world, the life to come) + *ika* (adjective suffix). The Itivuttaka commentary explains: *samparāye bhavaṃ samparāyikaṃ*, "what exists in the life to come is samparāyika, meaning it is included in the next world" (*paralokapariyāpannaṃ*), and it explains *samparāya* as the other world (*paraloko*) "to be approached in the future by way of Dhamma" (*dhammavasena samparetabbato*). It is paired with *diṭṭhadhammika*, "what exists in the visible state, included in this world" (*idhalokapariyāpannaṃ*).

### Orthodox Definition
*Samparāyika* refers to what belongs to the life after this one: the fault, the welfare, the fruit of an action that will be met after death. The Mūla uses it in three ways:

1. A fault (*vajja*): the Vajja Sutta defines the *samparāyikaṃ vajjaṃ* as the fault of someone who reflects that the result of bodily, verbal and mental misconduct is bad and painful in the next life, and that after death he could be reborn in hell; fearing it, he abandons the misconduct.
2. A fruit: *idaṃ samparāyikaṃ dānaphalaṃ*, the rebirth of a donor in a heavenly realm after death (A-v 199).
3. Welfare (*hita*) and happiness (*sukha*): the Dīghajāṇu Sutta (AN 8.54) names four things that lead to a clansman's welfare and happiness in the life to come (*samparāyahitāya, samparāyasukhāya*): accomplishment in faith (*[saddhā](/glossary/saddha/)*), virtue (*[sīla](/glossary/sila/)*), generosity (*cāga*) and wisdom (*[paññā](/glossary/panna/)*). The same sutta gives four other things for welfare and happiness in this life (*diṭṭhadhammahitāya*).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the four qualities)
> **“Cattārome, byagghapajja, dhammā kulaputtassa samparāyahitāya saṃvattanti samparāyasukhāya. Katame cattāro? Saddhāsampadā, sīlasampadā, cāgasampadā, paññāsampadā.**  
> TigerPaw, there are these four things that lead to a clansman’s welfare and happiness in the world beyond. What four? Acquisition of faith, acquisition of virtuous behavior, acquisition of generosity, and the acquisition of wisdom.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the fault in the next life)
> **“Katamañca, bhikkhave, samparāyikaṃ vajjaṃ? Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ kāyaduccaritassa kho pana pāpako dukkho vipāko abhisamparāyaṃ, vacīduccaritassa pāpako dukkho vipāko abhisamparāyaṃ, manoduccaritassa pāpako dukkho vipāko abhisamparāyaṃ. Ahañceva kho pana kāyena duccaritaṃ careyyaṃ, vācāya duccaritaṃ careyyaṃ, manasā duccaritaṃ careyyaṃ. Kiñca taṃ yāhaṃ na kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjeyyan ’ ti. So samparāyikassa vajjassa bhīto kāyaduccaritaṃ pahāya kāyasucaritaṃ bhāveti, vacīduccaritaṃ pahāya vacīsucaritaṃ bhāveti, manoduccaritaṃ pahāya manosucaritaṃ bhāveti, suddhaṃ attānaṃ pariharati.**  
> “And what, monks, is a fault in the next life? Here, monks, someone considers thus: ‘The result of bodily misconduct is indeed bad and painful in the next life; the result of verbal misconduct is bad and painful in the next life; the result of mental misconduct is bad and painful in the next life. Now if I were to commit bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, or mental misconduct, what would prevent me from being reborn in a state of deprivation, a bad destination, in perdition, in hell, after the breaking up of the body, upon death?’ That person, fearing the fault in the next life, having abandoned bodily misconduct, develops bodily good conduct; having abandoned verbal misconduct, develops verbal good conduct; having abandoned mental misconduct, develops mental good conduct; and keeps himself pure.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (Vajja Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=50&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtYcOxY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtcGFyxIF5aWth4bmDIHZhamph4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Diṭṭhadhammikanti diṭṭhadhammo vuccati paccakkhabhūto attabhāvo, diṭṭhadhamme bhavaṃ diṭṭhadhammikaṃ, idhalokapariyāpannanti attho. Samparāyikanti dhammavasena samparetabbato samparāyo, paraloko, samparāye bhavaṃ samparāyikaṃ, paralokapariyāpannanti vuttaṃ hoti.**  
> “Here and now”: the manifest individual existence is called the ‘here and now’ (diṭṭhadhamma); what exists in the ‘here and now’ is ‘here and now’ (diṭṭhadhammika), meaning it is included in this world. “In the life to come”: the other world is called the ‘life to come’ (samparāya) because it is to be approached in the future by way of Dhamma; what exists in the ‘life to come’ is ‘in the life to come’ (samparāyika), meaning it is included in the next world.  
>  
> — *Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=75&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RGnhua3hua1oYWRoYW1taWthbnRpIGRp4bmt4bmtaGFkaGFtbW8gdnVjY2F0aSBwYWNjYWtraGFiaMWrdG8gYXR0YWJoxIF2bywgZGnhua3hua1oYWRoYW1tZQ%3D%3D)
