### Grammatical Analysis
**Vicikicchāsaṃyojana**: [nt.] the fetter of doubt. Compound of *vicikicchā* (doubt, perplexity) + *[saṃyojana](/glossary/samyojana/)* (fetter). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it by the terms *kaṅkhā* (perplexity), *vimati* (uncertainty), *vicikicchā*, *dveḷhaka* ("a fork in the road"), *saṃsayo* and others.

### Orthodox Definition
In the discourse on the ten fetters (AN 10.13) *vicikicchā* is the second of the five lower fetters (*orambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*): *sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso, kāmacchando, byāpādo*. The Sarada Sutta (AN 3, CST ¶95) says that when the stainless vision of the Dhamma arises in a noble disciple, three fetters are abandoned together: embodiment view, doubt, and wrong grasp of behaviour and observances. In the Abhidhamma list of ten fetters (Dhammasaṅgaṇī, Dhs 1897) the fetter of doubt is the fifth, after sensual lust, aversion, conceit and view.

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī (§1123) defines it as being perplexed and in doubt about eight things: the Teacher, the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, the training, the past, the future, the past and future, and specific conditionality and dependently arisen phenomena. The Atthasālinī explains the first of these: doubt about the Teacher is doubt about his body (does the body with the thirty-two marks exist?), about his qualities (does omniscience exist?), or about both; doubt about the Dhamma is doubt about the four paths, the four fruits and Nibbāna, or whether this Dhamma leads out; and doubt about the Saṅgha, about whether the eight individuals exist or whether the Saṅgha practises well.

The Visuddhimagga (Vism-ii 379) lists the consciousness conjoined with doubt as one of the two kinds rooted in delusion (*mohamūlaṃ*).

### Quote
> **“Dasayimāni, bhikkhave, saṃyojanāni. Katamāni dasa? Pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni? Sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso, kāmacchando, byāpādo – imāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni.**  
> Monks, there are these ten fetters. What are the ten? The five lower fetters and the five higher fetters. What are the five lower fetters? Embodiment view, doubt, wrong grasp of behavior and observances, sensual desire, and ill will. These are the five lower fetters.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.13, Saṃyojana Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_10&page_number=269&name=dasakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTMu4oCY4oCYIERhc2F5aW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHNh4bmDeW9qYW7EgW5pLg%3D%3D)

> **Tattha katamaṃ vicikicchāsaṃyojanaṃ? Satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati, dhamme kaṅkhati vicikicchati, saṅghe kaṅkhati vicikicchati, sikkhāya kaṅkhati vicikicchati, pubbante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, aparante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, pubbantāparante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, idappaccayatā paṭiccasamuppannesu dhammesu kaṅkhati vicikicchati yā evarūpā kaṅkhā kaṅkhāyanā kaṅkhāyitattaṃ vimati vicikicchā dveḷhakaṃ dvedhāpatho saṃsayo anekaṃsaggāho āsappanā parisappanā apariyogāhanā thambhitattaṃ cittassa manovilekho – idaṃ vuccati vicikicchāsaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1123. Which among these is the fetter of doubt? One is perplexed about, and in doubt concerning, the Teacher, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the Dhamma, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the Saṅgha, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the training, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the past, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the future, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the past and future, perplexed about and in doubt concerning the conditionality of this and dependently arisen phenomena; whatever such doubt, perplexity, state of being perplexed, uncertainty, doubt, uncertainty, a fork in the road, doubt, not taking a single stance, wavering, vacillation, inability to fathom, stiffness of mind, mental friction there is — this is called the fetter of doubt.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1123) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=225&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEyMy4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyB2aWNpa2ljY2jEgXNh4bmDeW9qYW5h4bmDPw%3D%3D)

> **Satthari kaṅkhatīti satthu sarīre vā guṇe vā ubhayattha vā kaṅkhati. Sarīre kaṅkhamāno ‘ dvattiṃsavaralakkhaṇapaṭimaṇḍitaṃ nāma sarīraṃ atthi nu kho natthī ’ ti kaṅkhati. Guṇe kaṅkhamāno ‘ atītānāgatapaccuppannajānanasamatthaṃ sabbaññutañāṇaṃ atthi nu kho natthī ’ ti kaṅkhati. Ubhayattha kaṅkhamāno ‘ asītianubyañjanabyāmappabhānurañjitāya sarīranipphattiyā samannāgato sabbañeyyajānanasamatthaṃ sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ paṭivijjhitvā ṭhito lokatārako buddho nāma atthi nu kho natthī ’ ti kaṅkhati.**  
> The phrase ‘is perplexed about the Teacher’ (satthari kaṅkhati) means one is perplexed about the Teacher’s body, or his qualities, or both. If one is perplexed about the body, one is perplexed, thinking: ‘Does the body, adorned with the thirty-two marks of a great man, truly exist or not?’ If one is perplexed about the qualities, one is perplexed, thinking: ‘Does the omniscience, capable of knowing the past, future, and present, truly exist or not?’ If one is perplexed about both, one is perplexed, thinking: ‘Does a Buddha, a World-Leader, exist or not, one who is possessed of a perfected body adorned with the eighty minor marks and a fathom-long aura, and who, having penetrated omniscience—the knowledge capable of knowing all that is knowable—stands established?’  
>  
> — Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā ([Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=388&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MTAwOC4gU2F0dGhhcmkga2HhuYVraGF0xKt0aSBzYXR0aHUgc2FyxKtyZSB2xIE%3D)
