### Grammatical Analysis
**Natthipaccaya**: [m.] Absence condition. Formed by *natthi* (is not, does not exist, absence) + *paccaya* (condition).

### Orthodox Definition
*Natthi-paccaya* is the twenty-second of the 24 Conditions (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, 56:1). The Mūla defines it in one line: “samanantaraniruddhā cittacetasikā dhammā paṭuppannānaṃ cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ natthipaccayena paccayo”, mind and mental factors that have just ceased in immediate contiguity are a condition, by way of absence, for the present mind and mental factors (Paṭṭh-i 56:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā states it as the immediately ceased formless phenomena (*samanantaraniruddhā arūpadhammā*) that help by giving the opportunity for occurrence (*pavattiokāsassa dānena*) to the formless phenomena arising immediately after them (Paṭṭh-a 1942:1; Vism XVII 615). The Ṭīkā explains the relationship: while one group of contact and the rest is occurring, there is no second, so the states about to arise immediately after cannot find an opportunity (*okāsa*) for their own occurrence; the immediately ceased mind and mental factors help by giving them that opportunity (*okāsadānavasena*, Abh-s-t 1672:3).

> Eye sense base is related to eye-consciousness element and its associated phenomena by presence condition.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Attano anantaraṃ uppajjamānānaṃ arūpadhammānaṃ pavattiokāsassa dānena upakārakā samanantaraniruddhā arūpadhammā natthipaccayo.**  
> Formless phenomena that have just ceased (samanantaraniruddhā arūpadhammā), which help by providing an opportunity for the occurrence (pavattiokāsassa dānena) of formless phenomena arising immediately after themselves, are absence condition (natthipaccayo).  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Attano anantarā uppajjamānānaṃ arūpadhammānaṃ pavattiokāsadānena upakārakā samanantaraniruddhā arūpadhammā natthipaccayo.**  
> 615. Formless phenomena that have just ceased (samanantaraniruddhā) and assist formless phenomena arising immediately after them (anantarā) by granting the opportunity for their occurrence, are the absence condition (natthipaccayo).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, 615) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=172&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NjE1LiBBdHRhbm8gYW5hbnRhcsSBIHVwcGFqamFtxIFuxIFuYeG5gyBhcsWrcGFkaGFtbcSBbmHhuYMgcGF2YXR0aW9rxIFzYWTEgW5lbmE%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Ekasmiṃ phassādisamudāye pavattamāne dutiyassa abhāvato attano ṭhitiyā okāsaṃ alabhantānaṃ anantaramuppajjamānakacittacetasikānaṃ okāsadānavasena upakārakā anantaraniruddhā cittacetasikā natthipaccayo.**  
> Mind and mental factors that have ceased in immediate contiguity, which are helpful by way of giving an opportunity to mind and mental factors that are about to arise immediately after—which do not obtain an opportunity for their own endurance because of the absence of a second group while a single group of phenomena such as contact is occurring—are the absence condition (natthipaccayo).  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Paccayasaṅgaha, on the natthipaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=245&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RWthc21p4bmDIHBoYXNzxIFkaXNhbXVkxIF5ZSBwYXZhdHRhbcSBbmUgZHV0aXlhc3NhIGFiaMSBdmF0byBhdHRhbm8%3D)
