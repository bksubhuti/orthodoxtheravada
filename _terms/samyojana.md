### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṃyojana**: [nt.] fetter; bond; tie. Formed by prefix *saṃ* (together) + root *yuj* (to bind, yoke, join). The Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā explains the word by the verb: the fetters "bind beings to existences and tie them down (*satte bhavesu saṃyojenti upanibandhanti*), or tie one existence to another (*bhavaṃ vā bhavena saṃyojenti*)", and for this reason are called *bhavasaṃyojanāni*, fetters of existence (Ps-i 271).

### Orthodox Definition
The *saṃyojanas* are the **ten fetters** that bind beings to *[saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/)*. The Mūla and the Abhidhamma each give ten, but the lists are not the same, so each is given in its own place.

**1. The Suttanta ten (AN 10.13, Saṃyojana Sutta).** "Monks, there are these ten fetters: the five lower fetters (*orambhāgiyāni*) and the five higher fetters (*uddhambhāgiyāni*)."

*The five lower fetters:*
1. Identity view (*sakkāyadiṭṭhi*)
2. Doubt (*[vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)*)
3. Wrong grasp of rules and observances (*sīlabbataparāmāso*)
4. Sensual desire (*kāmacchando*)
5. Ill will (*byāpādo*)

*The five higher fetters:*
6. Lust for form (*[rūparāgo](/glossary/ruparaga/)*)
7. Lust for the formless (*[arūparāgo](/glossary/aruparaga/)*)
8. Conceit (*[māno](/glossary/mana/)*)
9. Restlessness (*[uddhaccaṃ](/glossary/uddhacca/)*)
10. Ignorance (*[avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)*)

The Mūla says that the stream-enterer is one who has completely destroyed three fetters (*tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā sotāpanno*, A-iv 896), and that the non-returner is reborn spontaneously and attains final Nibbāna there through the destruction of the five lower fetters (*pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā opapātiko*, A-iii 898). The Peṭakopadesa says that one established in the fruit of non-returning, developing serenity and insight further, abandons the five higher fetters (Pet 809).

**2. The Abhidhamma ten (Dhs 1118, Saṃyojanagocchaka).** "There are ten fetters": sensual passion (*kāmarāga*), aversion (*paṭigha*), conceit (*māna*), views (*diṭṭhi*), doubt (*vicikicchā*), adherence to rites and rituals (*sīlabbataparāmāsa*), desire for continued existence (*bhavarāga*), jealousy (*issā*), stinginess (*macchariya*) and ignorance (*avijjā*). The Atthasālinī says these may be presented by the sequence of defilements or by the sequence of paths: views, doubt, rites and rituals, jealousy and stinginess are abandoned by the stream-entry path; sensual desire and ill will by the non-returner path; conceit, desire for continued existence and ignorance by the path of Arahantship (As 2308). The Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā gives this same list as the "fetters of existence" that are completely destroyed for the arahant, "burnt by the fire of knowledge" (Ps-i 271).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the ten fetters)
> **“Dasayimāni, bhikkhave, saṃyojanāni. Katamāni dasa? Pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni? Sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso, kāmacchando, byāpādo – imāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. “Katamāni pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni? Rūparāgo, arūparāgo, māno, uddhaccaṃ, avijjā – imāni pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, dasa saṃyojanānī” ti.**  
> Monks, there are these ten fetters. What are the ten? The five lower fetters and the five higher fetters. What are the five lower fetters? Embodiment view, doubt, wrong grasp of behavior and observances, sensual desire, and ill will. These are the five lower fetters. What are the five higher fetters? Lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance. These are the five higher fetters. Indeed, monks, these are the ten fetters.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.13, Saṃyojana Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_10&page_number=269&name=dasakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTMu4oCY4oCYIERhc2F5aW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHNh4bmDeW9qYW7EgW5pLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā saṃyojanā? Dasa saṃyojanāni – kāmarāgasaṃyojanaṃ, paṭighasaṃyojanaṃ, mānasaṃyojanaṃ, diṭṭhisaṃyojanaṃ, vicikicchāsaṃyojanaṃ, sīlabbataparāmāsasaṃyojanaṃ, bhavarāgasaṃyojanaṃ, issāsaṃyojanaṃ, macchariyasaṃyojanaṃ, avijjāsaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1118. What states are fetters? There are ten fetters: the fetter of sensual passion, the fetter of aversion, the fetter of conceit, the fetter of views, the fetter of doubt, the fetter of adherence to rites and rituals, the fetter of desire for continued existence, the fetter of jealousy, the fetter of stinginess, and the fetter of ignorance.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Saṃyojanagocchaka, §1118) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=224&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTExOC4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Imāni pana saṃyojanāni kilesapaṭipāṭiyāpi āharituṃ vaṭṭati maggapaṭipāṭiyāpi. Kilesapaṭipāṭiyā kāmarāgapaṭighasaṃyojanāni anāgāmimaggena pahīyanti, mānasaṃyojanaṃ arahattamaggena, diṭṭhivicikicchāsīlabbataparāmāsā sotāpattimaggena, bhavarāgasaṃyojanaṃ arahattamaggena, issāmacchariyāni sotāpattimaggena, avijjā arahattamaggena. Maggapaṭipāṭiyā diṭṭhivicikicchāsīlabbataparāmāsaissāmacchariyāni sotāpattimaggena pahīyanti, kāmarāgapaṭighā anāgāmimaggena, mānabhavarāgaavijjā arahattamaggenāti.**  
> However, it is proper to present these fetters both by the sequence of defilements and by the path sequence. By the sequence of defilements: the fetters of sensual desire and ill will are abandoned by the non-returner path; the fetter of conceit by the path of Arahantship; views, doubt, and adherence to rites and rituals by the stream-entry path; the fetter of desire for continued existence by the path of Arahantship; jealousy and stinginess by the stream-entry path; and ignorance by the path of Arahantship. By the path sequence: views, doubt, adherence to rites and rituals, jealousy, and stinginess are abandoned by the stream-entry path; sensual desire and ill will by the non-returner path; and conceit, desire for continued existence, and ignorance by the path of Arahantship.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Saṃyojanagocchaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=408&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SW3EgW5pIHBhbmEgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBbmkga2lsZXNhcGHhua1pcMSB4bmtaXnEgXBpIMSBaGFyaXR14bmDIHZh4bmt4bmtYXRp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojanoti bhavasaṃyojanānīti dasa saṃyojanāni kāmarāgasaṃyojanaṃ paṭighamānadiṭṭhivicikicchāsīlabbataparāmāsabhavarāgaissāmacchariyasaṃyojanaṃ avijjāsaṃyojanaṃ. Imāni hi satte bhavesu saṃyojenti upanibandhanti, bhavaṃ vā bhavena saṃyojenti, tasmā “bhavasaṃyojanānī” ti vuccanti. Imāni bhavasaṃyojanāni arahato parikkhīṇāni pahīnāni ñāṇagginā daḍḍhāni, tena vuccati “parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano” ti.**  
> 'One who has totally ended the fetters of rebirth' (parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano) means: 'fetters of existence' are the ten fetters—the fetter of sensual passion, the fetter of aversion, of conceit, of wrong view, of doubt, of clinging to rites and rituals, of lust for existence, of jealousy, of stinginess, and the fetter of ignorance. For these indeed bind beings to existences, and tie existence to existence; therefore they are called 'fetters of existence'. These fetters of existence have been totally ended by the Arahant, abandoned, and burnt away by the fire of knowledge; therefore he is called 'one who has totally ended the fetters of rebirth'.  
>
