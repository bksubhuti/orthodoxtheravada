### Grammatical Analysis
**Māyā**: [f.] deceit; illusion; hiding faults; trickery. Derived from root *mā* (to measure, form, build, exhibit). 

### Orthodox Definition
As one of the sixteen minor defilements (*[upakkilesa](/glossary/upakkilesa/)*), *Māyā* is the specific psychological mechanism of deceit and cover-up. It is the active attempt to conceal one's transgressions, faults, and unwholesome actions from the community, one's teachers, or oneself.

The Vibhaṅga describes it (Vibh 2654:2-4): having done bodily, verbal and mental misconduct, one forms an evil wish in order to conceal it: "Mā maṃ jaññā" ("May no one know me"). The Papañcasūdanī gives its characteristic as concealing evil already done (*katapāpapaṭicchādanalakkhaṇā*), its function as hiding it and its manifestation as covering it (Ps-i 562:1). This is fundamentally opposed to the monastic principle of *āvikaroti* (revealing one's offenses openly), which is the absolute prerequisite for spiritual rehabilitation. 

Because the path to [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/) requires brutal honesty regarding one's own defilements, the presence of *māyā* ensures the practitioner remains trapped in their own illusions.

### Quote
> **(Ka) tattha katamā māyā? Idhekacco kāyena duccaritaṃ caritvā, vācāya duccaritaṃ caritvā, manasā duccaritaṃ caritvā tassa paṭicchādanahetuṃ pāpikaṃ icchaṃ paṇidahati. “Mā maṃ jaññā” ti icchati. “Mā maṃ jaññā” ti saṅkappeti.**  
> 894.(A) What is deceit (māyā) there? Here, someone, having committed bodily misconduct, having committed verbal misconduct, and having committed mental misconduct, formulates an evil desire for the purpose of concealing that. He wants, ‘May no one know me!’ He intends, ‘May no one know me!’  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Vibh §894, Khuddakavatthuvibhaṅga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=372&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=ODk0LihLYSkgdGF0dGhhIGthdGFtxIEgbcSBecSBPw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Vatthasutta* (MN 7, CST name of the "Vatthūpama" sutta; M-i 188:1) – *māyā cittassa upakkileso* (M-i 191:2).
* **Canonical**: *[Suttanipāta](/glossary/suttanipata/)* (Vasala Sutta, Sn 1.7, Sn 396:1: "Vipannadiṭṭhi māyāvī, taṃ jaññā vasalo iti"; also Sn 3.4, Sn 473 [Sn 1598:1]: "Yamhi na māyā vasati na māno", "in whom neither deceit nor conceit dwells"). The page's earlier citation of Sn 1.1 (Uraga Sutta) does not apply: *māyā* does not occur in it.
* **Commentary**: *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* on the Vatthasutta – māyā conceals one's own evil (Ps-i 562:1), while [sāṭheyya](/glossary/satheyya/) displays good qualities one does not have (Ps-i 562:2); also Ps-i 566:4-5.
