### Grammatical Analysis
**Bala**: [nt.] power; strength; unshakeable force. Derived from root *bal* (to live, be strong, nourish). 

### Orthodox Definition
The *Balas* are the **Five Spiritual Powers**, consisting of the exact same five mental factors as the faculties (*[indriya](/glossary/indriya/)*): Faith, Energy, Mindfulness, Concentration, and Wisdom. 

While the term *[indriya](/glossary/indriya/)* is used when these factors are actively exercising control over the mind and battling defilements, the term *bala* is applied when these factors have become so strong and stabilized that they are unshakeable (*akampiya*) by their opposites. 

For example, as the Manorathapūraṇī puts it (Mp-i 1521:3):
* The power of Faith (*[saddhā](/glossary/saddha/)-bala*) cannot be shaken by faithlessness (*assaddhiya*).
* The power of Energy (*[viriya](/glossary/viriya/)-bala*) cannot be shaken by laziness (*kosajja*).
* The power of Mindfulness (*sati-bala*) cannot be shaken by forgetfulness (*muṭṭhassacca*).
* The power of Concentration (*[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)-bala*) cannot be shaken by restlessness (*uddhacca*).
* The power of Wisdom (*[paññā](/glossary/panna/)-bala*) cannot be shaken by ignorance.

When fully matured on the supramundane path, these powers render the noble disciple absolutely invincible against the forces of Māra.

### Quote
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, pariyāyo yaṃ pariyāyaṃ āgamma yāni pañcindriyāni tāni pañca balāni honti, yāni pañca balāni tāni pañcindriyāni honti? Yaṃ, bhikkhave, saddhindriyaṃ taṃ saddhābalaṃ, yaṃ saddhābalaṃ taṃ saddhindriyaṃ; yaṃ vīriyindriyaṃ taṃ vīriyabalaṃ, yaṃ vīriyabalaṃ taṃ vīriyindriyaṃ; yaṃ satindriyaṃ taṃ satibalaṃ, yaṃ satibalaṃ taṃ satindriyaṃ; yaṃ samādhindriyaṃ taṃ samādhibalaṃ, yaṃ samādhibalaṃ taṃ samādhindriyaṃ; yaṃ paññindriyaṃ taṃ paññābalaṃ, yaṃ paññābalaṃ taṃ paññindriyaṃ.**  
> And what, monks, is the way owing to which the five faculties become the five powers, and the five powers become the five faculties? That, monks, which is the faculty of faith is the power of faith, and that which is the power of faith is the faculty of faith. That which is the faculty of energy is the power of energy, and that which is the power of energy is the faculty of energy. That which is the faculty of mindfulness is the power of mindfulness, and that which is the power of mindfulness is the faculty of mindfulness. That which is the faculty of concentration is the power of concentration, and that which is the power of concentration is the faculty of concentration. That which is the faculty of wisdom is the power of wisdom, and that which is the power of wisdom is the faculty of wisdom.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sāketa Sutta* (SN, Indriyasaṃyutta, S-v 1314-1318) – The way in which the five faculties are the five powers and the five powers the five faculties, with the simile of a river with an island in the middle (S-v 1317:7).
* **Canonical**: *Aṅguttara Nikāya* (Pañcaka Nipāta, A-v 56:1-3) – "These are the five powers: the power of faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom." Also *Bala-saṃyutta* (SN 50, S-v 1495-1545) on the powers; *Paṭisambhidāmagga* (Paṭis 936:2) defines the powers by *akampiyaṭṭhena* (in the sense of unshakeability).
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-i 1521:3; Mp-ii 39:2) – Each power is not shaken by its opposite: *akampiyaṭṭhena balāni*.
