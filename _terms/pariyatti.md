### Grammatical Analysis
**Pariyatti**: [f.] The scriptures; learning; mastery of the texts. From *pariyāpuṇāti* (to learn, master). The Aṭṭhakathā explains: “vuttappakāraṃ tepiṭakaṃ buddhavacanaṃ pariyāpuṇitabbaṭṭhena pariyatti”, the word of the Buddha in the three Piṭakas is *pariyatti* in the sense of what is to be learned (Nidd-a-i 1016:15). The Mūla gives the content of learning (*pariyāpuṭa*) as the nine *aṅgas*: *suttaṃ, geyyaṃ, veyyākaraṇaṃ, gāthā, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, jātakaṃ, abbhutadhammaṃ, vedallaṃ* (Nidd-i 758:4; A-v 584:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Pariyatti* is the first of the three kinds of the good Dhamma (*saddhamma*): *pariyattisaddhamma*, *paṭipattisaddhamma* and *adhigamasaddhamma*. The Samantapāsādikā defines the first: “piṭakattayasaṅgahitaṃ sabbampi buddhavacanaṃ pariyattisaddhammo nāma”, all the word of the Buddha comprised in the three Piṭakas (Sp-i 932:1-2); the second is the thirteen *dhutaguṇas*, the duties and the three trainings, and the third the four paths, four fruits and Nibbāna (Sp-i 932:3-4). The Mūla speaks of the *pariyattisāsana*, the teaching of learning, as one of the two ways the teaching can be lost, the other being the *paṭipattisāsana*, the teaching of practice (Nidd-i 758:2). The Papañcasūdanī lists three disappearances (*antaradhāna*): of *pariyatti* (the three Piṭakas), of *paṭivedha* (the penetration of the truths) and of *paṭipatti* (the way of practice) (Ps-iii 313:1-4).

On the relative importance, the Aṭṭhakathā says that *paṭivedha* and *paṭipatti* sometimes exist and sometimes do not, but for the lasting of the dispensation *pariyatti* is the measure (*sāsanaṭṭhitiyā pana pariyatti pamāṇaṃ*), and when *pariyatti* stands, the dispensation stands (*pariyattiyā ṭhitāya sāsanaṃ ṭhitaṃ hoti*) (Ps-iii 313:9, 314:4). Study is not an end in itself. In the Mūla the Buddha describes a monk who learns the Dhamma and spends the day in study, neglecting seclusion and internal tranquillity, as one intent on study (*pariyattibahulo*) but not a *dhammavihārī* (A-v 584:1-2, 589:1). The Aṭṭhakathā adds that the teacher's task is only the correct teaching; after that practice is the task of the disciples (Mp-v 172:5). The Alagaddūpama Sutta warns against learning the Dhamma for the wrong purpose, as in the simile of the snake (M-i 757:8).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāniddesa)
> **Mussate vāpi sāsananti. Dvīhi kāraṇehi sāsanaṃ mussati – pariyattisāsanampi mussati, paṭipattisāsanampi mussati. Katamaṃ pariyattisāsanaṃ? Yaṃ tassa pariyāpuṭaṃ – suttaṃ geyyaṃ veyyākaraṇaṃ gāthā udānaṃ itivuttakaṃ jātakaṃ abbhutadhammaṃ vedallaṃ – idaṃ pariyattisāsanaṃ.**  
> “The teaching also perishes”— The teaching perishes by two causes: the teaching of learning perishes, and the teaching of practice perishes. What is the teaching of learning? That which he has learned—discourse, mixed discourse, exposition, verses, exclamations, thus-said, birth stories, astounding, and question-and-answer—this is the teaching of learning.  
>  
> — [Khuddaka Nikāya](/glossary/khuddaka-nikaya/), Mahāniddesa (7. Tissametteyyasuttaniddeso, on Sn 821 “mussate vāpi sāsanaṃ”) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_16&page_number=111&name=mah%C4%81niddesap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=TXVzc2F0ZSB2xIFwaSBzxIFzYW5hbnRpLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Idha, bhikkhu, bhikkhu dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇāti – suttaṃ, geyyaṃ, veyyākaraṇaṃ, gāthaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, jātakaṃ, abbhutadhammaṃ, vedallaṃ. So tāya dhammapariyattiyā divasaṃ atināmeti, riñcati paṭisallānaṃ, nānuyuñjati ajjhattaṃ cetosamathaṃ.**  
> “Here, bhikkhu, a bhikkhu masters the Dhamma—discourse, stanzas, expositions, verses, exclamations, sayings, birth stories, marvels, and answers to questions. He spends the day in that study of the Dhamma; he neglects seclusion and does not commit himself to internal tranquillity of mind.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.73, Paṭhamadhammavihārīsutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=76&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIElkaGEsIGJoaWtraHUsIGJoaWtraHUgZGhhbW1h4bmDIHBhcml5xIFwdeG5h8SBdGk%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Iti kho, bhikkhu, desito mayā pariyattibahulo, desito paññattibahulo, desito sajjhāyabahulo, desito vitakkabahulo, desito dhammavihārī.**  
> Thus, bhikkhu, I have taught you the one intent on study, the one intent on description, the one intent on recitation, the one intent on thinking, and the one who dwells in the Dhamma.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.73, Paṭhamadhammavihārīsutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=77&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEl0aSBraG8sIGJoaWtraHUsIGRlc2l0byBtYXnEgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Idaṃ pariyattisāsananti idaṃ vuttappakāraṃ tepiṭakaṃ buddhavacanaṃ pariyāpuṇitabbaṭṭhena pariyatti, anusāsanaṭṭhena sāsananti katvā pariyattisāsanaṃ.**  
> “This teaching of learning” means this Tipitaka word of the Buddha mentioned above; it is called “learning” (pariyatti) because it is something to be learned, and “teaching” (sāsana) because it is an instruction.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Manorathapūraṇī)
> **Dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇātīti navaṅgavasena catusaccadhammaṃ pariyāpuṇāti vaḷañjeti katheti.**  
> 'He masters the Dhamma' means he learns, practices, and speaks the Dhamma of the Four Truths according to the ninefold teaching.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Saddhammaṭṭhitiyāti tividho saddhammo – pariyattisaddhammo, paṭipattisaddhammo, adhigamasaddhammoti. Tattha piṭakattayasaṅgahitaṃ sabbampi buddhavacanaṃ “pariyattisaddhammo” nāma. Terasa dhutaguṇā, cuddasa khandhakavattāni, dveasīti mahāvattāni, sīlasamādhivipassanāti ayaṃ “paṭipattisaddhammo” nāma. Cattāro ariyamaggā cattāri ca sāmaññaphalāni nibbānañcāti ayaṃ “adhigamasaddhammo” nāma.**  
> For the continuation of the true Dhamma (saddhammaṭṭhitiyā) means: the true Dhamma is threefold—the true Dhamma of scripture, the true Dhamma of practice, and the true Dhamma of attainment. Therein, the entire word of the Buddha contained within the three Pitakas is called the “true Dhamma of scripture.” The thirteen ascetic practices, the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, virtue, concentration, and insight are called the “true Dhamma of practice.” The four noble paths, the four fruits of the life of a recluse, and Nibbāna are called the “true Dhamma of attainment.”  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Pārājikakaṇḍa-aṭṭhakathā, on “saddhammaṭṭhitiyā”, the reasons for laying down the training rules) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=190&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FkZGhhbW1h4bmt4bmtaGl0aXnEgXRpIHRpdmlkaG8gc2FkZGhhbW1vIOKAkyBwYXJpeWF0dGlzYWRkaGFtbW8sIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc2FkZGhhbW1vLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Tīṇi hi antaradhānāni nāma pariyattiantaradhānaṃ, paṭivedhaantaradhānaṃ, paṭipattiantaradhānanti. Tattha pariyattīti tīṇi piṭakāni. Paṭivedhoti saccapaṭivedho. Paṭipattīti paṭipadā. Tattha paṭivedho ca paṭipatti ca hotipi na hotipi.**  
> ‘There are indeed three disappearances,’ namely, the disappearance of learning, the disappearance of penetration, and the disappearance of practice. There, ‘learning’ is the three baskets. ‘Penetration’ is the penetration of the truths. ‘Practice’ is the path. There, penetration and practice sometimes exist and sometimes do not.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (on MN 115, Bahudhātuka Sutta, the three disappearances) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_03&page_number=80&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VMSr4bmHaSBoaSBhbnRhcmFkaMSBbsSBbmkgbsSBbWEgcGFyaXlhdHRpYW50YXJhZGjEgW5h4bmDLCBwYeG5rWl2ZWRoYWFudGFyYWRoxIFuYeG5gyw%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Iti paṭivedho ca paṭipatti ca hotipi na hotipi, sāsanaṭṭhitiyā pana pariyatti pamāṇaṃ. Tasmā pariyattiyā ṭhitāya sāsanaṃ ṭhitaṃ hoti.**  
> Thus, penetration and practice sometimes exist and sometimes do not, but learning is the measure for the persistence of the Teaching. Therefore, when learning stands, the Teaching stands.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (on MN 115, Bahudhātuka Sutta; Ps-iii 313:9 and 314:4) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_03&page_number=80&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SXRpIHBh4bmtaXZlZGhvIGNhIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpIGNhIGhvdGlwaQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Sutta**: *Alagaddūpama Sutta* (MN 22, M-i 757:8) – learning the Dhamma (*dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇanti*) for the wrong purpose; *Paṭhamadhammavihārī Sutta* (AN 10.73, A-v 582–589) – the one intent on study and the one who dwells in the Dhamma.
* **Canonical**: *Mahāniddesa* (Nidd-i 758:2-4) – *pariyattisāsana* and *paṭipattisāsana*; *[Milindapañha](/glossary/milindapanha/)* (Mil 743:8, 2422:2) – *pariyatti* beside *āgama* and *adhigama*.
* **Commentary**: *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (Sp-i 932), *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* (Ps-iii 313–314, on MN 115), *[Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/)* (Mp-v 171–172, on AN 10.73) and the Mahāniddesa commentary (Nidd-a-i 1016:15).
