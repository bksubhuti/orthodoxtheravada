### Grammatical Analysis
**Majjhimanikāya**: [m.] the Collection of Middle-length Discourses. A compound of *majjhima* (middle) + *nikāya* (collection). The Papañcasūdanī calls it "the excellent Majjhima collection, marked by suttas of middle length" (*majjhimapamāṇasuttaṅkitassa … majjhimāgamavarassa*, Ps-i 15:1). The Ṭīkā explains *majjhimapaṇṇāsā* as "the fifty that exists in the middle" (*majjhe bhavā majjhimā*, Ps-t-i 78:6).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Majjhima-nikāya* is the second of the five Nikāyas, after the Dīgha (Sv-i 173:2). The word does not occur in the Mūla texts except in the CST book titles, so the earliest sources in TPR are the Aṭṭhakathās.

The Papañcasūdanī says that the Majjhima collection is comprised of three fifties: the Root Fifty, the Middle Fifty and the Upper Fifty; each fifty has five chapters, making fifteen chapters, and by discourses one hundred and fifty-two (Ps-i 40:1-3). The Paramatthajotikā gives the same count, beginning with the Mūlapariyāya Sutta (Pj-i 24:2; Sv-i 120:4).

The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī records that at the First Council, after the Dīgha Nikāya had been entrusted to the Venerable Ānanda's pupils, the elders recited the Majjhima Nikāya, "of eighty bhāṇavāras", and entrusted it to the pupils of the Elder Sāriputta, the Dhamma-General, saying "you should cherish this" (Sv-i 103:1-104:2). Examples in the collection: the Sabbāsava Sutta (MN 2) and the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī, Nidānakathā)
> **Tattha majjhimasaṅgīti nāma paṇṇāsato mūlapaṇṇāsā majjhimapaṇṇāsā uparipaṇṇāsāti paṇṇāsattayasaṅgahā. Vaggato ekekāya paṇṇāsāya pañca pañca vagge katvā pannarasavaggasamāyogā. Suttato diyaḍḍhasuttasataṃ dve ca suttantā.**  
> 1. In this context, the Middle Collection (majjhimasaṅgīti) is summarized by three fifties: the Root Fifty, the Middle Fifty, and the Final Fifty. By chapters, each fifty is divided into five chapters, resulting in fifteen chapters. By discourses, there are one hundred and fifty-two discourses.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī, opening verses)
> **Majjhimapamāṇasuttaṅkitassa idha majjhimāgamavarassa; Buddhānubuddhasaṃvaṇṇitassa paravādamathanassa.**  
> In this excellent Middle Collection (majjhimāgamavarassa), which is marked by the Middle-length discourses, Which was praised by the Buddha and his disciples, and which crushes the views of others.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā, Ganthārambhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=1&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=TWFqamhpbWFwYW3EgeG5h2FzdXR0YeG5hWtpdGFzc2EgaWRoYSBtYWpqaGltxIFnYW1hdmFyYXNzYTs%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, the First Council)
> **Tato anantaraṃ asītibhāṇavāraparimāṇaṃ majjhimanikāyaṃ saṅgāyitvā dhammasenāpatisāriputtattherassa nissitake paṭicchāpesuṃ – “imaṃ tumhe pariharathā” ti.**  
> Immediately after that, having recited the Majjhimanikāya, consisting of eighty bhāṇavāras, they entrusted it to the disciples of the Dhamma-General, the Elder Sāriputta— “You should cherish this.”  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Papañcasūdanī-ṭīkā)
> **Pañca dasakā paṇṇāsā, mūle ādimhi paṇṇāsā, mūlabhūtā vā paṇṇāsā mūlapaṇṇāsā. Majjhe bhavā majjhimā, majjhimā ca sā paṇṇāsā cāti majjhimapaṇṇāsā. Upari uddhaṃ paṇṇāsā uparipaṇṇāsā.**  
> There are five decads of fifty; the fifty at the beginning in the root, or the fifty that constitutes the root, is the Root Fifty (mūlapaṇṇāsā). The one existing in the middle is middle; the fifty that is middle is the Middle Fifty (majjhimapaṇṇāsā). The fifty above or upward is the Upper Fifty (uparipaṇṇāsā).  
>  
> — Papañcasūdanī-ṭīkā (Mūlapaṇṇāsa-ṭīkā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_ma_01&page_number=19&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sa%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=UGHDsWNhIGRhc2FrxIEgcGHhuYfhuYfEgXPEgSwgbcWrbGUgxIFkaW1oaSBwYeG5h%2BG5h8SBc8SBLA%3D%3D)
