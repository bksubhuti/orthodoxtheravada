### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṅkhāra**: [m.] conditioned thing; formation; mental concoction; volitional construction. Formed by prefix *saṃ* (together) + root *kar* (to do, make). The Mūla gives the explanation by verb: *saṅkhatamabhisaṅkharontīti saṅkhārā*, "they construct the conditioned, therefore they are called saṅkhārā" (S-iii 456, Khajjanīya Sutta). The Visuddhimagga repeats the same words.

### Orthodox Definition
*Saṅkhāra* is a multi-layered term that must be read in its context. The *Visuddhimagga* (XVII) first gives the sense of the verb, *saṅkhatamabhisaṅkharontīti saṅkhārā*, and then distinguishes the saṅkhāras of dependent origination from those that "appear under the term saṅkhāra"; the main contexts are these:

1. **As an aggregate (*saṅkhārakkhandha*):** the fourth of the five aggregates. The *Vibhaṅga* defines it as whatever saṅkhārā are past, future or present, internal or external, and so on, gathered together; in the Suttanta analysis the saṅkhāras are the volitions (*cetanā*) born of the six kinds of contact. The *Atthasālinī* says it is one phenomenon "in the sense of a group and in the sense of constructing (*abhisaṅkharaṇaṭṭhena*)". The *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* gives its content in the Abhidhamma analysis: the fifty mental factors (*cetasika*) other than feeling (*[vedanā](/glossary/vedana/)*) and perception (*[saññā](/glossary/sanna/)*).
2. **In dependent origination (*avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā*):** the Vibhaṅga Sutta (SN 12.2) says there are three, bodily, verbal and mental (*kāyasaṅkhāro, vacīsaṅkhāro, cittasaṅkhāro*). The *Visuddhimagga* adds the three of merit, demerit and the imperturbable (*puññāpuññāneñjābhisaṅkhārā*), and says that all these six are simply mundane wholesome and unwholesome volitions (*lokiyakusalākusalacetanāmatta*). In the *Visuddhimagga*'s own definition, they have the characteristic of constructing (*abhisaṅkharaṇalakkhaṇā*), the function of accumulating (*āyūhanarasā*), manifest as volition (*cetanāpaccupaṭṭhānā*), and have ignorance as proximate cause (*avijjāpadaṭṭhānā*).
3. **In the universal statement (*sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā*):** the *Visuddhimagga* calls all conditioned phenomena that arise from conditions in such passages as *aniccā vata saṅkhārā* "processed formations" (*saṅkhatasaṅkhārā*). The four kinds it lists under the word are *saṅkhatasaṅkhāra*, *abhisaṅkhatasaṅkhāra*, *abhisaṅkharaṇakasaṅkhāra* and *payogābhisaṅkhāra*.

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, the verb)
> **“Kiñca, bhikkhave, saṅkhāre vadetha? Saṅkhatamabhisaṅkharontīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ saṅkhārā ’ ti vuccati.**  
> “And why, bhikkhus, do you call them volitional formations? ‘They construct the conditioned,’ bhikkhus, therefore they are called volitional formations.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, the three saṅkhāras)
> **“Katame ca, bhikkhave, saṅkhārā? Tayome, bhikkhave, saṅkhārā – kāyasaṅkhāro, vacīsaṅkhāro, cittasaṅkhāro. Ime vuccanti, bhikkhave, saṅkhārā.**  
> ‘‘And what, monks, are volitional formations? Monks, there are these three volitional formations: bodily volitional formation, verbal volitional formation, mental volitional formation. These, monks, are called volitional formations.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 12.2, Vibhaṅga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=245&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtZSBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYeG5hWtoxIFyxIE%2F)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamo saṅkhārakkhandho? Ye keci saṅkhārā atītānāgatapaccuppannā ajjhattā vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikā vā sukhumā vā hīnā vā paṇītā vā ye dūre santike vā, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati saṅkhārakkhandho.**  
> 20. Among these, what is the formations aggregate? Whatever formations (saṅkhārā) are past, future, or present; internal or external; gross or fine; inferior or superior; distant or close—gathering these together and having summarized them, this is called the formations aggregate (saṅkhārakkhandho).  
>  
> — Vibhaṅga (Khandhavibhaṅga, Saṅkhārakkhandha) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=7&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjAuIFRhdHRoYSBrYXRhbW8gc2HhuYVraMSBcmFra2hhbmRobz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Rāsaṭṭhena ca abhisaṅkharaṇaṭṭhena ca ekova dhammo saṅkhārakkhandho.**  
> And in the sense of a group and in the sense of constructing (abhisaṅkharaṇaṭṭhena), only one mental phenomenon is the formations aggregate.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=198&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UsSBc2Hhua3hua1oZW5hIGNhIGFiaGlzYeG5hWtoYXJh4bmHYeG5reG5rWhlbmEgY2EgZWtvdmEgZGhhbW1v)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the senses of saṅkhāra)
> **Saṅkhatamabhisaṅkharontīti saṅkhārā. Apica avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā, saṅkhārasaddena āgatasaṅkhārāti duvidhā saṅkhārā. Tattha puññāpuññāneñjābhisaṅkhārā tayo, kāyavacīcittasaṅkhārā tayoti ime cha avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā. Saṅkhatasaṅkhāro, abhisaṅkhatasaṅkhāro, abhisaṅkharaṇakasaṅkhāro, payogābhisaṅkhāroti ime pana cattāro saṅkhāra-saddena āgatasaṅkhārā. Tattha “aniccā vata saṅkhārā” tiādīsu (dī. ni.2.221,272; saṃ. ni.1.186) vuttā sabbepi sappaccayā dhammā saṅkhatasaṅkhārā nāma.**  
> They process the processed; thus they are volitional formations (saṅkhārā). Furthermore, volitional formations are of two kinds: volitional formations with ignorance as condition, and volitional formations that appear under the term ‘formation’. Among these, the three—the volitional formation of merit, the volitional formation of demerit, and the volitional formation of the imperturbable—and the three—the bodily, verbal, and mental volitional formations—these six are the volitional formations with ignorance as condition. However, these four—the processed formation, the kamma-processed formation, the act of processing formation, and the momentum-impetus formation—are the types of formations that appear under the term ‘formation’. Among these, all conditioned phenomena mentioned in such passages as ‘Formations are truly impermanent’ are called processed formations (saṅkhatasaṅkhārā).  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVII) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=158&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=U2HhuYVraGF0YW1hYmhpc2HhuYVraGFyb250xKt0aSBzYeG5hWtoxIFyxIEu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, lakkhaṇa and so on)
> **Abhisaṅkharaṇalakkhaṇā saṅkhārā , āyūhanarasā, cetanāpaccupaṭṭhānā, avijjāpadaṭṭhānā.**  
> Volitional formations (saṅkhārā) have the characteristic of being prepared (abhisaṅkharaṇalakkhaṇā); their function is to accumulate (āyūhanarasā); they are manifested as volition (cetanāpaccupaṭṭhānā); and their proximate cause is ignorance (avijjāpadaṭṭhānā).  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVII, §588) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=159&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=QWJoaXNh4bmFa2hhcmHhuYdhbGFra2hh4bmHxIEgc2HhuYVraMSBcsSBICwgxIF5xatoYW5hcmFzxIEsIGNldGFuxIFwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLCBhdmlqasSBcGFkYeG5reG5rWjEgW7EgS4%3D)
