### Grammatical Analysis
**Arahat** (*arahaṃ*, *arahant*): [m.] a perfected one; one whose taints are destroyed. The Visuddhimagga explains *arahaṃ* with five reasons: *ārakattā arīnaṃ arānañca hatattā paccayādīnaṃ arahattā pāpakaraṇe rahābhāvā*, "because of remoteness, because of having destroyed the enemies and the spokes, because of worthiness of requisites and so on, and because of the absence of secret evil-doing" (Vism-i 1188:1). These are given there for the epithet *arahaṃ* of the Buddha, who "stands remote from all defilements, having destroyed the defilements together with their residual traces by the path" (Vism-i 1188:2); the defilements are the enemies destroyed by the path (*arīnaṃ hatattā*, Vism-i 1191:1); and he is worthy of the special honours (*paccayādīnaṃ arahattā*, Vism-i 1204:6).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla the arahant is stated in a fixed formula: *arahaṃ khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano sammadaññā vimutto*, "an arahant, whose taints are destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, attained the true goal, whose fetters of existence are utterly destroyed, liberated by right knowledge" (It 401:2; also A-iii 293:1). The Puggalapaññatti, in the Abhidhamma, defines the person: *yassa puggalassa rūparāgo arūparāgo māno uddhaccaṃ avijjā anavasesā pahīnā – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo "arahā"*, "the person whose attachment to form, attachment to the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance are abandoned without remainder is called an arahant" (Pp 234:2). These five are the *pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*, the five higher fetters (S-v 400:1-3; A-ix 462:1-3).

Arahatship is the end of the taints (*[āsava](/glossary/asava/)*): "a bhikkhu is an arahant, one whose taints are destroyed" (A-iii 59:2). The Itivuttaka speaks of the Nibbāna element with no residue left (*anupādisesā nibbānadhātu*) for the arahant, whose feelings, not delighted in, will be extinguished right here (It 401:1-3). The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha says that wholesome and unwholesome javanas are not found in those whose taints are ended (*khīṇāsavānaṃ kusalākusalajavanāni na labbhanti*, Abhidh-s 357:1), and its ṭīkā says that their javanas are kiriya javanas (*khīṇāsavānaṃ … kiriyajavanānipi*, Abhidh-s-t 1247:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Itivuttaka)
> **“Katamā ca, bhikkhave, anupādisesā nibbānadhātu? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu arahaṃ hoti khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano sammadaññā vimutto. Tassa idheva, bhikkhave, sabbavedayitāni anabhinanditāni sīti bhavissanti.**  
> “And what, bhikkhus, is the Nibbāna element with no residue left? Here, a bhikkhu is an Arahant, one whose taints are destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, attained the true goal, whose fetters of existence are utterly destroyed, and who is completely liberated by right knowledge. For him, right here in this life, all that is experienced, not being delighted in, will be extinguished.  
>  
> — [Itivuttaka](/glossary/itivuttaka/) (Iti 44, Nibbānadhātu Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_04&page_number=221&name=itivuttakap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtxIEgY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgYW51cMSBZGlzZXPEgSBuaWJixIFuYWRoxIF0dT8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Puggalapaññatti)
> **Rūparāgaarūparāgamānauddhaccaavijjāya anavasesappahānāya paṭipanno puggalo arahattaphalasacchikiriyāya paṭipanno. Yassa puggalassa rūparāgo arūparāgo māno uddhaccaṃ avijjā anavasesā pahīnā – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo “arahā”.**  
> 44. A person working for putting away attachment to form and the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance without any residuum is one working for the fruition stage of Arahantship. The person whose attachment to form and the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance has been entirely put away is said to be an Arahant.  
>  
> — [Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/) (Pp 234, §44 and the definition of the arahant) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_04&page_number=120&name=puggalapa%C3%B1%C3%B1attip%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDQuIFLFq3BhcsSBZ2FhcsWrcGFyxIFnYW3EgW5hdWRkaGFjY2FhdmlqasSBeWEgYW5hdmFzZXNhcHBhaMSBbsSBeWEgcGHhua1pcGFubm8gcHVnZ2FsbyBhcmFoYXR0YXBoYWxhc2FjY2hpa2lyaXnEgXlh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tattha ārakattā arīnaṃ arānañca hatattā paccayādīnaṃ arahattā pāpakaraṇe rahābhāvāti imehi tāva kāraṇehi so bhagavā arahanti anussarati. Ārakā hi so sabbakilesehi suvidūravidūre ṭhito maggena savāsanānaṃ kilesānaṃ viddhaṃsitattāti ārakattā arahaṃ.**  
> 125. Herein, first of all, he recollects that the Blessed One is an Arahant (arahaṃ) for these reasons: because of remoteness, because of having destroyed his enemies and the spokes, because of his worthiness of requisites and so on, and because of the absence of secret evil-doing. For he stands remote, very, very far from all defilements, because he has destroyed all defilements along with their residual traces by means of the path; thus he is an Arahant because of remoteness (ārakattā arahaṃ).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-i 1188, Ch. VII, Buddhānussati) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=192&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MTI1LiBUYXR0aGEgxIFyYWthdHTEgSBhcsSrbmHhuYMgYXLEgW5hw7FjYSBoYXRhdHTEgQ%3D%3D)
