### Grammatical Analysis
**Pācittiya**: [nt.] The name of a class of offences and of the training rules that establish them; also used for the offence incurred (“pācittiyaṃ hoti”, Kkh 1597:3). The word has no explanation of its derivation in the Mūla or in the Aṭṭhakathā passages checked (searches for *pācittiyanti*, *pācittiyāti* and *pācittiyasadda* found none), so the page gives none. Related class names: *nissaggiya pācittiya* (the thirty rules with forfeiture, Dvem-bhk 138:1), *pārājika*, *saṅghādisesa*, *pāṭidesanīya*, *sekhiya*.

### Orthodox Definition
The *Pācittiya* class is the largest in the monks' Pātimokkha: “Uddiṭṭhā kho āyasmanto dvenavuti pācittiyā dhammā”, ninety-two rules (Dvem-bhk 336:1); for the nuns “chasaṭṭhisatā pācittiyā dhammā”, one hundred and sixty-six (Dvem-bhni 1020:1; the Aṭṭhakathā explains them as seventy shared with the monks and ninety-six not shared, Kkh 2672:1). The First Council arranged the ninety-two rules under the name “pācittiyāni” (Sp-i 110:8). They are lighter than *[Pārājika](/glossary/parajika/)* and *[Saṅghādisesa](/glossary/sanghadisesa/)*.

The rules cover speech, daily conduct, property and food. Examples in the Pātimokkha: knowingly lying (1, “Sampajānamusāvāde pācittiyaṃ”, Dvem-bhk 144:1), digging the earth (10, Dvem-bhk 162:1), destroying plants (11, Dvem-bhk 165:1), eating food at the wrong time, after midday (37, Dvem-bhk 219:1) and drinking liquor (51, Dvem-bhk 249:1). The Aṭṭhakathā states the logic: for one who knowingly lies, a *pācittiya* arises on that account (Kkh 1597:3). The rule about teaching Dhamma to a person with an umbrella in hand is a *sekhiya*, not a *pācittiya* (Sekhiya 57, Dvem-bhk 459:1).

A light offence (*lahukā āpatti*) is cleared by acknowledgement: the monk goes to another bhikkhu, squats with joined palms and says “ahaṃ, āvuso, itthannāmaṃ āpattiṃ āpanno; taṃ paṭidesemī”; the other asks “passasī”ti, he answers “āma passāmī”, and is told “āyatiṃ saṃvareyyāsī”, practise restraint in the future (Vin-iv 1174:6-13). Some *pācittiyas* need more than confession: for the thirty *nissaggiya pācittiyas*, the item must first be forfeited (Dvem-bhk 120:1, “nissaggiyaṃ pācittiyaṃ”).

### Quote (Mūla: Cūḷavagga)
> **Idha pana, bhikkhave, bhikkhu lahukaṃ āpattiṃ āpanno hoti. Tena, bhikkhave, bhikkhunā ekaṃ bhikkhuṃ upasaṅkamitvā ekaṃsaṃ uttarāsaṅgaṃ karitvā ukkuṭikaṃ nisīditvā añjaliṃ paggahetvā evamassa vacanīyo – ‘ ahaṃ, āvuso, itthannāmaṃ āpattiṃ āpanno; taṃ paṭidesemī ’ ti. Tena vattabbo – ‘ passasī ’ ti? ‘ Āma passāmī ’ ti. ‘ Āyatiṃ saṃvareyyāsī ’ ti.**  
> “Here, monks, a bhikkhu has committed a light offense. That bhikkhu, monks, having approached a single bhikkhu, reverently uncovering his outer robe from one shoulder, squatting on his heels, and raising his joined palms, should say to him: ‘Friend, I have committed such and such an offense; I confess it.’ The other should say to him: ‘Do you see it?’ ‘Yes, I see it.’ ‘You should practice restraint in the future.’  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Cūḷavagga, 239, the settling of a light offence by acknowledgement) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_04&page_number=234&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SWRoYSBwYW5hLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGJoaWtraHUgbGFodWth4bmDIMSBcGF0dGnhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Pācittiyesu musāvādavaggassa paṭhame sampajānamusāvādeti pubbepi jānitvā vacanakkhaṇepi jānantasseva musāvādabhaṇane. Bhaṇanañca nāma idha abhūtassa vā bhūtataṃ, bhūtassa vā abhūtataṃ katvā kāyena vā vācāya vā viññāpanappayogo, nimittatthe cetaṃ bhummavacanaṃ. Tasmā yo sampajānamusāvādaṃ vadati, tassa taṃnimittaṃ taṃhetu tappaccayā pācittiyaṃ hotīti evamettha aññesu ca īdisesu attho veditabbo.**  
> Among the offenses of expiation, in the first of the Chapter on False Speech, “telling a conscious lie” (sampajānamusāvāde) means telling a lie while knowing it both beforehand and at the very moment of speaking. “Speaking” here means the attempt to make understand by body or speech, having made what is not a fact into a fact, or what is a fact into not a fact; this is used in the sense of the reason. Therefore, if anyone speaks a conscious lie, for that reason, due to that cause, on account of that, an offense of expiation occurs; thus, the meaning should be understood here and in other similar cases.  
>  
> — Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī (Pācittiyakaṇḍa, 1. Musāvādasikkhāpadavaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=190&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=UMSBY2l0dGl5ZXN1IG11c8SBdsSBZGF2YWdnYXNzYSBwYeG5rWhhbWUgc2FtcGFqxIFuYW11c8SBdsSBZGV0aSBwdWJiZXBpIGrEgW5pdHbEgQ%3D%3D)
