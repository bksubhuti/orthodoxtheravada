### Grammatical Analysis
**Dīghanikāya**: [m.] The Collection of Long Discourses. Formed by *dīgha* (long) + *nikāya* (collection, body). The Mūla uses the name in the colophons ("Dīghanikāyo", D-i 2; "Dīghanikāyo samatto", D-iii 1908:1-1910:1); the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī also calls it *dīghāgama* (Sv-i 39:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Dīgha-nikāya* is the first collection within the [Sutta-piṭaka](/glossary/sutta-pitaka/) (Sv-i 120:4 lists it first among the five nikāyas). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says that it consists of three divisions by way of sections (*vaggato tivaggo*): the Sīlakkhandhavagga, the Mahāvagga and the Pāthikavagga, and that by way of discourses it is a collection of thirty-four suttas (*catuttiṃsasuttasaṅgaho*). Among the divisions the Sīlakkhandhavagga is the first and among the discourses the Brahmajāla; the introductory story of the Brahmajāla ("Thus have I heard…") was spoken by the Venerable Ānanda at the time of the First Great Council (Sv-i 39:1-4). Elsewhere it describes it as "the collection of the thirty-four suttas beginning with the Brahmajāla" (*Brahmajālādicatuttiṃsasuttasaṅgaho dīghanikāyo*, Sv-i 120:4).

Among its discourses are the Brahmajāla (DN 1, which the Buddha tells Ānanda to remember also as the "Net of Views", *diṭṭhijāla*, D-i 214:3), the Sāmaññaphala (DN 2), the Mahāparinibbāna (DN 16; D-ii 287 "3. Mahāparinibbānasuttaṃ" is the third sutta of the Mahāvagga) and the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna (DN 22; D-ii 1341 "9. Mahāsatipaṭṭhānasuttaṃ", the ninth of the Mahāvagga).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya (colophon))
> **Pāthikavaggapāḷi niṭṭhitā. Tīhi vaggehi paṭimaṇḍito sakalo Dīghanikāyo samatto.**  
> The Pāthika Vagga is concluded. Adorned with three sections, the entire Dīgha Nikāya is finished.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Tattha dīghāgamo nāma sīlakkhandhavaggo, mahāvaggo, pāthikavaggoti vaggato tivaggo hoti; suttato catuttiṃsasuttasaṅgaho. Tassa vaggesu sīlakkhandhavaggo ādi, suttesu brahmajālaṃ . Brahmajālassāpi “evaṃ me sutan” tiādikaṃ āyasmatā ānandena paṭhamamahāsaṅgītikāle vuttaṃ nidānamādi.**  
> There, the Dīgha Nikāya consists of three divisions: the Sīlakkhandhavagga, the Mahāvagga, and the Pāthikavagga; it is a collection of thirty-four discourses. Among those divisions, the Sīlakkhandhavagga is the first; among the discourses, the Brahmajāla is the first. The introductory story of the Brahmajāla, beginning with 'Thus have I heard', was spoken by Venerable Ānanda at the time of the First Great Council.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, Nidānakathā, Sv-i 38-39) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=2&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGTEq2doxIFnYW1vIG7EgW1hIHPEq2xha2toYW5kaGF2YWdnbywgbWFoxIF2YWdnbywgcMSBdGhpa2F2YWdnb3Rp)
