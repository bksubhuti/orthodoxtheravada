### Grammatical Analysis
**Tathāgata**: [m.] Thus Gone; Thus Come; The One who has arrived at Truth. A complex compound, generally resolved as *tathā* (thus, in that way) + *gata* (gone) OR *āgata* (come).

### Orthodox Definition
*Tathāgata* is the title the Buddha uses of himself: in the Itivuttaka the Blessed One says that the world has been fully understood by the Tathāgata and that the Tathāgata is released from the world (It 1165:1).

The Mūla itself gives reasons for the name. Whatever is seen, heard, sensed or cognized in the world is fully understood by the Tathāgata, "therefore he is called Tathāgata"; whatever he speaks from the night of his awakening to the night of his final Nibbāna is so and not otherwise; and as he speaks so he acts, and as he acts so he speaks (It 1166-1168; also DN 29, D-iii 449:1-6, and AN 4.23).

The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī explains the word in **eight ways** (Sv-i 359:2):
1. *Tathā āgato*: he has come in the way the earlier Buddhas came, by the same resolve and the fulfilling of the thirty perfections (Sv-i 360).
2. *Tathā gato*: he has gone in the way they went, as the new-born Bodhisatta who took seven steps (Sv-i 366-367).
3. *Tathalakkhaṇaṃ āgato*: he has arrived at the true characteristic of the dhammas (the characteristic of each, such as of the elements, bases and truths; Sv-i 396-397).
4. *Tathadhamme yāthāvato abhisambuddho*: he has fully awakened to the true dhammas, which are the four noble truths (Sv-i 401:2).
5. *Tathadassitāya*: because of true seeing, he knows and sees every object in all its aspects, and it is just so, with no falseness (Sv-i 403).
6. *Tathavāditāya*: because of true speech, everything he said between the awakening and the final Nibbāna, forty-five years, is just so and not otherwise (Sv-i 404; AN 4.23).
7. *Tathākāritāya*: because he acts as he speaks and speaks as he acts (Sv-i 406).
8. *Abhibhavanaṭṭhena*: in the sense of overcoming, he overcomes all beings in the immeasurable world-systems by virtue, concentration, wisdom and liberation (Sv-i 407).

When wanderers asked Venerable Anurādha whether the Tathāgata exists, does not exist, and so on after death, the Buddha asked whether, when the Tathāgata is not found as a truth and reality even here and now, that answer was right (S-iv 1960:5, SN 44.2).

### Quote
> **“Loko, bhikkhave, tathāgatena abhisambuddho lokasmā tathāgato visaṃyutto. “Yaṃ, bhikkhave, sadevakassa lokassa samārakassa sabrahmakassa sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya diṭṭhaṃ sutaṃ mutaṃ viññātaṃ pattaṃ pariyesitaṃ anuvicaritaṃ manasā yasmā taṃ tathāgatena abhisambuddhaṃ, tasmā tathāgatoti vuccati. “Yañca, bhikkhave, rattiṃ tathāgato anuttaraṃ sammāsambodhiṃ abhisambujjhati, yañca rattiṃ anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbāyati, yaṃ etasmiṃ antare bhāsati lapati niddisati, sabbaṃ taṃ tatheva hoti no aññathā, tasmā tathāgatoti vuccati. “Yathāvādī, bhikkhave, tathāgato tathākārī, yathākārī tathāvādī, iti yathāvādī tathākārī yathākārī tathāvādī, tasmā tathāgatoti vuccati.**  
> “Bhikkhus, the world has been fully understood by the Tathāgata; the Tathāgata is released from the world. “Bhikkhus, in the world with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, with its recluses and brahmins, among humankind with its princes and people, whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized, attained, sought, and reflected upon by the mind—that is fully understood by the Tathāgata: therefore he is called the Tathāgata. “Bhikkhus, from the night when the Tathāgata awakened to unsurpassed full enlightenment until the night when he passes away into the Nibbāna element with no residue left, whatever he speaks, utters, and explains—all that is just so and not otherwise: therefore he is called the Tathāgata. “As the Tathāgata says, so he does; as the Tathāgata does, so he says: therefore he is called the Tathāgata.  
>

> **Tathāgatassāti aṭṭhahi kāraṇehi bhagavā tathāgato. Tathā āgatoti tathāgato, tathā gatoti tathāgato, tathalakkhaṇaṃ āgatoti tathāgato, tathadhamme yāthāvato abhisambuddhoti tathāgato, tathadassitāya tathāgato, tathavāditāya tathāgato, tathākāritāya tathāgato, abhibhavanaṭṭhena tathāgatoti.**  
> Regarding Tathāgatassa, the Blessed One is the Tathāgata for eight reasons. He is the Tathāgata because he has come that way; he is the Tathāgata because he has gone that way; he is the Tathāgata because he has come to the true characteristic; he is the Tathāgata because he has fully awakened to the true principles; he is the Tathāgata because of his true seeing; he is the Tathāgata because of his true speech; he is the Tathāgata because of his true action; he is the Tathāgata by the power of overcoming.  
>

> **Kathaṃ bhagavā tathā āgatoti tathāgato ? Yathā sabbalokahitāya ussukkamāpannā purimakā sammāsambuddhā āgatā, yathā vipassī bhagavā āgato, yathā sikhī bhagavā, yathā vessabhū bhagavā, yathā kakusandho bhagavā, yathā koṇāgamano bhagavā, yathā kassapo bhagavā āgato. Kiṃ vuttaṃ hoti? Yena abhinīhārena ete bhagavanto āgatā, teneva amhākampi bhagavā āgato.**  
> How is the Blessed One the Tathāgata because he has come that way? Just as the previous Buddhas who had reached a state of zeal for the benefit of the entire world came, just as the Blessed One Vipassī came, just as the Blessed One Sikhī, the Blessed One Vessabhū, the Blessed One Kakusandha, the Blessed One Koṇāgamana, the Blessed One Kassapa came. What is meant? By the same resolution with which those Blessed Ones came, the Blessed One of ours also came.  
>

> **Kathaṃ tathākāritāya tathāgato ? Bhagavato hi vācāya kāyo anulometi, kāyassapi vācā, tasmā yathāvādī tathākārī, yathākārī tathāvādī ca hoti.**  
> How is he a Tathāgata because of his acting according to his words (tathākāritā)? For the Blessed One’s body accords with his speech, and his speech accords with his body; therefore, he is one who speaks as he acts, and acts as he speaks.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Anurādha Sutta* (SN 44.2, S-iv 1943-1960) – The Buddha shows that even here and now the Tathāgata is not found as a truth and reality, so the question of his existence after death cannot be answered in any of the four ways (S-iv 1960:5).
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Pāsādika Sutta* (DN 29, D-iii 390-468) – The Buddha says that he is a speaker of the right time, of fact, of benefit, of the Dhamma and of the Discipline; that all he speaks between his awakening and his final Nibbāna is so and not otherwise; that as he speaks so he acts; and in each case "therefore he is called a Tathāgata" (D-iii 449:1-6).
* **Canonical**: *Itivuttaka* 112, Loka Sutta (It 1165-1168), and AN 4.23 (quoted at Sv-i 404:5).
* **Commentary**: *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* – the eight reasons for the title (Sv-i 359-408).
