### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṅghānussati**: [f.] recollection of the Saṅgha. Formed by *Saṅgha* (the community) + *anussati* (recollection).

### Orthodox Definition
*Saṅghānussati* is the recollection of the qualities of the *[Ariyasaṅgha](/glossary/ariyasangha/)*, the Saṅgha of the Blessed One's disciples, "the four pairs, the eight individual persons" (*cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā*), rather than the conventional monastic order. In the Mūla the noble disciple "recollects the Saṅgha" (*saṅghaṃ anussarati*) with the formula *suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho … anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā*, and it is the third of the six topics of recollection (*anussatiṭṭhāna*: Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, sīla, cāga, devatā; A-vi 35:3, 38:1-40:4).

At the time the disciple recollects the Saṅgha, his mind is not obsessed by greed, hatred or delusion; it is simply straight, focused on the Saṅgha (*ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti saṅghaṃ ārabbha*, A-vi 40:3-4). The Visuddhimagga gives the practice: one who wishes to develop it goes into solitary retreat and recollects the special qualities of the noble Saṅgha with this formula (Vism-i 1334:1). Because of the profundity of the Saṅgha's qualities, or because the mind is intent on recollecting qualities of many kinds, the jhāna reaches only access (*upacārappattameva jhānaṃ hoti*), not absorption (Vism-i 1350:4).

The Visuddhimagga lists the benefits: the bhikkhu has reverence and deference toward the Saṅgha, gains fullness of faith, much happiness and gladness, and overcomes fear and dread, and when faced with an opportunity for transgression shame and fear of wrongdoing arise as if he saw the Saṅgha face to face (Vism-i 1351:1-3). The Buddha also teaches that the defiled mind is cleansed through recollecting the Saṅgha (A-iii 744:1, 747:3-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahānāma Sutta)
> **“Puna caparaṃ, mahānāma, ariyasāvako saṅghaṃ anussarati – ‘ suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, yadidaṃ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā ’ ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako saṅghaṃ anussarati nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti saṅghaṃ ārabbha.**  
> “Furthermore, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects the Saṅgha: ‘The Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is practising the good way, is practising the straight way, is practising the true way, is practising the proper way. It consists of the four pairs, the eight individual persons. This is the Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples that is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.’” Mahānāma, at the time when a noble disciple recollects the Saṅgha, at that time their mind is not obsessed by greed, not obsessed by hate, not obsessed by delusion; at that time their mind is simply straight, focused on the Saṅgha.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Saṅghaguṇānaṃ pana gambhīratāya nānappakāraguṇānussaraṇādhimuttatāya vā appanaṃ appatvā upacārappattameva jhānaṃ hoti.**  
> But owing to the profundity of the Saṅgha’s special qualities, or owing to his being intent on recollecting special qualities of many kinds, the jhāna is only access and does not reach absorption.  
>
