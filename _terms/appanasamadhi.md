### Grammatical Analysis
**Appanāsamādhi**: [m.] absorption concentration. Formed by *appanā* (fixing, application) + *[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)* (concentration). The word *appanā* is in the Mūla (Dhs 397:2, a synonym of *vitakka*: *takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā*); the compound *appanāsamādhi* itself does not occur in the Mūla (count: 0 Mūla, 66 Aṭṭhakathā, 47 Ṭīkā) and is first found in the Aṭṭhakathā.

### Orthodox Definition
The Visuddhimagga divides concentration in two: *duvidho hi samādhi upacārasamādhi ca appanāsamādhi ca*, "concentration is of two kinds, access concentration and absorption concentration" (Vism-i 849:1). The mind is concentrated in two ways: on the plane of access (*upacārabhūmi*) by the abandonment of the hindrances, and on the plane of attainment (*paṭilābhabhūmi*) by the manifestation of the jhāna factors (*aṅgapātubhāvena*, Vism-i 849:2-4).

The Visuddhimagga gives the difference. In access the factors are not strong (*aṅgāni na thāmajātāni*), so the mind at times takes the sign as its object and at times descends into the life-continuum (*bhavaṅga*). In absorption the factors are strong, and once it has arisen the mind, having cut off the flow of the life-continuum, remains for a whole night and a whole day (*kevalampi rattiṃ kevalampi divasaṃ*), running only in the succession of wholesome impulsions (*kusalajavanapaṭipāṭi*, Vism-i 850:1-2). The Visuddhimagga also says that the counterpart sign (*[paṭibhāganimitta](/glossary/patibhaganimitta/)*) which arises with access concentration should be developed to reach absorption, and guarded if one cannot (Vism-i 851:1-3).

The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha says that the counterpart sign is obtained only in the kasiṇas, the foulnesses, the parts of the body and mindfulness of breathing, and that access and absorption concentration occur with the counterpart sign as object (Abhidh-s 790:1, §27); both kinds are called purification of mind, *cittavisuddhi* (Abhidh-s 819:1, §50).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye vitakko hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā sammāsaṅkappo – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vitakko hoti.**  
> 7. What is reflection at that time? Whatever thought (takka), reflection, intention, effecting of an aim, strong effecting of an aim, directing the mind onto, or right intention exists at that time—this is reflection at that time.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Duvidho hi samādhi upacārasamādhi ca appanāsamādhi ca. Dvīhākārehi cittaṃ samādhiyati upacārabhūmiyaṃ vā paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ vā. Tattha upacārabhūmiyaṃ nīvaraṇappahānena cittaṃ samāhitaṃ hoti. Paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ aṅgapātubhāvena. Dvinnaṃ pana samādhīnaṃ idaṃ nānākāraṇaṃ, upacāre aṅgāni na thāmajātāni honti, aṅgānaṃ athāmajātattā, yathā nāma daharo kumārako ukkhipitvā ṭhapiyamāno punappunaṃ bhūmiyaṃ patati, evameva upacāre uppanne cittaṃ kālena nimittamārammaṇaṃ karoti, kālena bhavaṅgamotarati. Appanāyaṃ pana aṅgāni thāmajātāni honti, tesaṃ thāmajātattā, yathā nāma balavā puriso āsanā vuṭṭhāya divasampi tiṭṭheyya, evameva appanāsamādhimhi uppanne cittaṃ sakiṃ bhavaṅgavāraṃ chinditvā kevalampi rattiṃ kevalampi divasaṃ tiṭṭhati, kusalajavanapaṭipāṭivaseneva pavattatīti.**  
> For concentration is of two kinds: access concentration and absorption concentration. In two ways the mind becomes concentrated: either on the plane of access or on the plane of obtainment. Herein, on the plane of access, the mind becomes concentrated by the abandonment of the hindrances. And on the plane of obtainment, by the manifestation of the factors. But this is the difference between the two concentrations: in access, the factors are not strong; because the factors are not strong, just as a young child when lifted up and stood up falls to the ground again and again, just so, when access concentration has arisen, the mind now makes the sign its object, and now enters the life-continuum. But in absorption, the factors are strong; because those factors are strong, just as a strong man, after rising from his seat, might stand even for a whole day, just so, when absorption concentration has arisen, the mind, having once cut off the flow of the life-continuum, remains active for a whole night and a whole day, operating solely by way of the succession of wholesome impulsions.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-i 849-850, Ch. IV) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=123&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RHV2aWRobyBoaSBzYW3EgWRoaSB1cGFjxIFyYXNhbcSBZGhpIGNhIGFwcGFuxIFzYW3EgWRoaQ%3D%3D)
