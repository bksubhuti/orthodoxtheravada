### Grammatical Analysis
**Sugati**: [f.] happy destination; blissful state; good rebirth. Formed by prefix *su* (good, happy, well) + *gati* (going, course, destination, rebirth). 

### Orthodox Definition
*Sugati* is the classification of the favorable, pleasant destinations of rebirth in [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/), the opposite of *duggati*. The Buddha names the *devas* and humans, "and whatever other *sugatiyo*" (AN 6.39), and says that they are recognized (that is, rebirth there happens) by deeds born of non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion, while hell, the animal realm and the ghost realm come from deeds born of greed, hatred and delusion. In the Dīgha Nikāya the virtuous person, accomplished in virtue (*sīlavā sīlasampanno*), is reborn after death in a *sugati*, a heavenly world (*sugatiṃ saggaṃ lokaṃ*).

The Papañcasūdanī gives the detailed definition: a *sugati* is of two kinds, *paṭipattisugati* (a happy destination of conduct) and *gatisugati* (a happy destination of rebirth). The householder with a pure mind who abstains from killing and stealing and fulfils the ten wholesome courses of action has a *paṭipattisugati*, and, standing in that, at the break-up of the body he reaches great status among humans or among devas: this is his *gatisugati* (Ps-i 846). The same is said in parallel for the one gone forth.

While rebirth in a *sugati* provides comfort, longevity and superior opportunities to practice the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/), the orthodox teachings warn that these states are not the final goal, so the aim of a Theravāda practitioner is to realize *[Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)* and exit the cycle of destinations entirely.

### Stock Phrase
>kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā sugatiṃ

### Quote

> “Mendicants, there are these three sources that give rise to deeds. rather, mendicants, it is because of deeds born of non-greed, deeds born of non-hatred, or deeds born of non-delusion that devas, humans, or any other good destinations are recognized.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 6.39, Nidāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_06&page_number=297&name=chakkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mzku4oCY4oCYIFTEq%2BG5h2ltxIFuaSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBuaWTEgW7EgW5pIGthbW3EgW5h4bmDIHNhbXVkYXnEgXlhLg%3D%3D)

> **Etthāpi ca sugati nāma paṭipattisugati gatisugatīti duvidhā hoti. Paṭipattisugatipi agāriyapaṭipattisugati anagāriyapaṭipattisugatīti duvidhā hoti. Agāriyo hi parisuddhacitto pāṇātipātāpi viramati, adinnādānāpi, sakalepi dasa kusalakammapathe paripūreti, ayamassa agāriyapaṭipattisugati. So tattha ṭhito kāyassa bhedā manussamahantatampi devamahantatampi upapajjati, ayamassa gatisugati.**  
> Here too, a happy destination is of two kinds: happy destination of conduct and happy destination of rebirth. Happy destination of conduct is also of two kinds: happy destination of conduct for a householder and happy destination of conduct for one who has gone forth. A householder with a pure mind refrains from killing and stealing, and fulfills the ten courses of skillful action; this is his happy destination of conduct. Established in that, he reaches great status among humans or great status among devas upon the dissolution of the body; this is his happy destination of rebirth.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *[Mahāparinibbāna](/glossary/mahaparinibbana/) Sutta* (DN 16) – The five benefits of virtue for the virtuous (*sīlavā*); the fifth is rebirth in a *sugati*, the heavenly world (D-ii 360:1), set against the five dangers of the immoral, the fifth being rebirth in the lower world, *duggati* (D-ii 354:1).
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Nidāna Sutta* (AN 6.39) – Devas, humans and other *sugatiyo* are recognized through non-greed, non-hatred and non-delusion (A-vi 274:11).
* **Canonical**: *[Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)* (v. 18) – "Here he rejoices, after death he rejoices; he who has done merit rejoices in both states" (Dhp 61:1), and "having gone to a good destination (*suggatiṃ gato*), he rejoices still more" (Dhp 62:1).
* **Commentary**: *Papañcasūdanī* on MN 7 (Ps-i 846), the two kinds of *sugati*.
