### Grammatical Analysis
**Paccayapariggahañāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of discerning conditions. Formed by *paccaya* (condition, cause) + *pariggaha* (grasping, discerning, embracing) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Mūla form is “paccayapariggahe paññā dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ” (Paṭis 9:1), understanding in embracing conditions is knowledge of the stability of phenomena. The Visuddhimagga uses the compound “nāmarūpassa paccayapariggahañāṇaṃ” (Vism XIX 691).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paccayapariggahañāṇa* is the second of the sixteen insight knowledges (*[vipassanā-ñāṇa](/glossary/vipassananana/)*), and it comes after the discernment of mentality-materiality (*[nāmarūpa-pariccheda-ñāṇa](/glossary/namarupaparicchedanana/)*). In the Visuddhimagga it is the Paccayapariggahakathā, in the chapter on Purification by Overcoming Doubt: “Etasseva nāmarūpassa paccayapariggahaṇena tīsu addhāsu kaṅkhaṃ vitaritvā ṭhitaṃ ñāṇaṃ kaṅkhāvitaraṇavisuddhi nāma” (Vism XIX 678).

The meditator reasons that this mind and body is not without cause, and not caused by a creator (*na issarādihetukaṃ*), since there is nothing beyond mentality-materiality such as a creator; so there must be causes and conditions (Vism XIX 678). Of the material body, ignorance, craving, clinging and kamma are the four causes (*hetu*) that produce it and nutriment is the condition (*paccaya*) that supports it; ignorance and the other two are like a mother, kamma is like a father, nutriment like a nurse (Vism XIX 679). He then discerns the conditions of the mental body in the same way. The Mūla gives the pattern: “avijjā paccayo, saṅkhārā paccayasamuppannā”, and so with each link of dependent origination, in nine ways, in the past, present and future (Paṭis 263:1-4, 264:1-6).

When the knowledge is firm, the sixteen kinds of doubt and eight kinds of doubt (such as doubt about the Teacher) are abandoned and the sixty-two views are suppressed; the knowledge is also called *dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇa*, *yathābhūtañāṇa* and *sammādassana* (Vism XIX 691). One who has it is called a lesser stream-enterer (*cūḷasotāpanna*), one who has found relief and a footing in the Buddha's dispensation and is of certain destination (*niyatagatika*) (Vism-ii 1452:1).

> 4. Understanding of embracing conditions is knowledge of the causal relationship of ideas.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Kathaṃ paccayapariggahe paññā dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ? Avijjā saṅkhārānaṃ uppādaṭṭhiti ca pavattaṭṭhiti ca nimittaṭṭhiti ca āyūhanaṭṭhiti ca saññogaṭṭhiti ca palibodhaṭṭhiti ca samudayaṭṭhiti ca hetuṭṭhiti ca paccayaṭṭhiti ca. Imehi navahākārehi avijjā paccayo, saṅkhārā paccayasamuppannā. Ubhopete dhammā paccayasamuppannāti – paccayapariggahe paññā dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ.**  
> 45. How is it that understanding of embracing conditions is knowledge of the causal relationship of ideas? Ignorance is a condition for the arising, occurrence, sign, accumulation, connection, impediment, origin, cause, and condition of volitional formations. In these nine ways, ignorance is the condition and volitional formations are dependently arisen. Thus both these ideas are dependently arisen—understanding of embracing conditions is knowledge of the causal relationship of ideas.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Ñāṇakathā, 45) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=48&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDUuIEthdGhh4bmDIHBhY2NheWFwYXJpZ2dhaGUgcGHDscOxxIEgZGhhbW1h4bmt4bmtaGl0acOxxIHhuYdh4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **So āditova iti paṭisañcikkhati “na tāvidhaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ahetukaṃ, sabbattha sabbadā sabbesañca ekasadisabhāvāpattito, na issarādihetukaṃ, nāmarūpato uddhaṃ issarādīnaṃ abhāvato. Yepi nāmarūpamattameva issarādayoti vadanti, tesaṃ issarādisaṅkhātanāmarūpassa ahetukabhāvappattito. Tasmā bhavitabbamassa hetupaccayehi, ke nu kho te” ti.**  
> First, he reflects thus: “Firstly, this mentality-materiality is not causeless, because it would follow that it would be identical everywhere, always, and for everyone. It does not have an Overlord or similar cause, because of the absence of any Overlord or the like beyond mentality-materiality. And as for those who say that mentality-materiality itself is the Overlord and so forth, it follows that the mentality-materiality which they call the Overlord, etc., would itself be causeless. Therefore, it must have a cause and condition; what are they?”  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIX, 678) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=233&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=U28gxIFkaXRvdmEgaXRpIHBh4bmtaXNhw7FjaWtraGF0aSDigJjigJggbmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tassevaṃ nibbattamānassa ‘ avijjā taṇhā upādānaṃ kamman ’ ti ime cattāro dhammā nibbattakattā hetu , āhāro upatthambhakattā paccayoti pañca dhammā hetupaccayā honti.**  
> As it is being born in this way, the four things—ignorance, craving, clinging, and kamma—are its causes (hetu) since they produce it, and nutriment is its condition (paccayo) since it supports it. Thus, five things constitute its cause and condition.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIX, 679) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=233&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2V2YeG5gyBuaWJiYXR0YW3EgW5hc3NhIOKAmCBhdmlqasSBIHRh4bmHaMSBIHVwxIFkxIFuYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Evaṃ cutipaṭisandhivasena viditasabbadhammassa sabbākārena nāmarūpassa paccayapariggahañāṇaṃ thāmagataṃ hoti, soḷasavidhā kaṅkhā suṭṭhutaraṃ pahīyati. Na kevalañca sā eva,“satthari kaṅkhatī” ti (dha. sa.1008) ādinayappavattā aṭṭhavidhāpi kaṅkhā pahīyatiyeva, dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhigatāni vikkhambhanti. Evaṃ nānānayehi nāmarūpapaccayapariggahaṇena tīsu addhāsu kaṅkhaṃ vitaritvā ṭhitaṃ ñāṇaṃ kaṅkhāvitaraṇavisuddhīti veditabbaṃ. Dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇantipi yathābhūtañāṇantipi sammādassanantipi etassevādhivacanaṃ.**  
> 691. Thus, for one who has understood all states by way of death and rebirth-linking, the knowledge of discerning the conditions of mentality-materiality in every way reaches a powerful state (thāmagataṃ), and the sixteen kinds of doubt are abandoned much more clearly (suṭṭhutaraṃ pahīyati). And not only that, but the eight kinds of doubt that occur in the way beginning with “he is doubtful about the Teacher (satthari)” are also certainly abandoned, and the sixty-two views (dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhigatāni) are suppressed. Thus, the knowledge established by overcoming doubt (kaṅkhaṃ vitaritvā) regarding the three periods of time (tīsu addhāsu) through discerning the conditions of mentality-materiality by various methods should be understood as purification by overcoming doubt (kaṅkhāvitaraṇavisuddhi). Knowledge of the stability of phenomena (dhammaṭṭhitiñāṇaṃ), knowledge of things as they really are (yathābhūtañāṇaṃ), and right seeing (sammādassanaṃ) are also synonyms for this very thing.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIX, 691) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=239&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NjkxLiBFdmHhuYMgY3V0aXBh4bmtaXNhbmRoaXZhc2VuYSB2aWRpdGFzYWJiYWRoYW1tYXNzYSBzYWJixIFrxIFyZW5hIG7EgW1hcsWrcGFzc2E%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Iminā pana ñāṇena samannāgato vipassako buddhasāsane laddhassāso laddhapatiṭṭho niyatagatiko cūḷasotāpanno nāma hoti.**  
> A vipassanā meditator who is possessed of this knowledge, who has found relief (laddhassāso) and found a footing (laddhapatiṭṭho) in the Buddha’s Dispensation, and who is of certain destination (niyatagatiko), is called a ‘lesser stream-enterer’ (cūḷasotāpanno).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIX, cūḷasotāpanna) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=240&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=SW1pbsSBIHBhbmEgw7HEgeG5h2VuYSBzYW1hbm7EgWdhdG8gdmlwYXNzYWtvIGJ1ZGRoYXPEgXNhbmU%3D)
