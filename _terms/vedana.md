### Grammatical Analysis
**Vedanā**: [f.] feeling. From *vedayati*, "it feels, experiences": "Vedayatīti vedanā" (As 1161:1). The Buddha says: "Why do you call it feeling? 'It feels', bhikkhus, therefore it is called feeling. And what does it feel? It feels pleasure, it feels pain, it feels neither-pain-nor-pleasure" (SN 22.79, S-iii 454:1-4).

### Orthodox Definition
*Vedanā* is the second of the five aggregates. In the Mūla: "And what, bhikkhus, is feeling? There are these six classes of feeling: feeling born of eye-contact, of ear-contact, of nose-contact, of tongue-contact, of body-contact, of mind-contact" (S-iii 287:1-2). It is of three kinds: *sukhā*, *dukkhā* and *adukkhamasukhā vedanā* (S-iii 454:4; A-vi 632:3). In the Abhidhamma it is one of the seven mental factors common to every consciousness, with phassa, saññā, cetanā, ekaggatā, jīvitindriya and manasikāra (Abh-s 84:2); for example, in the first wholesome consciousness: "a mental ease arisen from mind-contact, a mental pleasure, a pleasant feeling arisen from mind-contact" (Dhs 393:2, §3). In Dependent Origination: "phassapaccayā vedanā, vedanāpaccayā taṇhā" (SN 12.1, S-ii 6-8).

The Atthasālinī: "Vedayatīti vedanā. It has the characteristic of being experienced (*vedayitalakkhaṇā*), the function of experiencing (*anubhavanarasā*) or of enjoying the desirable aspect (*iṭṭhākārasambhogarasā*), the manifestation of mental gratification, and tranquillity as its proximate cause" (As 1161:1-2). The Visuddhimagga puts it in its summary of the characteristics of the links of Dependent Origination: "Anubhavanalakkhaṇā vedanā, visayarasasambhogarasā, sukhadukkhapaccupaṭṭhānā, phassapadaṭṭhānā": characteristic, experiencing; function, enjoying the taste of the object; manifestation, pleasure and pain; proximate cause, contact (Vism XVII §588, Vism-ii 899:9). The Atthasālinī gives the image: feeling is like a king, the other mental states like a cook, who tastes the dishes to test them but only the king eats what he pleases as master; so the other states experience the object only in part, feeling fully experiences its taste (As 1163:1-4).

The Salla Sutta (SN 36.6) shows how the uninstructed worldling, touched by painful feeling, feels two feelings, bodily and mental, while the instructed noble disciple feels the bodily one only (S-iv 1296:5-6, 1297:1-2).

> “And what, bhikkhus, is feeling? There are these six classes of feeling: feeling born of eye-contact, feeling born of ear-contact, feeling born of nose-contact, feeling born of tongue-contact, feeling born of body-contact, feeling born of mind-contact.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta, why it is called feeling)
> **“Kiñca, bhikkhave, vedanaṃ vadetha? Vedayatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ vedanā ’ ti vuccati. Kiñca vedayati? Sukhampi vedayati, dukkhampi vedayati, adukkhamasukhampi vedayati. Vedayatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ vedanā ’ ti vuccati.**  
> “And why, bhikkhus, do you call it feeling? ‘It feels,’ bhikkhus, therefore it is called feeling. And what does it feel? It feels pleasure, it feels pain, it feels neither-pain-nor-pleasure. ‘It feels,’ bhikkhus, therefore it is called feeling.  
>  
> — *[Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/)* (SN 22.79, Khajjanīya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_03&page_number=71&name=khandhavaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEtpw7FjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCB2ZWRhbmHhuYMgdmFkZXRoYT8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti? Yaṃ tasmiṃ samaye tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajaṃ cetasikaṃ sātaṃ cetasikaṃ sukhaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ sātaṃ sukhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā sātā sukhā vedanā – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti.**  
> 3. What is feeling at that time? Whatever at that time is a mental ease arisen from the contact of the corresponding mind-consciousness element (tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajaṃ), a mental pleasure, a pleasant, pleasurable feeling arisen from mind-contact (cetosamphassajaṃ), a pleasant, blissful feeling arisen from mind-contact—this is feeling at that time.  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§3, first consciousness) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=My4gS2F0YW3EgSB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgdmVkYW7EgSBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the definition)
> **Vedayatīti vedanā. Sā vedayitalakkhaṇā, anubhavanarasā iṭṭhākārasambhogarasā vā, cetasikaassādapaccupaṭṭhānā, passaddhipadaṭṭhānā.**  
> It experiences, thus it is feeling (vedanā). This is characterized by being experienced (vedayitalakkhaṇā), its function is experiencing (anubhavanarasā), or its function is the enjoyment of the desirable aspect (iṭṭhākārasambhogarasā); its manifestation is mental gratification (cetasikaassādapaccupaṭṭhānā), and its proximate cause is tranquility (passaddhipadaṭṭhānā).  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (§3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=152&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VmVkYXlhdMSrdGkgdmVkYW7EgS4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the king and the cook)
> **Rājā viya hi vedanā, sūdo viya sesadhammā. Yathā sūdo nānārasabhojanaṃ sampādetvā peḷāya pakkhipitvā lañchanaṃ datvā rañño santike otāretvā lañchanaṃ bhinditvā peḷaṃ vivaritvā sabbasūpabyañjanehi aggaggaṃ ādāya bhājane pakkhipitvā sadosaniddosabhāvavīmaṃsanatthaṃ ajjhoharati, tato rañño nānaggarasabhojanaṃ upanāmeti. Rājā issaravatāya vissavitāya sāmī hutvā icchiticchitaṃ bhuñjati. Tattha hi sūdassa bhattavīmaṃsanamattamiva avasesadhammānaṃ ārammaṇarasassa ekadesānubhavanaṃ.**  
> For feeling is like a king, and the rest of the phenomena are like a cook. Just as a cook prepares various kinds of choice food (nānārasabhojanaṃ), puts it in a basket, applies a seal, sets it down in the king's presence, breaks the seal, opens the basket, takes the best portions from all the soups and curries, puts them in a vessel, and swallows them to test for the presence or absence of defects, then serves the various kinds of choice food to the king— The king, acting as if he were the master, by way of his competence, and being the owner, eats whatever is wished for. For there, the experience of the essence of the object by the rest of the phenomena in part is like the cook's testing of the food.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (§3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=153&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UsSBasSBIHZpeWEgaGkgdmVkYW7EgSwgc8WrZG8gdml5YQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Anubhavanalakkhaṇā vedanā , visayarasasambhogarasā, sukhadukkhapaccupaṭṭhānā, phassapadaṭṭhānā.**  
> Feeling (vedanā) is characterized by being felt (anubhavanalakkhaṇā); its function is the experiencing of the object's flavor (visayarasasambhogarasā); it is manifested as pleasure and pain (sukhadukkhapaccupaṭṭhānā); and its proximate cause is contact (phassapadaṭṭhānā).  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVII, §588) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=159&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=QW51YmhhdmFuYWxha2toYeG5h8SBIHZlZGFuxIEgLCB2aXNheWFyYXNhc2FtYmhvZ2FyYXPEgSwgc3VraGFkdWtraGFwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLCBwaGFzc2FwYWRh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha)
> **Phasso vedanā saññā cetanā ekaggatā jīvitindriyaṃ manasikāro ceti sattime cetasikā sabbacittasādhāraṇā nāma.**  
> Contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, and attention—these seven mental factors are universal to every consciousness.  
>  
> — *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (II, the universal factors) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=7&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=UGhhc3NvIHZlZGFuxIEgc2HDscOxxIEgY2V0YW7EgSBla2FnZ2F0xIEgasSrdml0aW5kcml5YeG5gw%3D%3D)
