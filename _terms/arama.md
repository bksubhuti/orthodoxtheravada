### Grammatical Analysis
**Ārāma**: [m.] Park; pleasure grove; monastery grounds. Derived from prefix *ā* (towards, fully) + root *ram* (to delight, enjoy).

### Orthodox Definition
Originally meaning a "pleasure park" owned by royalty or wealthy merchants, *Ārāma* became the standard term for a large tract of land or grove donated to the Buddha and his [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/). 

Because monks required quiet, secluded places near enough to villages for alms but far enough away to avoid the noise of lay life, these donated parks became the epicenters of Buddhist monasticism. An *ārāma* refers more to the enclosed grounds, gardens, and forested areas, whereas a *[vihāra](/glossary/vihara/)* refers to the specific buildings constructed on those grounds.

The most famous examples in Buddhist history are *[Jetavana](/glossary/jeta-vana/) Anāthapiṇḍikassa Ārāma* (Jeta's Grove, [Anāthapiṇḍika](/glossary/anathapindika/)'s Park) in Sāvatthī, and *[Veḷuvana](/glossary/velu-vana/)* (The Bamboo Grove) donated by King Bimbisāra in Rājagaha.

### Textual References
* **[Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/)**: *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* – The story of King Bimbisāra offering the very first *ārāma* (Veḷuvana) to the Buddha by pouring water over his hands.
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2) – Describing the profound silence and peace found within an *ārāma* at night.
