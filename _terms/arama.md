### Grammatical Analysis
**Ārāma**: [m.] Park; pleasure grove; monastery grounds. Derived from prefix *ā* (towards, fully) + root *ram* (to delight, enjoy).

### Orthodox Definition
Originally meaning a "pleasure park" owned by royalty or wealthy merchants, *Ārāma* became the standard term for a large tract of land or grove donated to the Buddha and his [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/). 

Because monks required quiet, secluded places near enough to villages for alms but far enough away to avoid the noise of lay life, these donated parks became the epicenters of Buddhist monasticism. An *ārāma* refers more to the enclosed grounds, gardens, and forested areas, whereas a *[vihāra](/glossary/vihara/)* refers to the specific buildings constructed on those grounds.

The most famous examples in Buddhist history are *[Jetavana](/glossary/jeta-vana/) Anāthapiṇḍikassa Ārāma* (Jeta's Grove, [Anāthapiṇḍika](/glossary/anathapindika/)'s Park) in Sāvatthī, and *[Veḷuvana](/glossary/velu-vana/)* (The Bamboo Grove) donated by King Bimbisāra in Rājagaha.

### Quote
> **Atha kho rājā māgadho seniyo bimbisāro sovaṇṇamayaṃ bhiṅkāraṃ gahetvā bhagavato oṇojesi – “etāhaṃ, bhante, veḷuvanaṃ uyyānaṃ buddhappamukhassa bhikkhusaṅghassa dammī” ti. Paṭiggahesi bhagavā ārāmaṃ.**  
> Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, having taken a ceremonial vessel made of gold, dedicated it to the Blessed One: “May I, Lord, give this Bamboo Grove, a pleasure park, to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus with the Buddha at its head?” The Blessed One accepted the park. The Blessed One accepted the monastery.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/)**: *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* (Vin-iii 302) – King Bimbisāra dedicates the Veḷuvana, a pleasure park (*uyyāna*), with a golden vessel (*bhiṅkāra*); "The Blessed One accepted the *ārāma*."
* **Vinaya**: *Pācittiya* (Vin-i 225:1) – "An *ārāma* is a flower park or a fruit park" (*pupphārāmo phalārāmo*).
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2) – King Ajātasattu goes to the Buddha in Jīvaka's Mango Grove (*ambavana*) and sees the silent (*tuṇhībhūta*) Saṅgha, "like a clear lake" (D-i 229-234). The text calls it *ambavana*, not *ārāma*.
