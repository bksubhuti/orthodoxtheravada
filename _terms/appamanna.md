### Grammatical Analysis
**Appamaññā**: [f.] the Illimitables; boundless states. From *appamāṇa* (without measure). The Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā explains: *appamaññāyoti pharaṇaappamāṇavasena appamaññāyo*, "they are called appamaññā by way of the immeasurability of their pervading", for they pervade either measureless beings as object, or even a single being without remainder (Vibh-a 1720:2-3). The Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā gives another reason, *appamāṇagocaratāya*, because their domain is the measureless (Th-a-ii 1132:2).

### Orthodox Definition
The Vibhaṅga has a chapter on them, the Appamaññāvibhaṅga: *Catasso appamaññāyo*, "there are four illimitables" (Vibh 1855:1; the four are named *mettā, karuṇā, muditā, upekkhā* at Vibh 1914:1). In the Suttanta division each is a mind accompanied by that quality, pervading the four quarters, above, below and across, to all as to oneself, "far-reaching, grown great, beyond measure, free from enmity and ill will" (*vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena*, Vibh 1855:1-2; the same for mettā at D-i 1059:1-5).

The four are also the *brahmavihāras* (Vism Ch. IX, which describes the four in turn). The Atthasālinī defines loving-kindness: it has the characteristic of proceeding as desire for welfare (*hitākārappavattilakkhaṇā*), the function of bringing about welfare (*hitūpasaṃhārarasā*), the manifestation of removing hatred (*āghātavinayapaccupaṭṭhānā*), and the proximate cause of seeing the pleasing nature of beings (*sattānaṃ manāpabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā*) (As 1537:1). The Visuddhimagga teaches the breaking of the barriers (*sīmāsambheda*) for the practice of mettā: by repeatedly practising loving-kindness he makes the mind even towards four persons, himself, a dear person, a neutral person and a hostile one (Vism-i 1868:2); the test is that he does not wish any one of the four to be taken in place of another, even if bandits demand one (Vism-i 1868:3-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“So mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati. Tathā dutiyaṃ. Tathā tatiyaṃ. Tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena pharitvā viharati.**  
> ‘And he lets his mind pervade one quarter of the world with thoughts of Love.’ ‘And so the second.’ ‘And so the third.’ ‘And so the fourth.’ ‘And thus the whole wide world, above, below, around, and everywhere, does he continue to pervade with a heart of Love, far-reaching, grown great, and beyond measure, free from enmity and ill will.’  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 13, Tevijja Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_01&page_number=234&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNvIG1ldHTEgXNhaGFnYXRlbmEgY2V0YXPEgSBla2HhuYMgZGlzYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Catasso appamaññāyo – idha bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ mettāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena pharitvā viharati.**  
> 642. There are four illimitable states. Here, a bhikkhu abides pervading one quarter with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness; likewise the second, likewise the third, likewise the fourth. Thus upwards, downwards, and across, everywhere, to all as to himself, pervading the world in its entirety, with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness, far-reaching, grown great, and beyond measure, free from the least trace of anger or ill-will, he abides pervading.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Appamaññāvibhaṅga, §642) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=284&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjQyLiBDYXRhc3NvIGFwcGFtYcOxw7HEgXlvIOKAkyBpZGhhIGJoaWtraHU%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sammohavinodanī)
> **Idāni tadanantare appamaññāvibhaṅge catassoti gaṇanaparicchedo. Appamaññāyoti pharaṇaappamāṇavasena appamaññāyo. Etā hi ārammaṇavasena appamāṇe vā satte pharanti, ekasattampi vā anavasesapharaṇavasena pharantīti pharaṇaappamāṇavasena appamaññāyoti vuccanti.**  
> 642. Now, in the Analysis of the Illimitables, which immediately follows, the word “four” (catasso) is a numerical delimitation. “Illimitables” (appamaññāyo) means immeasurable by way of pervading. For indeed, they pervade either measureless beings by way of the object, or even a single being by way of pervading without remainder; thus, they are called “illimitables” because of the immeasurability of their pervading.  
>  
> — [Sammohavinodanī](/glossary/sammohavinodani/) (Vibh-a 1720, on Vibh §642) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_02&page_number=361&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NjQyLiBJZMSBbmkgdGFkYW5hbnRhcmUgYXBwYW1hw7HDscSBdmliaGHhuYVnZSBjYXRhc3NvdGkgZ2HhuYdhbmFwYXJpY2NoZWRvLg%3D%3D)
