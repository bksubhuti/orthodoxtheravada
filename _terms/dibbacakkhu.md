### Grammatical Analysis
**Dibbacakkhu**: [nt.] the divine eye. Formed by *dibba* (divine) + *cakkhu* (eye). In the formula it is directed to "the knowledge of the passing away and reappearing of beings" (*sattānaṃ cutūpapātañāṇāya*); the Visuddhimagga explains this as "the knowledge of the divine eye" (*dibbacakkhuñāṇa*, Vism-ii 219:2). The Visuddhimagga gives several reasons for calling it *dibba*: it is like the divine eye (*dibbasadisattā*) that deities have as a result of good conduct; it is obtained by way of a divine abiding; it is very brilliant through the grasping of light (*ālokapariggahena mahājutikattā*); and it has a great range, seeing forms behind walls and so on (Vism-ii 219:5-10). It is *cakkhu* in the sense of seeing (*dassanaṭṭhena*, Vism-ii 220:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Dibbacakkhu* is the knowledge by which the passing away and reappearing of beings is known. In the Mūla it is the second true knowledge (*dutiyā vijjā*) of the Buddha, attained "in the middle watch of the night" (M-i 129:8, Bhayabheravasutta), and the same formula is given as one of the fruits of the contemplative life in the Sāmaññaphalasutta (D-i 358:2). The Buddha says: "With the purified divine eye, surpassing human vision, I saw beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and I understood them according to their deeds" (M-i 129:2).

The Aṭṭhakathā explains that it is purified (*visuddha*) because seeing both passing away and reappearing is a cause of purity of view: one who sees only passing away adopts the annihilationist view, one who sees only reappearing adopts the view of the appearance of new beings, and one who sees both overcomes both views (Vism-ii 220:3-8). It does not see at the moment of passing away or of reappearing itself, but beings who are about to pass away or have just taken rebirth (Vism-ii 222:1-3). The knowledge of "faring according to deeds" (*yathākammupagañāṇa*) is distinguished from the function of the divine eye: the divine eye sees, and the other knowledge knows the deed that led to it (Vism-ii 224:2-12).

For one who wishes to develop it, the Visuddhimagga says the jhāna that is the basis of the higher knowledges is to be made able to be directed in every way, and one of three kasiṇas (fire, white, light) brought near; "of these three the light kasiṇa is the best" (Vism-ii 236:1, 236:5). In the Vibhaṅga's Ñāṇavibhaṅga, in the fivefold analysis of right concentration, "the divine eye is the extension of light" (*dibbacakkhu ālokapharaṇatā*, Vibh 2443:6).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte sattānaṃ cutūpapātañāṇāya cittaṃ abhininnāmesiṃ. So dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passāmi cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāmi – Iti dibbena cakkhunā visuddhena atikkantamānusakena satte passāmi cavamāne upapajjamāne hīne paṇīte suvaṇṇe dubbaṇṇe sugate duggate yathākammūpage satte pajānāmi. Ayaṃ kho me, brāhmaṇa, rattiyā majjhime yāme dutiyā vijjā adhigatā, avijjā vihatā vijjā uppannā, tamo vihato āloko uppanno, yathā taṃ appamattassa ātāpino pahitattassa viharato.**  
> 53. With my mind thus concentrated, purified, cleansed, spotless, rid of defilements, malleable, workable, steady, and attained to imperturbability, I directed my mind to the knowledge of the passing away and reappearing of beings. I saw beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and I understood them according to their deeds, with the purified divine eye (dibbena cakkhunā), surpassing human vision – Thus, with the purified divine eye, surpassing human vision, I saw beings passing away and reappearing, inferior and superior, beautiful and ugly, in good destinations and bad destinations, and I understood them according to their deeds. Indeed, brahmin, this was the second true knowledge (vijjā) I attained (adhigatā) in the middle watch of the night (rattiyā majjhime yāme). Ignorance was dispelled (vihatā), true knowledge arose; darkness (tamo) was dispelled, light (āloko) arose, as happens for the diligent, ardent, and resolute one living.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **(1) Tattha katamo pañcaṅgiko sammāsamādhi? Pītipharaṇatā, sukhapharaṇatā, cetopharaṇatā, ālokapharaṇatā, paccavekkhaṇānimittaṃ. Dvīsu jhānesu paññā pītipharaṇatā. Tīsu jhānesu paññā sukhapharaṇatā. Paracitte ñāṇaṃ cetopharaṇatā. Dibbacakkhu ālokapharaṇatā. Tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhitassa paccavekkhaṇāñāṇaṃ paccavekkhaṇānimittaṃ. Ayaṃ vuccati pañcaṅgiko sammāsamādhi.**  
> 804.(1) What is right concentration having five factors here? Extension of joy, extension of pleasure, extension of mind, extension of light, object of reviewing. Wisdom in two jhānas is extension of joy. Wisdom in three jhānas is extension of pleasure. Knowledge of others' minds is extension of mind. The divine eye is extension of light. The knowledge of reviewing for one who has emerged from such and such concentration is the object of reviewing. This is called right concentration having five factors.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (the meaning of the term))
> **Sattānaṃ cutūpapātañāṇakathāya cutūpapātañāṇāyāti (dī. ni.1.247) cutiyā ca upapāte ca ñāṇāya. Yena ñāṇena sattānaṃ cuti ca upapāto ca ñāyati, tadatthaṃ dibbacakkhuñāṇatthanti vuttaṃ hoti. Cittaṃ abhinīharati abhininnāmetīti parikammacittaṃ abhinīharati ceva abhininnāmeti ca. Soti so katacittābhinīhāro bhikkhu. Dibbenātiādīsu pana dibbasadisattā dibbaṃ. Devatānañhi sucaritakammanibbattaṃ pittasemharuhirādīhi apalibuddhaṃ upakkilesavimuttatāya dūrepi ārammaṇaṃ sampaṭicchanasamatthaṃ dibbaṃ pasādacakkhu hoti. Idañcāpi vīriyabhāvanābalanibbattaṃ ñāṇacakkhu tādisamevāti dibbasadisattā dibbaṃ. Dibbavihāravasena paṭiladdhattā attanā ca dibbavihārasannissitattāpi dibbaṃ. Ālokapariggahena mahājutikattāpi dibbaṃ. Tirokuṭṭādigatarūpadassanena mahāgatikattāpi dibbaṃ.**  
> 411. In the explanation of the knowledge of the passing away and reappearing of beings, for the knowledge of passing away and reappearing (cutūpapātañāṇāya) means for the knowledge of both passing away and reappearing. This means it is for the purpose of the knowledge by which the passing away and reappearing of beings is known, which is the knowledge of the divine eye. Directs his mind, directs it to (cittaṃ abhinīharati abhininnāmeti) means he both directs and directs his preliminary mind. He (so) means that bhikkhu who has directed his mind. Regarding the words beginning with with the divine (dibbenā), it is called divine because it is divine-like. For deities have a divine sensitive eye (pasādacakkhu) produced by good karma, which is not obstructed by bile, phlegm, blood, and so on, and is capable of receiving objects even at a distance because it is free from defilements. And this eye of knowledge (ñāṇacakkhu), produced by the strength of cultivated persistence, is just like that; hence it is called divine because it is divine-like. It is also divine because it is obtained by way of a divine abiding and because it is based on a divine abiding itself. It is also divine due to being very brilliant through the grasping of light. It is also divine due to having a great range by seeing forms that have gone behind walls and so on.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (preparation))
> **Evaṃ passitukāmena pana ādikammikena kulaputtena kasiṇārammaṇaṃ abhiññāpādakajjhānaṃ sabbākārena abhinīhārakkhamaṃ katvā “tejokasiṇaṃ, odātakasiṇaṃ, ālokakasiṇan” ti imesu tīsu kasiṇesu aññataraṃ āsannaṃ kātabbaṃ. Imesu ca pana tīsu ālokakasiṇaṃyeva seṭṭhataraṃ. Tasmā taṃ vā itaresaṃ vā aññataraṃ kasiṇaniddese vuttanayena uppādetvā upacārabhūmiyaṃyeva ṭhatvā vaḍḍhetabbaṃ.**  
> 412. But a clansman who is a beginner and wishes to see in this way should, after making the jhāna that has a kasiṇa as its object and serves as the basis for direct knowledge (abhiññāpādakajjhānaṃ) susceptible to being directed (abhinīhārakkhamaṃ) in every way, bring one or another of these three kasiṇas—the fire kasiṇa, the white kasiṇa, or the light kasiṇa—into close proximity. And indeed, among these three, the light kasiṇa is the best. Therefore, having produced either that one or one of the others in the manner stated in the Description of the Kasiṇas, he should extend it while staying at the level of access (upacārabhūmiyaṃ) itself.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Abhiññāniddesa, Cutūpapātañāṇakathā, Vism-ii 236) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=58&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDEyLiBFdmHhuYMgcGFzc2l0dWvEgW1lbmEgcGFuYSDEgWRpa2FtbWlrZW5hIGt1bGFwdXR0ZW5h)
