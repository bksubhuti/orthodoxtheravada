### Grammatical Analysis
**Apadāna**: [nt.] legend; account of deeds; cause. The Apadāna-aṭṭhakathā says the word *apadāna* is seen in the senses of cause (*kāraṇa*), taking (*gahaṇa*), departure (*apagamana*), sequence (*paṭipāṭi*) and insult (*akkosana*), and that here it is used in the sense of cause: *buddhānaṃ apadānāni buddhāpadāni, buddhakāraṇānīti attho* (Ap-a 920:1-8).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Apadāna* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya, in verse. Its Mūla opens with the summary verse "Atha buddhāpadānāni, suṇātha suddhamānasā" (Ap-i 20:1), "Now listen, with purified minds, to the legends of the Buddhas". The Aṭṭhakathā explains that the legends of the Buddhas are their causes, namely the thirty perfections of countless Buddhas (Ap-a 920:8-9; thirty = ten perfections, ten secondary perfections and ten ultimate perfections, Ap-a 935:2).

The same word is used for the legends of the elders: *therānaṃ apadānaṃ kāraṇaṃ therāpadānaṃ*, "the legend that is the account of the elders is the Legend of the Elders" (Ap-a 1443:11). Individual legends are introduced in the commentary as, for example, "the legend of the venerable Elder Sāriputta" (Ap-a 1443:12) or "of the venerable Elder Mahākassapa" (Ap-a 1839:1). The collection also has the *paccekabuddhāpadāna* (Ap-a 1092:1) and the legends of the nuns (*Therīapadāna*, Ap-iii).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhajanavilāsinī)
> **Apadānanti ettha apadāna-saddo kāraṇagahaṇaapagamanapaṭipāṭiakkosanādīsu dissati. Tathā hi esa “khattiyānaṃ apadānaṃ, brāhmaṇānaṃ apadānan” tiādīsu kāraṇe dissati, khattiyānaṃ kāraṇaṃ brāhmaṇānaṃ kāraṇanti attho. “Upāsakānaṃ apadānan” tiādīsu gahaṇe dissati, saṃsuṭṭhu gahaṇanti attho. “Vāṇijānaṃ apadānaṃ, suddānaṃ apadānan” tiādīsu apagamane dissati, tato tato tesaṃ apagamananti attho. “Piṇḍapātiko bhikkhu sapadānacāravasena piṇḍāya caratī” tiādīsu paṭipāṭiyā dissati, gharapaṭipāṭiyā caratīti attho. “Apagatā ime sāmaññā, apagatā ime brahmaññāti apadānetī” tiādīsu akkosane dissati, akkosati paribhāsatīti attho. Idha pana kāraṇe dissati. Tasmā buddhānaṃ apadānāni buddhāpadāni, buddhakāraṇānīti attho. Gaṅgāvālukūpamānaṃ anekesaṃ buddhānaṃ dānapāramitādisamattiṃsapāramitā kāraṇanti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> Here, apadāna: the term apadāna is seen in the senses of a cause (kāraṇa), taking (gahaṇa), departure (apagamana), sequence (paṭipāṭi), and insult (akkosana). It is seen in the sense of a cause in contexts such as, 'the legend of the Khattiyas,' 'the legend of the Brāhmaṇas,' meaning the cause of the Khattiyas or the cause of the Brāhmaṇas. It is seen in the sense of taking in contexts such as, 'the legend of the laymen,' meaning they have taken it up properly. It is seen in the sense of departure in contexts such as, 'the legend of the merchants,' 'the legend of the Śūdras,' meaning their departure from this place and that place. It is seen in the sense of sequence in contexts such as, 'the alms-eating bhikkhu wanders for alms by way of the sequential route,' meaning he wanders according to the sequence of houses. In passages such as: “These people (ime) have departed (apagatā) from behaving properly toward ascetics (sāmaññā), these people have departed from behaving properly toward brahmins; he insults (apadāneti),” is seen (dissati) in the sense of insulting; the meaning (attho) is that he insults (akkosati) and abuses (paribhāsati). But here, it is seen in the sense of a cause. Therefore, the legends of the Buddhas are Buddhāpadāni, meaning the causes of the Buddhas. It should be seen as the thirty perfections—including the perfection of giving—of the countless Buddhas, whose numbers are like the grains of sand in the Ganges, which are the causes of the Buddhas.  
>
