### Grammatical Analysis
**Attā**: [m.] self; soul; ego; abiding core; essence. (Equivalent to Sanskrit *Ātman*). 

### Orthodox Definition
In orthodox Theravāda doctrine, *Attā* represents the supreme existential illusion. It is the false, deeply ingrained assumption that within the shifting phenomena of the mind and body, there exists a permanent, independent, controlling "I", "me", or "soul" that owns experiences and transmigrates intact from life to life.

The Buddha dismantled the concept of *Attā* by analyzing the five aggregates (*[khandha](/glossary/khandha/)*). Each aggregate is *anattā* because it leads to affliction and cannot be controlled: if form were self, it would not lead to affliction, and one could say "let my form be thus, let my form not be thus" (SN 22.59, S-iii 324). The clinging to a doctrine of self (*attavādupādāna*) is the fourth of the four clingings, and the Papañcasūdanī glosses it as the twenty-fold identity view (*vīsativatthukā sakkāyadiṭṭhi*, Ps-i 1906:2).

Clinging to the concept of *Attā* manifests as *Sakkāya-diṭṭhi* (Identity View), which is the very first fetter binding beings to [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). The realization of *[Anattā](/glossary/anatta/)* (non-self) is the exclusive, definitive breakthrough that guarantees the attainment of Stream-entry and eventual [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).

### Quote
> **“Taṃ, bhikkhave, attavādupādānaṃ upādiyetha, yaṃsa attavādupādānaṃ upādiyato na uppajjeyyuṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā. Passatha no tumhe, bhikkhave, taṃ attavādupādānaṃ yaṃsa attavādupādānaṃ upādiyato na uppajjeyyuṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā” ti? “No hetaṃ, bhante”. “Sādhu, bhikkhave. Ahampi kho taṃ, bhikkhave, attavādupādānaṃ na samanupassāmi yaṃsa attavādupādānaṃ upādiyato na uppajjeyyuṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, you may well cling to that doctrine of self, clinging to which sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair would not arise. But do you see any such doctrine of self, bhikkhus, clinging to which sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair would not arise?” “No, venerable sir.” “Good, bhikkhus. I too, bhikkhus, do not see any doctrine of self, clinging to which sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair would not arise.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Alagaddūpama Sutta* (MN 22) – The famous "Simile of the Water Snake," where the Buddha declares that he sees no doctrine of self, clinging to which sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure and despair would not arise (M-i 767).
* **Canonical**: *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* (SN 22.59, S-iii 322ff) – Each of the five aggregates is not-self because it leads to affliction and cannot be controlled.
* **Commentary**: *Papañcasūdanī* (Ps-i 1906:2) – Explains *attavādupādānaṃ* in MN 22 as the twenty-fold identity view (*sakkāyadiṭṭhi*).
