### Grammatical Analysis
**Yoniso manasikāra**: [m.] wise attention; careful attention (TPR also renders the opposite, *ayoniso manasikāro*, as "careless attention"). Formed by *yoniso* (adverb: by means, by way of the proper path) + *manasikāra* (attention, bringing to mind). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī glosses *yoniso manasikaroti*: "one attends, by way of means (*upāyato*), by way of the path (*pathato*), by way of cause (*kāraṇato*), as 'impermanent' and so on".

### Orthodox Definition
The Papañcasūdanī (on the Sabbāsava Sutta, MN 2) defines it: "wise attention is attention that is the means (*upāyamanasikāro*), the primary attention (*pathamanasikāro*); it is the turning of the mind (*cittassa āvaṭṭanā*), the repeated turning, the engagement, the focusing and the mental settling, upon impermanence and so on, in the way of 'impermanent' (*aniccādīsu aniccanti*), or by way of knowledge in accordance with the truths (*saccānulomikena*)". The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī (Sv-ii 943:6) adds the four: seeing the impermanent as impermanent, the painful as painful, the not-self as not-self, and the unattractive as unattractive (*anicce aniccanti dukkhe dukkhanti anattani anattāti asubhe asubhanti*).

In the Mūla, MN 2 says the destruction of the taints is for one who knows and sees "wise attention and unwise attention": for one attending unwisely, unarisen taints arise and arisen taints increase; for one attending wisely, unarisen taints do not arise and arisen taints are abandoned. In the Ekanipāta of the Aṅguttara the Buddha says he sees no other single thing by which unarisen factors of enlightenment arise and arisen ones are fulfilled as much as *yoniso manasikāra* (A-i 94:1); and the Mahāvedalla Sutta names two conditions for the arising of right view: the voice of another (*parato ghoso*) and wise attention (*yoniso manasikāro*).

In the Abhidhamma, *manasikāra* is one of the seven universal mental factors (*sabbacittasādhāraṇā*: contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, attention), Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, Chapter II, §2; wise attention is this factor when it is directed in this wholesome way.

### Quote
> **“Jānato ahaṃ, bhikkhave, passato āsavānaṃ khayaṃ vadāmi, no ajānato no apassato. Kiñca, bhikkhave, jānato kiñca passato āsavānaṃ khayaṃ vadāmi? Yoniso ca manasikāraṃ ayoniso ca manasikāraṃ. Ayoniso, bhikkhave, manasikaroto anuppannā ceva āsavā uppajjanti, uppannā ca āsavā pavaḍḍhanti; yoniso ca kho, bhikkhave, manasikaroto anuppannā ceva āsavā na uppajjanti, uppannā ca āsavā pahīyanti.**  
> “Monks, I speak of the destruction of taints for one who knows and sees, not for one who does not know and does not see. Knowing what, seeing what, do I declare the destruction of taints? Wise attention and unwise attention. Monks, for one attending unwisely, unarisen taints arise, and arisen taints increase; but indeed, monks, for one attending wisely, unarisen taints do not arise, and arisen taints are abandoned.  
>
