### Grammatical Analysis
**Vassa**: [m./nt.] rain; the rains; the rains-residence (in the Vinaya, *vassaṃ upagantuṃ*, "to enter the rains"). The Samantapāsādikā glosses *vassāne vassaṃ upagantuṃ* as entering the rains in the three months named *vassāna* (*vassānanāmake temāse vassaṃ upagantabbaṃ*; Sp-iii 524:6). Related terms: *vassāvāsa* (rains-residence), *vassūpanāyikā* (the entering upon the rains), *vassaṃvuṭṭha* (one who has spent the rains).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Vassa* is the three-month rains-residence of bhikkhus. In the Mahāvagga (Vassūpanāyikakkhandhaka) the Buddha had not yet laid it down, and the monks wandered in the cold season, the hot season and the rains. People criticised them for trampling green grass, harming one-facultied life and crushing many small creatures, while other sects and even birds kept to a residence for the rains. The Buddha then allowed the monks to enter the rains (*anujānāmi, bhikkhave, vassaṃ upagantuṃ*), in the rains season (*vassāne*). There are two entries: the earlier (*purimikā*), after the day following the Āsāḷhī full moon, and the later (*pacchimikā*), a month after the Āsāḷhī full moon (Vin-iii 1064:2-3; Sp-iii 524:8-12).

Once he has entered, a monk may not leave to wander without spending the first three months or the last three months, and one who does so commits a *dukkaṭa*; nor may one fail to enter the rains, on pain of the same offence (Vin-iii 1067:12-13, 1068:3-4). The Samantapāsādikā describes the entering: having tended the monastery, set out drinking and washing water and finished the duties such as paying homage at the shrine, one says once or two or three times, "I enter the rains in this monastery for these three months" (*imasmiṃ vihāre imaṃ temāsaṃ vassaṃ upemī*; Sp-iii 524:12).

The Buddha also allowed a monk to go out within the rains for a matter to be done within seven days (*sattāhakaraṇīya*), and to return within seven days (*sattāhaṃ sannivatto kātabbo*): for example when a lay follower sends a messenger inviting the bhikkhus to a gift of a monastery (Vin-iii 1074:18-20, 1075:1-4), when a monk is sick (Vin-iii 1087:1-5), or when a monk's father is sick (Vin-iii 1120:1-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, the rains-residence allowed)
> **Tena kho pana samayena bhagavatā bhikkhūnaṃ vassāvāso apaññatto hoti. Teidha bhikkhū hemantampi gimhampi vassampi cārikaṃ caranti. Manussā ujjhāyanti khiyyanti vipācenti – Atha kho bhagavā etasmiṃ nidāne etasmiṃ pakaraṇe dhammiṃ kathaṃ katvā bhikkhū āmantesi – “anujānāmi, bhikkhave, vassaṃ upagantun” ti. Dvemā, bhikkhave, vassūpanāyikā – purimikā, pacchimikā. Aparajjugatāya āsāḷhiyā purimikā upagantabbā, māsagatāya āsāḷhiyā pacchimikā upagantabbā – imā kho, bhikkhave, dve vassūpanāyikāti.**  
> Now at that time the Blessed One had not yet prescribed a rains-residence for the bhikkhus. These bhikkhus wandered on tour during the cold weather, the hot weather, and the rains. People looked down upon them, criticised them, and spread it about, saying: Then the Blessed One, on this occasion and in this connection, having given a talk on the Dhamma, addressed the bhikkhus: “I allow you, bhikkhus, to enter upon the rains.” “Bhikkhus, there are these two periods for beginning the rains: the earlier and the later. The earlier may be entered upon the day after (the full moon of) Āsāḷhī; the later may be entered upon a month after (the full moon of) Āsāḷhī. These, bhikkhus, are the two periods for beginning the rains.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, not leaving and not failing to enter)
> **“na, bhikkhave, vassaṃ upagantvā purimaṃ vā temāsaṃ pacchimaṃ vā temāsaṃ avasitvā cārikā pakkamitabbā. Yo pakkameyya, āpatti dukkaṭassā” ti. 186. Tena kho pana samayena chabbaggiyā bhikkhū na icchanti vassaṃ upagantuṃ. Bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ. Na, bhikkhave, vassaṃ na upagantabbaṃ. Yo na upagaccheyya, āpatti dukkaṭassāti.**  
> “Bhikkhus, having entered upon the rains, but not having kept either the first three months or the last three months, one should not set out on tour. Whoever should set out, there is an offence of wrong-doing.” Now at that time the group of six bhikkhus did not want to enter upon the rains. They told this matter to the Blessed One. “Bhikkhus, one should not not enter upon the rains. Whoever should not enter upon (them), there is an offence of wrong-doing.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Vassāne vassaṃ upagantunti vassānanāmake temāse vassaṃ upagantabbanti attho. Kati nu kho vassūpanāyikāti kati nu kho vassūpagamanāni. Aparajjugatāyāti ettha aparajjugatāya assāti aparajjugatā, tassā aparajjugatāya; atikkantāya aparasmiṃ divaseti attho. Dutiyanayepi māso gatāya assāti māsagatā , tassā māsagatāya; atikkantāya māse paripuṇṇeti attho. Tasmā āsāḷhīpuṇṇamāya anantare pāṭipadadivase, āsāḷhīpuṇṇamito vā aparāya puṇṇamāya anantare pāṭipadadivaseyeva vihāraṃ paṭijaggitvā pānīyaṃ paribhojanīyaṃ upaṭṭhapetvā sabbaṃ cetiyavandanādisāmīcikammaṃ niṭṭhāpetvā “imasmiṃ vihāre imaṃ temāsaṃ vassaṃ upemī” ti sakiṃ vā dvattikkhattuṃ vā vācaṃ nicchāretvā vassaṃ upagantabbaṃ.**  
> 'To enter upon the rains in the rainy season' means one should enter upon the rains during the three months known as the rainy season. 'How many periods are there for beginning the rains?' means how many occasions are there for beginning the rains? In the passage 'the day after (the full moon of) Āsāḷhī', the day following the full moon is the 'day after'; that is the day after the full moon of Āsāḷhī; it means the day after it has passed. In the second method also, 'a month after' means a month following the full moon of Āsāḷhī; it means when the month has fully passed. Therefore, on the day after the full moon of Āsāḷhī, or on the day after the full moon following the Āsāḷhī full moon, after having prepared the dwelling, provided drinking water and water for washing, having completed all proper respectful duties such as bowing to the shrines, one should enter upon the rains after having uttered the words 'I enter upon the rains for these three months in this dwelling' once, or two or three times.  
>
