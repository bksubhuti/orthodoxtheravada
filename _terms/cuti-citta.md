### Grammatical Analysis
**Cuticitta**: [nt.] death-consciousness, a compound of *cuti* (death, passing away) + *citta* (consciousness). The Aṭṭhakathā gives the sense: "for the very last life-continuum consciousness in a single existence is called *cuti* because of falling away from that existence (*cavanattā*)" (Vism-ii 408:2). The Ṭīkā glosses *cuti* as *cavanaṃ*, falling away (Ps-t-i 998:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *cuticitta* is the last consciousness of a life: "in one existence, whatever is the very last life-continuum consciousness is called *cuti*", and it is of the same nineteen kinds as the life-continuum (Vism-ii 408:2-3). After death there is rebirth-linking again, after rebirth-linking the life-continuum again, so that the stream of consciousness of beings who wander through existences proceeds without a break; but for the one who reaches arahantship, when his death-consciousness has ceased it has simply ceased (Vism-ii 409:1-2). In the Mūla, the Paṭṭhāna names the *cuticitta* as a condition for the following rebirth-consciousness (*upapatticitta*): "death-consciousness associated with pleasant feeling is related by contiguity condition to rebirth-linking consciousness associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling" (Paṭṭh-i 2887:1).

The Visuddhimagga describes the sequence for a person in a sense-sphere good destiny at the deathbed: the kamma or kamma-sign comes to the mind-door; after the impulsion process ending in registration, or a pure impulsion process, the death-consciousness arises with an object belonging to the life-continuum; when it has ceased, rebirth-linking arises with that same kamma or sign as object (Vism-ii 1009:1-4). It also says that no death-consciousness has a present object, so rebirth-linking follows a death-consciousness whose object is past or not-to-be-designated (Vism-ii 1008:4-5).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Sukhāya vedanāya sampayutto dhammo adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttassa dhammassa anantarapaccayena paccayo – sukhāya vedanāya sampayuttaṃ cuticittaṃ adukkhamasukhāya vedanāya sampayuttassa upapatticittassa anantarapaccayena paccayo.**  
> A state associated with pleasant feeling is related to a state associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling by contiguity condition: death-consciousness associated with pleasant feeling is related to rebirth-linking consciousness associated with neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling by contiguity condition.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (definition))
> **Ekasmiṃ hi bhave yaṃ sabbapacchimaṃ bhavaṅgacittaṃ, taṃ tato cavanattā cutīti vuccati. Tasmā tampi ekūnavīsatividhameva hoti. Cutito pana puna paṭisandhi, paṭisandhito puna bhavaṅganti evaṃ bhavagatiṭhitinivāsesu saṃsaramānānaṃ sattānaṃ avicchinnaṃ cittasantānaṃ pavattatiyeva. Yo panettha arahattaṃ pāpuṇāti, tassa cuticitte niruddhe niruddhameva hotīti.**  
> For the very last life-continuum consciousness in a single existence is called death (cuti) because of the act of falling away from that existence. Therefore, that too is of nineteen kinds. After death, there is rebirth-linking again; after rebirth-linking, the life-continuum again. Thus, the continuous stream of consciousness of beings who wander through existences, destinies, stations of consciousness, and abodes of beings, proceeds without break. But for one who attains Arahantship here, when the death-consciousness has ceased, it has simply ceased.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (death and rebirth))
> **Evaṃ tīsu ārammaṇesu pavattamānā pana paṭisandhi yasmā atītārammaṇassa vā navattabbārammaṇassa vā cuticittassa anantarameva pavattati. Paccuppannārammaṇaṃ pana cuticittaṃ nāma natthi. 623. Seyyathidaṃ – kāmāvacarasugatiyaṃ tāva ṭhitassa pāpakammino puggalassa “tānissa tasmiṃ samaye olambantī” tiādivacanato (ma. ni.3.248) maraṇamañce nipannassa yathūpacitaṃ pāpakammaṃ vā kammanimittaṃ vā manodvāre āpāthamāgacchati. Taṃ ārabbha uppannāya tadārammaṇapariyosānāya javanavīthiyā anantaraṃ bhavaṅgavisayaṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā cuticittaṃ uppajjati. Tasmiṃ niruddhe tadeva āpāthagataṃ kammaṃ vā kammanimittaṃ vā ārabbha anupacchinnakilesabalavināmitaṃ duggatipariyāpannaṃ paṭisandhicittaṃ uppajjati. Ayaṃ atītārammaṇāya cutiyā anantarā atītārammaṇā paṭisandhi.**  
> However, although rebirth-linking occurs with these three kinds of objects, it occurs immediately after (anantarameva) a death-consciousness (cuticittassa) that has either a past object or a not-to-be-stated object. There is indeed no such thing as a death-consciousness with a present object. 623. That is to say: firstly, for an individual who has performed bad kamma (pāpakammino) while staying in a sense-sphere happy destination, when he is lying on his deathbed (maraṇamañce), his bad kamma (pāpakammaṃ) or sign of kamma (kammanimittaṃ), as it has been accumulated, arrives in the range (āpāthamāgacchati) of the mind's door, in accordance with the statement: 'Then hang over him,' and so on. Focusing on that, immediately after (anantaraṃ) a process of impulsions (javanavīthiyā) ending in registration, the death-consciousness arises making the objective field of the life-continuum its object. When that has ceased, focusing on that very same kamma or sign of kamma that had arrived in the range, a rebirth-linking consciousness (paṭisandhicittaṃ) pertaining to an unhappy destination (duggatipariyāpannaṃ) arises, driven by the force of defilements that have not been cut off (anupacchinnakilesabalavināmitaṃ). This is rebirth-linking with a past object occurring immediately after a death with a past object.  
>
