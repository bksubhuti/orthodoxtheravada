### Grammatical Analysis
**Lobha**: [m.] greed. The Atthasālinī and the Visuddhimagga explain it: "greed is so called because people covet by means of it, or it itself covets, or it is merely the act of coveting" (*lubbhanti tena, sayaṃ vā lubbhati, lubbhanamattameva vā tanti lobho*, As 1774:1; Vism-ii 463:1). It is from the verb *lubbhati* (to be greedy).

### Orthodox Definition
*Lobha* is the first of the three roots of the unwholesome (*akusalamūla*): "greed is a root of the unwholesome, hatred is a root of the unwholesome, delusion is a root of the unwholesome" (AN 3.70, A-iii 721:3).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it, in the first unwholesome consciousness, as *lobho lubbhanā lubbhitattaṃ sārāgo sārajjanā sārajjitattaṃ abhijjhā lobho akusalamūlaṃ* (Dhs 891:2). The Atthasālinī explains these terms one by one (As 1796:1-8). The Kilesagocchaka defines it with a longer list of synonyms, *yo rāgo sārāgo anunayo anurodho nandī … taṇhā …*, which the Atthasālinī says is given in more than a hundred terms in the clusters of roots, knots and defilements (Dhs 2029:2; As 2332:2).

The Atthasālinī gives its marks: characteristic, grasping an object (*ārammaṇaggahaṇalakkhaṇo*), like monkey glue; function, clinging (*abhisaṅgaraso*), like a piece of meat thrown on a hot pan; manifestation, non-relinquishing (*apariccāgapaccupaṭṭhāno*), like the dye of lampblack on oil; proximate cause, seeing gratification in things that bind (*saṃyojaniyadhammesu assādadassanapadaṭṭhāno*). Growing as a river of craving, it takes one to the lower realms, like a swiftly flowing river into the great ocean (As 1774:3-4).

In the Abhidhamma there are eight lobha-rooted consciousnesses (*aṭṭhadhā lobhamūlāni*, Abhidh-s 17:1). See also *[taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/)* and *[kilesa](/glossary/kilesa/)*.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, Akusalamūla Sutta)
> **“Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, akusalamūlāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Lobho akusalamūlaṃ, doso akusalamūlaṃ, moho akusalamūlaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these three roots of the unwholesome. What are these three? Greed is a root of the unwholesome, hatred is a root of the unwholesome, delusion is a root of the unwholesome.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye lobho hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye lobho lubbhanā lubbhitattaṃ sārāgo sārajjanā sārajjitattaṃ abhijjhā lobho akusalamūlaṃ – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye lobho hoti.**  
> 389. Which is greed at that time? Whatever at that time is the greed, the clinging, the state of being greedy, the infatuation, the manner of infatuation, the state of being infatuated, the covetousness, the greed, a root of the unwholesome—this is greed at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Lobha, §389) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=94&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mzg5LiBLYXRhbW8gdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGxvYmhvIGhvdGk%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the marks of lobha)
> **Lubbhanti tena, sayaṃ vā lubbhati, lubbhanamattameva vā tanti lobho. Muyhanti tena, sayaṃ vā muyhati, muyhanamattameva vā tanti moho. Tesu lobho ārammaṇaggahaṇalakkhaṇo makkaṭālepo viya, abhisaṅgaraso tattakapāle khittamaṃsapesi viya, apariccāgapaccupaṭṭhāno telañjanarāgo viya, saṃyojaniyadhammesu assādadassanapadaṭṭhāno. So taṇhānadībhāvena vaḍḍhamāno, sīghasotā nadī viya mahāsamuddaṃ, apāyameva gahetvā gacchatīti daṭṭhabbo.**  
> ‘Greed’ (lobho) is so called because people covet (lubbhanti) by means of it (tena), or (vā) it (greed) itself (sayaṃ) covets (lubbhati), or (vā) that (taṃ) is simply the act of craving (lubbhanamattameva). ‘Delusion’ (moho) is so called because people are deluded (muyhanti) by means of it (tena), or (vā) it (delusion) itself (sayaṃ) is deluded (muyhati), or (vā) that (taṃ) is merely the act of delusion (muyhanamattameva). Among these (tesu), greed (lobho) is characterized by grasping objects (ārammaṇaggahaṇa), like monkey glue (makkaṭālepo viya); it has the essence of intense clinging (abhisaṅgarasa), like a piece of meat thrown onto a hot pan (tattakapāle khittamaṃsapesi viya); it manifests as non-abandonment (apariccāga), like oil-based dye (telañjanarāgo viya); and it has seeing satisfaction (assādadassana) in things leading to fetters (saṃyojaniyadhammesu) as its proximate cause (padaṭṭhāna). It should be understood (daṭṭhabbo) that it (greed) (so), developing (vaḍḍhamāno) by way of being a river of craving (taṇhānadībhāvena), takes (gahetvā) one to the lower realms (apāya), just as a river with a swift current (sīghasotā nadī) flows (gacchati) into the great ocean (mahāsamuddaṃ).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=291&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=THViYmhhbnRpIHRlbmEsIHNheWHhuYMgdsSBIGx1YmJoYXRpLCBsdWJiaGFuYW1hdHRhbWV2YQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the terms of lobha)
> **Lobhamohaniddesesu lubbhatīti lobho . Lubbhanāti lubbhanākāro. Lobhasampayuttacittaṃ, puggalo vā lubbhito; lubbhitassa bhāvo lubbhitattaṃ.**  
> In the expositions of greed and delusion (moho), greed (lobho) means it is greedy. Clinging (lubbhanā) means the manner of being greedy. It is a mind associated with greed, or an individual (puggalo) greedy (lubbhito); the state (bhāvo) of being greedy (lubbhito) is state of being greedy (lubbhitattaṃ).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, §389) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=295&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Mzg5LiBMb2JoYW1vaGFuaWRkZXNlc3UgbHViYmhhdMSrdGkgbG9iaG8gLg%3D%3D)
