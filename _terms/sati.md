### Grammatical Analysis
**Sati**: [f.] mindfulness; awareness; attentiveness; memory. From root *sar* (to remember, call to mind). The Visuddhimagga gives the derivation *saranti tāya, sayaṃ vā sarati saraṇamattameva vā esāti sati*: they remember by means of it, or it itself remembers, or it is the mere act of remembering (Vism-ii 437:1).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla, a noble disciple is *satimā*, "mindful", endowed with supreme mindfulness and alertness (*paramena satinepakkena*), one who remembers and recollects what was done and said long ago (*cirakatampi cirabhāsitampi saritā anussaritā*, A-v 61:2). In the Abhidhamma, *sati* is defined by a string of synonyms: *sati anussati paṭissati sati saraṇatā dhāraṇatā apilāpanatā asammussanatā*, and it is the faculty of mindfulness (*satindriya*), the power of mindfulness (*satibala*) and right mindfulness (*sammāsati*) (Dhs 404:2). In the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha it is one of the beautiful universals (*sobhanasādhāraṇa*) found in all beautiful consciousness (Abhidh-s 90:1, 129:1).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* gives the commentary definition (Vism-ii 437:2; the same in As 1207:1):
* **Lakkhaṇa** (Characteristic): not-slipping, not floating away from the object (*apilāpanalakkhaṇā*).
* **Rasa** (Function): non-forgetfulness (*asammosarasā*).
* **Paccupaṭṭhāna** (Manifestation): protection (*ārakkhapaccupaṭṭhānā*), or the state of confronting the object (*visayābhimukhabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānā vā*).
* **Padaṭṭhāna** (Proximate Cause): firm perception (*thirasaññāpadaṭṭhānā*), or the foundations of mindfulness such as the body (*kāyādisatipaṭṭhānapadaṭṭhānā vā*).

It should be seen as like a pillar, because it is firmly established on the object, and like a gate-keeper, because it guards the eye-door and so on (Vism-ii 437:3). Sati appears among the five faculties (*[indriya](/glossary/indriya/)*), the five powers (*[bala](/glossary/bala/)*, A-v 61:1) and the seven factors of enlightenment (*[bojjhaṅga](/glossary/bojjhanga/)*); the Papañcasūdanī explains the mindfulness factor of awakening as having the characteristic of establishing, or of not-slipping (Mp-i 1522:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Satibala)
> **“Katamañca, bhikkhave, satibalaṃ? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako satimā hoti paramena satinepakkena samannāgato, cirakatampi cirabhāsitampi saritā anussaritā.**  
> “And what, bhikkhus, is the strength of mindfulness? Here, bhikkhus, a noble disciple is mindful, possessed of supreme mindfulness and alertness (satinepakka), one who remembers and recollects what was done and said long ago.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ tasmiṃ samaye satindriyaṃ hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye sati anussati paṭissati sati saraṇatā dhāraṇatā apilāpanatā asammussanatā sati satindriyaṃ satibalaṃ sammāsati – idaṃ tasmiṃ samaye satindriyaṃ hoti.**  
> 14. What is the faculty of mindfulness at that time? Whatever mindfulness, continuous recollection, specific mindfulness, mindfulness, recollecting, retention, penetration of the object, not forgetting, mindfulness, faculty of mindfulness, the power of mindfulness, or right mindfulness exists at that time—this is the faculty of mindfulness at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Satindriya, §14) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTQuIEthdGFtYeG5gyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgc2F0aW5kcml5YeG5gyBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Saranti tāya, sayaṃ vā sarati saraṇamattameva vā esāti sati. Sā apilāpanalakkhaṇā, asammosarasā, ārakkhapaccupaṭṭhānā, visayābhimukhabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānā vā, thirasaññāpadaṭṭhānā, kāyādisatipaṭṭhānapadaṭṭhānā vā. Ārammaṇe daḷhapatiṭṭhitattā pana esikā viya, cakkhudvārādirakkhaṇato dovāriko viya ca daṭṭhabbā.**  
> 465. They remember by means of it, or it itself remembers, or it is just the mere act of remembering—thus it is mindfulness (sati). It has the characteristic of not-slipping, the function of non-forgetfulness, and is manifested as protection or as the state of confronting an object. Its proximate cause is firm perception or the foundations of mindfulness such as the body. Because it is firmly established on the object, it should be seen as being like a pillar; and because it guards the eye-door and so on, it should be seen as being like a gate-keeper.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 437, Sati) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=94&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDY1LiBTYXJhbnRpIHTEgXlhLCBzYXlh4bmDIHbEgSBzYXJhdGk%3D)
