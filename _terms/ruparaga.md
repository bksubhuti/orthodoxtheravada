### Grammatical Analysis
**Rūparāga**: [m.] Desire for fine-material existence; lust for form. Formed by *rūpa* (form, fine-material) + *rāga* (desire, lust).

### Orthodox Definition
*Rūparāga* is the sixth of the ten fetters, and the first of the five "higher fetters" (*uddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*): "lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness, ignorance" (A-ix 462:3; S-v 400:3). The five lower fetters are identity view, doubt, adherence to rules and observances, sensual desire and ill will (D-iii 1476:1; A-ix 453:3), so *rūparāga* is the sixth when the two sets are counted together.

The Aṭṭhasālinī glosses *rūparāga* as "desire and lust in the form realm" (*rūpabhave chandarāgo*, As 1740:2), and says the five higher fetters are abandoned without remainder (As 1740:1); the Arahant path eradicates lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance, with the three underlying tendencies (As 1718:4). The Visuddhimagga calls the five "higher fetters" because they bind one to the aggregates and so on that arise in the higher realms (*uddhaṃnibbattanakakhandhādisaṃyojakattā*, Vism-ii 1948:3). The Peṭakopadesa says that the one who stands in the fruit of non-returning, developing serenity and insight further, abandons the five higher fetters (Pet 809:1).

### Quote
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, uddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañca? Rūparāgo, arūparāgo, māno, uddhaccaṃ, avijjā – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni.**  
> “Mendicants, there are five higher fetters. What five? Lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance—these indeed are the five higher fetters.  
>
