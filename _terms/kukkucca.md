### Grammatical Analysis
**Kukkucca**: [nt.] remorse; worry; anxiety. The Aṭṭhakathās explain: "what is done badly is *kukata*; its state is *kukkucca*" (*kucchitaṃ kataṃ kukataṃ, tassa bhāvo kukkuccaṃ*, Vism-ii 475:1-2; As 1815:10-11). So *ku* (contemptible, bad) + *kata* (done).

### Orthodox Definition
*Kukkucca* is remorse. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī (Dhs 1951:2-3) defines it as the perception of the allowable in what is unallowable, of the unallowable in what is allowable, of the blameworthy in what is faultless, of the faultless in what is blameworthy, with the synonyms *kukkuccaṃ kukkuccāyanā kukkuccāyitattaṃ cetaso vippaṭisāro manovilekho*.

The Atthasālinī explains *vippaṭisāra* as the mind's confronting what has been done and not done: it cannot make an evil deed done into one undone, nor a good deed not done into one done, so it is a contemptible regret. It arises "as if an awl were scratching a bronze plate, scraping the mind" (*manovilekho*, As 2323:4-8). The anxiety that appears in the Vinaya, such as the scruple of Sāriputta, is not the hindrance (As 2323:10).

In the Visuddhimagga its marks are: characteristic, later regret (*pacchānutāpalakkhaṇaṃ*); function, lamenting over what was done and not done (*katākatānusocanarasaṃ*); manifestation, remorse (*vippaṭisārapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ*); proximate cause, what was done and not done (*katākatapadaṭṭhānaṃ*); and it should be seen like servitude (*dāsabyamiva daṭṭhabbaṃ*) (Vism-ii 475:3). In the Sāmaññaphala Sutta the abandoning of "restlessness and worry" (*uddhaccakukkuccaṃ*) is the fourth step in purifying the mind of the hindrances (D-i 318:4), and the sutta compares being freed from the hindrances to a slave released from slavery (D-i 322:1-2).

The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha lists *kukkucca* among the fourteen unwholesome mental factors, and places it with hatred (*doso*), envy and avarice in the consciousness accompanied by aversion (Abhidh-s 88:1; 169:1). See *[uddhacca-kukkucca](/glossary/uddhacca-kukkucca/)*.

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, Sāmaññaphala Sutta)
> **Uddhaccakukkuccaṃ pahāya anuddhato viharati, ajjhattaṃ vūpasantacitto, uddhaccakukkuccā cittaṃ parisodheti.**  
> Having abandoned restlessness and worry, he dwells at ease within himself, with a peaceful mind; he purifies his mind from restlessness and worry.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamaṃ kukkuccaṃ? Akappiye kappiyasaññitā, kappiye akappiyasaññitā, avajje vajjasaññitā, vajje avajjasaññitā. Yaṃ evarūpaṃ kukkuccaṃ kukkuccāyanā kukkuccāyitattaṃ cetaso vippaṭisāro manovilekho – idaṃ vuccati kukkuccaṃ.**  
> 1166. Which is anxiety? The perception of allowable in what is unallowable, the perception of unallowable in what is allowable, the perception of blameworthy in what is faultless, the perception of faultless in what is blameworthy. Whatever such anxiety, the act of feeling anxiety, the state of feeling anxiety, the mind's remorse, mental friction there is — this is called anxiety (kukkuccaṃ).  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Nīvaraṇagocchaka, §1166) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=233&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTE2Ni4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyBrdWtrdWNjYeG5gz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Kucchitaṃ kataṃ kukataṃ. Tassa bhāvo kukkuccaṃ. Taṃ pacchānutāpalakkhaṇaṃ, katākatānusocanarasaṃ, vippaṭisārapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, katākatapadaṭṭhānaṃ, dāsabyamiva daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> 488. A contemptible act is a bad act. The state of that is remorse (kukkuccaṃ). It has the characteristic of sorrow after the fact; the function of grieving over what has been done or left undone; is manifested as regret; has what has been done or left undone as its proximate cause; and should be seen like slavery.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. XIV, §488) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=100&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDg4LiBLdWNjaGl0YeG5gyBrYXRh4bmDIGt1a2F0YeG5gy4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Cetaso vippaṭisāroti ettha katākatassa sāvajjānavajjassa vā abhimukhagamanaṃ ‘ vippaṭisāro ’ nāma. Yasmā pana so kataṃ vā pāpaṃ akataṃ na karoti, akataṃ vā kalyāṇaṃ kataṃ na karoti, tasmā virūpo kucchito vā paṭisāroti ‘ vippaṭisāro ’.**  
> As for the mind’s remorse (cetaso vippaṭisāro), herein ‘remorse’ is the mind’s confronting of what has been done and not done, or of what is blameworthy and blameless. Because that (remorse) cannot make evil deeds that have been done as undone, nor good deeds that have not been done as done, therefore, it is a disfigured or contemptible regret; hence it is called ‘remorse’ (vippaṭisāro).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, §1166) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=414&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2V0YXNvIHZpcHBh4bmtaXPEgXJvdGkgZXR0aGEga2F0xIFrYXRhc3NhIHPEgXZhamrEgW5hdmFqamFzc2EgdsSB)
