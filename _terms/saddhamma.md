### Grammatical Analysis
**Saddhamma**: [m.] The True [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/); The Good Law; The sublime doctrine. Formed by *sat* (true, good, real) + *[dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/)* (doctrine, teaching, reality). The Aṭṭhakathā glosses: *sataṃ dhammo, sobhano vā dhammoti saddhammo*, "the Dhamma of the good, or the excellent Dhamma" (Paṭis-a 1913).

### Orthodox Definition
*Saddhamma* is the true Dhamma of the Buddha. The Samantapāsādikā divides it in three (Sp-i 932):
1. **[Pariyatti](/glossary/pariyatti/)-saddhamma**: all the word of the Buddha comprised in the three Piṭakas.
2. **[Paṭipatti](/glossary/patipatti/)-saddhamma**: the thirteen ascetic practices (*dhutaguṇā*), the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, and virtue, concentration and insight.
3. **Adhigama-saddhamma**: the four noble paths, the four fruits of the life of a recluse, and Nibbāna. The Papañcasūdanī calls the same stage *paṭivedha*, "the penetration of the truths", when it lists the three disappearances (*antaradhāna*) of *pariyatti*, *paṭivedha* and *paṭipatti* (Ps-iii 313).

In the Mūla the Buddha tells Mahākassapa that the true Dhamma does not disappear so long as a counterfeit of the true Dhamma (*saddhammappatirūpakaṃ*) has not arisen in the world, as true gold does not disappear until counterfeit gold arises (SN 16.13). It is not the earth, water, fire or air element that makes it disappear; "worthless people who arise right here" do so. He lists five "detrimental things" that lead to its decline: monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen live without respect for the Teacher, the Dhamma, the Saṅgha, the training and concentration. Elsewhere five things lead to its decline when monks do not carefully listen to, learn, retain, examine the meaning of, and practise the Dhamma (AN 5.154); and five when they learn the discourses wrongly (AN 5.156).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, the counterfeit)
> **“Evañcetaṃ, kassapa, hoti sattesu hāyamānesu saddhamme antaradhāyamāne, bahutarāni ceva sikkhāpadāni honti appatarā ca bhikkhū aññāya saṇṭhahanti. Na tāva, kassapa, saddhammassa antaradhānaṃ hoti yāva na saddhammappatirūpakaṃ loke uppajjati. Yato ca kho, kassapa, saddhammappatirūpakaṃ loke uppajjati, atha saddhammassa antaradhānaṃ hoti”.**  
> “That is how it is, Kassapa. When beings are deteriorating and the true Dhamma is disappearing, there are more training rules but fewer monks are established in final knowledge. Kassapa, the true Dhamma does not disappear so long as a counterfeit of the true Dhamma has not arisen in the world. But when a counterfeit of the true Dhamma arises in the world, then the true Dhamma disappears.”  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 16.13, Saddhammappatirūpaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=422&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEV2YcOxY2V0YeG5gywga2Fzc2FwYSwgaG90aSBzYXR0ZXN1IGjEgXlhbcSBbmVzdQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, who makes it disappear)
> **“Na kho, kassapa, pathavīdhātu saddhammaṃ antaradhāpeti, na āpodhātu saddhammaṃ antaradhāpeti, na tejodhātu saddhammaṃ antaradhāpeti, na vāyodhātu saddhammaṃ antaradhāpeti; atha kho idheva te uppajjanti moghapurisā ye imaṃ saddhammaṃ antaradhāpenti.**  
> “It is not the earth element, Kassapa, that causes the true Dhamma to disappear; nor the water element, nor the fire element, nor the air element. It is the worthless people who arise right here who cause the true Dhamma to disappear.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 16.13, Saddhammappatirūpaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=423&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIE5hIGtobywga2Fzc2FwYSwgcGF0aGF2xKtkaMSBdHUgc2FkZGhhbW1h4bmD)

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, the five detrimental things)
> **“Pañca khome, kassapa, okkamaniyā dhammā saddhammassa sammosāya antaradhānāya saṃvattanti. Katame pañca? Idha, kassapa, bhikkhū bhikkhuniyo upāsakā upāsikāyo satthari agāravā viharanti appatissā, dhamme agāravā viharanti appatissā, saṅghe agāravā viharanti appatissā, sikkhāya agāravā viharanti appatissā, samādhismiṃ agāravā viharanti appatissā – ime kho, kassapa, pañca okkamaniyā dhammā saddhammassa sammosāya antaradhānāya saṃvattanti.**  
> “There are, Kassapa, five detrimental things that lead to the decline and disappearance of the true Dhamma. What are the five? Here, Kassapa, the monks, the nuns, the lay followers, and the female lay followers dwell without respect and without deference towards the Teacher; they dwell without respect and without deference towards the Dhamma; they dwell without respect and without deference towards the Saṅgha; they dwell without respect and without deference towards the training; they dwell without respect and without deference towards concentration—these, Kassapa, are the five detrimental things that lead to the decline and disappearance of the true Dhamma.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 16.13, Saddhammappatirūpaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=423&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFBhw7FjYSBraG9tZSwga2Fzc2FwYSwgb2trYW1hbml5xIEgZGhhbW3EgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Pañcime, bhikkhave, dhammā saddhammassa sammosāya antaradhānāya saṃvattanti. Katame pañca? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhū na sakkaccaṃ dhammaṃ suṇanti, na sakkaccaṃ dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇanti, na sakkaccaṃ dhammaṃ dhārenti, na sakkaccaṃ dhātānaṃ dhammānaṃ atthaṃ upaparikkhanti, na sakkaccaṃ atthamaññāya dhammamaññāya dhammānudhammaṃ paṭipajjanti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca dhammā saddhammassa sammosāya antaradhānāya saṃvattanti.**  
> “Mendicants, these five things lead to the decline and disappearance of the true Dhamma. What five? It’s when, mendicants, monks do not carefully listen to the Dhamma, do not carefully learn the Dhamma, do not carefully retain the Dhamma, do not carefully examine the meaning of the retained Dhamma, and do not carefully undertake the practice in accordance with the meaning and the Dhamma. These five things, mendicants, lead to the decline and disappearance of the true Dhamma.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 5.154, Paṭhamasaddhammasammosa Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=155&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTU0LuKAmOKAmCBQYcOxY2ltZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBkaGFtbcSBIHNhZGRoYW1tYXNzYSBzYW1tb3PEgXlh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Saddhammaṭṭhitiyāti tividho saddhammo – pariyattisaddhammo, paṭipattisaddhammo, adhigamasaddhammoti. Tattha piṭakattayasaṅgahitaṃ sabbampi buddhavacanaṃ “pariyattisaddhammo” nāma. Terasa dhutaguṇā, cuddasa khandhakavattāni, dveasīti mahāvattāni, sīlasamādhivipassanāti ayaṃ “paṭipattisaddhammo” nāma. Cattāro ariyamaggā cattāri ca sāmaññaphalāni nibbānañcāti ayaṃ “adhigamasaddhammo” nāma.**  
> For the continuation of the true Dhamma (saddhammaṭṭhitiyā) means: the true Dhamma is threefold—the true Dhamma of scripture, the true Dhamma of practice, and the true Dhamma of attainment. Therein, the entire word of the Buddha contained within the three Pitakas is called the “true Dhamma of scripture.” The thirteen ascetic practices, the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, virtue, concentration, and insight are called the “true Dhamma of practice.” The four noble paths, the four fruits of the life of a recluse, and Nibbāna are called the “true Dhamma of attainment.”  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Pārājikakaṇḍa-aṭṭhakathā, Sp-i 932) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=190&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FkZGhhbW1h4bmt4bmtaGl0aXnEgXRpIHRpdmlkaG8gc2FkZGhhbW1vIOKAkyBwYXJpeWF0dGlzYWRkaGFtbW8sIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc2FkZGhhbW1vLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Tīṇi hi antaradhānāni nāma pariyattiantaradhānaṃ, paṭivedhaantaradhānaṃ, paṭipattiantaradhānanti. Tattha pariyattīti tīṇi piṭakāni. Paṭivedhoti saccapaṭivedho. Paṭipattīti paṭipadā.**  
> ‘There are indeed three disappearances,’ namely, the disappearance of learning, the disappearance of penetration, and the disappearance of practice. There, ‘learning’ is the three baskets. ‘Penetration’ is the penetration of the truths. ‘Practice’ is the path.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Ps-iii 313) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_03&page_number=80&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VMSr4bmHaSBoaSBhbnRhcmFkaMSBbsSBbmkgbsSBbWEgcGFyaXlhdHRpYW50YXJhZGjEgW5h4bmDLCBwYeG5rWl2ZWRoYWFudGFyYWRoxIFuYeG5gyw%3D)

### Textual References
