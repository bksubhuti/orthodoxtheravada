### Grammatical Analysis
**Karuṇā**: [f.] compassion. The Atthasālinī gives three explanations: it is that which, when there is another's suffering, causes the hearts of the good to tremble (*paradukkhe sati sādhūnaṃ hadayakampanaṃ karotīti karuṇā*); or it removes, harms or destroys the suffering of others (*kiṇāti vā paradukkhaṃ hiṃsati vināsetīti*); or it is spread (*kirīyati*) among the suffering in the sense of pervading them (As 1536:3-5; Vism-i 1939:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Karuṇā* is the second of the four divine abodes (*[brahmavihāra](/glossary/brahmavihara/)*): loving-kindness, compassion, altruistic joy and equanimity (As 1536:1).

The Mahāgovinda Sutta (DN 19) gives its practice: one pervades one quarter with a mind imbued with compassion, and so the second, the third and the fourth, and thus above, below and all around (D-ii 906:5-7). The Vibhaṅga (Appamaññāvibhaṅga) explains by the simile of seeing a wretched, miserable person and feeling compassion, and so pervading all beings (Vibh 1871:2). The Abhidhamma defines it as *karuṇā karuṇāyanā karuṇāyitattaṃ karuṇācetovimutti*, in the first jhāna accompanied by compassion at the time of developing the way to the fine-material rebirth; the same is said for the second and the third jhāna (Vibh 1922:2; 1923:2; 1924:2).

The Visuddhimagga gives its marks: characteristic, the aspect of allaying suffering (*dukkhāpanayanākārappavattilakkhaṇā*); function, not bearing others' suffering (*paradukkhāsahanarasā*); manifestation, non-cruelty (*avihiṃsāpaccupaṭṭhānā*); proximate cause, seeing the helplessness of those overwhelmed by suffering. The subsiding of cruelty is its success; the arising of sorrow is its failure (Vism-i 1940:3-4).

Of its two enemies, the near enemy is house-based sorrow (*gehassitaṃ domanassaṃ*), because it resembles it in seeing misfortune; the far enemy is cruelty (*vihesā*), being dissimilar in nature; therefore one should practise compassion fearlessly from that (As 1541:1-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, Mahāgovinda Sutta)
> **‘ Karuṇedhimutto ’ ti ahaṃ bhoto ājānāmi. Idhekacco karuṇāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ. Iti uddhamadhotiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ karuṇāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjena pharitvā viharati.**  
> I understand, venerable sir, what you mean by ‘pervaded by pity’. Here, someone pervades one quarter with a mind imbued with pity, and so the second, and so the third, and so the fourth. Thus, above, below, around, and everywhere, they continue to pervade the entire world with a mind imbued with pity, abundant, expansive, immeasurable, without hostility and without ill-will.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 19, Mahāgovinda Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=194&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCYIEthcnXhuYdlZGhpbXV0dG8g4oCZIHRpIGFoYeG5gyBiaG90bw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamā karuṇā? Idha bhikkhu yasmiṃ samaye rūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti vivicceva kāmehi … pe … paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati karuṇāsahagataṃ, yā tasmiṃ samaye karuṇā karuṇāyanā karuṇāyitattaṃ karuṇācetovimutti – ayaṃ vuccati “karuṇā”. Avasesā dhammā karuṇāya sampayuttā.**  
> Therein, what is compassion? Here, bhikkhu, at the time when one develops the way for rebirth in the fine-material realm, quite secluded from sensual pleasures ... (as explained before) ... one enters and dwells in the first jhāna, accompanied by compassion—whatever compassion at that time, manner of compassion, state of being imbued with compassion, liberation of the mind by compassion—this is called compassion (karuṇā). The rest of the qualities are conjoined with compassion.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Appamaññāvibhaṅga, §686) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=291&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Njg2LiBUYXR0aGEga2F0YW3EgSBrYXJ14bmHxIE%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the meaning)
> **Etāsu hi mettākaruṇāmuditāupekkhāsu atthato tāva mejjatīti mettā , siniyhatīti attho. Mitte vā bhavā, mittassa vā esā pavattatīpi mettā. Paradukkhe sati sādhūnaṃ hadayakampanaṃ karotīti karuṇā. Kiṇāti vā paradukkhaṃ hiṃsati vināsetīti karuṇā. Kirīyati vā dukkhitesu pharaṇavasena pasāriyatīti karuṇā.**  
> Indeed, as to the meaning of these: loving-kindness, compassion, altruistic joy, and equanimity—first, ‘loving-kindness’ (mettā) is that which loves (mejjati); this means ‘it is affectionate’. Or it is ‘loving-kindness’ (mettā) because it arises in a friend (mitte bhavā), or because it proceeds for a friend (mittassa esā pavattati). ‘Compassion’ (karuṇā) is that which, when there is suffering in another, causes the hearts of good people to tremble (sādhūnaṃ hadayakampanaṃ karoti). Or it is ‘compassion’ (karuṇā) because it removes (kiṇāti), harms (hiṃsati), or destroys (vināseti) the suffering of others. Or it is ‘compassion’ (karuṇā) because it is spread (kirīyati) or diffused (pasāriyati) among the suffering in the sense of pervading.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=237&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RXTEgXN1IGhpIG1ldHTEgWthcnXhuYfEgW11ZGl0xIF1cGVra2jEgXN1IGF0dGhhdG8gdMSBdmEgbWVqamF0xKt0aQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the marks)
> **Dukkhāpanayanākārappavattilakkhaṇā karuṇā , paradukkhāsahanarasā, avihiṃsāpaccupaṭṭhānā, dukkhābhibhūtānaṃ anāthabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā. Vihiṃsūpasamo tassā sampatti, sokasambhavo vipatti.**  
> Compassion (karuṇā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of allaying suffering; its function is not bearing others' suffering; it is manifested as non-cruelty; and its proximate cause is seeing the helplessness of those overwhelmed by suffering. The subsiding of cruelty is its success (sampatti), and the arising of sorrow is its failure (vipatti).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. IX, §263) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=311&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RHVra2jEgXBhbmF5YW7EgWvEgXJhcHBhdmF0dGlsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBrYXJ14bmHxIEgLCBwYXJhZHVra2jEgXNhaGFuYXJhc8SBLCBhdmloaeG5g3PEgXBhY2N1cGHhua3hua1oxIFuxIEsIGR1a2toxIFiaGliaMWrdMSBbmHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the two enemies)
> **Karuṇābrahmavihārassa “cakkhuviññeyyānaṃ rūpānaṃ iṭṭhānaṃ kantānaṃ manāpānaṃ manoramānaṃ lokāmisapaṭisaṃyuttānaṃ appaṭilābhaṃ vā appaṭilābhato samanupassato pubbe vā paṭiladdhapubbaṃ atītaṃ niruddhaṃ vipariṇataṃ samanussarato uppajjati domanassaṃ. Yaṃ evarūpaṃ domanassaṃ idaṃ vuccati gehassitaṃ domanassan” tiādinā nayena āgataṃ gehassitaṃ domanassaṃ vipattidassanasabhāgatāya āsannapaccatthikaṃ. Sabhāvavisabhāgatāya vihesā dūrapaccatthikā. Tasmā tato nibbhayena karuṇāyitabbaṃ.**  
> For the divine abiding of compassion: ‘‘Mental pain arises in one who sees the non-acquisition or absence of acquisition of forms cognizable by the eye—which are desirable, lovely, pleasing, charming, and connected with worldly allurements—or in one who recalls those previously acquired, which are past, ceased, and transformed. Whatever such mental pain is called ‘worldly mental pain’—this worldly mental pain, which appeared by way of this method and so on, is the near enemy due to its similarity in seeing misfortune. Harmfulness is the far enemy due to its dissimilarity in nature. Therefore, compassion should be developed fearlessly from that.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=238&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2FydeG5h8SBYnJhaG1hdmloxIFyYXNzYSDigJjigJggY2Fra2h1dmnDscOxZXl5xIFuYeG5gyByxatwxIFuYeG5gyBp4bmt4bmtaMSBbmHhuYMga2FudMSBbmHhuYM%3D)
