### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammāvāyāma**: [m.] Right Effort; Right Endeavour. Formed by *sammā* (rightly) + *vāyāma* (effort, endeavour; from the verb *vāyamati*, "he endeavours"). The Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā gives its characteristic as *sammā paggahalakkhaṇo sammāvāyāmo*, "right effort has the characteristic of correctly exerting" (Vibh-a 515). The Abhidhamma analysis identifies it with the mental factor of energy: "whatever is the arousal of mental energy (*cetasiko vīriyārambho*) … is right effort, the energy enlightenment factor, a path factor" (Vibh 763).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammāvāyāma* is the sixth factor of the Noble Eightfold Path (*sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati, sammāsamādhi*, DN 22). In the Cūḷavedalla Sutta (MN 44) the bhikkhunī Dhammadinnā tells the lay follower Visākha that right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration are comprised within the aggregate of concentration (*samādhikkhandhe saṅgahitā*), so it is the first factor of the concentration group.

The Mūla defines it by a fourfold formula: the bhikkhu generates desire, endeavours, arouses energy, exerts his mind and strives (1) for the non-arising of unarisen unwholesome states, (2) for the abandonment of arisen unwholesome states, (3) for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, and (4) for the endurance, non-confusion, increase, extension, development and fulfilment of arisen wholesome states (DN 22; Vibh 747). The same formula, with the name *sammappadhāna*, "right striving", is in the Padhāna Sutta (AN 4.13), and the Saṃvara Sutta (AN 4.14) names the four strivings: *saṃvarappadhāna*, *pahānappadhāna*, *bhāvanāppadhāna* and *anurakkhaṇāppadhāna* (restraint, abandonment, development, protection). The Manorathapūraṇī explains them: the striving to restrain the defilements and shut the door of their entry, the striving to abandon, the striving to make wholesome states grow and increase, and the striving to protect those same states (Mp-iv 1305).

The Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā places right effort in the order of the path: for one established in the ground of virtue (right speech, right action, right livelihood), the arousal of energy that eradicates laziness and accomplishes the non-arising, abandonment, arising and endurance of unwholesome and wholesome states is right effort (Vibh-a 541).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāvāyāmo.**  
> “Now what, monks, is right endeavour? Here, monks, a monk regarding bad and unwholesome states that have not yet arisen generates desire for their non-arising, (in this regard) he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. Regarding bad and unwholesome states that have already arisen he generates desire for their abandonment, (in this regard) he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. He generates desire for the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen, (in this regard) he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. Regarding wholesome states that have arisen he generates desire for their endurance, persistence, multiplication, extension, development, and fulfilment, (in this regard) he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. This, monks, is called right endeavour.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=250&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYW1txIF2xIF5xIFtbz8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the four strivings)
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, padhānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Saṃvarappadhānaṃ, pahānappadhānaṃ, bhāvanāppadhānaṃ, anurakkhaṇāppadhānaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these four kinds of striving. What are the four? Striving by restraint, striving by abandonment, striving by development, and striving by protection.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 4.14, Saṃvara Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=323&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTQu4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJpbcSBbmksIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgcGFkaMSBbsSBbmku)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya, the concentration group)
> **Yo ca sammāvāyāmo yā ca sammāsati yo ca sammāsamādhi ime dhammā samādhikkhandhe saṅgahitā.**  
> Whatever is right effort, and whatever is right mindfulness, and whatever is right concentration—these qualities are comprised within the aggregate of concentration.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 44, Cūḷavedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=375&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=WW8gY2Egc2FtbcSBdsSBecSBbW8gecSBIGNhIHNhbW3EgXNhdGk%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamo sammāvāyāmo? Yo cetasiko vīriyārambho… pe … sammāvāyāmo vīriyasambojjhaṅgo maggaṅgaṃ maggapariyāpannaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati “sammāvāyāmo”.**  
> Therein, what (katamo) is right effort (sammāvāyāmo)? Whatever is the arousal of mental energy (cetasiko vīriyārambho) ... (pe) right effort (sammāvāyāmo), the energy enlightenment factor (vīriyasambojjhaṅgo), a path factor (maggaṅgaṃ), included in the path (maggapariyāpannaṃ) — this (ayaṃ) is called (vuccati) ‘‘right effort’’.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Saccavibhaṅga, Maggasacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=113&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyBzYW1txIF2xIF5xIFtbz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Imissā sammāvācākammantājīvasaṃkhātāya sīlabhūmiyaṃ patiṭṭhamānassa tadanurūpo taṃsampayuttova kosajjasamucchedako anuppannuppannānaṃ akusalakusalānaṃ anuppādapahānuppādaṭṭhitisādhako ca vīriyārambho sammāvāyāmo.**  
> For one established in this ground of virtue known as right speech, right action, and right livelihood, the arousal of energy (vīriyārambho) suited to that—associated with those factors—which eradicates laziness and accomplishes the non-arising, abandonment, arising, and endurance of unarisen and arisen unwholesome and wholesome states, is right effort.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Manorathapūraṇī)
> **Navame kilesānaṃ saṃvaratthāya pavesanadvāraṃ pidahanatthāya padhānaṃ saṃvarappadhānaṃ , pajahanatthāya padhānaṃ pahānappadhānaṃ , kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ brūhanatthāya vaḍḍhanatthāya padhānaṃ bhāvanāppadhānaṃ , tesaṃyeva anurakkhaṇatthāya padhānaṃ anurakkhaṇāppadhānaṃ.**  
> 69. In the ninth, the striving for the sake of restraining defilements and closing the door of entry is 'striving by restraint'; the striving for the sake of abandoning is 'striving by abandonment'; the striving for the sake of causing the wholesome states to grow and increase is 'striving by development'; and the striving for the sake of protecting those same is 'striving by protection'.  
>
