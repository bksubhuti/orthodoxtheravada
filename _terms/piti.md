### Grammatical Analysis
**Pīti**: [f.] rapture; joy; delight. The Visuddhimagga derives it: "it refreshes, thus it is *pīti*" (*pīṇayatīti pīti*). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it by its synonyms: *pīti pāmojjaṃ āmodanā pamodanā hāso pahāso vitti odagyaṃ attamanatā cittassa* (Dhs 399).

### Orthodox Definition
*Pīti* is a mental factor (*[cetasika](/glossary/cetasika/)*): the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha lists it among the six "miscellaneous" factors (*pakiṇṇaka*: *vitakka, vicāra, adhimokkha, vīriya, pīti, chanda*), which are found in the cittas "as fits" (*yathāyogaṃ*). The Mūla names it as the fourth factor of awakening (*pītisambojjhaṅga*; sati, dhammavicaya, vīriya, pīti…) and as a factor of the first jhāna ("born of seclusion, with rapture and happiness", *vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ*).

The Visuddhimagga gives its characteristic (*lakkhaṇa*), function (*rasa*) and manifestation (*paccupaṭṭhāna*): "it has the characteristic of endearing (*sampiyāyana*); its function is to refresh body and mind, or to pervade; its manifestation is elation (*odagya*)" (Vism-i 941). The Atthasālinī separates *pīti* from *sukha*: rapture is "the delight in obtaining a desirable object", happiness "the experience of the taste obtained"; where there is rapture there is happiness, but not always the reverse; rapture is included in the aggregate of formations (*saṅkhārakkhandha*), happiness in the aggregate of feeling (*vedanākkhandha*). The simile: *pīti* is like a traveller exhausted in the wilderness who sees or hears of water; *sukha* is like his entering the shade and drinking (As 1185-1186).

The Visuddhimagga distinguishes **five kinds of rapture** (Vism-i 941):
1. *Khuddikā pīti* (minor rapture): able only to raise the hairs of the body.
2. *Khaṇikā pīti* (momentary rapture): like flashes of lightning from moment to moment.
3. *Okkantikā pīti* (showering rapture): like waves breaking repeatedly on the seashore.
4. *Ubbegā pīti* (uplifting rapture): strong enough to lift the body into the air (the story of Mahātissa Thera).
5. *Pharaṇā pīti* (pervading rapture): when it arises the whole body is pervaded, like a bladder filled with air, or a mountain cavern invaded by a great flood of water (Vism-i 943). It is the rapture that is "the root of absorption concentration" and meant by *pīti* in the jhāna factors (Vism-i 944).


### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, the factor of awakening)
> **“Santaṃ vā ajjhattaṃ pītisambojjhaṅgaṃ ‘ atthi me ajjhattaṃ pītisambojjhaṅgo ’ ti pajānāti, asantaṃ vā ajjhattaṃ pītisambojjhaṅgaṃ ‘ natthi me ajjhattaṃ pītisambojjhaṅgo ’ ti pajānāti, yathā ca anuppannassa pītisambojjhaṅgassa uppādo hoti tañca pajānāti, yathā ca uppannassa pītisambojjhaṅgassa bhāvanāya pāripūrī hoti tañca pajānāti.**  
> Having the Joyful-Interest factor of Complete Awakening in himself he knows “there is the Joy factor of Complete Awakening in myself”; or, not having the Joyful-Interest factor of Complete Awakening in himself he knows “there is no Joyful-Interest factor of Complete Awakening in myself”. How there is an arising of the Joyful-Interest factor of Complete Awakening that has not arisen—that he knows; and how there is fulfilment of the development of the Joyful-Interest factor of Complete Awakening that has arisen—; that also he knows.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=242&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNhbnRh4bmDIHbEgSBhampoYXR0YeG5gyBwxKt0aXNhbWJvampoYeG5hWdh4bmDIOKAmA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye pīti hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye pīti pāmojjaṃ āmodanā pamodanā hāso pahāso vitti odagyaṃ attamanatā cittassa – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye pīti hoti.**  
> 9. What is rapture at that time? Whatever rapture, joy, delight, gladdening, joy, great joy, happiness, elation, or gladness of mind (attamanatā cittassa) exists at that time—this is rapture at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 9) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OS4gS2F0YW3EgSB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgcMSrdGkgaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī (pīti and sukha))
> **Aparo nayo – sātalakkhaṇaṃ sukhaṃ, sampayuttānaṃ upabrūhanarasaṃ, anuggahaṇapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ. Satipi ca nesaṃ pītisukhānaṃ katthaci avippayoge, iṭṭhārammaṇapaṭilābhatuṭṭhi pīti; paṭiladdharasānubhavanaṃ sukhaṃ. Yattha pīti tattha sukhaṃ. Yattha sukhaṃ tattha na niyamato pīti. Saṅkhārakkhandhasaṅgahitā pīti, vedanākkhandhasaṅgahitaṃ sukhaṃ. Kantārakhinnassa vanantodakadassanasavanesu viya pīti. Vanacchāyāpavesanaudakaparibhogesu viya sukhaṃ.**  
> Another method: happiness has the characteristic of pleasantness; its function is the increase of co-arisen states; it manifests as favoring. And even when there is no separation between rapture and happiness in some instances, rapture is the delight in obtaining a desirable object; happiness is the experience of the acquired taste. Where there is rapture, there is happiness. Where there is happiness, there is not necessarily rapture. Rapture is included in the aggregate of mental formations; happiness is included in the aggregate of feeling. Rapture, for one exhausted in the wilderness, is like seeing and hearing water in a forest grove. Happiness is like entering the shade of the forest and consuming water.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 1185) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=160&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBhcm8gbmF5byDigJMgc8SBdGFsYWtraGHhuYdh4bmDIHN1a2hh4bmDLCBzYW1wYXl1dHTEgW5h4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IV)
> **Pītisukhanti ettha pīṇayatīti pīti. Sā sampiyāyanalakkhaṇā, kāyacittapīnanarasā, pharaṇarasā vā, odagyapaccupaṭṭhānā. Sā panesā khuddikā pīti, khaṇikāpīti, okkantikāpīti, ubbegāpīti, pharaṇāpītīti pañcavidhā hoti. Tattha khuddikāpīti sarīre lomahaṃsamattameva kātuṃ sakkoti. Khaṇikāpīti khaṇe khaṇe vijjuppādasadisā hoti. Okkantikāpīti samuddatīraṃ vīci viya kāyaṃ okkamitvā okkamitvā bhijjati. Ubbegāpīti balavatī hoti kāyaṃ uddhaggaṃ katvā ākāse laṅghāpanappamāṇappattā. Tathā hi puṇṇavallikavāsī mahātissatthero puṇṇamadivase sāyaṃ cetiyaṅgaṇaṃ gantvā candālokaṃ disvā mahācetiyābhimukho hutvā “imāya vata velāya catasso parisā mahācetiyaṃ vandantī” ti pakatiyā diṭṭhārammaṇavasena buddhārammaṇaṃ ubbegāpītiṃ uppādetvā sudhātale pahaṭacitrageṇḍuko viya ākāse uppatitvā mahācetiyaṅgaṇeyeva patiṭṭhāsi.**  
> As for rapture and pleasure (pītisukhaṃ): herein, it refreshes (pīṇayati), thus it is rapture (pīti). It has the characteristic of endearing. Its function is to refresh the body and mind, or its function is to pervade. It is manifested as elation. But this is of five kinds: minor rapture, momentary rapture, showering rapture, uplifting rapture, and pervading rapture. Herein, minor rapture (khuddikā pīti) is only able to raise the hairs on the body. Momentary rapture (khaṇikā pīti) is like flashes of lightning moment by moment. Showering rapture (okkantikā pīti) breaks over the body again and again, like a wave on the seashore. Uplifting rapture (ubbegā pīti) is powerful, reaching the extent of causing the body to leap up into the air. For instance, on the evening of the full-moon day, the elder Mahātissa (mahātissatthero), resident at Puṇṇavallika, went to the courtyard of the shrine. Seeing the moonlight, he faced toward the Great Shrine and thought, 'At this very hour, the four assemblies are indeed worshipping the Great Shrine!' By virtue of an object of contemplation previously seen, he aroused uplifting rapture with the Buddha as its object. He then rose into the air like a painted ball bounced off a plastered floor, and landed right on the courtyard of the Great Shrine.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IV, para. 72) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=139&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=NzIuIFDEq3Rpc3VraGFudGkgZXR0aGEgcMSr4bmHYXlhdMSrdGkgcMSrdGku)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IV, pervading rapture)
> **Pharaṇāpītiyā pana uppannāya sakalasarīraṃ dhamitvā pūritavatthi viya mahatā udakoghena pakkhandapabbatakucchi viya ca anuparipphuṭaṃ hoti.**  
> But when pervading rapture arises, the entire body is completely pervaded, like a bladder filled with air, or like a mountain cavern invaded by a great flood of water.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IV, Vism-i 943) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=140&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=UGhhcmHhuYfEgXDEq3RpecSBIHBhbmEgdXBwYW5uxIF5YSBzYWthbGFzYXLEq3Jh4bmDIGRoYW1pdHbEgSBwxatyaXRhdmF0dGhp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IV, rapture and absorption)
> **Tāsu yā appanāsamādhissa mūlaṃ hutvā vaḍḍhamānā samādhisampayogaṃ gatā pharaṇāpīti, ayaṃ imasmiṃ atthe adhippetā pītīti.**  
> Among these, the pervading rapture that is the root of absorption concentration, and which, by developing, enters into association with concentration, is what is meant by 'rapture' in this context.
