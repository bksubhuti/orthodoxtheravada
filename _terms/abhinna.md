### Grammatical Analysis
**Abhiññā**: [f.] supernormal knowledge; direct knowledge; psychic power. Formed by prefix *abhi* (superior, higher, special) + *ñā* (to know). Meaning: a higher, transcendent form of knowing.

### Orthodox Definition
*Abhiññā* refers to six specific supernormal cognitive powers. The first five are mundane (*lokiya*) and can be achieved through mastery of the fourth *[jhāna](/glossary/jhana/)* (Vism-ii 6:1). The sixth, the knowledge of the destruction of the taints, is supramundane; the Visuddhimagga calls the first five "mundane direct knowledges" (*pañca lokikābhiññā*, Vism-ii 7:2), and the Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha lists only these five as *abhiññā* (Abhidh-s 805:1-807:1).

The Six Direct Knowledges (*chaḷabhiññā*) are:
1. *[Iddhividha](/glossary/iddhividha/)*: Magical/psychic powers (multiplying the body, walking on water, etc.).
2. *[Dibbasota](/glossary/dibbasota/)*: The Divine Ear (clairaudience).
3. *[Cetopariyañāṇa](/glossary/cetopariyanana/)*: Penetration of others' minds (telepathy).
4. *[Pubbenivāsānussatiñāṇa](/glossary/pubbenivasanussatinana/)*: Recollection of past lives.
5. *[Dibbacakkhu](/glossary/dibbacakkhu/)*: The Divine Eye (clairvoyance / seeing beings passing away and rearising according to [kamma](/glossary/kamma/)).
6. *[Āsavakkhayañāṇa](/glossary/asavakkhayanana/)*: Knowledge of the destruction of the taints (leading to Arahatship).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. XII, *Iddhividhaniddeso*, Abhiññākathā) says that effort must be applied by a meditator who has attained the fourth [jhāna](/glossary/jhana/) in the earth kasiṇa and the other kasiṇas (Vism-ii 6:1); a beginner develops the eight attainments in each of the eight kasiṇas and trains the mind in fourteen ways (Vism-ii 8:1-10:1). In the Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha the jhāna from which one rises to perform the abhiññā is the fifth jhāna of the fivefold scheme, the *abhiññāpādaka* (Abhidh-s 804:1).

### Quote

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Dasuttara Sutta* (DN 34; heading "11. Dasuttarasuttaṃ", D-iii 1662:1) – "Cha abhiññā" among the things to be realized (D-iii 1760:1-2), the six given in D-iii 1760-1765.
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2; heading D-i 217:1) – the six powers as progressive fruits of the ascetic life, from *iddhividhāya cittaṃ abhinīharati* to the destruction of the taints.
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Brahmajāla Sutta* (DN 1, D-i 64:1) – *sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedeti*, "having realized for himself through direct knowledge, he proclaims", used of the Tathāgata's own knowledge (the word *abhiññā* in its general sense).
* **Canonical**: *Ākaṅkheyya Sutta* (MN 6; heading M-i 165:1) – "Ākaṅkheyya ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu …" (M-i 168-171): the monk who wishes to wield these powers must fulfil virtue, practise inner calm and not neglect jhāna.
* **Abhidhamma**: *Vibhaṅga* (Vibh 2447:1-2, "Chasu abhiññāsu paññā") and *Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha* (Abhidh-s 805:1-807:1, fivefold).
* **Commentary**: *Visuddhimagga* Chapters XII (*Iddhividhaniddeso*, Vism-ii 4:1) and XIII (*Abhiññāniddeso*, Vism-ii 151:1) – the five mundane direct knowledges and the resolutions needed for each.
