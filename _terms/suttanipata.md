### Grammatical Analysis
**Suttanipāta**: [m.] The Group of Discourses. Formed by *sutta* (discourse) + *nipāta* (group, falling together, collection).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Suttanipāta* is the fifth book of the *Khuddaka Nikāya* in the order of the Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Suttanipāta … (Pj-i 24:5). It is divided into five chapters (*vagga*): the Uragavagga, the Cūḷavagga, the Mahāvagga, the Aṭṭhakavagga and the Pārāyanavagga (Pj-ii 24:1), and the commentary counts seventy suttas, beginning with the Uraga Sutta (*uragasuttādisattatisuttappabhedassa*; Pj-ii 2678:1). The Mūla itself counts twelve suttas in the Uragavagga (Sn 758:1), fourteen in the Cūḷavagga (*Dhammikasuttaṃ cuddasamaṃ*, Sn 1377:1), twelve in the Mahāvagga (Sn 2644:1) and sixteen in the Aṭṭhakavagga (Sn 3336:1, 3344:1).

It includes some of the best-known suttas: the *Mettasutta* (the eighth of the Uragavagga, Sn 510:1), the *Ratanasutta* (the first of the Cūḷavagga, Sn 825:1) and the *Maṅgalasutta* (the fourth of the Cūḷavagga, Sn 927:1). The Khuddakapāṭha commentary tells how the Buddha told Ānanda to learn the Ratana Sutta and to make a protection (*parittaṃ*) with it in Vesālī (Pj-i 798:5).

Its last two chapters, the *Aṭṭhakavagga* (Chapter of Octads) and the *Pārāyanavagga* (Chapter on the Way to the Far Shore), were recited while the Buddha was alive. At the Buddha's request the Venerable Soṇa recited all sixteen *aṭṭhakavaggikāni* in a melodious voice (Ud 566:1), and the laywoman Nandamātā used to recite the *pārāyana* at dawn (AN 7.53, A-vii 349:2).

Like all suttas, the *Suttanipāta* is a teaching by convention (*vohāra-desanā*). However, to deeply understand the teachings, a student must still rely on the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/), which explains in terms of ultimate realities (*paramattha-dhamma*).

The specific commentary for this book, which names its author as the Elder Buddhaghosa in its colophon (Pj-ii 2684:1), is the *Suttanipāta-aṭṭhakathā* (Commentary on the Suttanipāta), whose formal title is the *[Paramatthajotikā](/glossary/paramatthajotika/)* ("The Illuminator of the Ultimate Meaning"). The commentator explains why the book is called *Suttanipāta*: it is a collection (*nipāta*) of suttas gathered from here and there, a sutta being so called because it is well-spoken, to be heard, protects meanings well, points out and distils them (Pj-ii 13-21). For each poem it asks by whom, where, when and why it was spoken (Pj-ii 30:1), so it gives the background story, identifies the people or devas involved, and preserves the exact definitions of the words.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Maṅgala Sutta* (Sn 2.4, the fourth sutta of the Cūḷavagga; Sn 927:1) – The discourse on the highest blessings.
* **Canonical**: *Ratana Sutta* (Sn 2.1, the first sutta of the Cūḷavagga; Sn 825:1) – The jewel discourse; the commentary says it was learned by Ānanda to make protection in Vesālī (Pj-i 798:5).
* **Canonical**: *[Metta](/glossary/metta/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Sn 1.8, the eighth sutta of the Uragavagga; Sn 510:1) – The sutta on loving-kindness.
* **Canonical**: *Khaggavisāṇa Sutta* (Sn 1.3; heading at Sn 125:1) – The Rhinoceros Horn sutta praising solitary wandering.
* **Canonical**: *[Udāna](/glossary/udana/)* 5.6 (Ud 566:1) and *Nandamātā Sutta* (AN 7.53, A-vii 349:2) – the Aṭṭhakavagga and the Pārāyana recited in the Buddha's lifetime.
* **Commentary**: *Suttanipāta-aṭṭhakathā* (formally titled *[Paramatthajotikā](/glossary/paramatthajotika/)*, Pj-ii) – The essential commentary by Ācariya Buddhaghosa (Pj-ii 2684:1). It explains the background stories and defines the words of the text.
