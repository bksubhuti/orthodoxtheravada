### Grammatical Analysis
**Duggati**: [f.] bad destination; unhappy destination. Formed by prefix *du* (bad) + *gati* (going, destination). The Paramatthadīpanī calls it "a state that has gone by the name *duggati* because it is the place where suffering arises" (*dukkhassa nibbattiṭṭhānatāya duggatīti saṅkhaṃ gataṃ*) and names as such the hell (*niraya*), the animal womb (*tiracchānayoni*) and the realm of ghosts (*pettivisaya*) (It-a 206:4). It also explains *duggatisaṅkhātānaṃ catunnaṃ apāyānaṃ aññatarā gati*, a destination among the four lower worlds called *duggati* (It-a 433:14).

### Orthodox Definition
*Duggati* is the counterpart of *[sugati](/glossary/sugati/)*. In the Vatthasutta the Buddha says: "when the mind is defiled, an unhappy destination (*duggati*) is to be expected"; "when the mind is undefiled, a happy destination (*sugati*) is to be expected" (M-i 190:5, 190:10). The same expectation is stated for one who is unguarded in the doors of the faculties and immoderate in eating: "with the breakup of the body, after death, an unhappy destination is to be expected" (It 248:1-2).

The commentary on this phrase says that for one with such a (defiled) mind a bad destination is to be expected: "he will surely reach a bad destination and nothing else", the expectation being inevitable (*avassaṃ bhāvī*); and that *duggati* is of two kinds, the bad destination of conduct (*paṭipattiduggati*) and the bad destination of rebirth (*gatiduggati*), the former again of the householder's and of the one gone forth's conduct (Ps-i 843:1-3).

In the stock formula the Buddha says that a person with misconduct is reborn "after the breakup of the body, after death, in the plane of misery, the bad destination, the lower realm, hell" (*apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjati*, for example Vibh 2932:2; quoted by the Aṭṭhakathās at Ud-a 506:4 and It-a 623:5).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, vatthaṃ saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ malaggahitaṃ; tamenaṃ rajako yasmiṃ yasmiṃ raṅgajāte upasaṃhareyya – yadi nīlakāya yadi pītakāya yadi lohitakāya yadi mañjiṭṭhakāya durattavaṇṇamevassa aparisuddhavaṇṇamevassa. Taṃ kissa hetu? Aparisuddhattā, bhikkhave, vatthassa. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, citte saṃkiliṭṭhe, duggati pāṭikaṅkhā. Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, vatthaṃ parisuddhaṃ pariyodātaṃ; tamenaṃ rajako yasmiṃ yasmiṃ raṅgajāte upasaṃhareyya – yadi nīlakāya yadi pītakāya yadi lohitakāya yadi mañjiṭṭhakāya – surattavaṇṇamevassa parisuddhavaṇṇamevassa. Taṃ kissa hetu? Parisuddhattā, bhikkhave, vatthassa. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, citte asaṃkiliṭṭhe, sugati pāṭikaṅkhā.**  
> “Monks, just as though a cloth were defiled and stained, and a dyer dipped it in some dye or other—whether blue, or yellow, or red, or carmine—it would look poorly dyed and impure in color. What is the reason for that? Because of the impurity of the cloth, monks. Even so, monks, when the mind is defiled, an unhappy destination is to be expected. Just as though, monks, a cloth were pure and bright, and a dyer dipped it in some dye or other—whether blue, or yellow, or red, or carmine—it would look well dyed and pure in color. What is the reason for that? Because of the purity of the cloth, monks. Even so, monks, when the mind is undefiled, a happy destination is to be expected.  
>

> “Monks, possessed of two qualities, a bhikkhu lives in discomfort here and now, with distress, with anguish, and with fever; with the breakup of the body, after death, an unhappy destination is to be expected.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthadīpanī (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā))
> **Duggati pāṭikaṅkhāti duggatisaṅkhātānaṃ catunnaṃ apāyānaṃ aññatarā gati icchitabbā, avassaṃbhāvinīti attho.**  
> ‘An unhappy destination is to be expected’ means that one of the four destinations termed 'unhappy' (duggati) should be anticipated; the meaning is that this is certain.  
>  
> — [Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/) (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā, It-a 433) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=92&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RHVnZ2F0aSBwxIHhua1pa2HhuYVraMSBdGkgZHVnZ2F0aXNh4bmFa2jEgXTEgW5h4bmDIGNhdHVubmHhuYMgYXDEgXnEgW5h4bmDIGHDscOxYXRhcsSB)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī (Majjhima-aṭṭhakathā))
> **Duggati pāṭikaṅkhāti īdise citte duggati pāṭikaṅkhitabbā, duggatiṃ eva esa pāpuṇissati, nāññanti evaṃ duggati icchitabbā, avassaṃ bhāvīti vuttaṃ hoti. Sā cāyaṃ duggati nāma paṭipattiduggati, gatiduggatīti duvidhā hoti. Paṭipattiduggatipi agāriyapaṭipattiduggati, anagāriyapaṭipattiduggatīti duvidhā hoti.**  
> 'An unhappy destination is to be expected' (duggati pāṭikaṅkhā) means an unhappy destination is to be expected for one with such a mind. That he will surely reach a bad destination, and nothing else, should be expected; it is said that a bad destination is to be desired (in the sense of being the inevitable outcome), as it is sure to happen. This bad destination is of two kinds: bad destination of conduct and bad destination of rebirth. Bad destination of conduct is also of two kinds: bad destination of conduct for a householder and bad destination of conduct for one who has gone forth.  
>  
> — Papañcasūdanī (Majjhimanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, Ps-i 843, on "duggati pāṭikaṅkhā") · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=172&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RHVnZ2F0aSBwxIHhua1pa2HhuYVraMSBdGkgxKtkaXNlIGNpdHRlIGR1Z2dhdGkgcMSB4bmtaWth4bmFa2hpdGFiYsSBLA%3D%3D)
