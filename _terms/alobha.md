### Grammatical Analysis
**Alobha**: [m.] Non-greed; unselfishness; detachment. Formed by negative prefix *a* (not, without) + *lobha* (greed, clinging, lust).

### Orthodox Definition
*Alobha* is the first of the three Wholesome Roots (*[kusala-mūla](/glossary/kusala-mula/)*). It is a beautiful mental factor (*sobhana-[cetasika](/glossary/cetasika/)*) present in every single wholesome state of consciousness. 

Although formulated as a negative word (non-greed), its psychological function is entirely positive. It ranges from the basic generosity (*[dāna](/glossary/dana/)*) of giving away material possessions, to the profound detachment of renunciation (*[nekkhamma](/glossary/nekkhamma/)*). The commentaries compare *Alobha* to a drop of water on a lotus leaf; just as the water rolls off without adhering to the leaf, a mind rooted in *alobha* experiences the sensory world without adhering or clinging to objects. It is the direct psychological antidote to the sticky, adhesive quality of craving.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mūla Sutta* (AN 3.69) – The Buddha explains that actions born of non-greed do not bind a person to [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/), but cut the root of future suffering.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* – Defining non-greed as the absence of lust, the absence of infatuation, and the disposition of letting go.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* – Providing the lotus leaf simile to illustrate its non-adhering characteristic.
