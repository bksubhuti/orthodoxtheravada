### Grammatical Analysis
**Bhaṅgañāṇa**: [nt.] knowledge of dissolution, a compound of *bhaṅga* (dissolution, breaking up) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Mūla speaks of the same knowledge as "*bhaṅgānupassane paññā*", wisdom in the contemplation of dissolution (Paṭis 12:1, 295:1). The Ṭīkā defines the term itself: "*udayaṃ muccitvā vaye pavattaṃ ñāṇaṃ bhaṅgañāṇaṃ*", "knowledge occurring regarding dissolution, after letting go of arising, is knowledge of dissolution" (Abhidh-s-t 1767:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Bhaṅgañāṇa* is the knowledge of dissolution. In the Mūla (Paṭisambhidāmagga) it is described thus: consciousness with materiality as its object arises and breaks up; having reflected on that object, one contemplates the dissolution of that consciousness. One contemplates as impermanent, not as permanent; as suffering, not as pleasurable; as not-self, not as self; one is disenchanted, dispassionate, causes to cease and relinquishes (Paṭis 295:1-10). This is repeated for each object, from feeling to ageing-and-death (Vism-ii 1665:1).

The Visuddhimagga says that when the meditator examines material and immaterial states again and again as impermanent, suffering and not-self, the knowledge becomes keen and formations appear quickly; it does not reach their arising, presence, occurrence or sign, and stays only on their cessation as destruction, fall and breakup. Seeing "having arisen thus, the conditioned things cease thus", the insight knowledge called contemplation of dissolution arises (Vism-ii 1662:1-3). The Ṭīkā adds that one contemplates as impermanent by way of seeing dissolution, letting go of arising, by the power of the knowledge of rise and fall; of one who contemplates thus not even a trace of grasping at permanence remains (Vism-mht-ii 1455:4).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Kathaṃ ārammaṇaṃ paṭisaṅkhā bhaṅgānupassane paññā vipassane ñāṇaṃ? Rūpārammaṇatā cittaṃ uppajjitvā bhijjati. Taṃ ārammaṇaṃ paṭisaṅkhā tassa cittassa bhaṅgaṃ anupassati.**  
> 51. How is it that understanding of contemplating dissolution after reflecting on an object is insight knowledge? Cognizance with materiality as its object arises and dissolves. Having reflected on that object, he contemplates the dissolution of that cognizance.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tassevaṃ sallakkhetvā punappunaṃ “aniccaṃ dukkhamanattā” ti rūpārūpadhamme tulayato tīrayato taṃ ñāṇaṃ tikkhaṃ hutvā vahati, saṅkhārā lahuṃ upaṭṭhahanti, ñāṇe tikkhe vahante saṅkhāresu lahuṃ upaṭṭhahantesu uppādaṃ vā ṭhitiṃ vā pavattaṃ vā nimittaṃ vā na sampāpuṇāti. Khayavayabhedanirodheyeva sati santiṭṭhati. Tassa “evaṃ uppajjitvā evaṃ nāma saṅkhāragataṃ nirujjhatī” ti passato etasmiṃ ṭhāne bhaṅgānupassanaṃ nāma vipassanāñāṇaṃ uppajjati.**  
> 741. When he has discerned thus, and is examining and investigating material and immaterial states again and again as “impermanent, suffering, not-self,” that knowledge occurs keenly; formations appear quickly. When the knowledge occurs keenly and formations appear quickly, it does not reach their arising, or presence, or occurrence, or sign. It becomes steadied only in cessation as destruction, fall, and breakup. For one seeing thus, “having arisen thus, the conditioned things indeed cease thus,” the insight-knowledge called knowledge of contemplation of dissolution arises at that stage.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Bhaṅgānupassanāñāṇakathā, Vism-ii 1661-1662) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=277&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzQxLiBUYXNzZXZh4bmDIHNhbGxha2toZXR2xIEgcHVuYXBwdW5h4bmDIOKAmOKAmCBhbmljY2HhuYM%3D)
