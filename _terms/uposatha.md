### Grammatical Analysis
**Uposatha**: [m.] observance day; the Pātimokkha recitation; the eight-precept observance. The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī explains the word: *upavasanti etthāti uposatho*, "they dwell (*upavasanti*) here", meaning they dwell endowed with virtue or with fasting (*sīlena vā anasanena vā upetā hutvā vasanti*).

### Orthodox Definition
The commentary (Sv-i 653) lists the senses of the word by example: the recitation of the Pātimokkha (*pātimokkhuddeso*), as in "Come, friend Kappina, let us go to the Uposatha"; virtue (*sīlaṃ*), as in "the Uposatha complete in eight factors" (AN 8.43); fasting (*upavāso*); a designation (*paññatti*), as in the nāga king named Uposatha; and the day to be observed (*upavasitabbadivaso*).

1. **For the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)**: in the Uposathakkhandhaka of the Mahāvagga the Buddha first allows the monks to assemble on the fourteenth, fifteenth and eighth days of the half-month, after other wanderers had been doing so and people went to listen to them. Then, reflecting in seclusion, he allows the recitation of the *[Pātimokkha](/glossary/patimokkha/)*: "that will be their Uposatha observance" (*uposathakamma*). The formal motion is "Saṅgho uposathaṃ kareyya, pātimokkhaṃ uddiseyya". The Vinayasaṅgaha-aṭṭhakathā (quoting the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī) says there are three kinds by day (*cātuddasiko*, *pannarasiko*, *sāmaggīuposatho*), and twenty-four Uposathas in a year.
2. **For the laity**: in the Visākhā Sutta (AN 8.43) the Buddha tells Visākhā that the Uposatha "complete in eight factors" (*aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato uposatho*) is of great fruit. A noble disciple reflects: "All his life the Arahants abstain from killing ... I too, for this day and night, abstain ... by this factor I emulate the Arahants, and my Uposatha will be observed." The first factor is abstaining from killing, and the eighth is abstaining from high and luxurious beds.

### Quote
> **Atha kho bhagavato rahogatassa paṭisallīnassa evaṃ cetaso parivitakko udapādi – “yaṃnūnāhaṃ yāni mayā bhikkhūnaṃ paññattāni sikkhāpadāni, tāni nesaṃ pātimokkhuddesaṃ anujāneyyaṃ. So nesaṃ bhavissati uposathakamman” ti.**  
> 133. Then, while the Blessed One was meditating in seclusion, this course of thought occurred to him: “What if I were to allow for those training rules laid down by me for the monks a recitation of the Pātimokkha for them? That would be their Uposatha observance.”

> **“aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato kho, visākhe, uposatho upavuttho mahapphalo hoti mahānisaṃso mahājutiko mahāvipphāro. Kathaṃ upavuttho ca, visākhe, aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato uposatho mahapphalo hoti mahānisaṃso mahājutiko mahāvipphāro? Idha, visākhe, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ yāvajīvaṃ arahanto pāṇātipātaṃ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭiviratā nihitadaṇḍā nihitasatthā lajjī dayāpannā, sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampino viharanti. Ahaṃ pajja imañca rattiṃ imañca divasaṃ pāṇātipātaṃ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭivirato nihitadaṇḍo nihitasattho lajjī dayāpanno, sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī viharāmi. Imināpaṅgena arahataṃ anukaromi, uposatho ca me upavuttho bhavissatī”’ ti.**  
> “Visākhā, observed complete in eight factors, the Uposatha is of great fruit and benefit, extraordinarily brilliant and pervasive. And how is the Uposatha observed complete in eight factors, so that it is of great fruit and benefit, extraordinarily brilliant and pervasive? Here, Visākhā, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘All his life the Arahants, having abandoned the killing of living beings, abstain from killing living creatures, with rod laid aside, with weapon laid aside, conscientious, merciful, and dwell compassionate for the welfare of all living beings. I too, for this day and night, having abandoned the killing of living beings, abstain from killing living creatures, with rod laid aside, with weapon laid aside, conscientious, merciful, and dwell compassionate for the welfare of all living beings. By this factor, I emulate the Arahants, and my Uposatha will be observed.’  
>

> **Upavasanti etthāti uposatho, upavasantīti sīlena vā anasanena vā upetā hutvā vasantīti attho. Ayaṃ panettha atthuddhāro – “āyāmāvuso, kappina, uposathaṃ gamissāmā” tiādīsu pātimokkhuddeso uposatho. “Evaṃ aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato kho, visākhe, uposatho upavuttho” tiādīsu (a. ni.8.43) sīlaṃ. “Suddhassa ve sadā phaggu, suddhassuposatho sadā” tiādīsu (ma. ni.1.79) upavāso. “Uposatho nāma nāgarājā” tiādīsu (dī. ni.2.246) paññatti. “Na, bhikkhave, tadahuposathe sabhikkhukā āvāsā” tiādīsu (mahāva.181) upavasitabbadivaso.**  
> They observe here, thus it is the Sabbath (uposatho); "they observe" (upavasanti) means that they dwell endowed either with virtue or with fasting. Here, moreover, is the summary of meanings: In passages such as, “Come, friend Kappina, let us go to the Uposatha,” uposatho means the recitation of the Pātimokkha. In passages such as, “So, Visākhā, the Uposatha complete in eight factors has been undertaken,” it means virtue. In passages such as, “For one pure in heart, there is always the Feast of Spring; for one pure in heart, it is always the Holy Day,” it means fasting. In passages such as, “A nāga king named Uposatha,” it is a designation. In passages such as, “Monks, on an Observance day, monasteries with monks should not...,” it means the day to be observed.
