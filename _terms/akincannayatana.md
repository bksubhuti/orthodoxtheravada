### Grammatical Analysis
**Ākiñcaññāyatana**: [nt.] the base of nothingness; the sphere of voidness. Formed by *akiñcana* (having nothing, nothingness) + *āyatana* (base).

### Orthodox Definition
*Ākiñcaññāyatana* is the third of the four immaterial absorptions (*[arūpajhāna](/glossary/arupajhana/)*). This is the attainment that Āḷāra Kālāma, the Bodhisatta's first teacher, declared to him (M-i 915:4).

The Mūla formula is "sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma 'natthi kiñcī' ti ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati" (M-i 1559:1; Vibh 2501:1, the sixth liberation). The Abhidhamma defines the phrase: "one develops that same consciousness, clarifies it, brings it to an end, and sees that 'there is nothing'" (*natthi kiñcī*), and the *ākiñcaññāyatana* itself is "the mind and mental factors of one who has attained the dimension of nothingness, or one who has been reborn in it, or one dwelling happily in this very life" (Vibh 1785:1, 1786:1).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. X) teaches that the meditator who has mastered the dimension of infinite consciousness in five ways sees its danger (it is close to the dimension of infinite space, the enemy, and not as peaceful as the dimension of nothingness), gives up attachment to it, and attends to the absence, emptiness and seclusion of that very consciousness, repeating "natthi natthi" ("there is nothing"), "suññaṃ suññaṃ" or "vivittaṃ vivittaṃ" (Vism-i 2010:1-3). When the mind is absorbed in that non-existence, the mind of the dimension of nothingness arises (Vism-i 2011:3). The commentary's simile: a man sees a gathering of monks, comes back after they have left, and looks at the empty place; he sees only emptiness, not thinking how many monks have died or gone away (Vism-i 2012:1-2).

The Mūla gives the result of dwelling there: the devas who have reached the dimension of nothingness have a lifespan of sixty thousand aeons (A-iii 1079:3). This is the attainment that Āḷāra Kālāma declared to the Bodhisatta (M-i 915:4).

### Quote

> 615. Regarding “there is nothing”: one develops that same consciousness, clarifies it, brings it to an end, and sees that “there is nothing.” Because of that, it is called “there is nothing.” 616. Regarding “the dimension of nothingness”: it is the mind and mental factors of one who has attained the dimension of nothingness, or one who has been reborn in it, or one dwelling happily in this very life.  
>

### Quote
> **Ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ bhāvetukāmena pana pañcahākārehi viññāṇañcāyatanasamāpattiyaṃ ciṇṇavasībhāvena “āsannaākāsānañcāyatanapaccatthikā ayaṃ samāpatti, no ca ākiñcaññāyatanamiva santā” ti viññāṇañcāyatane ādīnavaṃ disvā tattha nikantiṃ pariyādāya ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ santato manasikaritvā tasseva viññāṇañcāyatanārammaṇabhūtassa ākāsānañcāyatanaviññāṇassa abhāvo suññatā vivittākāro manasikātabbo. Taṃ viññāṇaṃ amanasikaritvā “natthi natthī” ti vā,“suññaṃ suññan” ti vā,“vivittaṃ vivittan” ti vā punappunaṃ āvajjitabbaṃ, manasikātabbaṃ, paccavekkhitabbaṃ, takkāhataṃ vitakkāhataṃ kātabbaṃ. Tassevaṃ karoto ākāse phuṭe mahaggataviññāṇe viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ viya tasseva ākāsaṃ pharitvā pavattassa mahaggataviññāṇassa suññavivittanatthibhāve ākiñcaññāyatanacittaṃ appeti. Ayaṃ pana viseso, tasmiṃ hi appanācitte uppanne so bhikkhu yathā nāma puriso maṇḍalamāḷādīsu kenacideva karaṇīyena sannipatitaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ disvā katthaci gantvā sannipātakiccāvasāneva uṭṭhāya pakkantesu bhikkhūsu āgantvā dvāre ṭhatvā puna taṃ ṭhānaṃ olokento suññameva passati, vivittameva passati.**  
> 283. But by one who wishes to develop the dimension of nothingness, having achieved mastery in the five ways in the attainment of the dimension of infinite consciousness, should see the danger in the dimension of infinite consciousness in this way: ‘This attainment has the dimension of infinite space as its near enemy, and it is not as peaceful as the dimension of nothingness.’ Thus, having ended his attachment to that, and attending to the dimension of nothingness as peaceful, he should pay attention to the non-arising, emptiness, and secluded aspect of that very consciousness of the dimension of infinite space which had become the object of the dimension of infinite consciousness. Without paying attention to that consciousness, he should advert to, pay attention to, and review it again and again as ‘there is none, there is none,’ or ‘empty, empty,’ or ‘secluded, secluded,’ and strike and hammer at it with thought and applied thought. As he does so, just as the dimension of infinite consciousness arose in absorption on the lofty consciousness that had pervaded space, the mind of the dimension of nothingness fixes in absorption on the empty, secluded, and non-arisen state of that very lofty consciousness which occurred by pervading space. But there is this difference: when that mind of absorption has arisen, that bhikkhu is just like a man who, having seen the community of bhikkhus assembled for some business in an assembly hall or similar place, goes elsewhere, and then, after the bhikkhus have risen and departed at the conclusion of their business, he returns, stands at the door, and looking at that place again, sees it only as empty, sees it only as secluded.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. X, Ākiñcaññāyatana) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=326&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MjgzLiDEgGtpw7FjYcOxw7HEgXlhdGFuYeG5gyBiaMSBdmV0dWvEgW1lbmEgcGFuYSBwYcOxY2FoxIFrxIFyZWhpIHZpw7HDscSB4bmHYcOxY8SBeWF0YW5hc2FtxIFwYXR0aXlh4bmD)

* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. X, *Āruppaniddeso*, Vism-i 1980:1; Vism-i 2010:1-2014:3) – the method, and the simile of the man who looks at the empty place after the monks have left.
