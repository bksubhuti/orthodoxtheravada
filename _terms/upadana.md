### Grammatical Analysis
**Upādāna**: [nt.] clinging; grasping; fuel. Formed from the prefix *upa* + *ādāna* (taking). Atthasālinī and Visuddhimagga gloss it *daḷhaggahaṇaṃ* ("firm grasping"), and explain that the prefix *upa-* here has an emphatic sense (*daḷhattho*), as in *upāyāsa* and *upakaṭṭha*. The commentaries also derive the word from the verb: "one clings (*upādiyati*)" to the object (*kāmaṃ upādiyatīti kāmupādānaṃ*).

### Orthodox Definition
In the chain of Dependent Origination (*[paṭiccasamuppāda](/glossary/paticcasamuppada/)*), clinging stands between craving and becoming: the Vibhaṅga Sutta (SN 12.2) and the Abhidhamma Vibhaṅga take it as "clinging conditioned by craving" (*taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṃ*), and the same sequence gives "becoming conditioned by clinging". Craving (*[taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/)*) is the reaching out; clinging is the firm grasp.

The Mūla (SN 12.2, MN 9, DN 15) and the Abhidhamma (Dhammasaṅgaṇī, Vibhaṅga) all give four kinds:
1. *Kāmupādāna* (clinging to sensual pleasures). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it by the terms for sensual desire, sensual lust and sensual craving.
2. *Diṭṭhupādāna* (clinging to views). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as the view "there is no fruit of what is given" and the like.
3. *Sīlabbatupādāna* (clinging to rules and observances).
4. *Attavādupādāna* (clinging to a doctrine of self). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī gives the case of the untaught ordinary person who sees form as self.

The Atthasālinī explains each name in two ways: one clings to the object (sensual pleasure, a view, a rule and observance), or the thing is both that object and a clinging (*kāmo ca so upādānañca*).

### Quote
> **Yato kho, āvuso, ariyasāvako upādānañca pajānāti, upādānasamudayañca pajānāti, upādānanirodhañca pajānāti, upādānanirodhagāminiṃ paṭipadañca pajānāti – ettāvatāpi kho, āvuso, ariyasāvako sammādiṭṭhi hoti, ujugatāssa diṭṭhi, dhamme aveccappasādena samannāgato, āgato imaṃ saddhammaṃ. Katamaṃ panāvuso, upādānaṃ, katamo upādānasamudayo, katamo upādānanirodho, katamā upādānanirodhagāminī paṭipadā? Cattārimāni, āvuso, upādānāni – kāmupādānaṃ, diṭṭhupādānaṃ, sīlabbatupādānaṃ, attavādupādānaṃ.**  
> When, friends, a noble disciple discerns clinging, the origin of clinging, the cessation of clinging, and the path leading to the cessation of clinging, in that way he is one of right view, whose view is straight, who has unwavering confidence in the Dhamma, and has arrived at this true Dhamma. And what, friends, is clinging, what is the origin of clinging, what is the cessation of clinging, what is the path leading to the cessation of clinging? These, friends, are four kinds of clinging: clinging to sensual pleasures, clinging to views, clinging to rules and observances, and clinging to a doctrine of self.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 9, Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=62&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=WWF0byBraG8sIMSBdnVzbywgYXJpeWFzxIF2YWtvIHVwxIFkxIFuYcOxY2EgcGFqxIFuxIF0aSw%3D)

> **“Katamañca, bhikkhave, upādānaṃ? Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, upādānāni – kāmupādānaṃ, diṭṭhupādānaṃ, sīlabbatupādānaṃ, attavādupādānaṃ. Idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, upādānaṃ.**  
> “And what, monks, is clinging? Monks, these are the four kinds of clinging: clinging to sensual pleasures, clinging to views, clinging to rules and observances, and clinging to a doctrine of self. This, monks, is called clinging.  
>  
> — [Saṁyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 12.2, Vibhaṅga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=245&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtYcOxY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgdXDEgWTEgW5h4bmDPw%3D%3D)

> **Katame dhammā upādānā? Cattāri upādānāni – kāmupādānaṃ, diṭṭhupādānaṃ, sīlabbatupādānaṃ, attavādupādānaṃ.**  
> 1219. What mental phenomena are clingings? There are four kinds of clinging: clinging to sensual pleasures, clinging to views, clinging to rules and observances, and clinging to a doctrine of self.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1219) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=240&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTIxOS4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgdXDEgWTEgW7EgT8%3D)

> **Upādānaniddese vatthusaṅkhātaṃ kāmaṃ upādiyatīti kāmupādānaṃ kāmo ca so upādānañcātipi kāmupādānaṃ. Upādānanti daḷhaggahaṇaṃ. Daḷhattho hi ettha upasaddo upāyāsaupakaṭṭhādīsu viya.**  
> 1219. In the exposition of clinging, ‘clinging to sensual pleasures’ (kāmupādānaṃ) is so called because one clings to sensual pleasure designated as an object; and it is also called clinging to sensual pleasures because it is both sensual pleasure and a clinging. Clinging (upādānaṃ) means firm grasping. Indeed, the prefix upa- here has an emphatic meaning, like in (the words) beginning with upāyāsa and upakaṭṭha.  
>  
> — Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā ([Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=415&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MTIxOS4gVXDEgWTEgW5hbmlkZGVzZSB2YXR0aHVzYeG5hWtoxIF0YeG5gyBrxIFtYeG5gyB1cMSBZGl5YXTEq3RpIGvEgW11cMSBZMSBbmHhuYM%3D)
