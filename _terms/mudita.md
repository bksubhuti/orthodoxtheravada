### Grammatical Analysis
**Muditā**: [f.] sympathetic joy; altruistic joy; gladness. The Visuddhimagga derives it: “modanti tāya taṃsamaṅgino, sayaṃ vā modati, modanamattameva vā taṃ, muditā” – those endowed with it rejoice by means of it, or it itself rejoices, or it is just the act of rejoicing (Vism IX 262). The Vibhaṅga defines it as “yā sattesu muditā muditāyanā muditāyitattaṃ muditācetovimutti” (Vibh 1886:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Muditā* is the third of the four divine abodes (*[brahmavihāra](/glossary/brahmavihara/)*) or immeasurables (*appamaññā*), given in the suttas as the mind accompanied by muditā (*muditāsahagatena cetasā*) pervading the four directions (DN 33, D-iii 1415:7). The Vibhaṅga gives the picture: just as one would be glad on seeing a dear, agreeable person, so one pervades all beings with muditā (Vibh 1885:2).

The Visuddhimagga (IX 263) gives the four marks: its characteristic is rejoicing (*pamodanalakkhaṇā*), its function is being unenvious (*anissāyanarasā*), it is manifested as the removal of discontent (*arativighātapaccupaṭṭhānā*), and its proximate cause is seeing the success of beings (*sattānaṃ sampattidassanapadaṭṭhānā*). Its success is the subsiding of discontent (*arati*); its failure is the arising of merriment (*pahāsa*).

Its near enemy (*āsannapaccatthika*) is joy based on the household life (*gehasitaṃ somanassaṃ*), which resembles it by seeing success; its far enemy (*dūrapaccatthikā*) is discontent (*arati*) (Vism IX 265). Muditā is thus the specific remedy for *arati*; in the Visuddhimagga's reckoning of the practitioner's temperament, it suits one who is much given to discontent (Vism-i 1956:1).

Of the divine abodes the first three lead to three jhānas (*tikajjhānikā*), and the fourth, equanimity, to the fourth jhāna (Vism III, Vism-i 777:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Karuṇāsahagatena cetasā … pe … muditāsahagatena cetasā … pe … upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati.**  
> He dwells pervading one quarter with a mind of compassion… with a mind of sympathetic joy… with a mind of equanimity.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Kathañca bhikkhu muditāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati? Seyyathāpi nāma ekaṃ puggalaṃ piyaṃ manāpaṃ disvā mudito assa, evameva sabbe satte muditāya pharati. Tattha katamā muditā? Yā sattesu muditā muditāyanā muditāyitattaṃ muditācetovimutti – ayaṃ vuccati “muditā”.**  
> 663. And how does a bhikkhu dwell focused on pervading one direction with a mind accompanied by altruistic joy? Just as though, on seeing a dear, agreeable person, one would be happy, just so he pervades all beings with altruistic joy. Therein, which is altruistic joy? Whatever altruistic joy, manner of altruistic joy, state of being imbued with altruistic joy, liberation of the mind by altruistic joy there is towards beings – this is called altruistic joy (muditā).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Appamaññāvibhaṅga, muditā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=287&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjYzLiBLYXRoYcOxY2EgYmhpa2todSBtdWRpdMSBc2FoYWdhdGVuYSBjZXRhc8SBIGVrYeG5gw%3D%3D)

> Those endowed with it rejoice (modanti) by means of it, or it itself rejoices (modati), or it is just the act of rejoicing (modanamatta), thus it is altruistic joy (muditā).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Pamodanalakkhaṇā muditā , anissāyanarasā, arativighātapaccupaṭṭhānā, sattānaṃ sampattidassanapadaṭṭhānā. Arativūpasamo tassā sampatti, pahāsasambhavo vipatti.**  
> Altruistic joy (muditā) is characterized by rejoicing; its function is being unenvious; it is manifested as the elimination of discontent; and its proximate cause is seeing the success of beings. The subsiding of discontent is its success (sampatti), and the arising of merriment is its failure (vipatti).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IX, 263, lakkhaṇa-rasa-paccupaṭṭhāna-padaṭṭhāna) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=311&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=UGFtb2RhbmFsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBtdWRpdMSBICwgYW5pc3PEgXlhbmFyYXPEgSwgYXJhdGl2aWdoxIF0YXBhY2N1cGHhua3hua1oxIFuxIEsIHNhdHTEgW5h4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Muditābrahmavihārassa “cakkhuviññeyyānaṃ rūpānaṃ iṭṭhānaṃ … pe … lokāmisapaṭisaṃyuttānaṃ paṭilābhaṃ vā paṭilābhato samanupassato pubbe vā paṭiladdhapubbaṃ atītaṃ niruddhaṃ vipariṇataṃ samanussarato uppajjati somanassaṃ, yaṃ evarūpaṃ somanassaṃ, idaṃ vuccati gehasitaṃ somanassan” tiādinā (ma. ni.3.306) nayena āgataṃ gehasitaṃ somanassaṃ sampattidassanasabhāgatāya āsannapaccatthikaṃ, sabhāgavisabhāgatāya arati dūrapaccatthikā. Tasmā tato nibbhayena muditā bhāvetabbā.**  
> The near enemy of the divine abiding of altruistic joy is joy based on the household life, since both share in seeing success, as described in the passage beginning: ‘When one who is perceiving the attainment of forms cognizable by the eye... and connected with worldly gain, or who is recollecting what was previously attained in the past, ceased, and changed, experiences joy—this is called joy based on the household life.’ Discontent is its far enemy because of its complete dissimilarity. Therefore, one should develop altruistic joy with no fear of it.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IX, near and far enemy) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=313&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=TXVkaXTEgWJyYWhtYXZpaMSBcmFzc2Eg4oCY4oCYIGNha2todXZpw7HDsWV5ecSBbmHhuYMgcsWrcMSBbmHhuYMgaeG5reG5rWjEgW5h4bmDIOKApg%3D%3D)
