### Grammatical Analysis
**Sāratthappakāsinī**: [f.] The Revealer of the Essential Meaning. Formed by *sāra* (essence, core, pith) + *attha* (meaning) + *pakāsinī* (revealing, clarifying).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Sāratthappakāsinī* is the Aṭṭhakathā on the *[Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/)*. In its introductory verses it says it was compiled to reveal the meaning (*atthappakāsanatthaṃ*) of the Saṃyutta collection, "adorned with its vaggas and saṃyuttas" (*saṃyuttavaggapaṭimaṇḍitassa*): the commentary was first recited by five hundred masters, later brought to Sri Lanka by Mahāmahinda and set down in the Sinhala language, and the author now renders it from Sinhala into the Pāḷi language, without opposing the convention of the elders of the Mahāvihāra (Spk-i 14:1-23:1).

Its sections close with the words *iti sāratthappakāsiniyā* ("thus in the Sāratthappakāsinī", e.g. Spk-i 501:1), and its explanations are headed *-suttavaṇṇanā*, for example "10. Pajjotasuttavaṇṇanā" (Spk-i 496:1) and "1. Dhammacakkappavattanasuttavaṇṇanā" (Spk-v 881:1). It is divided in five parts matching the five books of the Saṃyutta: Spk-i (Sagāthāvagga), Spk-ii (Nidānavagga), Spk-iii (Khandhavagga), Spk-iv (Saḷāyatanavagga), Spk-v (Mahāvagga). A sub-commentary (Ṭīkā) on it exists for each part (Spk-t-i to Spk-t-v).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Spk introductory verses)
> **Saṃyuttavaggapaṭimaṇḍitassa, saṃyuttaāgamavarassa; Buddhānubuddhasaṃvaṇṇitassa, ñāṇappabhedajananassa. Atthappakāsanatthaṃ, aṭṭhakathā ādito vasisatehi; Pañcahi yā saṅgītā, anusaṅgītā ca pacchāpi. Sīhaḷadīpaṃ pana ābhatātha, vasinā mahāmahindena; Ṭhapitā sīhaḷabhāsāya, dīpavāsīnamatthāya.**  
> Of the saṃyuttaāgamavara (supreme Saṃyutta collection), adorned with sections and chapters, Praised by the Buddhas and those who followed the Buddhas, which generates the discernment of knowledge. To reveal the meaning, the aṭṭhakathā (commentary) was originally compiled by five hundred masters, And later subjected to subsequent recitations. It was then brought to the Sinhala island by the powerful Mahāmahinda, And established in the Sinhala language for the benefit of the island's inhabitants.  
>

### Textual References
* **Subject Matter**: Comments on the discourses of the *Saṃyutta Nikāya* (Spk-i 2:1, 3:1 "Sagāthāvagga-aṭṭhakathā", Ganthārambhakathā at Spk-i 4:1).
* **Key Features**: The explanation of the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* (Spk-v 881:1, "1. Dhammacakkappavattanasuttavaṇṇanā").
