### Grammatical Analysis
**Parinibbāna**: [nt.] Final [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/); complete blowing out; final passing away. Formed by intensive prefix *pari* (complete, total) + *[nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)* (extinguishment).

### Orthodox Definition
Doctrinally, Theravāda distinguishes between two phases of [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/). 
1. *Sa-upādisesa-[nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)* ([Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/) with residue remaining): This occurs when a practitioner attains Arahatship during their life. The defilements are completely destroyed, but the physical body and mental faculties (the five aggregates) continue to operate until their karmic lifespan is exhausted.
2. *Anupādisesa-[nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)* ([Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/) with no residue remaining): This is **Parinibbāna**. It occurs at the exact moment of biological death of an [Arahat](/glossary/arahat/) or a Buddha. 

The texts speak of an Arahant "attaining final Nibbāna in the Nibbāna element with no residue left" (*anupādisesāya nibbānadhātuyā parinibbāyati*), of the Tathāgata himself (A-iv 254:1; D-ii 435:1). The Aṭṭhasālinī quotes the Niddesa: "with the cessation of the final consciousness (*carimaviññāṇassa nirodhena*), wisdom and mindfulness, and mind and materiality, cease right here" (As 1723:2). The *Itivuttaka* commentary explains "residue" (*upādi*) as the group of five aggregates, which is taken up as a result by craving and the like (It-a 802:4). In the Aggivaccha Sutta the Buddha asks Vaccha where a fire that has gone out has gone, east, south, west or north, and Vaccha answers *na upeti*, "that does not apply": the fire is simply reckoned as "out" (M-ii 539:3, 542:2-3). "Even so" (*evameva*), "reappears", "does not reappear", "both" and "neither" do not apply to the Tathāgata (M-ii 543:1-3).

### Quote
> **“Katamā ca, bhikkhave, anupādisesā nibbānadhātu? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu arahaṃ hoti khīṇāsavo vusitavā katakaraṇīyo ohitabhāro anuppattasadattho parikkhīṇabhavasaṃyojano sammadaññā vimutto. Tassa idheva, bhikkhave, sabbavedayitāni anabhinanditāni sīti bhavissanti. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, anupādisesā nibbānadhātu.**  
> “And what, bhikkhus, is the Nibbāna element with no residue left? Here, a bhikkhu is an Arahant, one whose taints are destroyed, who has lived the holy life, done what had to be done, laid down the burden, attained the true goal, whose fetters of existence are utterly destroyed, and who is completely liberated by right knowledge. For him, right here in this life, all that is experienced, not being delighted in, will be extinguished. This, bhikkhus, is called the Nibbāna element with no residue left.  
>  
> — [Itivuttaka](/glossary/itivuttaka/) (Iti 44, Nibbānadhātu Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_04&page_number=221&name=itivuttakap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtxIEgY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgYW51cMSBZGlzZXPEgSBuaWJixIFuYWRoxIF0dT8%3D)
