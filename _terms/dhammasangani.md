### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhammasaṅgaṇī**: [f.] the Enumeration of Dhammas. Formed by *dhamma* (state, phenomenon) + *saṅgaṇī* (enumeration, grouping). The Mūla gives it as a title: "In the Abhidhamma Piṭaka" (Dhs 2:1), "Dhammasaṅgaṇīpāḷi" (Dhs 3:1). The Aṭṭhasālinī calls the whole of it "the *dhammasaṅgaṇīpakaraṇa*" (As 65:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Dhammasaṅgaṇī* is the first of the seven books of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka: "the Dhammasaṅgaṇī and the other six books are what is called the Abhidhamma Piṭaka" (As 151:10; the order is Dhammasaṅgaha, Vibhaṅga, Dhātukathā, Puggalapaññatti, Kathāvatthu, Yamaka, Paṭṭhāna, Sp-i 133:1).

According to the Aṭṭhasālinī, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī has four sections (*kaṇḍa*) beginning with the Cittuppādakaṇḍa; the Cittuppādakaṇḍa is twofold, the Mātikā and the Padabhājanīya; the Mātikā is twofold, the Tikamātikā (triplets) and the Dukamātikā (duplets); and the first triplet is the *kusalattika* with the term "wholesome phenomena" first (As 836:2-8). The four sections are 1. Cittuppādakaṇḍa (Dhs 388), 2. Rūpakaṇḍa (Dhs 1164), 3. Nikkhepakaṇḍa (Dhs 1751), 4. Aṭṭhakathākaṇḍa (Dhs 2182).

The matrix of the seven books consists of twenty-two triplets (*tikā*) and a hundred duplets (*dukā*): "the twenty-two triplets beginning with the wholesome triplet, and the hundred duplets beginning with 'states that are roots, states that are not roots'" (As 75:2, 4), together with forty-two Suttanta duplets (As 75:3, 839:17). The first triplet is "wholesome states, unwholesome states, indeterminate states" (As 839:1). The Aṭṭhasālinī says the whole of the Dhammasaṅgaṇī is about thirteen recitation-sections (*bhāṇavāra*) by the recitation tradition (As 65:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī (the sections of the Dhammasaṅgaṇī))
> **Tattha yasmā abhidhammo nāma dhammasaṅgaṇīādīni sattappakaraṇāni; dhammasaṅgaṇīpi cittuppādakaṇḍādīnaṃ vasena cattāri kaṇḍāni; cittuppādakaṇḍampi mātikāpadabhājanīyavasena duvidhaṃ; tattha mātikā ādi; sāpi tikamātikā dukamātikāti duvidhā; tattha tikamātikā ādi; tikamātikāyapi kusalattikaṃ kusalattikepi kusalā dhammāti idaṃ padaṃ;**  
> In this regard, because the Abhidhamma consists of seven treatises, namely, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī and so on; and the Dhammasaṅgaṇī also has four sections by way of the Chapter on the Genesis of Consciousness and so forth; and the Chapter on the Genesis of Consciousness is also twofold, by way of the Matrix and the Analysis of Terms; among these, the Matrix comes first; that also is twofold, as the Triplet Matrix and the Duplet Matrix; and among these, the Triplet Matrix comes first; and in the Triplet Matrix also, the Wholesome Triplet (comes first), and in the Wholesome Triplet, the term “wholesome mental phenomena” (is primary);  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī (the matrix))
> **Imasmiṃ pana ṭhāne paṭṭhānaṃ samānetabbaṃ. Kusalattikādayo hi dvāvīsati tikā, nāma hetū dhammā nahetū dhammā … pe … saraṇā dhammā araṇā dhammāti ime sataṃ dukā. Aparepi vijjābhāgino dhammā avijjābhāgino dhammā … pe … khaye ñāṇaṃ, anuppāde ñāṇanti dvācattālīsa suttantikadukā nāma. Tesu dvāvīsati tikā sataṃ dukāti ayaṃ āhaccabhāsitā jinavacanabhūtā sabbaññubuddhena desitā sattannaṃ pakaraṇānaṃ mātikā nāma.**  
> In this place, however, the Paṭṭhāna should be presented. For there are the twenty-two triplets beginning with the Wholesome Triplet, and the hundred duos such as: states that are roots, states that are not roots... and so on... states with corruptions, states without corruptions. Furthermore, there are the forty-two Suttanta duos: states partaking in knowledge, states partaking in ignorance... and so on... knowledge about exhaustion, and knowledge about non-arising. Among these, the twenty-two triplets and the hundred duos are called the matrix of the seven treatises, which was directly declared as the word of the Conqueror and taught by the Omniscient Buddha.  
>
