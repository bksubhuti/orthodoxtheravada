### Grammatical Analysis
**Vīriya** (written *viriya* in modern spelling): [nt.] energy; effort; vigour; exertion. The Aṭṭhakathā explains the word: *vīrabhāvo vīriyaṃ, vīrānaṃ vā kammaṃ, vidhinā vā nayena upāyena īrayitabbaṃ pavattayitabbanti vīriyaṃ*, "the state of one who is a hero is *vīriya*, or the deed of heroes, or that which is to be set in motion by a rule, method or means" (Paṭis-a 217:1; Vism-ii 433:1, XIV 461).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Abhidhamma, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as the faculty of energy: *yo tasmiṃ samaye cetasiko vīriyārambho nikkamo parakkamo uyyāmo vāyāmo ussāho ussoḷhī thāmo dhiti asithilaparakkamatā anikkhittachandatā anikkhittadhuratā dhurasampaggāho vīriyaṃ vīriyindriyaṃ vīriyabalaṃ sammāvāyāmo* (Dhs §13, Dhs 403:2). So energy is at once a faculty (*[indriya](/glossary/indriya/)*), a power (*[bala](/glossary/bala/)*) and right effort. The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha counts it among the six *pakiṇṇaka* (occasional) mental factors (Abhidh-s 85:1).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XIV, 461) gives its criteria (Vism-ii 433:2-3; also As 1202:1-2, Paṭis-a 217:2):
* **Lakkhaṇa** (Characteristic): exertion (*ussahanalakkhaṇaṃ*).
* **Rasa** (Function): supporting its co-arisen states (*sahajātānaṃ upatthambhanarasaṃ*).
* **Paccupaṭṭhāna** (Manifestation): non-sinking (*asaṃsīdanabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ*).
* **Padaṭṭhāna** (Proximate cause): a sense of urgency (*saṃvegapadaṭṭhānaṃ*), by the saying "being stirred, he strives wisely" (*saṃviggo yoniso padahati*, AN 4.113), or the grounds for arousing energy (*vīriyārambhavatthupadaṭṭhānaṃ*). When rightly begun, it is to be seen as the root of all attainments (*sammā āraddhaṃ sabbasampattīnaṃ mūlaṃ*).

In the Soṇa Sutta (AN 6.55) the Buddha tells Soṇa that overly aroused energy leads to restlessness (*uddhacca*) and overly slack energy leads to laziness (*kosajja*), and that he should determine the right pitch of energy and see the evenness of the faculties (A-vi 501:1-2).

### Quote (Mūla: Soṇa Sutta)
> **“Evamevaṃ kho, soṇa, accāraddhavīriyaṃ uddhaccāya saṃvattati, atisithilavīriyaṃ kosajjāya saṃvattati. Tasmātiha tvaṃ, soṇa, vīriyasamathaṃ adhiṭṭhahaṃ, indriyānañca samataṃ paṭivijjha, tattha ca nimittaṃ gaṇhāhī” ti.**  
> “So too, Soṇa, overly aroused persistence leads to restlessness; overly slack persistence leads to laziness. Therefore, Soṇa, you should determine the right pitch for your persistence, comprehend the evenness of the faculties, and there take up your ground.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 6.55, Soṇa Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_06&page_number=330&name=chakkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEV2YW1ldmHhuYMga2hvLCBzb%2BG5h2EsIGFjY8SBcmFkZGhhdsSrcml5YeG5gyB1ZGRoYWNjxIF5YQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vīriyindriyaṃ hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye cetasiko vīriyārambho nikkamo parakkamo uyyāmo vāyāmo ussāho ussoḷhī thāmo dhiti asithilaparakkamatā anikkhittachandatā anikkhittadhuratā dhurasampaggāho vīriyaṃ vīriyindriyaṃ vīriyabalaṃ sammāvāyāmo – idaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vīriyindriyaṃ hoti.**  
> 13. What is the faculty of energy at that time? Whatever arousal of mental energy, exertion, perseverance, striving, effort, zeal, great vigor, fortitude, fixed resolve, unflagging perseverance, unabandoned zeal, unabandoned effort, firm grip on effort, energy, faculty of energy, the power of energy, or right effort exists at that time—this is the faculty of energy at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§13, vīriyindriya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTMuIEthdGFtYeG5gyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgdsSrcml5aW5kcml5YeG5gyBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Vīrabhāvo vīriyaṃ. Taṃ ussahanalakkhaṇaṃ, sahajātānaṃ upatthambhanarasaṃ, asaṃsīdanabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ. “Saṃviggo yoniso padahatī” ti (a. ni.4.113) vacanato saṃvegapadaṭṭhānaṃ, vīriyārambhavatthupadaṭṭhānaṃ vā, sammā āraddhaṃ sabbasampattīnaṃ mūlaṃ hotīti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> 461. The state of one who is vigorous is energy (vīriya). It has the characteristic of striving, the function of supporting co-arisen states, and is manifested as non-collapse. Because of the statement, “Being agitated, he strives wisely,” its proximate cause is a sense of urgency, or its proximate cause is the grounds for initiating energy. When rightly undertaken, it should be seen as the root of all achievements.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIV, 461) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=93&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDYxLiBWxKtyYWJoxIF2byB2xKtyaXlh4bmDLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha: pakiṇṇaka)
> **Vitakko vicāro adhimokkho vīriyaṃ pīti chando cāti cha ime cetasikā pakiṇṇakā nāma.**  
> 3. Applied thought, sustained thought, decision, energy, zest, and zeal—these six mental factors are called occasionals.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (II, 3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=8&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=My4gVml0YWtrbyB2aWPEgXJvIGFkaGltb2traG8gdsSrcml5YeG5gyBwxKt0aQ%3D%3D)
