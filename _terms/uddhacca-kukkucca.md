### Grammatical Analysis
**[Uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)-kukkucca**: [nt.] restlessness and remorse; agitation and worry. Formed by *[uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)* (restlessness, scattering upward) + *[kukkucca](/glossary/kukkucca/)* (remorse, regret over misdeeds, wrong behavior).

### Orthodox Definition
*[Uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)-kukkucca* is the fourth of the five hindrances (*[nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*): *kāmacchanda, byāpāda, thinamiddha, uddhaccakukkucca, vicikicchā* (A-ix 444:3). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī takes it as a pair: "There is restlessness, there is anxiety" (Dhs 1949:2).

1. *[Uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)*: "the mind's restlessness, lack of calming, scattered mind, agitation of the mind" (*yaṃ cittassa uddhaccaṃ avūpasamo cetaso vikkhepo bhantattaṃ cittassa*, Dhs 1950:2). The Aṭṭhakathā: its characteristic is the mind's non-calm, like water struck by the wind; its function is instability, like a flag struck by the wind; its manifestation is bewilderment, like ashes struck by a stone and scattered; its proximate cause is unwise attention to the mind's non-calm (Vism-ii 466:2; As 1780:3-4).
2. *Kukkucca*: "the perception of allowable in the unallowable, of the unallowable in the allowable, of the blameworthy in the faultless, of the faultless in the blameworthy; remorse, mental friction" (Dhs 1951:2-3). The Aṭṭhakathā explains it as *kucchitaṃ kataṃ kukataṃ, tassa bhāvo kukkuccaṃ*, "what is badly done, the state of that" (Nidd-a-i 1218:2), and it arises scratching the mind like the edge of a bronze plate (*manovilekho*, As 2323:8).

The Buddha compares the mind overcome by it to a bowl of water whipped by the wind, stirred and agitated, in which one would not see one's own reflection as it really is (S-v 708:1-2); and its abandoning to release from slavery (D-i 322:1-2). The commentaries list six things that lead to its abandonment: being learned, questioning, skill in the Vinaya, association with elders, good friends and suitable talk (*bahussutatā, paripucchakatā, vinaye pakataññutā, vuddhasevitā, kalyāṇamittatā, sappāyakathā*, Mp-i 214:1; Ps-i 1371:1; It-a 1684:1). It is countered in jhāna by happiness (*sukhaṃ uddhaccakukkuccassa*, As 1415:9). In the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha both are among the fourteen unwholesome cetasikas, uddhacca being one of the four present in all unwholesome consciousness (*akusalasādhāraṇā cattāro*, Abhidh-s 88:1, 165:1). By way of the paths: the arahatta-magga abandons uddhacca and the third (anāgāmi) path abandons kukkucca (Spk-i 752:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṅgārava Sutta)
> **“Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, udapatto vāterito calito bhanto ūmijāto. Tattha cakkhumā puriso sakaṃ mukhanimittaṃ paccavekkhamāno yathābhūtaṃ na jāneyya na passeyya. Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, yasmiṃ samaye uddhaccakukkuccapariyuṭṭhitena cetasā viharati uddhaccakukkuccaparetena, uppannassa ca uddhaccakukkuccassa nissaraṇaṃ yathābhūtaṃ nappajānāti, attatthampi tasmiṃ samaye yathābhūtaṃ na jānāti na passati, paratthampi … pe … ubhayatthampi tasmiṃ samaye yathābhūtaṃ na jānāti na passati;**  
> “Suppose, brahmin, there is a bowl of water stirred by the wind, rippling, swirling, churned into wavelets. If a man with good vision were to examine his own facial reflection in it, he would not know or see it as it really is. Even so, brahmin, when one dwells with a mind obsessed by restlessness and remorse, overwhelmed by restlessness and remorse, and one does not understand as it really is the escape from arisen restlessness and remorse, on that occasion one does not know or see as it really is one’s own good, nor the good of others, nor the good of both;  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 46.55, Saṅgārava Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=107&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNleXlhdGjEgXBpLCBicsSBaG1h4bmHYSwgdWRhcGF0dG8gdsSBdGVyaXRvIGNhbGl0bw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamaṃ uddhaccakukkuccanīvaraṇaṃ? Atthi uddhaccaṃ, atthi kukkuccaṃ. 1165. Tattha katamaṃ uddhaccaṃ? Yaṃ cittassa uddhaccaṃ avūpasamo cetaso vikkhepo bhantattaṃ cittassa – idaṃ vuccati uddhaccaṃ. 1166. Tattha katamaṃ kukkuccaṃ? Akappiye kappiyasaññitā, kappiye akappiyasaññitā, avajje vajjasaññitā, vajje avajjasaññitā. Yaṃ evarūpaṃ kukkuccaṃ kukkuccāyanā kukkuccāyitattaṃ cetaso vippaṭisāro manovilekho – idaṃ vuccati kukkuccaṃ.**  
> 1164. Which is restlessness and anxiety as a hindrance? There is restlessness, there is anxiety. 1165. Which is restlessness? Whatever is the mind's restlessness, lack of calming, a scattered mind, the agitation of the mind — this is called restlessness (uddhaccaṃ). 1166. Which is anxiety? The perception of allowable in what is unallowable, the perception of unallowable in what is allowable, the perception of blameworthy in what is faultless, the perception of faultless in what is blameworthy. Whatever such anxiety, the act of feeling anxiety, the state of feeling anxiety, the mind's remorse, mental friction there is — this is called anxiety (kukkuccaṃ).  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs 1164-1166, Uddhaccakukkuccanīvaraṇaṃ) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=233&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTE2NC4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyB1ZGRoYWNjYWt1a2t1Y2NhbsSrdmFyYeG5h2HhuYM%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Uddhatabhāvo uddhaccaṃ. Taṃ avūpasamalakkhaṇaṃ vātābhighātacalajalaṃ viya, anavaṭṭhānarasaṃ vātābhighātacaladhajapaṭākā viya, bhantattapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ pāsāṇābhighātasamuddhatabhasmaṃ viya, cetaso avūpasame ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānaṃ, cittavikkhepoti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> 482. An agitated state is restlessness (uddhaccaṃ). It has the characteristic of non-calm like water disturbed by the wind; its function is lack of stability like a flag or banner disturbed by the wind; it manifests as turmoil like ashes disturbed by a stone; it is rooted in unwise attention to the non-calm of the mind, and should be seen as mental distraction.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 466:1-2, Uddhacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=99&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDgyLiBVZGRoYXRhYmjEgXZvIHVkZGhhY2Nh4bmDLg%3D%3D)
