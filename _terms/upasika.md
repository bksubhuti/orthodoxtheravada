### Grammatical Analysis
**Upāsikā**: [f.] female lay follower. Feminine of *[upāsaka](/glossary/upasaka/)*, from *upa* + *āsati* ("sits near", "attends"). The Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā gives the explanation: one is called an *upāsikā* because she "approaches" (*upāsati*) the Triple Gem, through unwavering confidence, through confidence of experience, or through going for refuge to the Triple Gem.

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mahānāma Sutta (SN 55.37) the Buddha says that one becomes a lay follower (*upāsako*) when one has gone for refuge to the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha; and that the lay follower is accomplished in virtue (*sīlasampanno*) when he abstains from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech and intoxicants. The same terms apply to the *upāsikā*.

In the Etadagga section of the Aṅguttara Nikāya, the Buddha names the foremost of his female lay disciples (*sāvikānaṃ upāsikānaṃ*): Sujātā, daughter of Seniya, as the first to go for refuge; [Visākhā](/glossary/visakha/) Migāramātā among those who give; and Khujjuttarā among the very learned.

The Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā says of Khujjuttarā that she was an ariyan disciple, a learner (*sekkha*) who had attained the analytical modes (*paṭisambhidā*), and that she first spoke the introduction of the Itivuttaka to the five hundred women led by Sāmāvatī. The Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā calls her a servant woman (*dāsī*).

### Quote
> **“Kittāvatā nu kho, bhante, upāsako hotī” ti? “Yato kho, mahānāma, buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti, dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti, saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti – ettāvatā kho, mahānāma, upāsako hotī” ti.**  
> “In what way, venerable sir, does one become a lay follower?” “Mahānāma, when one has gone for refuge to the Buddha, has gone for refuge to the Dhamma, and has gone for refuge to the Saṅgha—by that much, Mahānāma, one becomes a lay follower.”  
>  
> — [Saṁyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 55.37, Mahānāma Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=344&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEtpdHTEgXZhdMSBIG51IGtobywgYmhhbnRlLCB1cMSBc2Frbw%3D%3D)

> **“Etadaggaṃ, bhikkhave, mama sāvikānaṃ upāsikānaṃ paṭhamaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchantīnaṃ yadidaṃ sujātā seniyadhītā”. 259.… Dāyikānaṃ yadidaṃ visākhā migāramātā. 260.… Bahussutānaṃ yadidaṃ khujjuttarā.**  
> “Monks, the foremost of my female disciples among the laywomen who first went for refuge is Sujātā, the daughter of Seniya. … of those who are givers is Visākhā Migāramātā. … of those who are very learned is Khujjuttarā.  
>

> **Ettha ca aveccappasādena vā okappanapasādena vā ratanattayasaraṇagamanena vā ratanattayaṃ upāsatīti upāsikāti vuccatīti.**  
> And here, one is called a lay follower (upāsikā) because they approach (upāsati) the Triple Gem through unwavering confidence, experiential confidence, or by going for refuge to the Triple Gem.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahānāma Sutta* (SN 55.37, S-v 2264–2267) – how one becomes a lay follower (refuge) and is accomplished in virtue (five precepts).
* **Canonical**: *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (Ekanipāta, Etadagga, ¶258 Sujātā, ¶259 Visākhā, ¶260 Khujjuttarā) – the foremost *upāsikās*. *Paṭhamaidhalokika Sutta* (AN 8.49) – the Buddha teaches Visākhā the four qualities by which a woman is committed to victory in this world, and four for the next world.
* **Commentary**: *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 2398:14) – the explanation of the word *upāsikā*. *Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā* (It-a 146:4) – Khujjuttarā. *Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā* (Dhp-a 641–642) – Khujjuttarā placed in the etadagga.
