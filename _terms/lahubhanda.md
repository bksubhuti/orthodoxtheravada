### Grammatical Analysis
**Lahubhaṇḍa**: [nt.] light goods. A compound of *lahu* (light, small) + *bhaṇḍa* (goods, property). In the Vinaya it is paired with *garubhaṇḍa* (heavy goods): "whatever light goods and light requisites (*lahubhaṇḍaṃ lahuparikkhāraṃ*) there are, the Saṅgha present is to divide; whatever heavy goods and heavy requisites (*garubhaṇḍaṃ garuparikkhāraṃ*) there are, are the property of the Saṅgha of the four quarters, not to be disposed of or divided" (Vin-iii 2224:5-6).

### Orthodox Definition
In monastic law, *lahubhaṇḍa* is the Saṅgha's property that may be divided and distributed, in contrast to *[garubhaṇḍa](/glossary/garubhanda/)*. The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī defines the distributable and divisible (*vissajjiyavebhaṅgiyo*) as everything except the five heavy goods (*ṭhapetvā pañca garubhaṇḍāni avaseso*) (Kkh 1941:1).

The five heavy goods are grouped as: (1) a park and park site; (2) a monastery and its site; (3) couch, bench, mattress, bolster; (4) metal pot, jar, bucket, bowl, adze, axe, hatchet, spade, chisel; (5) creeper, bamboo, muñja grass, reed, grass, clay, wooden goods, earthenware goods (Kkh 1941:2). The Vinaya (Cūḷavagga, Senāsanakkhandhaka) says these five are non-relinquishable (*avissajjiyāni*), so that even if relinquished they remain unrelinquished, and whoever relinquishes them commits a thullaccaya offence (Vin-iv 1669:1-7). They cannot be given or divided even to the Saṅgha, groups or individuals (Kkh 1941:3).

The Vinaya rule on the property of a deceased monk comes in the Cīvarakkhandhaka: when a monk who had many goods died, the Buddha said that the Saṅgha is the owner of the bowl and robes of a monk who has died, but those who nursed him were of great service; so the Saṅgha may give the three robes and the bowl to the nurses, while whatever light goods and light requisites are there are divided by the Saṅgha present, and whatever heavy goods are there belong to the Saṅgha of the four quarters (Vin-iii 2224:1-6).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, Cīvarakkhandhaka)
> **Bhikkhussa, bhikkhave, kālaṅkate saṅgho sāmī pattacīvare, api ca gilānupaṭṭhākā bahūpakārā. Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, saṅghena ticīvarañca pattañca gilānupaṭṭhākānaṃ dātuṃ. Yaṃ tattha lahubhaṇḍaṃ lahuparikkhāraṃ taṃ sammukhībhūtena saṅghena bhājetuṃ. Yaṃ tattha garubhaṇḍaṃ garuparikkhāraṃ taṃ āgatānāgatassa cātuddisassa saṅghassa avissajjikaṃ avebhaṅgikanti.**  
> Monks, the Saṅgha is the owner of the bowl and robes of a bhikkhu who has passed away, but truly those who tend the sick are of great service. I allow you, monks, to give through the Saṅgha the three robes and the bowl to those who tended the sick. Distribute through the Saṅgha that is present whatever few goods, few requisites are there. But whatever many goods, many requisites are there, these are for the Saṅgha of the four quarters—those who have come in, those who have not come in—they are not to be disposed of, not to be divided up.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, Senāsanakkhandhaka)
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, avissajjiyāni, na vissajjetabbāni, saṅghena vā gaṇena vā puggalena vā. Vissajjitānipi avissajjitāni honti. Yo vissajjeyya, āpatti thullaccayassa. Katamāni pañca? Ārāmo, ārāmavatthu – idaṃ paṭhamaṃ avissajjiyaṃ, na vissajjetabbaṃ, saṅghena vā gaṇena vā puggalena vā. Vissajjitampi avissajjitaṃ hoti. Yo vissajjeyya, āpatti thullaccayassa.**  
> “Bhikkhus, these five things are non-relinquishable; they are not to be relinquished by the Sangha, by a group, or by an individual. Even if they are relinquished, they remain unrelinquished. Whoever should relinquish them, commits a serious offense. What are the five? A park, a park site—this is the first non-relinquishable item; it is not to be relinquished by the Sangha, by a group, or by an individual. Even if it is relinquished, it remains unrelinquished. Whoever should relinquish it, commits a serious offense.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Rājagahe chabbaggiye ārabbha cīvaraṃ datvā pacchā khīyanavatthusmiṃ paññattaṃ, sādhāraṇapaññatti, anāṇattikaṃ, dhammakamme tikapācittiyaṃ, cīvaraṃ ṭhapetvā aññaṃ vissajjiyavebhaṅgiyaṃ parikkhāraṃ datvā pacchākhīyantassa dukkaṭaṃ, vissajjiyavebhaṅgiyo nāma ṭhapetvā pañca garubhaṇḍāni avaseso. Rāsivasena hi pañca garubhaṇḍāni vuttāni, tattha ārāmo ārāmavatthūti ekaṃ, vihāro vihāravatthūti dutiyaṃ, mañco pīṭhaṃ bhisi bimbohananti tatiyaṃ, lohakumbhī lohabhāṇakaṃ lohavārako lohakaṭāhaṃ vāsi parasu kuṭhārī kudālo nikhādananti catutthaṃ, valli veḷu muñjaṃ pabbajaṃ tiṇaṃ mattikā dārubhaṇḍaṃ mattikābhaṇḍanti pañcamaṃ. Etāni hi pañca saṅghasantakāni neva saṅghassa, na gaṇapuggalānaṃ vissajjetuṃ vā vibhajituṃ vā vaṭṭanti, vissajjitavibhattānipi saṅghikāneva honti.**  
> It was laid down at Rājagaha regarding the group of six bhikkhus concerning the matter of criticism after giving a robe; it is a common laying down, not involving a command, there is a triplet of offenses entailing confession in a legitimate formal act. For one who has given any other distributable requisite apart from the robe and later criticizes, there is an offense of wrong conduct; that which is called 'distributable' is that which remains after setting aside the five valuable items. Indeed, the five valuable items are mentioned by way of a group: therein, a garden and a garden-site is one; a monastery and a monastery-site is the second; a couch, a bench, a mattress, and a bolster is the third; a metal pot, a metal jar, a metal bucket, a metal bowl, an adze, an axe, a hatchet, a spade, and a chisel is the fourth; a creeper, bamboo, muñja-grass, reed, ordinary grass, clay, wooden goods, and clay goods is the fifth. For these five indeed, belonging to the Saṅgha, are not proper to be given or distributed to the Saṅgha or to groups of individuals, even if they are given and distributed, they remain belonging to the Saṅgha.  
>
