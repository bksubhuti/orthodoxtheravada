### Grammatical Analysis
**Samudaya**: [m.] origin; source; cause; arising; coming together. Formed by prefix *saṃ* (together) + *ud* (up) + root *i* (to go). The commentaries explain it as *samudeti etasmā*, "it arises from this" (Abhidh-s-t 1628:4; Nidd-a-i 1332:8), i.e. that from which a result arises.

### Orthodox Definition
In the fundamental layout of the Four Noble Truths, *Samudaya* is the Second Noble Truth, the origin of suffering (*[dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)-samudaya*). The Buddha gives it as craving (*[taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/)*) that leads to renewed existence, of three kinds: *kāmataṇhā*, *bhavataṇhā*, *vibhavataṇhā* (S-v 2477:2; Vibh 713:2). The Aṭṭhakathā explains the word thus: craving generates and produces suffering, and from it that suffering arises (*tato taṃ dukkhaṃ samudeti*), so one understands it as the origin of suffering (Pp-a 241:4-5).

The Visuddhimagga adds that wholesome and unwholesome kamma are stated "without distinction" as the truth of origin in the Saccavibhaṅga (*kusalākusalaṃ kammaṃ avisesena samudayasaccanti saccavibhaṅge vuttaṃ*, Vism-ii 1281:1).

As a task of the path, the Truth of Origin is to be abandoned: *dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccaṃ pahātabbaṃ* (S-v 2479:2); the Papañcasūdanī glosses *pahātabbaṃ samudayasaccaṃ* as "the craving-truth" (Ps-ii 1181:4).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta)
> **Idaṃ kho pana, bhikkhave, dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccaṃ – yāyaṃ taṇhā ponobbhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī, seyyathidaṃ– kāmataṇhā, bhavataṇhā, vibhavataṇhā.**  
> “Now this, monks, is the noble truth of the origin of suffering: it is this craving which leads to the continuation of existence, accompanied by delight and lust, seeking delight here and there; that is, craving for sensual pleasures, craving for existence, craving for extermination.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 56.11, Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=369&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SWRh4bmDIGtobyBwYW5hLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGR1a2toYXNhbXVkYXlh4bmDIGFyaXlhc2FjY2HhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamaṃ dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccaṃ? Yāyaṃ taṇhā ponobhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī, seyyathidaṃ – kāmataṇhā, bhavataṇhā, vibhavataṇhā.**  
> 203. Which therein (tattha) is the Noble Truth of the origin of suffering (dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccaṃ)? Whatever craving (yāyaṃ taṇhā) leads to the continuation of existence (ponobhavikā), accompanied by delight and greed (nandirāgasahagatā), greatly enjoying this and that (tatratatrābhinandinī) — that is to say (seyyathidaṃ) — craving in regard to sense pleasures (kāmataṇhā), craving in regard to the continuation of existence (bhavataṇhā), craving in regard to the discontinuation of existence (vibhavataṇhā).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Saccavibhaṅga, 2. Samudayasaccaṃ) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=106&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjAzLiBUYXR0aGEga2F0YW1h4bmDIGR1a2toYXNhbXVkYXlh4bmDIGFyaXlhc2FjY2HhuYM%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Taṇhā pana dukkhaṃ janeti nibbatteti, pabhāveti, tato taṃ dukkhaṃ samudeti; tasmā naṃ ayaṃ ‘ dukkhasamudayo ’ ti yathābhūtaṃ pajānāti.**  
> Craving, however, generates suffering, produces it, brings it into being; from that, that suffering arises. Therefore, he understands that this (craving) is ‘the origin of suffering’ as it really is.  
>  
> — Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā (Pp-a 241:4-5) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=42&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGHhuYdoxIEgcGFuYSBkdWtraGHhuYMgamFuZXRpIG5pYmJhdHRldGksIHBhYmjEgXZldGks)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* (SN 56.11, S-v 2474:1, "1. Dhammacakkappavattanasuttaṃ") – the Noble Truth of the origin of suffering (S-v 2477:2), the knowledge that it is to be abandoned (S-v 2479:2). The same definition recurs in the Khandha Sutta (S-v 2493:1, 2496:2) and in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (D-ii 1433:1).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Saccavibhaṅga, 2. Samudayasaccaṃ, Vibh 712:1; 203. Vibh 713:1-2) – the definition of the Noble Truth of the origin of suffering.
* **Commentary**: *Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā* (Pp-a 241:4-5), *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 1281:1), *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha-ṭīkā* (Abhidh-s-t 1628:4).
