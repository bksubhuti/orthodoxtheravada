### Grammatical Analysis
**Vāyodhātu**: [f.] wind element; air element. Formed by *vāyo* (wind, air) + *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element). The Atthasālinī explains *dhātu* in the parallel entry for water: it is an element "by reason of its nature as being devoid of a being and self" (*nissattasuññatasabhāvaṭṭhena*, As 2135:4).

### Orthodox Definition
*Vāyodhātu* is the fourth of the four great elements, after earth, water and fire (Dhs 1284:1, §587). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it: "Whatever is wind, airy, the stiffness of form (*thambhitattaṃ rūpassa*), whether internal or external, grasped or ungrasped, this is that form which is the air element" (Dhs 1713:2, §970).

The Atthasālinī gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: "Vāyodhātu vitthambhanalakkhaṇā, samudīraṇarasā, abhinīhārapaccupaṭṭhānā": the air element has the characteristic of stiffening or distension (*vitthambhana*), the function of propulsion (*samudīraṇa*), and the manifestation of conveying or movement (*abhinīhāra*) (As 2135:9; in the translation of TPR, "distension, propulsion, movement"). The Visuddhimagga says that, founded on earth, held together by water and matured by fire, the air element is a condition for the other three great elements by acting as their distension (*vitthambhana*) (Vism XI §357); in the body, the air element that courses through the limbs "stiffens this body" (*imaṃ kāyaṃ vitthambheti*, Vism-i 2171:1).

The Mūla (MN 28) divides it into internal and external: the internal wind element is "whatever internally, belonging to oneself, is air, airy, clung-to", that is, six kinds: up-going winds, down-going winds, winds in the belly, winds in the bowels, winds that course through the limbs, and in-breath and out-breath. The Papañcasūdanī explains them (Ps-i 2268:1-7):
1. Up-going winds: ascending winds that cause belching and hiccups.
2. Down-going winds: descending winds that discharge excrement, urine and the like.
3. Winds in the belly (*kucchisayā*): the winds outside the bowels.
4. Winds in the bowels (*koṭṭhāsayā*): the winds inside the bowels.
5. Winds that course through the limbs: winds that course through the whole body along the network of veins, causing bending, stretching and so on.
6. In-breath and out-breath: the nasal wind entering and leaving.

The external wind element can be disturbed so that it sweeps away villages, towns and districts; yet in the last month of the hot season people seek wind with a fan, and even the straw on the thatch does not stir. So even this great external wind element is seen to be impermanent, subject to destruction, disappearance and change (M-i 1047:1-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima, the wind element)
> **“Katamā cāvuso, vāyodhātu? Vāyodhātu siyā ajjhattikā, siyā bāhirā. Katamā cāvuso, ajjhattikā vāyodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – uddhaṅgamā vātā, adhogamā vātā, kucchisayā vātā, koṭṭhāsayā vātā, aṅgamaṅgānusārino vātā, assāso passāso iti, yaṃ vā panaññampi kiñci ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccatāvuso, ajjhattikā vāyodhātu. “Hoti kho so, āvuso, samayo yaṃ bāhirā vāyodhātu pakuppati. Sā gāmampi vahati, nigamampi vahati, nagarampi vahati, janapadampi vahati, janapadapadesampi vahati.**  
> “And what, friends, is the wind element? The wind element might be either internal or external. What, friends, is the internal wind element? Whatever internally, belonging to oneself, is air, airy, and clung-to; namely—up-going winds, down-going winds, winds in the belly, winds in the bowels, winds that course through the limbs, in-breath and out-breath, or whatever else internally, belonging to oneself, is air, airy, and clung-to—this, friends, is called the internal wind element. “Now there comes a time, friends, when the external wind element is disturbed. It sweeps away villages, towns, cities, districts, and countries.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ vāyodhātu? Yaṃ vāyo vāyogataṃ thambhitattaṃ rūpassa ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā upādiṇṇaṃ vā anupādiṇṇaṃ vā – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ vāyodhātu.**  
> 970. Which form is the air element? Whatever is wind, airy, the stiffness regarding form, whether internal or external, whether grasped by kamma or ungrasped by kamma – this is that form which is the air element.  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§970) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=202&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTcwLiBLYXRhbWHhuYMgdGHhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgdsSBeW9kaMSBdHU%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, its function as condition)
> **Vāyodhātu pathavīpatiṭṭhitā āposaṅgahitā tejoparipācitā tiṇṇaṃ mahābhūtānaṃ vitthambhanaṃ hutvā paccayo hotīti evaṃ paccayato manasikātabbā.**  
> The air element, being founded on the earth element, held together by the water element, and matured by the fire element, is a condition for the other three primary elements by acting as their distension. In this way they should be considered 'as to condition.'  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XI, §357, Samādhiniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=365&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VsSBeW9kaMSBdHUgcGF0aGF2xKtwYXRp4bmt4bmtaGl0xIEgxIFwb3Nh4bmFZ2FoaXTEgSB0ZWpvcGFyaXDEgWNpdMSBIHRp4bmH4bmHYeG5gyBtYWjEgWJoxat0xIFuYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī, the six winds explained)
> **Vāyodhātuniddese uddhaṅgamā vātāti uggārahikkārādipavattakā uddhaṃ ārohanavātā. Adhogamā vātāti uccārapassāvādinīharaṇakā adho orohanavātā. Kucchisayā vātāti antānaṃ bahivātā. Koṭṭhāsayā vātāti antānaṃ antovātā. Aṅgamaṅgānusārinoti dhamanījālānusārena sakalasarīre aṅgamaṅgāni anusaṭā samiñjanapasāraṇādinibbattakavātā. Assāsoti antopavisananāsikavāto. Passāsoti bahinikkhamananāsikavāto.**  
> 305. In the section on the wind element, ‘up-going winds’ (uddhaṅgamā vātā) are the winds that ascend upward, causing belching, hiccups, and the like. ‘Down-going winds’ (adhogamā vātā) are the winds that descend downward, discharging excrement, urine, and the like. ‘Winds in the belly’ (kucchisayā vātā) are the winds outside the bowels. ‘Winds in the bowels’ (koṭṭhāsayā vātā) are the winds inside the bowels. ‘Winds that course through the limbs’ (aṅgamaṅgānusārino) are the winds that course throughout the entire body according to the network of veins, reaching all the limbs and causing the actions of bending, stretching, and so forth. ‘In-breath’ (assāso) is the nasal wind entering inside. ‘Out-breath’ (passāso) is the nasal wind exiting outside.  
>  
> — *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* (on MN 28, §305) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_02&page_number=131&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=MzA1LiBWxIF5b2RoxIF0dW5pZGRlc2UgdWRkaGHhuYVnYW3EgSB2xIF0xIF0aSB1Z2fEgXJhaGlra8SBcsSBZGlwYXZhdHRha8SBIHVkZGhh4bmD)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta* (MN 28, M-i 1032-1055, §305 = M-i 1046-1047) – the internal wind element with its six kinds, and the external wind element that sweeps away villages, both seen as impermanent. The same two aspects occur in the Mahārāhulovāda Sutta (MN 62, M-ii 330) and the Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta (MN 140, M-iii 1149).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* §587 (Dhs 1284) and §970 (Dhs 1713) – the air element among the four great elements and its definition.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 2135:9) – lakkhaṇa, rasa, paccupaṭṭhāna; *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter XI, §357, Vism-i 2209; Vism-i 2171) – its role among the four elements; *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* (Ps-i 2268) – the six winds.
