### Grammatical Analysis
**Upacārasamādhi**: [m.] access concentration; neighbourhood concentration. Formed by *upacāra* (access; from *upa*, near + *cāra*, moving, "moving near", the Ṭīkā's *samīpacārittā*, Vism-mht-i 515:9) + *[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)* (concentration). The word is not found in the Mūla (0 hits); it belongs to the commentaries (63 hits in the Aṭṭhakathā, 23 sentences in the Visuddhimagga).

### Orthodox Definition
The Visuddhimagga says there are two concentrations, "upacārasamādhi ca appanāsamādhi ca", access concentration and absorption concentration (Vism IV §58). The mind becomes concentrated in two ways: on the plane of access (*upacārabhūmi*) by the abandonment of the hindrances (*nīvaraṇappahānena*), and on the plane of obtainment (*paṭilābhabhūmi*) by the manifestation of the factors (*aṅgapātubhāvena*) (Vism-i 849:2-4).

The Visuddhimagga describes how it arises: as the meditator develops the sign, "the hindrances are suppressed, the defilements subside, the mind becomes concentrated with access concentration (*upacārasamādhinā*), and the counterpart sign (*[paṭibhāganimitta](/glossary/patibhaganimitta/)*) arises" (Vism IV §57); "from the time of its arising the hindrances are indeed entirely suppressed, the defilements fully subsided, and the mind is concentrated in access concentration" (Vism IV §58). The Saṅgaha adds that the counterpart sign is found in the kasiṇas, the foulness meditation and mindfulness of breathing, "for in that, access concentration and absorption concentration occur, taking the counterpart sign as object" (Abh-s IX §27); both together are called "purification of mind" (*cittavisuddhi*, Abh-s IX §50).

The difference from absorption: "in access the factors are not strong; because the factors are not strong, just as a young child lifted up and stood on his feet falls to the ground again and again, so when access has arisen the mind now makes the sign its object, and now enters the life-continuum (*bhavaṅga*)" (Vism-i 850:1). In absorption the factors are strong, "like a strong man who after rising from his seat might stand for a whole day", and the mind stays for a whole night or day (Vism-i 850:2). For some subjects only access is reached: for the recollection of the Buddha, "owing to the profundity of the Buddha's qualities, or because of being intent on recollecting various kinds of qualities, the jhāna is only access, without reaching absorption" (Vism VII, Vism-i 1306:8; the same is said of the recollections of Dhamma, Saṅgha and virtue).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the two concentrations)
> **Uppannakālato ca panassa paṭṭhāya nīvaraṇāni vikkhambhitāneva honti, kilesā sannisinnāva, upacārasamādhinā cittaṃ samāhitamevāti. Duvidho hi samādhi upacārasamādhi ca appanāsamādhi ca. Dvīhākārehi cittaṃ samādhiyati upacārabhūmiyaṃ vā paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ vā. Tattha upacārabhūmiyaṃ nīvaraṇappahānena cittaṃ samāhitaṃ hoti. Paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ aṅgapātubhāvena. Dvinnaṃ pana samādhīnaṃ idaṃ nānākāraṇaṃ, upacāre aṅgāni na thāmajātāni honti, aṅgānaṃ athāmajātattā, yathā nāma daharo kumārako ukkhipitvā ṭhapiyamāno punappunaṃ bhūmiyaṃ patati, evameva upacāre uppanne cittaṃ kālena nimittamārammaṇaṃ karoti, kālena bhavaṅgamotarati. Appanāyaṃ pana aṅgāni thāmajātāni honti, tesaṃ thāmajātattā, yathā nāma balavā puriso āsanā vuṭṭhāya divasampi tiṭṭheyya, evameva appanāsamādhimhi uppanne cittaṃ sakiṃ bhavaṅgavāraṃ chinditvā kevalampi rattiṃ kevalampi divasaṃ tiṭṭhati, kusalajavanapaṭipāṭivaseneva pavattatīti.**  
> 58. And from the time of its arising, the hindrances are indeed entirely suppressed, the defilements fully subsided, and the mind is indeed concentrated in access concentration. For concentration is of two kinds: access concentration and absorption concentration. In two ways the mind becomes concentrated: either on the plane of access or on the plane of obtainment. Herein, on the plane of access, the mind becomes concentrated by the abandonment of the hindrances. And on the plane of obtainment, by the manifestation of the factors. But this is the difference between the two concentrations: in access, the factors are not strong; because the factors are not strong, just as a young child when lifted up and stood up falls to the ground again and again, just so, when access concentration has arisen, the mind now makes the sign its object, and now enters the life-continuum. But in absorption, the factors are strong; because those factors are strong, just as a strong man, after rising from his seat, might stand even for a whole day, just so, when absorption concentration has arisen, the mind, having once cut off the flow of the life-continuum, remains active for a whole night and a whole day, operating solely by way of the succession of wholesome impulsions.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IV, §58) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=122&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=NTguIFVwcGFubmFrxIFsYXRvIGNhIHBhbmFzc2EgcGHhua3hua1oxIF5YSBuxKt2YXJh4bmHxIFuaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, the counterpart sign)
> **Paṭibhāganimittaṃ pana kasiṇāsubhakoṭṭhāsaānāpānesveva labbhati, tattha hi paṭibhāganimittamārabbha upacārasamādhi appanāsamādhi ca pavattanti.**  
> 27. But the counterpart sign (paṭibhāganimitta) is obtained only in the kasiṇas, the foulnesses, the parts of the body, and mindfulness of breathing; for it is with respect to the counterpart sign that access concentration and absorption concentration occur.  
>  
> — *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (IX, §27) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=61&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=MjcuIFBh4bmtaWJoxIFnYW5pbWl0dGHhuYMgcGFuYSBrYXNp4bmHxIFzdWJoYWtv4bmt4bmtaMSBc2HEgW7EgXDEgW5lc3ZldmEgbGFiYmhhdGksIHRhdHRoYQ%3D%3D)

> 50. Both kinds of concentration—namely, access concentration and absorption concentration—are called purification of mind (cittavisuddhi).
