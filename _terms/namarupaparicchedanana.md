### Grammatical Analysis
**Nāmarūpaparicchedañāṇa**: [nt.] knowledge of the delimitation (definition) of mentality-materiality. Formed by *[nāmarūpa](/glossary/namarupa/)* (mentality-materiality) + *pariccheda* (delimitation, distinguishing) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The compound occurs in the commentaries, not in the Mūla (TPR: 0 Mūla, 1 Aṭṭhakathā: Mp-v 47:2). The *Visuddhimagga* calls the same stage *nāmarūpavavatthāna* ("defining mind and materiality") and *saṅkhārapariccheda* ("delimitation of formations"), and says that these are names for purification of view (Vism-ii 1389:2).

### Orthodox Definition
The Pañcakanipāta-aṭṭhakathā glosses "knowledge and vision of things as they really are" as "incipient insight (*taruṇavipassanā*), starting from the knowledge of distinguishing name and form" (Mp-v 47:2). So it is the beginning of insight.

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVIII) explains it as the *diṭṭhivisuddhi*, purification of view: "the correct vision of mind and materiality (*nāmarūpānaṃ yāthāvadassanaṃ*), which has been established on the plane of non-confusion by overcoming the perception of a being, while defining mind and materiality by various methods". The meditator sees that mentality and materiality, taken separately, are powerless and empty; yet they occur through mutual dependence, as a wooden puppet moves and stands through the combination of wood and strings, and as a blind man and a cripple travel together: the blind man, who has feet but no eyes, carries the cripple, who has eyes but no feet, on his shoulder. Mentality does not arise by its own power, and materiality does not either, yet their arising and occurrence are not prevented when they depend on each other.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Pañcakanipāta-aṭṭhakathā, the term itself)
> **Yathābhūtañāṇadassananti nāmarūpaparicchedañāṇaṃ ādiṃ katvā taruṇavipassanā.**  
> ‘The knowledge and vision of things as they really are’ refers to incipient insight, starting from the knowledge of distinguishing name and form.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, purification of view)
> **Evaṃ nānānayehi nāmarūpaṃ vavatthāpayato sattasaññaṃ abhibhavitvā asammohabhūmiyaṃ ṭhitaṃ nāmarūpānaṃ yāthāvadassanaṃ diṭṭhivisuddhīti veditabbaṃ. Nāmarūpavavatthānantipi saṅkhāraparicchedotipi etasseva adhivacanaṃ.**  
> The correct vision of mind and materiality, which has been established on the plane of non-confusion by overcoming the perception of a being while defining mind and materiality by these various methods, should be understood as purification of view (diṭṭhivisuddhi). ‘Defining mind and materiality’ (nāmarūpavavatthāna) and ‘delimitation of formations’ (saṅkhārapariccheda) are also names for this same thing.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVIII, Nāmarūpapariggahakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=232&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=RXZh4bmDIG7EgW7EgW5heWVoaSBuxIFtYXLFq3Bh4bmDIHZhdmF0dGjEgXBheWF0byBzYXR0YXNhw7HDsWHhuYMgYWJoaWJoYXZpdHbEgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the wooden puppet)
> **Tasmā yathā dāruyantaṃ suññaṃ nijjīvaṃ nirīhakaṃ, atha ca pana dārurajjukasamāyogavasena gacchatipi tiṭṭhatipi. Saīhakaṃ sabyāpāraṃ viya khāyati, evamidaṃ nāmarūpampi suññaṃ nijjīvaṃ nirīhakaṃ, atha ca pana aññamaññasamāyogavasena gacchatipi tiṭṭhatipi. Saīhakaṃ sabyāpāraṃ viya khāyatīti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> 675. Therefore, just as a wooden machine is void, soulless, and without motivation, and yet it walks and stands through the combination of wood and strings; and just as it appears as if with motivation and activity; so too this mind and materiality are void, soulless, and without motivation, and yet they walk and stand through their mutual combination. It should be seen as appearing as if with motivation and activity.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVIII, 675) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=230&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=Njc1LiBUYXNtxIEgeWF0aMSBIGTEgXJ1eWFudGHhuYMgc3XDscOxYeG5gyBuaWpqxKt2YeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the blind man and the cripple)
> **Imassa panatthassa vibhāvanatthāya imaṃ upamaṃ udāharanti – yathā jaccandho ca pīṭhasappī ca disāpakkamitukāmā assu, jaccandho pīṭhasappiṃ evamāha “ahaṃ kho bhaṇe, sakkomi pādehi pādakaraṇīyaṃ kātuṃ, natthi ca me cakkhūni yehi samavisamaṃ passeyyan” ti. Pīṭhasappīpi jaccandhaṃ evamāha “ahaṃ kho bhaṇe, sakkomi cakkhunā cakkhukaraṇīyaṃ kātuṃ, natthi ca me pādāni yehi abhikkameyyaṃ vā paṭikkameyyaṃ vā” ti. So tuṭṭhahaṭṭho jaccandho pīṭhasappiṃ aṃsakūṭaṃ āropesi. Pīṭhasappī jaccandhassa aṃsakūṭe nisīditvā evamāha “vāmaṃ muñca dakkhiṇaṃ gaṇha, dakkhiṇaṃ muñca vāmaṃ gaṇhā” ti. Tattha jaccandhopi nittejo dubbalo na sakena tejena sakena balena gacchati, pīṭhasappīpi nittejo dubbalo na sakena tejena sakena balena gacchati, na ca tesaṃ aññamaññaṃ nissāya gamanaṃ nappavattati, evamevaṃ nāmampi nittejaṃ na sakena tejena uppajjati, na tāsu tāsu kiriyāsu pavattati. Rūpampi nittejaṃ na sakena tejena uppajjati, na tāsu tāsu kiriyāsu pavattati, na ca tesaṃ aññamaññaṃ nissāya uppatti vā pavatti vā na hoti.**  
> To clarify this meaning, they give this simile: suppose a man born blind and a cripple wanted to go on a journey. The blind man said to the cripple, “My friend, I am able to do what should be done by feet, but I have no eyes with which to see what is even or uneven.” Materiality too is powerless; it does not arise by its own power, nor does it operate in various functions. And yet it is not the case that their arising or occurrence does not happen when they depend on each other. Being delighted, the blind man lifted the cripple onto his shoulder. Sitting on the blind man’s shoulder, the cripple spoke thus, “Leave the left, take the right; leave the right, take the left.” In that case, the blind man is also powerless and weak; he cannot go by his own power or his own strength. The cripple is also powerless and weak; he cannot go by his own power or his own strength. And yet it is not the case that their journey does not proceed when they depend on each other. Just so, mentality is powerless; it does not arise by its own power, nor does it operate in various functions. Materiality too is powerless; it does not arise by its own power, nor does it occur in such and such functions. But there is nothing to prevent their arising or occurrence when they depend on each other.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVIII, 677) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=231&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=SW1hc3NhIHBhbmF0dGhhc3NhIHZpYmjEgXZhbmF0dGjEgXlhIGltYeG5gyB1cGFtYeG5gyB1ZMSBaGFyYW50aQ%3D%3D)
