### Grammatical Analysis
**Puthujjana**: [m.] a worldling; common person; ordinary person; one of the multitude. Derived from *puthu* (many, diverse, separate, thick) + *jana* (person). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī (Sv-i 358:1-2) quotes the explanations "puthu kilese janentīti puthujjanā" ("those who generate many defilements are puthujjanā"), "puthu avihatasakkāyadiṭṭhikā" (those with much unremoved identity view), and adds that he is one included among the many who turn away from the noble Dhamma, and is separate from the noble ones.

### Orthodox Definition
A *Puthujjana* is a person who has not yet directly realized the supramundane [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) and so remains outside the lineage of the noble disciples (*ariya-saṅgha*). The Puggalapaññatti defines the worldling as the person whose three fetters have not been abandoned and who is not proceeding to abandon them (Pp 199:2-3). The three are identity view, doubt and clinging to rules and observances, which are abandoned by seeing (Nett 230:6; D-iii 1476:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā on the Puggalapaññatti settles what this definition covers. "The three fetters" are the view-fetter, the fetter of adherence to rules and observances, and the doubt-fetter; they are abandoned at the moment of fruition (Pp-a 214:1-2). "Of those qualities" means those fettering qualities, and at the moment of the path one is called "proceeding for their abandonment". The person defined in Pp 199 is neither one who has the fruition nor one who has the path: "To this extent it should be understood that only the gross, foolish worldling who has abandoned his meditation subject is spoken of here" (*Maggakkhaṇasmiñhi tesaṃ pahānāya paṭipanno nāma hoti … ettāvatā vissaṭṭhakammaṭṭhāno thūlabālaputhujjanova idha kathito*, Pp-a 214:5-7). So the Puggalapaññatti does not say that every worldling is "not proceeding": it speaks here only of the gross, foolish worldling who has given up meditation, and it does not conflict with the commentarial distinction between two worldlings, "the blind worldling is one, and the good worldling is another" (Ps-i 141:1; 142:1-2):
1. *Andha-puthujjana* (The Blind Worldling): one for whom there is no learning, inquiry, listening, retention or reflection on the aggregates, elements, sense bases and so on (Sv-i 354:1).
2. *Kalyāṇa-puthujjana* (The Good Worldling): one for whom those exist (Sv-i 354:2). The Papañcasūdanī describes such a person as accomplished in the practice that conforms to the path, possessed of virtue, guarding the sense doors, moderate in food, devoted to wakefulness and to developing the aids to enlightenment (Ps-i 257:1).

The kalyāṇa-puthujjana is the worldling who is practising in this way; he is not the "gross, foolish worldling who has abandoned meditation" of Pp 199 (Pp-a 214:7).

### Quote
> **Katamo ca puggalo puthujjano? Yassa puggalassa tīṇi saṃyojanāni appahīnāni; na ca tesaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya paṭipanno – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo “puthujjano”.**  
> 9. What sort of person is said to be an average person? The person whose three fetters have not been put away; and who is not proceeding to put these away—such a person is said to be an average person.  
>
