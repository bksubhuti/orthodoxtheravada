### Grammatical Analysis
**Pāṭidesanīya**: [adj.] To be acknowledged; requiring specific confession. Formed by *paṭideseti* (to confess, acknowledge).

### Orthodox Definition
*Pāṭidesanīya* is a very small, highly specific category containing only **four rules** for monks (and eight for nuns): "Ime kho panāyasmanto cattāro pāṭidesanīyā" (Vin-ii 1642:1); "Ime kho panāyyāyo aṭṭha pāṭidesanīyā" (Dvem-bhni 1023:1). These rules deal strictly with the acceptance of food.

The four rules for monks (Vin-ii 1640ff) are:
1. Accepting with one's own hand, from an unrelated bhikkhunī who has entered among the houses, solid or soft food, and eating it (Vin-ii 1646:1).
2. When monks eat invited at a family and a bhikkhunī stands there giving directions ("give curry here, give rice here"), the monks must rebuke her; if none does, it is an offence (Vin-ii 1659:1-2).
3. Accepting food with one's own hand, uninvited beforehand and when not ill, at a family agreed upon as "learners" (*sekkhasammata*): a family that grows in faith and declines in wealth, to which the Saṅgha has given this agreement by a formal act (*ñattidutiyena kammena*) (Vin-ii 1683:1, 1684:1-2).
4. Accepting food within a monastery with one's own hand, not announced beforehand, in jungle lodgings held to be dangerous and frightening, when not ill (Vin-ii 1703:1).

The confession requires a specific formula, distinct from a *[pācittiya](/glossary/pacittiya/)* confession: "*gārayhaṃ, āvuso, dhammaṃ āpajjiṃ asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ, taṃ paṭidesemī*", "I have fallen, friends, into a blameworthy, improper matter which ought to be acknowledged; I acknowledge it" (Vin-ii 1646:2).

### Quote
> **“Yo pana bhikkhu aññātikāya bhikkhuniyā antaragharaṃ paviṭṭhāya hatthato khādanīyaṃ vā bhojanīyaṃ vā sahatthā paṭiggahetvā khādeyya vā bhuñjeyya vā , paṭidesetabbaṃ tena bhikkhunā – ‘ gārayhaṃ , āvuso , dhammaṃ āpajjiṃ asappāyaṃ pāṭidesanīyaṃ , taṃ paṭidesemī”’ti.**  
> “Whatever bhikkhu should eat or partake of solid food or soft food, having accepted it with his own hand from the hand of a bhikkhunī who is not a relation (and) who has entered among the houses, it should be acknowledged by that bhikkhu, saying: ‘I have fallen, friends, into a censurable, improper matter, which ought to be acknowledged; I acknowledge it.’”  
>
