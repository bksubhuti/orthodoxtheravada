### Grammatical Analysis
**Sampayuttapaccaya**: [m.] Association condition. Formed by *sampayutta* (associated, conjoined) + *paccaya* (condition). The Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā explains *sampayutta* as that which "has a nature of non-separation in the giving of fruit (*phalappadāne aviyogadhammo*), having made no distinction (*vinibbhogaṃ akatvā*), and being of a co-arising nature (*ekuppādādidhammo*)" (Nidd-a-i 118).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sampayutta-paccaya* is the nineteenth of the 24 conditions in the Paṭṭhāna: "The four immaterial aggregates are mutually related to one another by association condition" (*cattāro khandhā arūpino aññamaññaṃ sampayuttapaccayena paccayo*, Paṭṭh-i 53). It applies only to mental phenomena (*nāma*): the Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā says that in brief it is all the formless aggregates (Paṭṭh-a 2058), and defines it as "the formless phenomena that help by the state of association, known as having a single base, a single object, a single arising and a single cessation (*ekavatthukaekārammaṇaekuppādekanirodhasaṅkhātena sampayuttabhāvena*)" (Paṭṭh-a 1939).

The same four marks are the stock phrase of the Mahāniddesa, where a mind is "accompanied by that rapture, born together with it, associated with it, conjoined with it, with the same arising, the same cessation, the same base and the same object" (*sahagato sahajāto saṃsaṭṭho sampayutto ekuppādo ekanirodho ekavatthuko ekārammaṇo*, Nidd-i 16). The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha sums up in the same four marks for consciousness and the mental factors (Abhidh-s 81: "they arise and cease together, and have the same object and base"), and the Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī describes the association condition as the mental phenomena which, "though distinct in the ultimate sense, are as if they had reached unity by the characteristic of association known as having a common arising and so on" (Abhidh-s-t 1671); its contrast is the dissociation condition (*vippayutta-paccaya*), the physical basis and the mind and mental factors which are not blended (*visaṃsaṭṭha*).

The Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā makes the sense of the word clear with similes. It says that the word *sampayutta* is used to show that the association is not like that of milk and water. It then says that, in the sense of being impossible to separate (*vinibbhogaṃ kātuṃ asakkuṇeyyaṭṭhena*), there are things arisen together that are called associated, "like milk and oil" (*khīratelaṃ viya*), and in the same way there are dissociated things, "like butter removed from milk" (Nidd-a-i 119).

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Sampayuttapaccayoti – cattāro khandhā arūpino aññamaññaṃ sampayuttapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 19. Association condition: The four immaterial aggregates are mutually related to one another by association condition.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāniddesa)
> **Ayaṃ mano imāya pītiyā sahagato hoti sahajāto saṃsaṭṭho sampayutto ekuppādo ekanirodho ekavatthuko ekārammaṇo.**  
> This mind is accompanied by that rapture, born simultaneously with it, associated with it, conjoined with it, has the same genesis, the same cessation, the same physical basis, and the same object.  
>  
> — Mahāniddesa (Kāmasuttaniddeso) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_16&page_number=2&name=mah%C4%81niddesap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QXlh4bmDIG1hbm8gaW3EgXlhIHDEq3RpecSBIHNhaGFnYXRvIGhvdGk%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha)
> **Ekuppādanirodhā ca, ekālambaṇavatthukā.**  
> 1. They arise and cease together, and they have the same object and base.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=7&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=MS4gRWt1cHDEgWRhbmlyb2RoxIEgY2EsIGVrxIFsYW1iYeG5h2F2YXR0aHVrxIEu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Ekavatthukaekārammaṇaekuppādekanirodhasaṅkhātena sampayuttabhāvena upakārakā arūpadhammā sampayuttapaccayo.**  
> Formless phenomena that help by way of the state of association (sampayuttabhāvena), known as having a single base, a single object, a single arising, and a single cessation (ekavatthukaekārammaṇaekuppādekanirodhasaṅkhātena), are association condition (sampayuttapaccayo).  
>  
> — *Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=351&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RWthdmF0dGh1a2Fla8SBcmFtbWHhuYdhZWt1cHDEgWRla2FuaXJvZGhhc2HhuYVraMSBdGVuYSBzYW1wYXl1dHRhYmjEgXZlbmEgdXBha8SBcmFrxIEgYXLFq3BhZGhhbW3EgSBzYW1wYXl1dHRhcGFjY2F5by4%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Paramatthato bhinnāpi ekībhāvagatā viya ekuppādādibhāvasaṅkhātasampayogalakkhaṇena upakārakā nāmadhammā va sampayuttapaccayo.**  
> Only mental phenomena, which though distinct in the ultimate sense (paramatthato) are as if they had reached unity by the characteristic of association known as the state of having a common arising and so on, are the association condition (sampayuttapaccayo).  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=245&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UGFyYW1hdHRoYXRvIGJoaW5uxIFwaSBla8SrYmjEgXZhZ2F0xIEgdml5YSBla3VwcMSBZMSBZGliaMSBdmFzYeG5hWtoxIF0YXNhbXBheW9nYWxha2toYeG5h2VuYSB1cGFrxIFyYWvEgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Khīrodakaṃ viya ca sopi na hotīti dassetuṃ “sampayutto”ti vuttaṃ. Vinibbhogaṃ kātuṃ asakkuṇeyyaṭṭhena hi sahuppannā dhammā sampayuttāpi atthi khīratelaṃ viya. Tathā vippayuttāpi khīrato apanītaṃ navanītaṃ viya.**  
> And to show that it is not even like milk and water, it is stated as 'sampayutto'. Indeed, there are co-arising phenomena that are associated—like milk and oil—in the sense that it is impossible to make a distinction. Likewise, there are also dissociated phenomena, like butter removed from milk.  
>  
> — *Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā* ([Saddhammapajjotikā](/glossary/saddhammapajjotika/)) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_16&page_number=22&name=mah%C4%81niddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2jEq3JvZGFrYeG5gyB2aXlhIGNhIHNvcGkgbmEgaG90xKt0aQ%3D%3D)
