### Grammatical Analysis
**Visuddhajanavilāsinī**: [f.] the name of the Apadāna commentary. Compound of *visuddhajana* (pure, purified people) + *vilāsinī* (shining, charming). The commentary names itself in its closing lines: "Thus, in the Visuddhajanavilāsinī, the Apadāna commentary (*iti visuddhajanavilāsiniyā apadāna-aṭṭhakathāya*)" (Ap-a 924:1, 1089:1, 1440:1, 3863:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Visuddhajanavilāsinī* is the commentary (*aṭṭhakathā*) on the *[Apadāna](/glossary/apadana/)* of the Khuddaka Nikāya. In its introductory verses (*Ganthārambhakathā*) the author says that wise elders who know the tradition asked him again and again: "Venerable sir, the Apadāna commentary should be written" (*Apadānaṭṭhakathā bhante, kātabbā*), and that he will give an explanation of the meaning "following the method of the Pāḷi" (*yathā pāḷinayeneva atthasaṃvaṇṇanaṃ*).

The author is not named in the text of the closing verses: the concluding passage (*Nigamanakathā*, Ap-a 3866) says only that this Apadāna commentary was brought forth by "a learned master, a bearer of the three Piṭakas", within seven months, for the elders in Sri Lanka such as Venerable Ānanda. The work is divided to match the Apadāna: for example, the Buddhāpadāna commentary (Ap-a 926 ff.), and the commentary on the legends of the Buddhas, Paccekabuddhas and disciple elders ends at Ap-a 3864:1. It quotes the Buddhavaṃsa on earlier Buddhas, for example Padumuttara (Ap-a 583:1).

### Quote
> **Therehi dhīradhīrehi, āgamaññūhi viññubhi; “Apadānaṭṭhakathā bhante, kātabbā” ti visesato. Punappunādareneva, yācitohaṃ yasassibhi; Tasmāhaṃ sāpadānassa, apadānassasesato. Visesanayadīpassa, dīpissaṃ piṭakattaye; Yathā pāḷinayeneva, atthasaṃvaṇṇanaṃ subhaṃ.**  
> By the steady, wise, and knowing elders who understand the tradition, it was said: “Venerable sir, the Commentary on the Apadāna should be done.” Again and again, requested with reverence by those of high repute, therefore I, for the sake of the Apadāna, in its entirety, as a lamp that illuminates the meaning of the Pitakas, will provide a beautiful explanation of the meaning, following the method of the Pāli text.  
>
