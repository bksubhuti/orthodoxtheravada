### Grammatical Analysis
**Pubbārāma**: [m.] The Eastern Park; The Eastern Monastery. Formed by *pubba* (east, former) + *[ārāma](/glossary/arama/)* (park, monastery).

### Orthodox Definition
*Pubbārāma* ("Eastern Park") was a monastery at Sāvatthī, built by [Visākhā](/glossary/visakha/), the chief female lay disciple. The texts name its mansion the *Migāramātupāsāda* (the Mansion of Migāra's Mother): "Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Sāvatthī in the Eastern Park, in the Mansion of Migāra's Mother" (D-iii 261:1, A-iii 738:1).

The Dhammapada commentary tells how it was built. When Visākhā's great-creeper ornament (*mahālatāpasādhana*, worth over nine crores) had been touched by the Elder Ānanda, she could not wear it, and since no one could buy it she herself paid its price and gave it to the Saṅgha for the land at the east gate; she then spent nine crores on the monastery and nine on the festival, twenty-seven crores in all (Dhp-a 1149:1-4, 1152:5). The mansion had two floors, with five hundred rooms on the lower floor and five hundred on the upper, a thousand in all (Dhp-a 1150:18).

The Buddha took alms at Anāthapiṇḍika's house and then left by the east gate to stay at Pubbārāma, while after Visākhā's house he would go to Jetavana (Dhp-a 1150:2-3). The commentary counts his rains residences relying on Sāvatthī as nineteen at Jetavana and six at Pubbārāma, twenty-five in all (Dhp-a 46:2).

### Quote
> **Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati pubbārāme migāramātupāsāde.**  
> Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthī, in the Eastern Park, at the mansion of the Mother of Migāra.  
>
