### Grammatical Analysis
**Akusalamūla**: [nt.] Unwholesome root; Root of evil. Formed by *akusala* (unwholesome, unskillful, karmically unprofitable) + *mūla* (root).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Akusala-mūlas* are the three toxic, fundamental mental factors that poison consciousness. They are the ultimate psychological source of all unwholesome karma, suffering, and continued rebirth in [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). 

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī names them: "Tattha katame tayo akusalahetū? Lobho, doso, moho" (Dhs 1836:1-2) and defines each (Dhs 1837:2 for lobha, 1838:2-3 for dosa, 1839:2 for moha). The three unwholesome roots are:
1. **[Lobha](/glossary/lobha/)** (Greed): the Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists synonyms such as *rāgo sārāgo … icchā mucchā … gedho* (Dhs 1837:2); the Visuddhimagga gives it the characteristic of grasping the object, like a monkey caught in sticky lime (*ārammaṇaggahaṇalakkhaṇo makkaṭālepo viya*, Vism-ii 463:3).
2. **[Dosa](/glossary/dosa/)** (Hatred): the Dhammasaṅgaṇī begins with "Anatthaṃ me acarīti āghāto jāyati" – resentment arises at the thought "he has done me harm" (Dhs 1838:2); the Visuddhimagga gives it the characteristic of ferocity, like a struck snake (*caṇḍikkalakkhaṇo pahaṭāsīviso viya*, Vism-ii 472:2).
3. **[Moha](/glossary/moha/)** (Delusion): the Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as ignorance of suffering, its origin, its cessation and the path, of the past, the future and dependent origination (*dukkhe aññāṇaṃ …*, Dhs 1839:2); the Visuddhimagga gives it the characteristic of the mind's blindness, or ignorance (*andhabhāvalakkhaṇo, aññāṇalakkhaṇo vā*), and calls it the root of all that is unwholesome (*sabbākusalānaṃ mūlaṃ*, Vism-ii 464:1).

*[Moha](/glossary/moha/)* is present in every unwholesome mind-moment: the Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha lists moha, ahirika, anottappa and uddhacca as the four universal unwholesome factors, found in all twelve unwholesome cittas (Abhidh-s 115:1). The twelve are eight rooted in greed, two in hatred and two in delusion (Vism-ii 1964:1), so no citta is rooted in both greed and hatred. The destruction of the three is the definition of Nibbāna: "Yo kho, āvuso, rāgakkhayo dosakkhayo mohakkhayo – idaṃ vuccati nibbānaṃ" (S-iv 1531:3).

### Quote
> **“Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, akusalamūlāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Lobho akusalamūlaṃ, doso akusalamūlaṃ, moho akusalamūlaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these three roots of the unwholesome. What are these three? Greed is a root of the unwholesome, hatred is a root of the unwholesome, delusion is a root of the unwholesome.  
>

### Quote
> **Tesu lobho ārammaṇaggahaṇalakkhaṇo makkaṭālepo viya, abhisaṅgaraso tattakapāle khittamaṃsapesi viya. 480. Moho cittassa andhabhāvalakkhaṇo, aññāṇalakkhaṇo vā, asampaṭivedharaso, ārammaṇasabhāvacchādanaraso vā, asammāpaṭipattipaccupaṭṭhāno, andhakārapaccupaṭṭhāno vā, ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhāno, sabbākusalānaṃ mūlanti daṭṭhabbo.**  
> Among them, greed (lobho) has the characteristic of seizing an object like monkey glue, and the function of clinging like a piece of meat thrown onto a hot pan. 480. Delusion (moho) has the characteristic of the mind's blindness, or the characteristic of ignorance; its function is non-penetration, or the function of concealing the object's true nature; it manifests as improper practice, or manifests as darkness; it is rooted in unwise attention, and should be seen as the root of all unwholesome states.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (on lobha and moha, §479-480) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=98&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VGVzdSBsb2JobyDEgXJhbW1h4bmHYWdnYWhh4bmHYWxha2toYeG5h28gbWFra2Hhua3EgWxlcG8gdml5YSwgYWJoaXNh4bmFZ2FyYXNv)

### Quote
> **‘“Nibbānaṃ, nibbānan ’ ti, āvuso sāriputta, vuccati. Katamaṃ nu kho, āvuso, nibbānan” ti? “Yo kho, āvuso, rāgakkhayo dosakkhayo mohakkhayo – idaṃ vuccati nibbānan” ti.**
