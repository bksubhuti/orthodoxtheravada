### Grammatical Analysis
**Paccekabuddha**: [m.] Solitary Buddha; Silent Buddha. Formed by *pacceka* (separate, individual, singular) + *buddha* (awakened).

### Orthodox Definition
A *Paccekabuddha* is an individual who attains full enlightenment and the destruction of all defilements entirely on their own (*sayambhū*), in a world empty of a Buddha (*buddhasuññe loke*, e.g. Th-a-i 2638:3, Ud-a 330:3). Having fulfilled the perfections for two incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand aeons (Sv-ii 8:7, Ps-iii 492:7), he realises the awakening with his own knowledge (*sayambhuñāṇena paccekabodhiyā adhigatattā*, Cp-a 227:6). 

The Apadāna commentary says that by that same knowledge the Paccekabuddha, too, destroys all defilements and attains Nibbāna (Ap-a 902:5). The Buddhas, however, have fulfilled the perfections for four incalculable aeons and a hundred thousand aeons, and have attained the knowledge of the faculties of others, the great compassion attainment, the twin miracle, omniscience and the other knowledges not shared with others; by a single teaching of the Dhamma they can make countless beings drink the nectar of the Dhamma and bring them to Nibbāna (Ap-a 902:6). The Paccekabuddhas are not able to do so (*paccekabuddhā pana na tathā kātuṃ samatthā*, Ap-a 902:8): even when they teach, they cannot awaken countless beings, so they are not like the omniscient Buddhas and are called Paccekabuddhas because they awaken individually (Ap-a 907:2). The commentary adds that they teach the Dhamma using only two verses, the verses of blessing for the donor beginning "May your wish, your hope, quickly be fulfilled" (*Imāhi dvīhiyeva gāthāhi dhammaṃ desenti*, Ap-a 907:1; verses at Ap-a 903:1-904:1). The texts place them on mountains: five hundred of them lived on Mount Isigili (M-iii 368:1), and one lived on the mountain called Bhūtagaṇa (Th-a-i 2638:3).

### Quote
> **“Bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhave, pañca paccekabuddhasatāni imasmiṃ isigilismiṃ pabbate ciranivāsino ahesuṃ. Te imaṃ pabbataṃ pavisantā dissanti, paviṭṭhā na dissanti.**  
> “Formerly, monks, five hundred Solitary Awakened Ones dwelt for a long time on this Mount Isigili. They were seen entering this mountain, but once entered, they were seen no more.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Isigili Sutta* (MN 116; CST heading "6. Isigilisuttaṃ" M-iii 361:1) – "Bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhave, pañca paccekabuddhasatāni imasmiṃ isigilismiṃ pabbate ciranivāsino ahesuṃ" (M-iii 368:1); the Buddha then names them, from Ariṭṭha (M-iii 369:1) on.
* **Canonical**: *Khaggavisāṇa Sutta* (Sn 1.3, Sn 125:1; refrain "eko care khaggavisāṇakappo", Sn 128:1) – The "Rhinoceros Horn" discourse; the Suttanipāta commentary (Pj-ii 223:9, 503:1: 41 verses) has the Buddha tell Ānanda of the aspiration and resolve (*patthanā*, *abhinīhāra*) of the Paccekabuddhas whose verses these are. Also Ap-i 256:1 "Paccekabuddhaapadānaṃ".
* **Commentary**: the two incalculable aeons and 100,000 aeons are stated in the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī (Sv-ii 8:7), Papañcasūdanī (Ps-iii 492:7), Sāratthappakāsinī (Spk-i 147:12) and Apadāna commentary (Ap-a 1220:2); the Theragāthā commentary (Th-a-i 65:1-67:7) describes the paccekabodhisatta's course. The Paramatthadīpanī is Dhammapāla's commentary on the Khuddaka texts: the colophons read "Paramatthadīpaniyā cariyāpiṭakasaṃvaṇṇanāya" (Cp-a 224:1) and "Paramatthadīpaniyā khuddakanikāya-aṭṭhakathāya" (It-a 209:1), and the Ṭīkā names the Udāna commentary as such ("paramatthadīpaniyaṃ udānaṭṭhakathāyaṃ", Ps-t-i 2073:1). Cp-a 227:6, Ud-a 330:3 and Th-a-i 2638:3, cited above, are therefore Paramatthadīpanī passages; Sv-ii, Ps-iii, Spk-i and Ap-a are other commentaries.
