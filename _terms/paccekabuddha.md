### Grammatical Analysis
**Paccekabuddha**: [m.] Solitary Buddha; Silent Buddha. Formed by *pacceka* (separate, individual, singular) + *buddha* (awakened).

### Orthodox Definition
A *Paccekabuddha* is an individual who attains full enlightenment and the destruction of all defilements entirely on their own, in an era where the teachings of a *[Sammā-sambuddha](/glossary/samma-sambuddha/)* have completely vanished from the world. 

While their liberation from [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/) is identical to that of a Fully Enlightened Buddha, their scope of knowledge is limited. Crucially, a *Paccekabuddha* lacks the supreme pedagogical mastery required to teach the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) to others in a systematic, liberating way. They cannot establish a [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/) or a dispensation. They often teach merely through their silent, perfect behavior or by giving brief moral exhortations. They arise in the world either singly or in large groups, often living secluded in the mountains.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Isigili Sutta* (MN 116) – The Buddha names hundreds of Paccekabuddhas who previously lived on Mount Isigili, praising their solitary purity.
* **Canonical**: *Khaggavisāṇa Sutta* (Sn 1.3) – The famous "Rhinoceros Horn" discourse, widely attributed to the inspired utterances (*[udāna](/glossary/udana/)*) of various Paccekabuddhas praising solitary asceticism.
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* – Detailing the specific *pāramīs* (two incalculable aeons and 100,000 aeons) required to reach this state.
