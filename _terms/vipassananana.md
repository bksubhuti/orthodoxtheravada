### Grammatical Analysis
**Vipassanāñāṇa**: [nt.] insight knowledge. Compound of *[vipassanā](/glossary/vipassana/)* (insight) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā glosses: "when one contemplates the characteristics of impermanence and so on, the insight knowledge is called *lakkhaṇūpanijjhānaṃ* (contemplation of characteristics)" (Bv-a 541:6).

### Orthodox Definition
The word appears in the Mūla as the heading *Vipassanāñāṇaṃ* ("Knowledge and Vision", TPR) of the passage in the Sāmaññaphala Sutta (DN 2) where the meditator, with mind concentrated, pure and bright, "directs and inclines it to knowledge and vision" and understands: "this is my body, having material form, composed of the four primary elements ... impermanent ...; and this is my consciousness, supported by it and bound up with it".

The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha (§46) lists **ten** insight knowledges (*dasa vipassanāñāṇāni*): *sammasanañāṇaṃ, udayabbayañāṇaṃ, bhaṅgañāṇaṃ, bhayañāṇaṃ, ādīnavañāṇaṃ, nibbidāñāṇaṃ, muccitukamyatāñāṇaṃ, paṭisaṅkhāñāṇaṃ, saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇaṃ, anulomañāṇaṃ*.

The Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā gives the **nine** that make up the "purification by knowledge and vision of the way" (*paṭipadāñāṇadassanavisuddhi*): rising and falling, dissolution, appearance as terror, danger, dispassion, desire for deliverance, reflection, equanimity about formations, and conformity (*imāni nava vipassanāñāṇāni*). It also says the ten imperfections of insight (light, knowledge, rapture, and so on) that arise at the stage of rise and fall "are not the path", and that this is "knowledge of what is path and not-path"; the purification that comes after, "by knowledge and vision", is the wisdom of the four noble paths.

The Mūla analysis of these knowledges is in the Paṭisambhidāmagga (Ñāṇakathā), for example *udayabbayānupassane ñāṇaṃ*, "knowledge of the contemplation of rise and fall".

> Knowledge and Vision 234. When his mind is thus concentrated, pure and bright, unblemished, free from defects, malleable, wieldy, steady and attained to imperturbability, he directs and inclines it to knowledge and vision. He understands thus: ‘This is my body, having material form, composed of the four primary elements, originating from father and mother, built up out of rice and gruel, impermanent, subject to rubbing and pressing, to dissolution and dispersion. And this is my consciousness, supported by it and bound up with it.’

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2, D-i 339–340, ¶234) – the heading *Vipassanāñāṇaṃ* and the knowledge and vision of the body and consciousness.
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)*, Ñāṇakathā (Paṭis 81–681) – the knowledges one by one (e.g. Paṭis 283, *udayabbayānupassane ñāṇaṃ*); Mahāpaññākathā (Paṭis 1810–1862).
* **Commentary**: *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 516:9–16) – the nine knowledges and the purifications; *Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā* (Bv-a 541:6). *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha* §46 (Abhidh-s 815:1) – the ten knowledges. The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. XX, Vism-ii 1459–1649, for the stage of what is and is not the path) takes the sequence further.
