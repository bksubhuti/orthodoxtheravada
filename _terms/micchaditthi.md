### Grammatical Analysis
**Micchādiṭṭhi**: [f.] Wrong View; False Belief; Corrupt Perspective. Formed by *micchā* (falsely, wrongly, pervertedly) + *[diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)* (view/perspective). Doctrinally identical to the unwholesome mental factor of wrong view (*[diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)-cetasika*).

### Orthodox Definition
*Micchādiṭṭhi* is the exact psychological and ethical inverse of *[Sammādiṭṭhi](/glossary/sammaditthi/)*. It represents the mind actively grasping an erroneous, inverted interpretation of reality, completely blinding the creature to the laws of morality and liberation.

The Mūla speaks of *niyatamicchādiṭṭhi* (e.g. Paṭṭh-i 1475:9, as a cause alongside the five immediate-result kammas), and the Ṭīkā identifies it with three views: "ahetukadiṭṭhi-akiriyadiṭṭhi-natthikadiṭṭhiyova niyatabhāvaṃ pattā niyatamicchādiṭṭhidhammā" (Ps-t-i 1569:12). The Papañcasūdanī distinguishes them: natthikadiṭṭhi denies the result (*vipāka*), akiriyadiṭṭhi denies kamma, and ahetukadiṭṭhi denies both (Ps-ii 394:1). The three are:
1. *Natthika-[diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)* (Annihilationism/Nihilism): Asserting that there is no fruit to [kamma](/glossary/kamma/), no next life, and that actions have zero moral repercussions.
2. *Ahetuka-[diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)* (No-Cause Theory): Asserting that the defilement or purification of beings happens completely by chance, without any distinct causes or conditions.
3. *Akiriya-[diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)* (The View of Inaction): Asserting that performing deeds like killing or stealing deposits zero demerit, and giving deposits zero merit—denying the physics of volition.

Micchādiṭṭhi is the single most destructive unwholesome factor; if held firmly at the moment of death, it drives consciousness directly into the lowest hell realms.

### Quote
> **Tattha katamā micchādiṭṭhi? “Natthi dinnaṃ, natthi yiṭṭhaṃ … pe … ye imañca lokaṃ parañca lokaṃ sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā pavedentī” ti – yā evarūpā diṭṭhi diṭṭhigataṃ … pe … vipariyāsaggāho – ayaṃ vuccati “micchādiṭṭhi”.**  
> Therein, what is wrong view? “There is no merit in what is given, no merit in what is offered... there are no ascetics and brahmins who, having realized this world and the other world for themselves with direct knowledge, proclaim them.” Whatever such a view, view-point... grasping of perversion, this is called wrong view (micchādiṭṭhi).  
>
