### Grammatical Analysis
**Itivuttaka**: [nt.] "thus it was said." Formed by *iti* (thus) + *vutta* (said, spoken, past participle of *vadati*) + *ka* (suffix). The word comes from the formula that opens each discourse in the Pāḷi, "*Vuttañhetaṃ bhagavatā, vuttamarahatāti me sutaṃ*" (This was said by the Blessed One, said by the Arahant, so I have heard), and from the closing "*Tatthetaṃ iti vuccati*" (in regard to this it is said).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Itivuttaka* belongs to the Khuddaka Nikāya, where the Paramatthajotikā lists it fourth, after the Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada and Udāna (Pj-i 24:5). The Paramatthadīpanī says: "*itivuttakaṃ nāma ekakanipāto, dukanipāto, tikanipāto, catukkanipāto*", that is, it is the collection of four *nipātas*, and it is the *itivuttaka* division among the nine divisions of the teaching (*navaṅga*). The Pāḷi has 112 discourses (the *suttasaṅgaha* verse at It 1190-1191 gives 27 in the Book of Ones, 22 in Twos, 50 in Threes and 13 in Fours; the Aṭṭhakathā verse at It-a 1740:1 gives "*dasuttarasataṃ dve ca*"), in prose and verse, and each of the 112 opens with the same formula (count of "Vuttañhetaṃ bhagavatā" in the Itivuttaka: 112).

The Paramatthadīpanī explains why the discourses have no usual nidāna ("thus have I heard, at one time the Blessed One…"): the nidāna of this collection was not first spoken by the Elder Ānanda but by Khujjuttarā, a noble female disciple whom the Buddha placed foremost among the learned lay women, who spoke it first to the five hundred women led by Sāmāvatī (It-a 146:2-4). The commentary's account of her (It-a 147-154): in a past life she aspired to that position; in this life she was born to a bondswoman in the house of the treasurer Ghosaka and was called Khujjuttarā because she was born hunchbacked; as a servant of Sāmāvatī, queen of King Udena, she went to buy flowers, served the Buddha's meal at the house of the chief garland-maker, heard the Dhamma and was established in the fruit of stream-entry (It-a 148:9); she then taught the five hundred women, and all became stream-enterers (It-a 149:6-7). The women asked her to go to the Teacher every day, hear the Dhamma and teach it to them; she became a bearer of the three baskets and the Buddha placed her foremost (It-a 153:1-4). Because she taught the same day in the same city, no time or place was stated, and later Ānanda, reciting the Dhamma at the First Council in the Sattapaṇṇi cave, raised the nidāna in the form she had set (It-a 154:1-4).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthadīpanī)
> **Tattha itivuttakaṃ nāma ekakanipāto, dukanipāto, tikanipāto, catukkanipātoti catunipātasaṅgahaṃ. Tampi vinayapiṭakaṃ, suttantapiṭakaṃ, abhidhammapiṭakanti tīsu piṭakesu suttantapiṭakapariyāpannaṃ; dīghanikāyo majjhimanikāyo, saṃyuttanikāyo, aṅguttaranikāyo, khuddakanikāyoti pañcasu nikāyesu khuddakanikāyapariyāpannaṃ; suttaṃ, geyyaṃ, veyyākaraṇaṃ, gāthā, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, jātakaṃ, abbhutadhammaṃ, vedallanti navasu sāsanaṅgesu itivuttakaṅgabhūtaṃ.**  
> In this regard, Itivuttaka is the collection of four: the single-set, the two-set, the three-set, and the four-set. Among the three Piṭakas—Vinaya Piṭaka, Sutta Piṭaka, and Abhidhamma Piṭaka—it is included in the Sutta Piṭaka. Among the five Nikāyas—Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara, and Khuddaka—it is included in the Khuddaka Nikāya. Among the nine divisions of the Buddha's teachings—Sutta, Geyya, Veyyākaraṇa, Gāthā, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Jātaka, Abbhutadhamma, and Vedalla—it constitutes the Itivuttaka division.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthadīpanī)
> **Etthāha – “kasmā panettha yathā aññesu suttesu ‘ evaṃ me sutaṃ, ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā ’ tiādinā kāladese apadisitvāva nidānaṃ bhāsitaṃ, evaṃ na bhāsitan” ti? Apare tāva āhu – na pana therena bhāsitattā. Idañhi nidānaṃ na āyasmatā ānandena paṭhamaṃ bhāsitaṃ khujjuttarāya pana bhagavatā upāsikāsu bahussutabhāvena etadagge ṭhapitāya sekkhappaṭisambhidāppattāya ariyasāvikāya sāmāvatippamukhānaṃ pañcannaṃ itthisatānaṃ paṭhamaṃ bhāsitaṃ.**  
> Here, it is asked— ‘Why, in this case, unlike other suttas where the nidāna is spoken by pointing out the time and place with phrases like “thus have I heard, on one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at...”, was it not spoken so?’ Some say: ‘Because it was not spoken by the Elder.’ This nidāna was not spoken by the venerable Ānanda first. It was spoken first by Khujjuttarā, the noble disciple, the sekkha who had attained the analytical modes, and who was placed by the Blessed One in the foremost position among the lay followers for being learned, to the five hundred women led by Sāmāvatī.  
>

* **Canonical**: *Bahukāra Sutta* (Iti 107, It 1093-1097) – householders and those gone forth, "both based on each other, accomplish the true Dhamma" (*ārādhayanti saddhammaṃ*; It 1096:1-1097:1). (The page formerly cited Iti 106, which is the Sabrahmaka Sutta on honouring parents.)
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* (It-a 31:1-4; 146-154 on Khujjuttarā; 1740:1 on the number of discourses).
