### Grammatical Analysis
**Khuddakanikāya**: [m.] the Collection of Minor Texts. A compound of *khuddaka* (small, minor) + *nikāya* (collection, assemblage). The Paramatthajotikā explains why it is so called: "because it is a collection and abode of many minor sections of the Dhamma (*bahūnaṃ khuddakānaṃ dhammakkhandhānaṃ samūhato nivāsato ca*); for collections and abodes are called *nikāya*" (Pj-i 25:1-3).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Khuddaka-nikāya* is the fifth of the five Nikāyas of the Buddha's word, after the Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta and Aṅguttara (Sv-i 173:2; Sp-i 118:1). The word does not occur in the Mūla texts except in the CST book titles ("Khuddakanikāye"), so the earliest sources in TPR are the Aṭṭhakathās.

The Atthasālinī and the Paramatthajotikā give its content: the Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Suttanipāta, Vimānavatthu, Petavatthu, Theragāthā, Therīgāthā, Jātaka, Niddesa, Paṭisambhidā, Apadāna, Buddhavaṃsa and Cariyāpiṭaka, together with the Vinaya and Abhidhamma Piṭakas; or, leaving aside the four Nikāyas, the remaining Buddha-word is the Khuddaka Nikāya (Pj-i 24:5; As 217:2-3).

The Atthasālinī records that all the Buddha's word, including the Khuddaka Nikāya, was classified and compiled at the Council of Five Hundred by the group of arahants led by the Venerable Mahākassapa (As 227:1).

The Khuddakapāṭha's own commentary calls the "minor teachings" (*khuddakāni*) of the Khuddakapāṭha a portion of the Khuddaka Nikāya (Pj-i 21:2). ### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, definition)
> **Katamo khuddakanikāyo? Sakalaṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ, abhidhammapiṭakaṃ, khuddakapāṭha, dhammapadādayo ca pubbe dassitā pañcadasappabhedā; ṭhapetvā cattāro nikāye avasesaṃ buddhavacananti.**  
> What is the Khuddaka Nikāya (khuddakanikāyo)? It is the entire Vinaya Piṭaka, the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, and the fifteen divisions previously shown—the Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, and so on; having set aside the four Nikāyas, it is the remainder of the Buddha's word.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, Nidānakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=26&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1vIGtodWRkYWthbmlrxIF5bz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthajotikā, content and name)
> **Khuddakapāṭho, dhammapadaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, suttanipāto, vimānavatthu, petavatthu, theragāthā, therīgāthā, jātakaṃ, niddeso, paṭisambhidā, apadānaṃ, buddhavaṃso, cariyāpiṭakaṃ, vinayābhidhammapiṭakāni, ṭhapetvā vā cattāro nikāye avasesaṃ buddhavacanaṃ khuddakanikāyo. Kasmā panesa khuddakanikāyoti vuccati? Bahūnaṃ khuddakānaṃ dhammakkhandhānaṃ samūhato nivāsato ca. Samūhanivāsā hi “nikāyo” ti vuccanti.**  
> The Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Suttanipāta, Vimānavatthu, Petavatthu, Theragāthā, Therīgāthā, Jātaka, Niddesa, Paṭisambhidā, Apadāna, Buddhavamsa, Cariyāpiṭaka, the Vinaya Piṭaka, and Abhidhamma Piṭaka—or, leaving aside the four Nikāyas, the remaining word of the Buddha—is the Khuddaka Nikāya. But why is this called the Khuddaka Nikāya? Because it is a collection and dwelling place for many minor doctrinal units. For collections and dwelling places are indeed called “Nikāyas.”  
>  
> — [Paramatthajotikā](/glossary/paramatthajotika/) (Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā, Ganthārambhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_01&page_number=2&name=khuddakap%C4%81%E1%B9%ADhaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2h1ZGRha2FwxIHhua1obywgZGhhbW1hcGFkYeG5gywgdWTEgW5h4bmDLCBpdGl2dXR0YWth4bmDLCBzdXR0YW5pcMSBdG8sIHZpbcSBbmF2YXR0aHUs)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the First Council)
> **Evametaṃ sabbampi buddhavacanaṃ pañcasatikasaṅgītikāle saṅgāyantena mahākassapappamukhena vasīgaṇena ayaṃ dhammo ayaṃ vinayo, idaṃ paṭhamabuddhavacanaṃ, idaṃ majjhimabuddhavacanaṃ, idaṃ pacchimabuddhavacanaṃ, idaṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ, idaṃ suttantapiṭakaṃ, idaṃ abhidhammapiṭakaṃ, ayaṃ dīghanikāyo … pe … ayaṃ khuddakanikāyo, imāni suttādīni navaṅgāni, imāni caturāsīti dhammakkhandhasahassānīti imaṃ pabhedaṃ vavatthapetvāva saṅgītaṃ.**  
> All of this word of the Buddha was compiled at the time of the Council of Five Hundred by the group of those who had mastered themselves, led by the Venerable Mahākassapa, having first defined these divisions: “This is the Dhamma, this is the Vinaya; this is the first word of the Buddha, this the middle, this the last; this is the Vinaya Piṭaka, this the Suttanta Piṭaka, this the Abhidhamma Piṭaka; this is the Dīgha Nikāya ... this the Khuddaka Nikāya; these are the nine Angas, such as the discourses; these are the eighty-four thousand masses of Dhamma.”  
>
