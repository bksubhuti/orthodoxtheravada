### Grammatical Analysis
**Upanāha**: [m.] resentment; enmity; holding a grudge; bearing ill-will. Formed by prefix *upa* (closely) + root *nah* (to tie, bind). The Abhidhamma explains it by *upanayhanā upanayhitattaṃ*, the act of binding (Vibh 2646:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Upanāha* is the continuation of *[kodha](/glossary/kodha/)* (anger). It is one of the sixteen imperfections that defile the mind (M-i 191:2) and appears immediately after anger in the list of evil states (*kodho ca pāpako upanāho ca pāpako*, M-i 105:1).

The Abhidhamma gives the relation: "Anger in the former time is resentment in the later time" (*pubbakālaṃ kodho, aparakālaṃ upanāho*), and defines it as the binding, the establishing, the firm establishing, continuous connection, uninterrupted succession, the strengthening of anger (*upanayhanā … anuppabandhanā daḷhīkammaṃ kodhassa*, Vibh 2646:2-3; the same in Pp 238:2-3).

The Papañcasūdanī gives the characteristics: *upanandhanalakkhaṇo upanāho, vera appaṭinissajjanaraso, kodhānupabandhabhāvapaccupaṭṭhāno*: its characteristic is binding, its function is not letting go of enmity, its manifestation is following on from anger (Ps-i 559:3). Anger that arises once is just anger; going beyond that is resentment (Ps-i 566:1); resentment is what covers the mind again and again (*punappunaṃ cittapariyonandhano*, Ps-i 850:3). It is abandoned by the path of non-return (Ps-i 855:6).

In the Dhammapada the Buddha says: "He insulted me, he struck me, he defeated me, he robbed me": for those who bind themselves to such thoughts (*ye ca taṃ upanayhanti*), enmity is not appeased (Dhp 14:1-15:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammapada)
> **Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me; Ye ca taṃ upanayhanti, veraṃ tesaṃ na sammati.**  
> He insulted me; he struck me; he defeated me; he robbed me. For those who dwell on such resentments, enmity never ceases.  
>  
> — [Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/) (Dhp 3, Yamakavagga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_02&page_number=13&name=dhammapadap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=My4gQWtrb2NjaGkgbWHhuYMgYXZhZGhpIG1h4bmDLCBhamluaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **(Kha) tattha katamo upanāho? Pubbakālaṃ kodho, aparakālaṃ upanāho. Yo evarūpo upanāho upanayhanā upanayhitattaṃ aṭṭhapanā ṭhapanā saṇṭhapanā anusaṃsandanā anuppabandhanā daḷhīkammaṃ kodhassa – ayaṃ vuccati “upanāho”.**  
> (B) What is resentment (upanāho) there? Anger in former times is resentment in later times. Whatever such resentment, act of binding, state of having bound, non-establishment, establishment, firm establishment, continuous connection, uninterrupted succession, strengthening of anger, there is—this is called resentment (upanāho).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Dukaniddesa, (1) Kodho ca upanāho ca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=371&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=KEtoYSkgdGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyB1cGFuxIFobz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Upanandhanalakkhaṇo upanāho , vera appaṭinissajjanaraso, kodhānupabandhabhāvapaccupaṭṭhāno.**  
> Resentment has the characteristic of binding; it has the function of not abandoning enmity, and manifests as the state of following anger.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Ps-i 559:3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=110&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VXBhbmFuZGhhbmFsYWtraGHhuYdvIHVwYW7EgWhvICwgdmVyYSBhcHBh4bmtaW5pc3NhamphbmFyYXNvLCBrb2RoxIFudXBhYmFuZGhhYmjEgXZhcGFjY3VwYeG5reG5rWjEgW5vLg%3D%3D)
