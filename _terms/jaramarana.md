### Grammatical Analysis
**Jarāmaraṇa**: [nt.] aging and death; decay and dissolution. Formed by *jarā* (old age, decay, decrepitude) + *maraṇa* (death, dying, passing away).

### Orthodox Definition
*Jarāmaraṇa* constitutes the twelfth and final link within the chain of Dependent Origination, conditioned directly by birth (*[jāti](/glossary/jati/)-paccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ*). It represents the inevitable existential collapse that awaits every single conditioned consciousness-stream initialized in [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/).

The orthodox [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) clinically splits this compound to strip away sentimental or anthropomorphic definitions:
1. *Jarā*: Aging: broken teeth, grey hair, wrinkled skin, the dwindling of the life span and the decay of the sense faculties (*khaṇḍiccaṃ pāliccaṃ valittacatā āyuno saṃhāni indriyānaṃ paripāko*).
2. *Maraṇa*: Death: the falling away of beings from their group of beings, the dissolution of the aggregates (*khandhānaṃ bhedo*), the laying down of the body and the cutting off of the life faculty (*kaḷevarassa nikkhepo jīvitindriyassupacchedo*).

Within the First Noble Truth, jarāmaraṇa forms the absolute climax of *[dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)*. It is explicitly accompanied by the psychological derivatives of grief (*soka*), lamentation (*parideva*), physical pain (*[dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)*), mental misery (*domanassa*), and existential despair (*upāyāsa*).

### Quote
> **Tattha katamā jarā? Yā tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ tamhi tamhi sattanikāye jarā jīraṇatā khaṇḍiccaṃ pāliccaṃ valittacatā āyuno saṃhāni indriyānaṃ paripāko – ayaṃ vuccati “jarā”. Tattha katamaṃ maraṇaṃ? Yā tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ tamhā tamhā sattanikāyā cuti cavanatā bhedo antaradhānaṃ maccu maraṇaṃ kālakiriyā khandhānaṃ bhedo kaḷevarassa nikkhepo jīvitindriyassupacchedo – idaṃ vuccati “maraṇaṃ”. Iti ayañca jarā, idañca maraṇaṃ.**  
> Therein, what is old age (jarā)? Whatever old age (jarā), state of aging (jīraṇatā), broken teeth (khaṇḍiccaṃ), gray hair (pāliccaṃ), wrinkled skin (valittacatā), decline of life (āyuno saṃhāni), and decay of the sense faculties (indriyānaṃ paripāko) there is for the various beings in their respective groups of beings — this is called old age (jarā). Therein, what is death (maraṇaṃ)? Whatever passing away (cuti), falling away (cavanatā), breaking up (bhedo), disappearance (antaradhānaṃ), dying (maccu), death (maraṇaṃ), completion of time (kālakiriyā), breaking up of the aggregates (khandhānaṃ bhedo), laying down of the corpse (kaḷevarassa nikkhepo), and cutting off of the life faculty (jīvitindriyassupacchedo) there is for the various beings from their respective groups of beings — this is called death (maraṇaṃ). Thus, there is this old age (jarā) and this death (maraṇaṃ).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Paṭiccasamuppādavibhaṅga, Vibh §236) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=144&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGthdGFtxIEgamFyxIE%2F)
