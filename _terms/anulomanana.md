### Grammatical Analysis
**Anulomañāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of conformity; adaptation knowledge. Formed by *anuloma* (in regular order, conforming, adapting) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge).

### Orthodox Definition
*Anulomañāṇa* is the final fleeting phase of mundane insight. As equanimity towards formations (*[saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇa](/glossary/sankharupekkhanana/)*) peaks, the cognitive process (*citta-vīthi*) shifts into a rapid, hyper-focused sequence that directly precedes awakening. 

The Visuddhimagga (Chapter XXI, *Anulomañāṇakathā*, heading Vism-ii 1854:1) describes the process. When the knowledge of equanimity towards formations has grown sharp, the mind-door adverting arises, taking the formations as impermanent, suffering or not-self (Vism-ii 1855:2-3); then three javana cittas follow, each taking the formations as object: the first is called *parikamma* (preparation), the second *upacāra* (access), the third *anuloma* (conformity) (Vism-ii 1855:4-7). They "conform in two directions": the knowledge conforms to the function of the eight preceding insight knowledges and to the thirty-seven bodhipakkhiya dhammas above (*purimānaṃ aṭṭhannaṃ vipassanāñāṇānaṃ tathakiccatāya ca anulometi, upari ca sattatiṃsāya bodhipakkhiyadhammānaṃ*, Vism-ii 1856:1-4; 1857:3), and so it is called "knowledge conforming to the truths" (*saccānulomikañāṇa*, Vism-ii 1857:4). The simile is a king who sits in judgment and says "so be it" to the decisions of his eight chief judges, thereby conforming both to their decision and to the ancient royal law (Vism-ii 1857:1-3). It is the end of the insight that leads to emergence (*vuṭṭhānagāminī vipassanā*) as far as formations are its object; the change-of-lineage knowledge then takes Nibbāna as object (Vism-ii 1860:1-2).

The Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha counts it as the tenth of the ten insight knowledges (Abhidh-s 815:1), and its sub-commentary explains it as "the knowledge that conforms to the accomplishment of the tasks of the preceding nine (knowledges), and to the thirty-seven bodhipakkhiya dhammas above" (Abhidh-s-t 1767:8; the nine count the *sammasanañāṇa* that Vism does not separate, hence eight in Vism). In the Paṭṭhāna, anuloma is a condition for the change-of-lineage and for the change of lineage (*anulomaṃ gotrabhussa … anantarapaccayena paccayo*, Paṭṭh-i 1416:2).

Immediately after that as well, the third javana-consciousness arises, making those same formations its object; this is called conformity (anuloma). These are their individual names. In a general sense, however, it is appropriate to call all three repetition, preparatory, access, and conformity. To what does it conform? To the earlier and later parts. For it conforms to the performance of the function of the eight preceding insight knowledges, and it conforms to the thirty-seven factors partaking of awakening that follow.
