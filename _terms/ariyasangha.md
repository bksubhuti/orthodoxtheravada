### Grammatical Analysis
**Ariyasaṅgha**: [m.] The noble Saṅgha, a compound of *ariya* (noble) + *[saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)* (assembly). The Mūla uses the word in the verse "*Ariyasaṅgho niccaṃ viññūnaṃ pūjito*", "the noble Saṅgha is always worshipped by the wise" (Nett 1602:1). The canonical formula for the same community is *bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho*, "the Saṅgha of the Blessed One's disciples" (A-iii 747:4). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī defines the word *saṅgho* as *puggalasamūhavacanaṃ*, "a term for a group of individuals" (Kkh 1234:4).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Ariyasaṅgha* is the Saṅgha of the noble disciples of the Buddha. The Mūla describes it in the recollection of the Saṅgha: the disciples are *suppaṭipanno, ujuppaṭipanno, ñāyappaṭipanno, sāmīcippaṭipanno*, "that is to say, the four pairs of men, the eight individuals", and are "worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world" (A-iii 747:4). The Visuddhimagga explains the four pairs: taking the one standing on the first path and the one standing in the first fruition as one pair, there are four pairs; taking them individually there are eight individuals (Vism-i 1341:2-3).

The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī distinguishes two kinds of Saṅgha: the *dakkhiṇeyyasaṅgha*, "the assembly of the eight noble individuals", and the *sammutisaṅgha*, in general an assembly of bhikkhus (Kkh 1235:1-3). The Ratana Sutta also praises "the eight persons extolled by the virtuous, who make four pairs", as worthy of offerings, "gifts given to them yield rich results" (Khp 107:1-108:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **Idha, visākhe, ariyasāvako saṅghaṃ anussarati – ‘ suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, yadidaṃ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā ’ ti.**  
> Here, Visākhā, a noble disciple recollects the Saṅgha thus: ‘The Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is practising the good way, practising the straight way, practising the true way, correct in their practice—that is to say, the four types of individuals when taken as pairs, the eight when taken as individual types—this is the Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples: worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.’  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (Uposatha Sutta, A-iii 737 "10. Uposathasuttaṃ") · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_03&page_number=209&name=tikanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SWRoYSwgdmlzxIFraGUsIGFyaXlhc8SBdmFrbyBzYeG5hWdoYeG5gyBhbnVzc2FyYXRpIOKAkw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Cattāri purisayugānīti yugaḷavasena paṭhamamaggaṭṭho phalaṭṭhoti idamekaṃ yugaḷanti evaṃ cattāri purisayugaḷāni honti. Aṭṭha purisapuggalāti purisapuggalavasena eko paṭhamamaggaṭṭho eko phalaṭṭhoti iminā nayena aṭṭheva purisapuggalā honti.**  
> Four pairs of men (cattāri purisayugāni) means that, taking them in pairs, the one who stands on the first path and the one who stands in the first fruition make one pair; thus, there are four pairs of men. Eight types of individuals (aṭṭha purisapuggalā) means that, taking them as individuals, the one who stands on the first path is one, and the one who stands in the first fruition is one; in this way, there are exactly eight individuals.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Saṅghānussatikathā, Vism-i 1333) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=212&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dMSBcmkgcHVyaXNheXVnxIFuxKt0aSB5dWdh4bi3YXZhc2VuYSBwYeG5rWhhbWFtYWdnYeG5reG5rWhvIHBoYWxh4bmt4bmtaG90aSBpZGFtZWth4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **“Saṅgho” tiiminā pana padena kiñcāpi avisesato puggalasamūho vutto, atha kho so dakkhiṇeyyasaṅgho, sammutisaṅgho cāti duvidho hoti. Tattha dakkhiṇeyyasaṅghoti aṭṭha ariyapuggalasamūho vuccati. Sammutisaṅghoti avisesena bhikkhusamūho, so idha adhippeto. So panesa kammavasena pañcavidho (mahāva.388) hoti – catuvaggo pañcavaggo dasavaggo vīsativaggo atirekavīsativaggoti.**  
> By the word saṅgho, although a group of individuals is spoken of in general, it is twofold: the Saṅgha of those worthy of offerings and the Saṅgha of designation. Therein, the Saṅgha of those worthy of offerings is called the group of eight noble individuals. The Saṅgha of designation is a group of bhikkhus in general, and that is what is intended here. It is fivefold according to the legal procedure: the group of four, the group of five, the group of ten, the group of twenty, and the group of more than twenty.  
>  
> — Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī (Kkh 1235, Dvemātikā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=85&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNh4bmFZ2hvIOKAmeKAmSB0aWltaW7EgSBwYW5hIHBhZGVuYQ%3D%3D)
