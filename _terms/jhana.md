### Grammatical Analysis
**Jhāna**: [nt.] meditative absorption; deep mental integration. Derived from root *jhā* (to contemplate, think, meditate) or root *jhe* (to burn up), because it burns up opposing unwholesome mental states like the five hindrances (*nīvaraṇa*).

### Orthodox Definition
*Jhāna* refers to states of deep mental absorption achieved through the systematic cultivation of tranquility (*[samatha](/glossary/samatha/)*). In the Noble Eightfold Path, it forms the explicit definition of Right Concentration (*Sammāsamādhi*).

The *Visuddhimagga* outlines four distinct material-sphere jhānas (*rūpajhāna*), each defined by the abandonment of coarser mental qualities and the presence of specific jhāna factors (*jhānaṅga*):
1. **First Jhāna**: Accompanied by applied thought (*[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)*), sustained thought (*[vicāra](/glossary/vicara/)*), rapture (*[pīti](/glossary/piti/)*), pleasure (*[sukha](/glossary/sukha/)*), and unmoving one-pointedness (*[ekaggatā](/glossary/ekaggata/)*).
2. **Second Jhāna**: Devoid of *[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)* and *[vicāra](/glossary/vicara/)*; characterized by inner confidence, *[pīti](/glossary/piti/)*, *[sukha](/glossary/sukha/)*, and *[ekaggatā](/glossary/ekaggata/)*.
3. **Third Jhāna**: Devoid of *[pīti](/glossary/piti/)*; characterized by equanimity (*upekkhā*), mindfulness (*sati*), clear comprehension (*sampajañña*), *[sukha](/glossary/sukha/)*, and *[ekaggatā](/glossary/ekaggata/)*.
4. **Fourth Jhāna**: Devoid of *[sukha](/glossary/sukha/)*; characterized by pure equanimity, purified mindfulness, and *[ekaggatā](/glossary/ekaggata/)*.

Jhānas serve as an exceptionally powerful, purified mind-state that can then be turned toward insight meditation (*vipassanā*) to achieve the cutting of defilements.
