### Grammatical Analysis
**Jhāna**: [nt.] meditative absorption. The Visuddhimagga gives two explanations of the word: "*ārammaṇūpanijjhānato paccanīkajhāpanato vā jhānaṃ*" (it is *jhāna* because of closely contemplating the object, or because of burning up opposing states; Vism-i 963:2). The Atthasālinī divides it into two: *ārammaṇūpanijjhāna* (the eight attainments, which contemplate an object such as the earth kasiṇa) and *lakkhaṇūpanijjhāna* (insight, path and fruit; As 1420:1-3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Jhāna* is the fourfold absorption by which the Mūla defines Right Concentration (*[Sammāsamādhi](/glossary/sammasamadhi/)*, DN 22): "*Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi*" after the four jhānas have been described. The same four are listed in the Jhānavibhaṅga (Vibh 1797:1) and each is defined there by its factors (*jhānaṅga*).

The four jhānas by the formula of the suttas:
1. **First Jhāna**: secluded from sense desires and unwholesome states, with applied thought (*[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)*) and sustained thought (*[vicāra](/glossary/vicara/)*), and the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion (*vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ*).
2. **Second Jhāna**: with the stilling of *vitakka* and *vicāra*, inner clarity (*ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ*) and unification of mind (*cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ*), without *vitakka* and *vicāra*, with the rapture and pleasure born of concentration (*samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ*).
3. **Third Jhāna**: with the fading away of rapture, he dwells equanimous (*upekkhako*), mindful (*sato*) and clearly comprehending (*sampajāno*), and experiences happiness through the body.
4. **Fourth Jhāna**: with the abandoning of pleasure and pain and the earlier disappearance of gladness and sorrow, neither painful nor pleasant, with purity of mindfulness due to equanimity (*upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ*).

The Vibhaṅga counts the factors (*jhānaṅga*) of each jhāna in the fourfold system: the first is five-factored (*vitakko, vicāro, pīti, sukhaṃ, cittassekaggatā*; Vibh 1798:2), the second three-factored (*pīti, sukhaṃ, cittassekaggatā*; Vibh 1799:2), the third two-factored (*sukhaṃ, cittassekaggatā*; Vibh 1800:2), and the fourth two-factored (*upekkhā, cittassekaggatā*; Vibh 1801:2). The Vibhaṅga also has a fifth jhāna, two-factored (*upekkhā, cittassekaggatā*; Vibh 1807:1), which is the fivefold system, where the second jhāna of the sutta formula is split into two.

Jhāna is a basis for insight: the *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* speaks of one who has produced the jhānas and "developed insight with jhāna as its proximate cause" (*jhānapadaṭṭhānaṃ vipassanaṃ vaḍḍhetvā*, Vism-i 716:4).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati, sato ca sampajāno, sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti, yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti ‘ upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī ’ ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi.**  
> “Now what, monks, is right concentration? Here, monks, a monk, quite secluded from sense desires, secluded from unwholesome states, with applied and sustained thought, and the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, dwells having attained the first absorption. From the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal clarity, and unification of mind, being without applied and sustained thought, having the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, he dwells having attained the second absorption. With the fading away of rapture he dwells equanimous, mindful, clearly comprehending, experiencing happiness through the body, about which the noble ones announce: ‘The man indifferent and mindful is well at ease,’ he dwells having attained the third absorption. Having abandoned pleasure, abandoned pain, and by the previous dying away both of gladness and of sorrow, without ease, and without pain, and with complete purity of mindfulness owing to equanimity, he dwells having attained the fourth absorption. This, monks, is called right concentration.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, Sammāsamādhi) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=250&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYW1txIFzYW3EgWRoaT8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Cattāri jhānāni – paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ, dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ, tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ, catutthaṃ jhānaṃ. 624. Tattha katamaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ? Idha bhikkhu yasmiṃ samaye rūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti vivicceva kāmehi … pe … paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati pathavīkasiṇaṃ, tasmiṃ samaye pañcaṅgikaṃ jhānaṃ hoti – vitakko, vicāro, pīti, sukhaṃ, cittassekaggatā. Idaṃ vuccati “paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ”.**  
> 623. There are four absorptions: the first absorption, the second absorption, the third absorption, and the fourth absorption. 624. Among these, which is the first absorption? Here, when a bhikkhu develops the path for rebirth in the fine-material realm, quite secluded from sensual pleasures ... he enters and dwells in the first absorption, the earth kasiṇa. At that time, there is a five-factored absorption: applied thought, sustained thought, rapture, pleasure, and unification of mind. This is called “the first absorption”.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Jhānavibhaṅga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=274&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjIzLiBDYXR0xIFyaSBqaMSBbsSBbmkg4oCTIHBh4bmtaGFtYeG5gyBqaMSBbmHhuYMs)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Idāni paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ adhigataṃ hoti pathavīkasiṇanti ettha gaṇanānupubbatā paṭhamaṃ , paṭhamaṃ uppannantipi paṭhamaṃ. Ārammaṇūpanijjhānato paccanīkajhāpanato vā jhānaṃ.**  
> Now, as to the words “he has attained the first absorption... of the earth kasiṇa”: herein, the first (paṭhamaṃ) is so called because it starts a numerical series, and it is also the first because it is the first to arise. It is called absorption (jhānaṃ) because of closely contemplating the object or because of burning up opposing states.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. IV, Pathavīkasiṇaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=145&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=SWTEgW5pIHBh4bmtaGFtYeG5gyBqaMSBbmHhuYMgYWRoaWdhdGHhuYMgaG90aSBwYXRoYXbEq2thc2nhuYdhbnRp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Jhānanti duvidhaṃ jhānaṃ – ārammaṇūpanijjhānaṃ lakkhaṇūpanijjhānanti. Tattha aṭṭha samāpattiyo pathavikasiṇādiārammaṇaṃ upanijjhāyantīti ārammaṇūpanijjhānanti saṅkhyaṃ gatā. Vipassanāmaggaphalāni pana lakkhaṇūpanijjhānaṃ nāma.**  
> ‘Jhāna’ (jhānaṃ)—jhāna is twofold: meditation on the object (ārammaṇūpanijjhāna) and meditation on the characteristic (lakkhaṇūpanijjhāna). There, the eight attainments, meditating on an object like the earth kasiṇa and so on, are called ‘meditation on the object’ (ārammaṇūpanijjhāna). But insight, path, and fruit are called ‘meditation on the characteristic’ (lakkhaṇūpanijjhāna).  
>  
> — [Aṭṭhasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=211&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SmjEgW5hbnRpIGR1dmlkaGHhuYMgamjEgW5h4bmDIOKAkyDEgXJhbW1h4bmHxatwYW5pampoxIFuYeG5gyBsYWtraGHhuYfFq3BhbmlqamjEgW5hbnRpLg%3D%3D)
