### Grammatical Analysis
**Samatha**: [m.] tranquillity; calm. The Atthasālinī derives it: "it calms (*sameti*) the opposing states such as sensual desire", *kāmacchandādayo paccanīkadhamme sametīti samatho* (As 1256).

### Orthodox Definition
*Samatha* is paired with *vipassanā*: "these two things are conducive to knowledge (*vijjābhāgiyā*): *samatha* and *vipassanā*. When *samatha* is developed, what benefit does it have? The mind is developed. When the mind is developed, what benefit? Any lust is abandoned. When *vipassanā* is developed: wisdom is developed; when wisdom is developed, any ignorance is abandoned" (AN, Dukanipāta). "The mind defiled by lust is not liberated, and wisdom defiled by ignorance is not developed."

The Atthasālinī: *samatha* "calms the opposing states such as sensual desire", *vipassanā* "sees things in various ways, by way of impermanence and so on", and the latter "by meaning is wisdom" (As 1256). It says *samatha* is of three kinds: *cittasamatha*, *adhikaraṇasamatha* and *sabbasaṅkhārasamatha*; of these, "the one-pointedness of mind in the eight attainments is called *cittasamatha*; owing to it the movement and agitation of the mind subside" (As 1316). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī includes *samatho* among the synonyms of the unification of mind (Dhs 401).

The practice (*samathakammaṭṭhāna*) is summarised in the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha as seven groups: ten kasiṇas, ten kinds of foulness, ten recollections, four illimitables (*appamaññā*), one perception, one defining, four immaterial states (para. 2). Its signs develop from the preliminary sign through the learning sign to the counterpart sign (see *[Paṭibhāganimitta](/glossary/patibhaganimitta/)*), the object of access and absorption concentration (Abhidh-s 763).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Dve me, bhikkhave, dhammā vijjābhāgiyā. Katame dve? Samatho ca vipassanā ca. Samatho, bhikkhave, bhāvito kamattha manubhoti? Cittaṃ bhāvīyati. Cittaṃ bhāvitaṃ kamatthamanubhoti? Yo rāgo so pahīyati. Vipassanā, bhikkhave, bhāvitā kamatthamanubhoti? Paññā bhāvīyati. Paññā bhāvitā kamatthamanubhoti? Yā avijjā sā pahīyati. Rāgupakkiliṭṭhaṃ vā, bhikkhave, cittaṃ na vimuccati, avijjupakkiliṭṭhā vā paññā bhāvīyati.**  
> “Monks, these two mental phenomena are conducive to knowledge (vijjābhāgiyā). Which two? Tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā). Monks, when tranquility is developed, what benefit does it experience? The mind is developed. When the mind is developed, what benefit does it experience? Any lust (rāga) is abandoned. Monks, when insight is developed, what benefit does it experience? Wisdom is developed. When wisdom is developed, what benefit does it experience? Any ignorance is abandoned. Monks, the mind defiled by lust is not liberated, and wisdom defiled by ignorance is not developed.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (Dukanipāta, para. 32, Vijjābhāgiya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=62&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzIu4oCY4oCYIER2ZSBtZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBkaGFtbcSBIHZpamrEgWJoxIFnaXnEgS4%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti avaṭṭhiti avisāhāro avikkhepo avisāhaṭamānasatā samatho samādhindriyaṃ samādhibalaṃ sammāsamādhi – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti.**  
> 11. What is unification of mind at that time? Whatever stability of mind (cittassa ṭhiti), firm stability, established stability, non-scattering, non-distraction, non-scatteredness of mind, tranquility, faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, or right concentration exists at that time—this is unification of mind at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 11) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEuIEthdGFtxIEgdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGNpdHRhc3Nla2FnZ2F0xIEgaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Kāmacchandādayo paccanīkadhamme sametīti samatho. Aniccādivasena vividhehi ākārehi dhamme passatīti vipassanā. Paññāvesā atthato.**  
> ‘Calms counteracting states like sensual desire and so on’ is tranquility (samatho). ‘Sees qualities in various ways by way of impermanence and so on’ is insight (vipassanā). This insight, by meaning, is indeed wisdom.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 1256) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=175&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S8SBbWFjY2hhbmTEgWRheW8gcGFjY2FuxKtrYWRoYW1tZSBzYW1ldMSrdGkgc2FtYXRoby4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, three kinds)
> **Samathoti tividho samatho – cittasamatho, adhikaraṇasamatho, sabbasaṅkhārasamathoti. Tattha aṭṭhasu samāpattīsu cittekaggatā cittasamatho nāma. Tañhi āgamma cittacalanaṃ cittavipphanditaṃ sammati vūpasammati, tasmā so cittasamathoti vuccati.**  
> Tranquility (samatho) is threefold (tividho): tranquility of mind (cittasamatho), tranquility concerning legal questions (adhikaraṇāni) (`adhikaraṇasamatho`), and the stilling of all formations (sabbasaṅkhārasamatho). Among those, one-pointedness of mind (cittekaggatā) in the eight attainments (aṭṭhasu samāpattīsu) is called (nāma) tranquility of mind (cittasamatho). Indeed (hi), owing to (āgamma) that, the movement of mind (cittaṃ) and the agitation of mind subside (sammanti) and are stilled (vūpasammanti); therefore (tasmā), it is called (vuccati) tranquility of mind (cittasamatho).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 1316) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=188&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2FtYXRob3RpIHRpdmlkaG8gc2FtYXRobyDigJMgY2l0dGFzYW1hdGhvLCBhZGhpa2FyYeG5h2FzYW1hdGhvLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha)
> **Tattha samathasaṅgahe tāva dasa kasiṇāni, dasa asubhā, dasa anussatiyo, catasso appamaññāyo, ekā saññā, ekaṃ vavatthānaṃ, cattāro āruppā ceti sattavidhena samathakammaṭṭhānasaṅgaho.**  
> 2. Therein, in the Compendium of Serenity (samathasaṅgahe), first the compendium of serenity meditation subjects is sevenfold: ten kasiṇas, ten kinds of ugliness, ten recollections, four illimitables, one perception, one defining, and four immaterial states.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (para. 2) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=58&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=Mi4gVGF0dGhhIHNhbWF0aGFzYeG5hWdhaGUgdMSBdmEgZGFzYSBrYXNp4bmHxIFuaSw%3D)
