### Grammatical Analysis
**Aṅguttaranikāya**: [m.] The Collection of Numerical Discourses; The "Further-factored" Collection. Formed by *aṅga* (factor, part, item) + *uttara* (higher, further) + *nikāya* (collection). 

### Orthodox Definition
The *Aṅguttara-nikāya* is the fourth collection of the [Sutta-piṭaka](/glossary/sutta-pitaka/) (As 151:5-8), containing **9,557 discourses**, "spoken by way of the excess of one by one" (*ekekaaṅgātirekavasena kathitāni*, Sv-i 183:2), beginning with the Cittapariyādāna Sutta (As 151:8). It is structured purely by numerical progression, functioning essentially as the encyclopedia or index of the Buddha's teachings.

It begins with the "Book of the Ones" (lists of single items), proceeds to the "Book of the Twos", and progresses up to the "Book of the Elevens" (A-i 3:1 to A-xi 245:1). 

Because it deals heavily with lists, the *Aṅguttara* is incredibly broad. It contains many numbered lists on conduct and on meditation. At the First Council, after reciting the Aṅguttara Nikāya the elders entrusted it to the Elder Anuruddha, saying "teach this to those who rely on you" (Sv-i 106:1-2).

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Kesamutti Sutta* (the Kālāma Sutta, AN 3.65; heading "5. Kesamuttisuttaṃ", A-iii 587:1) – the famous discourse in the Book of the Threes on the criteria for accepting a teaching.
* **Canonical**: *Mettā Sutta* (AN 11.15; heading "5. Mettāsuttaṃ", A-xi 150:1; text at A-xi 151:1) – the eleven benefits of the liberation of the mind by loving-kindness.
* **Mūla**: the title "Aṅguttaranikāyo" heads each book (A-i 2:1 to A-xi 2:1), ending with "Aṅguttaranikāyo samatto" (A-xi 245:1).
* **Commentary**: *[Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/)* (Mp-i) – Buddhaghosa's commentary, which includes the Etadaggavagga section on the foremost disciples (heading "14. Etadaggavaggo", Mp-i 474:1); *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* (Sv-i 183:1-184:1) – the nine thousand five hundred and fifty-seven discourses.
