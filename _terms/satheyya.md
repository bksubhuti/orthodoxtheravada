### Grammatical Analysis
**Sāṭheyya**: [nt.] fraud; hypocrisy; craftiness; trickery. From *saṭha* (crafty, fraudulent): the Abhidhamma defines it by *saṭhaṃ saṭhatā sāṭheyyaṃ* (Vibh 2655:3; Pp 243:4).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sāṭheyya* is one of the sixteen imperfections that defile the mind (*cittassa upakkilesā*) taught in the Vatthasutta: *abhijjhāvisamalobho, byāpādo, kodho, upanāho, makkho, paḷāso, issā, macchariyaṃ, māyā, sāṭheyyaṃ, thambho, sārambho, māno, atimāno, mado, pamādo* (M-i 191:2). The bhikkhu who knows "fraud is an imperfection of the mind" abandons it (M-i 192:10).

The Abhidhamma explains it: "Here a person is crafty, utterly crafty (*saṭho hoti parisaṭho*): the craft, craftiness, fraud, coarseness, harshness, showiness connected with that, this is called *sāṭheyya*" (Vibh 2655:2-3; Pp 243:3-4). It is paired with *[māyā](/glossary/maya/)* in the Vibhaṅga's Dukaniddesa, "(4) Māyā ca sāṭheyyañca" (Vibh 2653:1).

The Papañcasūdanī on the Vatthasutta says: *vañcanikacariyabhūtā māyā* (deceit is deceitful conduct) and *kerāṭikabhāvena uppajjamānaṃ sāṭheyyaṃ* (fraud arises from being a hypocrite), illustrated by the "possession-fish" that shows its tail to fish and its head to snakes to say "I am like you" (Ps-i 851:4-7). Another passage of the Papañcasūdanī gives the characteristics: *māyā* has the characteristic of concealing one's committed evil (*katapāpapaṭicchādanalakkhaṇā*), *sāṭheyya* that of displaying virtues that do not exist (*attano avijjamānaguṇapakāsanalakkhaṇaṃ*), with the function of exhibiting them (Ps-i 562:1-2). The commentary adds that deceit and fraud, with contempt, insolence, envy and avarice, are abandoned by the stream-entry path (Ps-i 855:7).

### Quote (Mūla: Vatthasutta)
> **“Katame ca, bhikkhave, cittassa upakkilesā? Abhijjhāvisamalobho cittassa upakkileso, byāpādo cittassa upakkileso, kodho cittassa upakkileso, upanāho cittassa upakkileso, makkho cittassa upakkileso, paḷāso cittassa upakkileso, issā cittassa upakkileso, macchariyaṃ cittassa upakkileso, māyā cittassa upakkileso, sāṭheyyaṃ cittassa upakkileso, thambho cittassa upakkileso, sārambho cittassa upakkileso, māno cittassa upakkileso, atimāno cittassa upakkileso, mado cittassa upakkileso, pamādo cittassa upakkileso.**  
> “What, monks, are the imperfections that defile the mind? Covetousness and unrighteous greed is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Ill will is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Anger is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Resentment is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Contempt is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Insolence is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Envy is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Avarice is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Deceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Fraud is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Obstinacy is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Rivalry is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Conceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Over-conceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Intoxication is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Negligence is an imperfection that defiles the mind.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **(Kha) tattha katamaṃ sāṭheyyaṃ? Idhekacco saṭho hoti parisaṭho. Yaṃ tattha saṭhaṃ saṭhatā sāṭheyyaṃ kakkaratā kakkariyaṃ parikkhattatā pārikkhattiyaṃ – idaṃ vuccati “sāṭheyyaṃ”.**  
> (B) What is fraud (sāṭheyyaṃ) there? Here, someone is deceitful, utterly deceitful. Whatever deceitfulness, state of deceitfulness, fraud, coarseness / roughness, harshness, flamboyance, showiness is connected with that—this is called fraud (sāṭheyyaṃ).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Dukaniddesa, (4) Māyā ca sāṭheyyañca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=372&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=KEtoYSkgdGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyBzxIHhua1oZXl5YeG5gz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Vañcanikacariyabhūtā māyā. Kerāṭikabhāvena uppajjamānaṃ sāṭheyyaṃ. Kerāṭiko hi āyatanamaccho viya hoti. Āyatanamaccho nāma kira macchānaṃ naṅguṭṭhaṃ dasseti sappānaṃ sīsaṃ,“tumhehi sadiso ahan” ti jānāpetuṃ.**  
> Deceit is a mode of conduct that is fraudulent. Fraud is what arises from being a hypocrite. For a hypocrite is like a ‘possession-fish.’ The possession-fish, it is said, shows its tail to the fish and its head to the snakes, to make it known, ‘I am like you.’  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Ps-i 851, Vatthasuttavaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=174&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VmHDsWNhbmlrYWNhcml5YWJoxat0xIEgbcSBecSBLg%3D%3D)
