### Grammatical Analysis
**Āsevanapaccaya**: [m.] Repetition condition, a compound of *āsevana* (repeated practice, cultivation) + *paccaya* (condition). The Aṭṭhakathā gives its definition: *āsevanaṭṭhena anantarānaṃ paguṇabalavabhāvāya upakārako dhammo āsevanapaccayo*, "the state that is helpful, in the sense of repeated practice, to the proficiency and power of the states that follow immediately is the repetition condition", and compares it to the repeated application, earlier and earlier, to a book and the like (*ganthādīsu purimāpurimābhiyogo viya*) (Paṭṭh-a 1932:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Āsevana-paccaya* is the twelfth of the twenty-four conditions of the *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (Paṭṭh-i 45:1). The Mūla states it in three sentences: preceding wholesome states are related to subsequent wholesome states by repetition condition; likewise preceding unwholesome states to subsequent unwholesome states; and preceding functional indeterminate (*kiriyābyākata*) states to subsequent functional indeterminate states (Paṭṭh-i 45:1-3).

The Aṭṭhakathā says accordingly that it is of three kinds by way of wholesome, unwholesome and functional impulsion (*kusalākusalakiriyajavanavasena*), and repeats the Mūla statement (Paṭṭh-a 1932:2-4). The Ṭīkā adds that the words "extremely refined and extremely strong" (*paguṇatarabalavatarabhāvavisiṭṭhaṃ*) distinguish it from the indeterminate resultant states (*vipākābyākata*) (Paṭṭh-mt 1154:1): the Mūla's list contains no resultant states.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Āsevanapaccayoti – purimā purimā kusalā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ āsevanapaccayena paccayo. Purimā purimā akusalā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ āsevanapaccayena paccayo. Purimā purimā kiriyābyākatā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kiriyābyākatānaṃ dhammānaṃ āsevanapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 12. Repetition condition: Preceding wholesome states are related to subsequent wholesome states by repetition condition. Preceding unwholesome states are related to subsequent unwholesome states by repetition condition. Preceding functional indeterminate states are related to subsequent functional indeterminate states by repetition condition.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Āsevanaṭṭhena anantarānaṃ paguṇabalavabhāvāya upakārako dhammo āsevanapaccayo , ganthādīsu purimāpurimābhiyogo viya. So kusalākusalakiriyajavanavasena tividho hoti. Yathāha – purimā purimā kusalā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ āsevanapaccayena paccayo. Purimā purimā akusalā … pe … kiriyābyākatā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kiriyābyākatānaṃ dhammānaṃ āsevanapaccayena paccayoti.**  
> A phenomenon that helps in the sense of cultivation (āsevanaṭṭhena) for the purpose of the proficiency and power (paguṇabalavabhāvāya) of subsequent states (anantarānaṃ) is repetition condition (āsevanapaccayo), like the earlier and earlier applications (purimāpurimābhiyogo) in books and so on. It is of three kinds by way of wholesome, unwholesome, and functional impulsion (kusalākusalakiriyajavanavasena). Just as he said: ‘Earlier and earlier wholesome states (purimā purimā kusalā dhammā) are a condition for later and later wholesome states (pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ) by way of repetition condition (āsevanapaccayena paccayo).’ ‘Earlier and earlier unwholesome... and so on... functional indeterminate states are a condition for later and later functional indeterminate states by way of repetition condition.’  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā (Āsevanapaccayo) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=349&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=xIBzZXZhbmHhua3hua1oZW5hIGFuYW50YXLEgW5h4bmDIHBhZ3XhuYdhYmFsYXZhYmjEgXbEgXlhIHVwYWvEgXJha28gZGhhbW1vIMSBc2V2YW5hcGFjY2F5bw%3D%3D)
