### Grammatical Analysis
**Viññāṇañcāyatana**: [nt.] the dimension (base) of infinite consciousness. Compound of *viññāṇaṃ* + *ānañca* ("infinity", *anantameva ānañcaṃ*) + *āyatana*. The Visuddhimagga and Atthasālinī explain: "there is no end to it, hence *ananta*; *ananta* itself is *ānañca*"; one says *viññāṇañca* rather than *viññāṇānañca* by conventional usage (*rūḷhīsaddo*). *Āyatana* is the "base" in the sense of foundation (*adhiṭṭhānaṭṭhena*), so both the jhāna and its object can be called by the name.

### Orthodox Definition
*Viññāṇañcāyatana* is the second of the four immaterial attainments (*āruppa*: *ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ, viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ, ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ*). The Mūla formula (for example MN 111, of Sāriputta) is: "by completely surmounting the dimension of infinite space, aware that 'consciousness is infinite', he entered and dwelt in the dimension of infinite consciousness" (*sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma 'anantaṃ viññāṇan' ti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati*). In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī (§266) the same attainment is the wholesome state "accompanied by the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness" and is reckoned as the fourth jhāna.

The Visuddhimagga (Ch. X, Āruppaniddesa) gives the method. The meditator, having mastered the dimension of infinite space in five ways, sees its danger: it has the fine-material jhāna as its near enemy and "is not as peaceful as the dimension of infinite consciousness". Dropping attachment to it, he turns to the consciousness that pervaded the space (*ākāsaṃ pharitvā pavattaviññāṇaṃ*) and adverts to it again and again as "*viññāṇaṃ viññāṇaṃ*" (consciousness, consciousness); but he should *not* attend to it as "infinite, infinite" (*anantaṃ anantan ti pana na manasikātabbaṃ*). Then the hindrances are suppressed and the mind of the dimension of infinite consciousness reaches absorption.

The lifespan of the devas who have reached it is forty thousand eons (AN 3 ¶116; Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha §46).

### Quote
> **“Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, sāriputto sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma ‘ anantaṃ viññāṇan ’ ti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati. Ye ca viññāṇañcāyatane dhammā – viññāṇañcāyatanasaññā ca cittekaggatā ca, phasso vedanā saññā cetanā cittaṃ chando adhimokkho vīriyaṃ sati upekkhā manasikāro – tyāssa dhammā anupadavavatthitā honti.**  
> Furthermore, mendicants, by completely surmounting the dimension of infinite space, aware that ‘consciousness is infinite’, Sāriputta entered and remained in the dimension of infinite consciousness. And he distinguished the phenomena of the dimension of infinite consciousness one by one: the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness and one-pointedness of mind; contact, feeling, perception, volition, mind, enthusiasm, decision, energy, mindfulness, equanimity, and attention. These phenomena were distinguished by him one by one.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 111, Anupada Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=77&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFB1bmEgY2FwYXJh4bmDLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHPEgXJpcHV0dG8gc2FiYmFzbw%3D%3D)

> **Katame dhammā kusalā? Yasmiṃ samaye arūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāsahagataṃ sukhassa ca pahānā … pe … catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati upekkhāsahagataṃ, tasmiṃ samaye phasso hoti … pe … avikkhepo hoti … pe … ime dhammā kusalā.**  
> 266. What mental phenomena are wholesome? At the time when one develops the path for rebirth in the immaterial realm, by completely surmounting the dimension of infinite space, having attained the fourth jhāna accompanied by the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness, having abandoned pleasure… and dwells accompanied by equanimity. At that time contact exists… non-distraction exists… these mental phenomena are wholesome.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§266) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=68&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjY2LiBLYXRhbWUgZGhhbW3EgSBrdXNhbMSBPw%3D%3D)

> **Viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ bhāvetukāmena pana pañcahākārehi ākāsānañcāyatanasamāpattiyaṃ ciṇṇavasībhāvena “āsannarūpāvacarajjhānapaccatthikā ayaṃ samāpatti, no ca viññāṇañcāyatanamiva santā” ti ākāsānañcāyatane ādīnavaṃ disvā tattha nikantiṃ pariyādāya viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ santato manasikaritvā taṃ ākāsaṃ pharitvā pavattaviññāṇaṃ “viññāṇaṃ viññāṇan” ti punappunaṃ āvajjitabbaṃ, manasikātabbaṃ, paccavekkhitabbaṃ, takkāhataṃ vitakkāhataṃ kātabbaṃ. “Anantaṃ anantan” ti pana na manasikātabbaṃ.**  
> 281. But by one who wishes to develop the dimension of infinite consciousness, having achieved mastery in the five ways in the attainment of the dimension of infinite space, should see the danger in the dimension of infinite space in this way: ‘This attainment has fine-material-sphere jhāna as its near enemy, and it is not as peaceful as the dimension of infinite consciousness.’ Thus, having ended his attachment to that, and attending to the dimension of infinite consciousness as peaceful, he should advert to, pay attention to, review, and strike and hammer with thought and applied thought again and again, as ‘consciousness, consciousness’, that very consciousness which occurred by pervading that space. But he should not pay attention to it as ‘infinite, infinite’.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. X, Āruppaniddesa, §281) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=325&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MjgxLiBWacOxw7HEgeG5h2HDsWPEgXlhdGFuYeG5gyBiaMSBdmV0dWvEgW1lbmEgcGFuYSBwYcOxY2FoxIFrxIFyZWhpIMSBa8SBc8SBbmHDsWPEgXlhdGFuYXNhbcSBcGF0dGl5YeG5gw%3D%3D)

> **Viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāsahagatanti, ettha pana anantanti manasikātabbavasena nāssa antoti anantaṃ. Anantameva ānañcaṃ. Viññāṇaṃ ānañcaṃ viññāṇānañcanti avatvā viññāṇañcanti vuttaṃ.**  
> However, here, in the phrase "accompanied by the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness" (viññāṇañcāyatanasaññāsahagataṃ), "infinite" (anantaṃ) is because, by way of being an object of mental attention as "infinite," there is no limit to it. "Infinite" itself is "infinity" (ānañcaṃ). Without saying "consciousness-infinity" (viññāṇānañcaṃ), it was said "consciousness" (viññāṇañcaṃ).  
>  
> — Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā ([Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=250&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VmnDscOxxIHhuYdhw7FjxIF5YXRhbmFzYcOxw7HEgXNhaGFnYXRhbnRpLCBldHRoYSBwYW5hIGFuYW50YW50aSBtYW5hc2lrxIF0YWJiYXZhc2VuYSBuxIFzc2E%3D)
