### Grammatical Analysis
**Vippayuttapaccaya**: [m.] Dissociation condition. Formed by *vippayutta* (dissociated; *vi* + *payutta*, "yoked") + *paccaya* (condition). It is the counterpart of *sampayutta* (associated), the nineteenth condition.

### Orthodox Definition
*Vippayutta-paccaya* is the twentieth of the 24 conditions (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, 54:1).

The Mūla gives it in two lines: *rūpino dhammā arūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo; arūpino dhammā rūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo*, material phenomena are a condition for non-material phenomena, and non-material phenomena for material phenomena, by the dissociation condition (Paṭṭh-i 54:1-2). It is thus the mind-and-matter condition, and unlike the association condition (Paṭṭh-i 53:1) it does not apply among the formless aggregates alone.

The Aṭṭhakathā defines it by the absence of the four shared features of association: *ekavatthukādibhāvānupagamena upakārakā rūpino dhammā arūpīnaṃ, arūpino dhammā rūpīnaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo*, "material phenomena for formless, and formless for material, which help by not coming to a shared base and so on" (Paṭṭh-a 1940:1; Vism-ii 942:1, XVII 613). The association condition, by contrast, is formless phenomena helping by having one base, one object, one arising and one cessation (Paṭṭh-a 1939:1). The Aṭṭhakathā divides it into three, by way of co-nascence, post-nascence and pre-nascence (*sahajāta, pacchājāta, purejāta*; Paṭṭh-a 1940:2), with examples: co-nascent wholesome aggregates for mind-originated matter (Paṭṭh-a 1940:3), post-arisen wholesome aggregates for this earlier-arisen body (1940:4), the aggregates and the heart-base for one another at rebirth (1940:6), and the eye base and so on, pre-arisen, for the consciousnesses (1940:8).

The Ṭīkā says the physical base and the mind and mental factors, though joined by mutual connection, are dissociated because they are not blended (*visaṃsaṭṭhatāya*), having reached different natures (*nānattupagamanena*; Abhidh-s-t 1671:2).

The Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā uses butter taken from milk as an image for *vippayutta* phenomena (*tathā vippayuttāpi khīrato apanītaṃ navanītaṃ viya*), in contrast with the associated phenomena that are inseparable like milk and oil (Nidd-a-i 119:2-3). That is a comment on the words *sampayutto* and *vippayutto* in the Mahāniddesa, not on the Paṭṭhāna condition.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Vippayuttapaccayoti – rūpino dhammā arūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo. Arūpino dhammā rūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 20. Dissociation condition: material phenomena are related to non-material phenomena by dissociation condition. Non-material phenomena are related to material phenomena by dissociation condition.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Ekavatthukādibhāvānupagamena upakārakā rūpino dhammā arūpīnaṃ, arūpino dhammā rūpīnaṃ vippayuttapaccayena paccayo. So sahajātapacchājātapurejātavasena tividho hoti.**  
> Material phenomena (rūpino dhammā) for formless phenomena (arūpīnaṃ), and formless phenomena (arūpino dhammā) for material phenomena (rūpīnaṃ), which help by not following the state of having a single base and so on (ekavatthukādibhāvānupagamena), are a condition by dissociation condition (vippayuttapaccayena). It is of three kinds: by way of co-nascence (sahajāta), post-nascence (pacchājāta), and pre-nascence (purejāta).  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā (on the vippayuttapaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=351&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RWthdmF0dGh1a8SBZGliaMSBdsSBbnVwYWdhbWVuYSB1cGFrxIFyYWvEgSByxatwaW5vIGRoYW1txIEgYXLFq3DEq25h4bmDLCBhcsWrcGlubw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Ekavatthukādibhāvānupagamena upakārakā rūpino dhammā arūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ, arūpinopi rūpīnaṃ vippayuttapaccayo.**  
> 613. Material phenomena (rūpino dhammā) that assist formless phenomena (arūpīnaṃ dhammānaṃ), and formless phenomena that assist material phenomena, by not having a shared basis and so on, are the dissociation condition (vippayuttapaccayo).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, 613) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=171&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NjEzLiBFa2F2YXR0aHVrxIFkaWJoxIF2xIFudXBhZ2FtZW5hIHVwYWvEgXJha8SBIHLFq3Bpbm8gZGhhbW3EgSBhcsWrcMSrbmHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Aññamaññasambandhatāya yuttāpi samānā vippayuttabhāvena visaṃsaṭṭhatāya nānattupagamanena upakārakā vatthucittacetasikā vippayuttapaccayo.**  
> The physical basis and the mind and mental factors, which though similar in being joined by mutual connection are unassociated (vippayuttā) because they are not blended (visaṃsaṭṭhatāya) due to their having reached different natures, are the dissociation condition (vippayuttapaccayo).  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (on the vippayuttapaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=245&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QcOxw7FhbWHDscOxYXNhbWJhbmRoYXTEgXlhIHl1dHTEgXBpIHNhbcSBbsSBIHZpcHBheXV0dGFiaMSBdmVuYSB2aXNh4bmDc2Hhua3hua1oYXTEgXlhIG7EgW5hdHR1cGFnYW1hbmVuYQ%3D%3D)
