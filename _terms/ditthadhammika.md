### Grammatical Analysis
**Diṭṭhadhammika**: [adj.] belonging to this very life; of the here and now. Formed by *diṭṭhadhamma* + *ika*. The Paramatthadīpanī: "*diṭṭhadhammo* is the manifest individual existence (*paccakkhabhūto attabhāvo*); what exists in the *diṭṭhadhamma* is *diṭṭhadhammika*, meaning what is included in this world (*idhalokapariyāpanna*)" (It-a 358:6).

### Orthodox Definition
*Diṭṭhadhammika* is the counterpart of *samparāyika* ("belonging to the life to come"): the same commentary says *samparāya* is the other world, to be reached in the future by way of Dhamma, and what exists there is *samparāyika*, "included in the next world" (It-a 358:7).

In the Mūla the pair is used of faults: "there are two kinds of fault, a fault of this life (*diṭṭhadhammika*) and a fault of the next life (*samparāyika*)". A fault of this life is seen when one watches a robber whom kings arrest and torture in many ways, and thinks that if he did such a bad deed the kings would do the same to him, so that he restrains himself; a fault of the next life is considered when someone reflects that the result of bodily, verbal and mental misconduct is painful in the next life, and so abandons them (A-ii 8:1-4, 9:1-9:7, 10:1-3). It is also used of what is good for oneself: the brahmin Mahāgovinda considers whether to ask Brahmā Sanaṅkumāra "for something for the good in this life or for the good in the next" (*diṭṭhadhammikaṃ vā atthaṃ samparāyikaṃ vā*, D-ii 896:3).

In the Dīghajāṇu (Vyagghapajja) Sutta the layman asks for Dhamma that leads to welfare and happiness in this visible life and in the world beyond (*diṭṭhadhammahitāya diṭṭhadhammasukhāya, samparāyahitāya samparāyasukhāya*, A-viii 715:6), and the Buddha gives four things for the welfare of this life (*diṭṭhadhammahitāya*): accomplishment in effort (*uṭṭhānasampadā*), accomplishment in protection (*ārakkhasampadā*), good friendship (*kalyāṇamittatā*) and balanced livelihood (*samajīvitā*) (A-viii 716:1-2; 719:1); the text there uses the word *diṭṭhadhammahita*, not *diṭṭhadhammika*.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Dvemāni, bhikkhave, vajjāni. Katamāni dve? Diṭṭhadhammikañca vajjaṃ samparāyikañca vajjaṃ. Katamañca, bhikkhave, diṭṭhadhammikaṃ vajjaṃ? Idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, diṭṭhadhammikaṃ vajjaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these two kinds of faults. What are the two? Faults in this very life and faults in the next life. And what, monks, are faults in this very life? This, monks, is called a fault in this very life.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 2.1, Vajjasutta, A-ii 6) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=49&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIER2ZW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHZhamrEgW5pLg%3D%3D)


### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Kiṃ nu kho ahaṃ brahmānaṃ sanaṅkumāraṃ puccheyyaṃ diṭṭhadhammikaṃ vā atthaṃ samparāyikaṃ vā ’ ti?**  
> What indeed should I ask Brahmā Sanaṅkumāra—something for the good in this life, or something for the good in the next?”  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 19, Mahāgovinda Sutta, D-ii 807) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=193&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=S2nhuYMgbnUga2hvIGFoYeG5gyBicmFobcSBbmHhuYMgc2FuYeG5hWt1bcSBcmHhuYM%3D)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthadīpanī (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā))
> **Atthoti hitaṃ. Tañhi araṇīyato upagantabbato atthoti vuccati. Samadhigayha tiṭṭhatīti sammā pariggahetvā avijahitvā vattati. Diṭṭhadhammikanti diṭṭhadhammo vuccati paccakkhabhūto attabhāvo, diṭṭhadhamme bhavaṃ diṭṭhadhammikaṃ, idhalokapariyāpannanti attho. Samparāyikanti dhammavasena samparetabbato samparāyo, paraloko, samparāye bhavaṃ samparāyikaṃ, paralokapariyāpannanti vuttaṃ hoti.**  
> “Welfare” means benefit. Indeed, it is called ‘welfare’ because it is blameless and because it is to be approached. “Persists having secured” means it remains well-taken and not abandoned. “Here and now”: the manifest individual existence is called the ‘here and now’ (diṭṭhadhamma); what exists in the ‘here and now’ is ‘here and now’ (diṭṭhadhammika), meaning it is included in this world. “In the life to come”: the other world is called the ‘life to come’ (samparāya) because it is to be approached in the future by way of Dhamma; what exists in the ‘life to come’ is ‘in the life to come’ (samparāyika), meaning it is included in the next world.  
>  
> — [Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/) (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā, It-a 358) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=75&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXR0aG90aSBoaXRh4bmDLg%3D%3D)
