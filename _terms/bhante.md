### Grammatical Analysis
**Bhante**: [vocative] The Vinaya Aṭṭhakathā says: "*bhanteti sagāravasappatissavacanaṃ*", "*bhante* is a word of respect and deference" (Kkh 1234:3). The Dīgha Aṭṭhakathā shows the form in use: "*bhanteti vā āyasmāti vāti bhante tissa, āyasmā tissāti evaṃ samudācaritabbo*", "'*bhante* or *āyasmā*' means that he should be addressed as 'Bhante Tissa' or 'Āyasmā Tissa'" (Sv-ii 730:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Bhante* is the form of address a junior bhikkhu uses to a senior. The Buddha laid this down shortly before his parinibbāna: at present the bhikkhus address each other with *āvuso*, but after his passing they should not; a senior bhikkhu should address a junior by name, by clan name or by *āvuso*, and a junior bhikkhu should address a senior bhikkhu as *bhante* or *āyasmā* (D-ii 586:6-8). The Dīgha Aṭṭhakathā glosses *samudācarantīti kathenti voharanti*, "they address means they speak, they call" (Sv-ii 730:1). The word is also used toward the Buddha himself: "Evaṃ, bhante", said Ānanda in reply (D-ii 583:3).

In the Pātimokkha recitation, the Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī says, the reciter, if he is the senior of the Saṅgha, should say "āvuso", and if he is a junior, "bhante", according to the method that has come down in the Pāli (Kkh 1234:6-7).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Yo vo, ānanda, mayā dhammo ca vinayo ca desito paññatto, so vo mamaccayena satthā. Yathā kho panānanda, etarahi bhikkhū aññamaññaṃ āvusovādena samudācaranti, na kho mamaccayena evaṃ samudācaritabbaṃ. Theratarena, ānanda, bhikkhunā navakataro bhikkhu nāmena vā gottena vā āvusovādena vā samudācaritabbo. Navakatarena bhikkhunā therataro bhikkhu ‘ bhante ’ ti vā ‘ āyasmā ’ ti vā samudācaritabbo.**  
> Whatever Teaching and Discipline has been taught by me or laid down, Ānanda, that is your Teacher after my passing away. At present, Ānanda, the monks address each other with the word ‘friend’, but after my passing away they are not to address one another thus. The elder monk, Ānanda, should address the younger monk by his name or by his clan name or by the word ‘friend’. But the younger monk should address the elder monk as reverend Sir or venerable Sir.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 16, Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, D-ii 586, §216) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=126&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=WW8gdm8sIMSBbmFuZGEsIG1hecSBIGRoYW1tbyBjYQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Tattha samudācarantīti kathenti voharanti. Nāmena vā gottena vāti navakāti avatvā “tissa, nāgā” ti evaṃ nāmena vā,“kassapa, gotamā” ti evaṃ gottena vā,“āvuso tissa, āvuso kassapā” ti evaṃ āvusovādena vā samudācaritabbo. Bhanteti vā āyasmāti vāti bhante tissa, āyasmā tissāti evaṃ samudācaritabbo.**  
> There, “address” means they speak or call one another. “By name or by clan” means that, without saying ‘novice,’ one should address by name, such as ‘Tissa’ or ‘Nāga,’ or by clan name, such as ‘Kassapa’ or ‘Gotama,’ or by the word ‘friend,’ such as ‘Friend Tissa’ or ‘Friend Kassapa’. “As reverend Sir or venerable Sir” means he should be addressed as ‘Reverend Sir Tissa’ or ‘Venerable Sir Tissa’.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Mahāvagga-aṭṭhakathā, Sv-ii 730) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_02&page_number=184&name=mah%C4%81vagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHNhbXVkxIFjYXJhbnTEq3RpIGthdGhlbnRpIHZvaGFyYW50aS4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Bhanteti sagāravasappatissavacanaṃ. Bhagavatā hi pātimokkhuddesaṃ anujānantena rājagahe vuttaṃ, tasmā yo pātimokkhaṃ uddisati, tena sace saṅghatthero hoti,“āvuso” ti vattabbaṃ. Sace navakataro hoti, pāḷiyaṃ (mahāva.134) āgatanayeneva “bhante” ti vattabbaṃ.**  
> Bhante is a word of respect and veneration. For the Buddha spoke this in Rājagaha while granting the recitation of the Pātimokkha; therefore, whoever recites the Pātimokkha, if he is the senior monk of the Saṅgha, should say “āvuso.” If he is a junior, he should say “bhante,” according to the method come down in the Pāli.  
>  
> — Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī (Kkh 1234) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=85&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=QmhhbnRldGkgc2FnxIFyYXZhc2FwcGF0aXNzYXZhY2FuYeG5gy4%3D)
