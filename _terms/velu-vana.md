### Grammatical Analysis
**Veḷuvana**: [nt.] The Bamboo Grove. Formed by *veḷu* (bamboo) + *vana* (grove, wood). The Samantapāsādikā says: "Veḷuvana is the name of that park; it was surrounded by bamboo thickets and an eighteen-cubit wall, equipped with gate-towers and watchtowers, green and delightful, and for that reason it is called Veḷuvana" (Sp-i 2302:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Veḷuvana* was the park near Rājagaha in Magadha that King Seniya Bimbisāra gave to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus with the Buddha at its head. The Mahāvagga tells how the king thought: "This Bamboo Grove of ours, a pleasure park, is neither too far from a village nor too near, suitable for coming and going, ... quiet at night, suitable for seclusion", poured water from a golden vessel and gave it; the Blessed One accepted the monastery and then said: "I allow you, bhikkhus, a monastery (*ārāma*)" (Vin-iii 302:7-14, §59).

Its full name in the suttas is "Veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe", in the Bamboo Grove at the *kalandakanivāpa*, which the Aṭṭhakathā explain: "food was given there to the kalandakas (TPR: squirrels), therefore it is called the *kalandakanivāpa*" (Sp-i 2302:2; Ps-i 1976:8; Sv-iii 515:4). It is the setting of many suttas, for example the Piṇḍapātapārisuddhi Sutta (MN 151), in which Sāriputta, rising from seclusion, tells the Buddha that he now mostly dwells in emptiness (M-iii 1420-1421).

The Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā says that in the first twenty years the Buddha had no fixed residence (*anibaddhavāso*), living wherever it was pleasant, and lists the second rains retreat "relying on Rājagaha, in the Great Monastery in the Bamboo Grove", with the third and fourth in the same place (Bv-a 41:1-5). Sāriputta and Moggallāna, who had been living the holy life under the wanderer Sañcaya at Rājagaha (Vin-iii 305:2), went to the Blessed One (Vin-iii 329:1), and the Buddha foretold that they would be his chief pair of disciples (Vin-iii 328:1). The Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā adds that on that same day, the day of their going forth, the Teacher gathered the disciples at Veḷuvana, gave the two elders the office of chief disciples and recited the Pātimokkha (Dhp-a 350-352).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima)
> **Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā rājagahe viharati veḷuvane kalandakanivāpe.**  
> Thus have I heard — on one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Rājagaha in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrel sanctuary.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, the gift of the Bamboo Grove)
> **“idaṃ kho amhākaṃ veḷuvanaṃ uyyānaṃ gāmato neva avidūre na accāsanne gamanāgamanasampannaṃ atthikānaṃ atthikānaṃ manussānaṃ abhikkamanīyaṃ divā appākiṇṇaṃ rattiṃ appasaddaṃ appanigghosaṃ vijanavātaṃ manussarāhasseyyakaṃ paṭisallānasāruppaṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ veḷuvanaṃ uyyānaṃ buddhappamukhassa bhikkhusaṅghassa dadeyyan” ti. Atha kho rājā māgadho seniyo bimbisāro sovaṇṇamayaṃ bhiṅkāraṃ gahetvā bhagavato oṇojesi – “etāhaṃ, bhante, veḷuvanaṃ uyyānaṃ buddhappamukhassa bhikkhusaṅghassa dammī” ti. Paṭiggahesi bhagavā ārāmaṃ. Atha kho bhagavā rājānaṃ māgadhaṃ seniyaṃ bimbisāraṃ dhammiyā kathāya sandassetvā samādapetvā samuttejetvā sampahaṃsetvā uṭṭhāyāsanā pakkāmi. Atha kho bhagavā etasmiṃ nidāne etasmiṃ pakaraṇe dhammiṃ kathaṃ katvā bhikkhū āmantesi – “anujānāmi, bhikkhave, ārāman” ti.**  
> “This Bamboo Grove of ours, a pleasure park, is neither too far from a village nor too near, suitable for coming and going, accessible for people whenever they want, not disturbed by people during the day, quiet at night, still, free from the wind of passers-by, suitable for seclusion. What if I were to give the Bamboo Grove, a pleasure park, to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus with the Buddha at its head?” Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, having taken a ceremonial vessel made of gold, dedicated it to the Blessed One: “May I, Lord, give this Bamboo Grove, a pleasure park, to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus with the Buddha at its head?” The Blessed One accepted the park. The Blessed One accepted the monastery. Then the Blessed One, having instructed, encouraged, inspired, and gladdened King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha with a talk on the Dhamma, rose from his seat and departed. Then the Blessed One, on this occasion, in this connection, having given a talk on the Dhamma, addressed the bhikkhus: “I allow you, bhikkhus, a monastery.”  
>  
> — *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* (Mahākhandhaka, §59, Bimbisārasamāgamakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=49&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIGlkYeG5gyBraG8gYW1oxIFrYeG5gyB2ZeG4t3V2YW5h4bmDIHV5ecSBbmHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā, the names)
> **Tattha veḷuvane kalandakanivāpeti veḷuvananti tassa uyyānassa nāmaṃ, taṃ kira veḷuhi ca parikkhittaṃ ahosi aṭṭhārasahatthena ca pākārena gopuraṭṭālakayuttaṃ nīlobhāsaṃ manoramaṃ tena “veḷuvanan” ti vuccati, kalandakānañcettha nivāpaṃ adaṃsu tena “kalandakanivāpa” ti vuccati.**  
> There, in the Bamboo Grove, the squirrels' feeding ground: 'Bamboo Grove' is the name of that park. It is said that it was surrounded by bamboo thickets and an eighteen-cubit wall, was equipped with gate-towers and watchtowers, and was green and delightful; for that reason it is called 'Bamboo Grove.' And since food was given there to the squirrels, for that reason it is called 'the squirrels' feeding ground'.  
>  
> — *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (on the Duṭṭhadosa rule, §380) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_02&page_number=161&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHZl4bi3dXZhbmUga2FsYW5kYWthbml2xIFwZXRpIHZl4bi3dXZhbmFudGkgdGFzc2EgdXl5xIFuYXNzYQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā, the rains)
> **Paṭhamaṃ vassaṃ isipatane dhammacakkaṃ (saṃ. ni.5.1081; mahāva.13 ādayo; paṭi. ma.2.30) pavattetvā aṭṭhārasa brahmakoṭiyo amatapānaṃ pāyetvā bārāṇasiṃ upanissāya isipatane migadāye vasi. Dutiyaṃ vassaṃ rājagahaṃ upanissāya veḷuvane mahāvihāre. Tatiyacatutthāni pi tattheva.**  
> In the first rainy season, having set in motion the Wheel of the Dhamma and made eighteen crores of Brahmās drink the deathless draught, he dwelt in the Deer Park at Isipatana, relying on Benares. In the second rainy season, relying on Rājagaha, in the Great Monastery in the Bamboo Grove. The third and fourth also, right there.  
>  
> — *Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_12&page_number=4&name=buddhava%E1%B9%83saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UGHhua1oYW1h4bmDIHZhc3Nh4bmDIGlzaXBhdGFuZSBkaGFtbWFjYWtrYeG5gyAoc2HhuYMuIG5pLjUuMTA4MTs%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā, the chief disciples)
> **Taṃ divasaññeva pana satthā vaḍḍhamānakacchāyāya veḷuvane sāvakasannipātaṃ katvā dvinnaṃ therānaṃ aggasāvakaṭṭhānaṃ datvā pātimokkhaṃ uddisi.**  
> Then, on that very day, as the shadows lengthened, the Teacher gathered the disciples at Veḷuvana, assigned the office of chief disciples to the two elders, and recited the Pātimokkha.
