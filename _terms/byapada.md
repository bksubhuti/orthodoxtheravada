### Grammatical Analysis
**Byāpāda**: [m.] ill will. The Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā gives the derivation: "*hitasukhaṃ byāpādayatīti byāpādo*", "ill will is that which causes harm to welfare and happiness" (Nidd-a-i 875:3). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī's definition runs through a string of synonyms: *āghāto, paṭighāto, paṭigho, kopo, doso, padoso, byāpatti, byāpajjanā, virodho* and so on, "this is called ill will" (Dhs 1923:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Byāpāda* is the second of the hindrances: the Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists "*kāmacchandanīvaraṇaṃ, byāpādanīvaraṇaṃ, thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ, uddhaccakukkuccanīvaraṇaṃ, vicikicchānīvaraṇaṃ, avijjānīvaraṇaṃ*" (Dhs 1943:2, six hindrances, of which the first five are the usual five). In the Aṅguttara the Buddha says he sees no other single thing leading to the arising of unarisen ill will, or the increase of arisen ill will, as much as the sign of irritation (*paṭighanimitta*), when given careless attention (A-i 19:1-2).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī describes the bodily knot of ill will (*byāpādo kāyagantho*) as the annoyance that arises thinking "he acted, acts, or will act in a way that is not worthwhile for me", or for someone pleasing to me, or that is worthwhile for someone displeasing to me, or without reason (Dhs 1923:2). The Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā says that the bodily knot of ill will is abandoned by the Anāgāmī path (Nidd-a-i 877:1). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī gives six things that lead to the abandonment of ill will: grasping the sign of loving-kindness, devotion to the cultivation of loving-kindness, reflection on the ownership of kamma, frequent reflection (*paṭisaṅkhāna*), good friendship, and suitable talk (Sv-ii 1560:1-7). One who has abandoned ill will by these six things understands that it does not arise again by the Anāgāmī path (Sv-ii 1560:8).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Nāhaṃ, bhikkhave, aññaṃ ekadhammampi samanupassāmi yena anuppanno vā byāpādo uppajjati uppanno vā byāpādo bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattati yathayidaṃ, bhikkhave, paṭighanimittaṃ. Paṭighanimittaṃ, bhikkhave, ayoniso manasi karoto anuppanno ceva byāpādo uppajjati uppanno ca byāpādo bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya saṃvattatī” ti.**  
> “Monks, I do not see any other single thing that leads to the arising of unarisen ill will, or to the multiplication and extension of arisen ill will, as much as the sign of irritation. For one who gives careless attention to the sign of irritation, unarisen ill will arises and arisen ill will leads to its multiplication and extension.”  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamo byāpādo kāyagantho? Anatthaṃ me acarīti āghāto jāyati, anatthaṃ me caratīti āghāto jāyati, anatthaṃ me carissatīti āghāto jāyati, piyassa me manāpassa anatthaṃ acari … pe … anatthaṃ carati … pe … anatthaṃ carissatīti āghāto jāyati, appiyassa me amanāpassa atthaṃ acari … pe … atthaṃ carati … pe … atthaṃ carissatīti āghāto jāyati, aṭṭhāne vā pana āghāto jāyati. Yo evarūpo cittassa āghāto paṭighāto paṭighaṃ paṭivirodho kopo pakopo sampakopo doso padoso sampadoso cittassa byāpatti manopadoso kodho kujjhanā kujjhitattaṃ doso dussanā dussitattaṃ byāpatti byāpajjanā byāpajjitattaṃ virodho paṭivirodho caṇḍikkaṃ asuropo anattamanatā cittassa – ayaṃ vuccati byāpādo kāyagantho.**  
> 1142. Which among these is the bodily knot of ill will? Annoyance arises, thinking: “He acted in a way that was not worthwhile for me,” annoyance arises, thinking: “He acts in a way that is not worthwhile for me,” annoyance arises, thinking: “He will act in a way that is not worthwhile for me”; annoyance arises, thinking: “He acted...or acts...or will act in a way that was not worthwhile for someone pleasing and agreeable to me”; annoyance arises, thinking: “He acted...or acts...or will act in a way that was worthwhile for someone displeasing and disagreeable to me”; or indeed, annoyance arises without a reason. Whatever such vexation of mind, hostility, resentment, recurrent hostility, anger, strong anger, intense anger, hatred, corruption, intense corruption, the mind's impairment, mental corruption, anger, the act of anger, the state of being angry, hatred, the act of corrupting, the state of being corrupted, vexation, the act of vexing, the state of being vexed, hostility, recurrent hostility, fierceness, bad speech, disgruntlement of mind there is — this is called the bodily knot of ill will.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs 1923, §1142) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=229&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTE0Mi4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyBiecSBcMSBZG8ga8SBeWFnYW50aG8%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Hitasukhaṃ byāpādayatīti byāpādo. Byāpādo ca so vuttanayena gantho cāti byāpādo kāyagantho .**  
> “Ill will”—that which causes harm. And since ill will is a knot by the way mentioned, it is the “bodily knot of ill will.”  
>  
> — Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā (Nidd-a-i 875) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_16&page_number=204&name=mah%C4%81niddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SGl0YXN1a2hh4bmDIGJ5xIFwxIFkYXlhdMSrdGkgYnnEgXDEgWRvLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Apica cha dhammā byāpādassa pahānāya saṃvattanti mettānimittassa uggaho mettābhāvanānuyogo kammassakatāpaccavekkhaṇā paṭisaṅkhānabahulatā kalyāṇamittatā sappāyakathāti. Odissakaanodissakadisāpharaṇānañhi aññataravasena mettaṃ uggaṇhantassāpi byāpādo pahīyati, odhisoanodhisopharaṇavasena mettaṃ bhāventassāpi. “Tvaṃ etassa kuddho kiṃ karissasi, kimassa sīlādīni vināsetuṃ sakkhissasi, nanu tvaṃ attano kammena āgantvā attano kammeneva gamissasi, parassa kujjhanaṃ nāma vītaccitaṅgāra tattaaya salākagūthādīni gahetvā paraṃ paharitukāmatāsadisaṃ hoti. Esopi tava kuddho kiṃ karissati, kiṃ te sīlādīni vināsetuṃ sakkhissati, esa attano kammena āgantvā attano kammeneva gamissati, appaṭicchitapaheṇakaṃ viya paṭivātaṃ khittarajomuṭṭhi viya ca etassevesa kodho matthake patissatī” ti evaṃ attano ca parassa ca kammassakataṃ paccavekkhatopi, ubhayakammassakataṃ paccavekkhitvā paṭisaṅkhāne ṭhitassāpi, assaguttattherasadise mettābhāvanārate kalyāṇamitte sevantassāpi byāpādo pahīyati. Ṭhānanisajjādīsu mettānissitasappāyakathāyapi pahīyati. Tena vuttaṃ – “cha dhammā byāpādassa pahānāya saṃvattantī” ti. Imehi pana chahi dhammehi pahīnassa byāpādassa anāgāmimaggena āyatiṃ anuppādo hotīti pajānāti.**  
> Furthermore, six qualities conduce to the abandonment of ill will: grasping the sign of loving-kindness, devotion to meditation on loving-kindness, reflection on ownership of actions, frequent consideration, good friendship, and relevant discussion. Ill will is abandoned for one who grasps loving-kindness by way of one of the types of direction-spreading, specified or unspecified, and for one who meditates by spreading loving-kindness in a limited or unlimited way. “What can you do, angry at him? Can you destroy his virtue? Don't you come by your own action and go by your own action? Anger towards another is like one wishing to strike someone else but grabbing hold of hot coals, a hot iron rod, or excrement.” “What can he do, angry at you? Can he destroy your virtue? He comes by his own action and goes by his own action. His anger will fall on his own head, like an unclaimed gift or like a handful of dust thrown against the wind.” Ill will is abandoned for one who reflects on ownership of actions for both oneself and another, for one who is settled in the consideration of both, and for one who associates with good friends devoted to meditation on loving-kindness, like the Elder Assagutta. It is also abandoned through relevant discussion based on loving-kindness in postures like standing and sitting. Therefore it was said: “Six qualities conduce to the abandonment of ill will.” He discerns that for one whose ill will has been abandoned by these six qualities, there is no further arising in the future by the path of the Non-returner.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Mahāvagga-aṭṭhakathā, Sv-ii 1560) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_02&page_number=369&name=mah%C4%81vagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBpY2EgY2hhIGRoYW1txIEgYnnEgXDEgWRhc3NhIHBhaMSBbsSBeWEgc2HhuYN2YXR0YW50aQ%3D%3D)
