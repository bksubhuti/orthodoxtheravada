### Grammatical Analysis
**Cetiyagiri**: [m.] the Cetiya mountain. Formed by *[cetiya](/glossary/cetiya/)* (shrine, monument) + *giri* (mountain). The same place is called *Cetiyapabbata* (Mhv 3809, 3821, 3896). The name does not occur in the Mūla (`count`: Mūla 0, Aṭṭhakathā 20, Ṭīkā 13, Añña 7).

### Orthodox Definition
*Cetiyagiri* is the mountain and monastery in Tambapaṇṇidīpa (Laṅkā) where the Elder Mahinda and his companions spent their first rains retreat. The Samantapāsādikā relates that, when the king had been established in the three refuges, the elders went to Cetiyagiri; the king had the cetiya courtyard enclosed with a thorn fence, began work on sixty-two caves, and the elders spent the rains at Cetiyagiri, where sixty-two arahants kept their first rains retreat (Sp-i 433:17, 434:6, 435:1-3). The Elder Mahinda's walkway (*caṅkama*) at the Cetiyagiri monastery is mentioned as the model of a suitable walkway (Ap-a 115:9). In the Mahāvaṃsa the chapter on the acceptance of the monastery is named *Cetiyapabbatavihārapaṭiggahako* (the sixteenth chapter, Mhv 3867-3868).

The first meeting of the king with Mahinda took place on a different mountain, Missakapabbata, where Devānampiyatissa had gone to hunt and where the Elder called out to him (Sp-i 397:2-8); there the Elder taught him the Cūḷahatthipadopamasutta (Sp-i 427:20) and the king took refuge (Sp-i 427:21). The texts I checked (Sp-i, Mhv) do not call this mountain Cetiyagiri.

The Ṭīkā uses the name for the place of the great offering (*giribhaṇḍamahāpūjā*): at Cetiyagiri, throughout the whole island of Laṅkā and in the sea for a yojana's extent, flowers and perfumes were offered (Sv-pt-ii 443:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā (Vinaya commentary))
> **Rājāpi kho taṅkhaṇeyeva kaṇṭakena cetiyaṅgaṇaṃ parikkhipitvā dvāsaṭṭhiyā leṇesu kammaṃ paṭṭhapetvā nagarameva agamāsi. Tepi therā dasabhātikasamākulaṃ rājakulaṃ pasādetvā mahājanaṃ ovadamānā cetiyagirimhi vassaṃ vasiṃsu. Tadāpi cetiyagirimhi paṭhamaṃ vassaṃ upagatā dvāsaṭṭhi arahanto ahesuṃ.**  
> The King, at that very moment, had the Cetiya courtyard enclosed with a thorn fence, commenced construction work on sixty-two caves, and returned to the city. Those Elders, having made the royal family, composed of ten brothers, confident, and admonishing the multitude, spent the rains retreat at Cetiyagiri. At that time, there were sixty-two arahants who had spent their first rains retreat at Cetiyagiri.  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Mahāvaggaṭīkā (Dīgha Nikāya subcommentary))
> **Giribhaṇḍamahāpūjāyāti cetiyagirimhi sakalalaṅkādīpe, yojanappamāṇe samudde ca nāvāsaṅghāṭādike ṭhapetvā dīpapupphagandhādīhi kariyamānamahāpūjāyaṃ.**  
> 'Giribhaṇḍa offering' refers to a great offering made on Cetiyagiri, by placing from the whole of Laṅkā and the ocean for a yojana, excluding ships and rafts, and by making offerings of lamps, flowers, and incense.  
>
