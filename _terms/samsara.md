### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṃsāra**: [m.] perpetual wandering; the cycle of rebirth existence. Derived from prefix *saṃ* (together) + root *sar* (to run, move, flow). The commentaries gloss it as *saṃsaraṇaṃ saṃsāro*, "wandering on is saṃsāra" (Mp-t-iv 1061:13; Bv-a 1010:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Saṃsāra* is the beginningless round of rebirth. The Buddha says: *anamataggoyaṃ, bhikkhave, saṃsāro* ("this saṃsāra has no known beginning"), and no first point is discovered for beings hindered by ignorance (*avijjā*) and fettered by craving (*taṇhā*) who run on and transmigrate (S-ii 869:6-7).

The commentaries define it as the uninterrupted occurrence of the succession of aggregates, elements and sense bases: *khandhānañca paṭipāṭi, dhātuāyatanāna ca; abbocchinnaṃ vattamānā, saṃsāroti pavuccati* (As 99:1-100:1; Vism-ii 975:1). The Aṭṭhakathā also explains it as the wandering hither and thither, by way of kamma and defilements, among the modes of birth, destinations, stations of consciousness and abodes of beings (*kammakilesavasena … ito cito ca saṃsaraṇaṃ saṃsāro*, Bv-a 1010:1).

It is not a place but this continuing process; it is to be wearied of (*nibbindituṃ*), turned away from (*virajjituṃ*) and freed from (*vimuccituṃ*) in all conditioned things (*sabbasaṅkhāresu*, S-ii 869:9).

### Quote (Mūla: Tiṇakaṭṭha Sutta)
> **Anamataggoyaṃ, bhikkhave, saṃsāro. Pubbā koṭi na paññāyati avijjānīvaraṇānaṃ sattānaṃ taṇhāsaṃyojanānaṃ sandhāvataṃ saṃsarataṃ.**  
> Monks, this saṃsāra has no known beginning. No past term is recognized of living beings, hindered by ignorance and fettered by craving, who are running on and transmigrating.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 15.1, Tiṇakaṭṭha Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=387&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QW5hbWF0YWdnb3lh4bmDLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHNh4bmDc8SBcm8u)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Khandhānañca paṭipāṭi, dhātuāyatanāna ca; Abbocchinnaṃ vattamānā, saṃsāroti pavuccatīti.**  
> The succession of aggregates, elements, and sense bases, Proceeding without interruption, is called saṃsāra.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 98-100, saṃsārasāgara) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=11&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2hhbmRoxIFuYcOxY2EgcGHhua1pcMSB4bmtaSwgZGjEgXR1xIF5YXRhbsSBbmEgY2E7)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Tiṇakaṭṭha Sutta* (SN 15.1, S-ii 867:1, 869:6-7) – the saṃsāra has no known beginning. *Assu Sutta* (SN 15.3, S-ii 872:1, 874:2) – the tears shed while running on and transmigrating are more than the water in the four great oceans.
* **Canonical**: *[Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)* (Dhp 153, Dhp 499:1-500:1) – *anekajātisaṃsāraṃ sandhāvissaṃ anibbisaṃ, gahakāraṃ gavesanto*, the Buddha's verse of victory; v. 154 (Dhp 502:1, *gahakāraka diṭṭhosi*) follows.
