### Grammatical Analysis
**Ārammaṇapaccaya**: [m.] Object condition, a tappurisa-type compound of *ārammaṇa* (object) + *paccaya* (condition). The Ṭīkā glosses it "*ārammaṇañca taṃ paccayo cāti ārammaṇapaccayo, ārammaṇaṃ hutvā paccayo, ārammaṇabhāvena paccayoti attho*", "it is an object and it is a condition, hence object condition; being an object it is a condition, that is, a condition by way of being an object" (Vism-mht-ii 880:1). The Aṭṭhakathā gives the definition *ārammaṇavasena upakārako dhammo ārammaṇapaccayo*, "the state that is helpful by way of being an object is the object condition" (Paṭṭh-a 1920:1). For *ārammaṇa* itself the Ṭīkā offers the derivations "*ālambīyati*" (it is leaned upon) and "*tāni vā āgantvā ettha ramantīti*" (they come and delight in it) (Abhidh-s-t 1155:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Ārammaṇa-paccaya* is the second of the twenty-four conditions listed in the *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, Paṭṭh-i 11:1). The Mūla defines it first by an example, "visible object-base is a condition by way of object condition for the eye-consciousness element and its associated states" (Paṭṭh-i 11:1), and then in general: "whichever states consciousness and mental factors (*cittacetasikā dhammā*) arise referring to, those states are related by object condition to those arising states" (Paṭṭh-i 12:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā explains that, because the Mūla begins with the visible object-base and ends with "whichever state", there is no state that cannot be an object condition. It adds the simile of the weak person: just as a weak person rises and stands only by leaning on a staff or a rope, so consciousness and mental factors arise and stand only by leaning on an object such as visible form (Paṭṭh-a 1920:2-3). Hence "all states that have become objects of consciousness and mental factors should be understood as the object condition" (Paṭṭh-a 1920:4). Nibbāna is included: the Mūla gives it as an object condition for the path and fruition (Paṭṭh-i 3895:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Ārammaṇapaccayoti – rūpāyatanaṃ cakkhuviññāṇadhātuyā taṃsampayuttakānañca dhammānaṃ ārammaṇapaccayena paccayo. Yaṃ yaṃ dhammaṃ ārabbha ye ye dhammā uppajjanti cittacetasikā dhammā, te te dhammā tesaṃ tesaṃ dhammānaṃ ārammaṇapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 2. Object condition: Visible object-base is related to eye-consciousness-element and its associated states by object condition. Referring to any state, those states that arise—consciousness and mental factors—the former states are related to the latter states by object condition.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tato paresu ārammaṇavasena upakārako dhammo ārammaṇapaccayo. So “rūpāyatanaṃ cakkhuviññāṇadhātuyā” ti ārabhitvāpi “yaṃ yaṃ dhammaṃ ārabbha ye ye dhammā uppajjanti cittacetasikā dhammā, te te dhammā tesaṃ tesaṃ dhammānaṃ ārammaṇapaccayena paccayo” ti osāpitattā na koci dhammo na hoti. Yathā hi dubbalo puriso daṇḍaṃ vā rajjuṃ vā ālambitvāva uṭṭhahati ceva tiṭṭhati ca, evaṃ cittacetasikā dhammā rūpādiārammaṇaṃ ārabbheva uppajjanti ceva tiṭṭhanti ca. Tasmā sabbepi cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ ārammaṇabhūtā dhammā ārammaṇapaccayoti veditabbā.**  
> Then, among the subsequent (conditions), the phenomenon that is helpful by way of object is the object condition. Beginning with 'the form sense base (is a condition) for the eye-consciousness element', and because it was concluded with 'whichever phenomena consciousness and its concomitants arise focusing on whichever phenomena, those phenomena are a condition for those phenomena by way of object condition', there is no phenomenon that is not (a condition). Just as indeed a weak person rises and remains only by relying on a staff or a rope, so too consciousness and its concomitants arise and remain only based on an object of form and so on. Therefore, all phenomena that have become objects for mental phenomena should be understood as object condition.  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā (Ārammaṇapaccayo) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=345&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0byBwYXJlc3UgxIFyYW1tYeG5h2F2YXNlbmEgdXBha8SBcmFrbyBkaGFtbW8gxIFyYW1tYeG5h2FwYWNjYXlvLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha-ṭīkā)
> **Vaṇṇavikāraṃ āpajjamānaṃ rūpayati hadayaṅgatabhāvaṃ pakāsetīti rūpaṃ, tadeva dubbalapurisena daṇḍādi viya cittacetasikehi ālambīyati, tāni vā āgantvā ettha ramantīti ārammaṇanti rūpārammaṇaṃ.**  
> It is called form (rūpaṃ) because it undergoes changes in color and manifests the state that has entered the heart; that very thing is leaned upon by consciousness and mental factors like a weak man leaning on a staff and so on, or because they come and delight here—thus it is an object (ārammaṇa); hence, it is a visual object (rūpārammaṇaṃ).  
>
