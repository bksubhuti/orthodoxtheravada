### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭivedha**: [m.] Penetration; breakthrough. From *paṭivijjhati* (to penetrate), from *paṭi* + *vidh*. The Aṭṭhakathā glosses *abhisamaya* (convergence) with "*paṭivedhoti attho*" (Paṭis-a 2426), and *paṭivedha* in the three disappearances as "*saccapaṭivedho*", the penetration of the truths (Ps-iii 313; Vibh-a 1939).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paṭivedha* is the penetration of the four noble truths. The Buddha says that for lack of understanding and penetration (*ananubodhā appaṭivedhā*) of the four truths he and the monks wandered on for a long time (DN 16; SN 56.21). The Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, on the same phrase in DN 15, explains *ananubodhā* as not knowing by way of full understanding through knowing (*ñātapariññā*), and *appaṭivedhā* as not penetrating by way of the full understanding of investigation and abandoning (*tīraṇa-pahāna-pariññā*) (Sv-ii 336).

Paṭivedha is the third of the three disappearances (*pariyatti*, *paṭivedha*, *paṭipatti*): "*pariyattīti tīṇi piṭakāni, paṭivedhoti saccapaṭivedho, paṭipattīti paṭipadā*" (Ps-iii 313). In the threefold true Dhamma it is the *adhigamasaddhamma*: the four noble paths, the four fruits of the recluse's life and Nibbāna (Sp-i 932).

The Papañcasūdanī says that at the moment of the path, "he penetrates the four truths by a single penetration (*ekapaṭivedhena*)": suffering by the penetration of full understanding (*pariññāpaṭivedha*), the origin by that of abandoning, cessation by that of realisation, and the path by that of development (Ps-i 421). The Saddhammappakāsinī gives the simile of a boat that in one moment abandons the near shore, cuts the stream, carries its cargo and reaches the far shore: so path knowledge breaks through the four truths at once (Paṭis-a 505).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Catunnaṃ, bhikkhave, ariyasaccānaṃ ananubodhā appaṭivedhā evamidaṃ dīghamaddhānaṃ sandhāvitaṃ saṃsaritaṃ mamañceva tumhākañca.**  
> “Because of a lack of understanding (ananubodha) and a lack of penetration (appaṭivedha), monks, of Four Noble Truths both you and I have been wandering and running along (sandhāvitaṃ saṃsaritaṃ) in Saṁsāra for a long time.  
>

> “Bhikkhus, it is because of not understanding and not penetrating the Four Noble Truths that you and I have roamed and wandered for such a long stretch of time.”

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Kathaṃ sabbadhammānaṃ ekasaṅgahatānānattekattapaṭivedhe paññā dassanavisuddhiñāṇaṃ?**  
> 91. How is it that understanding of penetrating the includability of all ideas as one, and of their difference and unity, is knowledge of purity in seeing (dassanavisuddhiñāṇaṃ)?  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā, the threefold true Dhamma)
> **Saddhammaṭṭhitiyāti tividho saddhammo – pariyattisaddhammo, paṭipattisaddhammo, adhigamasaddhammoti. Tattha piṭakattayasaṅgahitaṃ sabbampi buddhavacanaṃ “pariyattisaddhammo” nāma. Terasa dhutaguṇā, cuddasa khandhakavattāni, dveasīti mahāvattāni, sīlasamādhivipassanāti ayaṃ “paṭipattisaddhammo” nāma. Cattāro ariyamaggā cattāri ca sāmaññaphalāni nibbānañcāti ayaṃ “adhigamasaddhammo” nāma.**  
> For the continuation of the true Dhamma (saddhammaṭṭhitiyā) means: the true Dhamma is threefold—the true Dhamma of scripture, the true Dhamma of practice, and the true Dhamma of attainment. Therein, the entire word of the Buddha contained within the three Pitakas is called the “true Dhamma of scripture.” The thirteen ascetic practices, the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, virtue, concentration, and insight are called the “true Dhamma of practice.” The four noble paths, the four fruits of the life of a recluse, and Nibbāna are called the “true Dhamma of attainment.”  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Pārājikakaṇḍa-aṭṭhakathā, Sp-i 932) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=190&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FkZGhhbW1h4bmt4bmtaGl0aXnEgXRpIHRpdmlkaG8gc2FkZGhhbW1vIOKAkyBwYXJpeWF0dGlzYWRkaGFtbW8sIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc2FkZGhhbW1vLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī, the three disappearances)
> **Tīṇi hi antaradhānāni nāma pariyattiantaradhānaṃ, paṭivedhaantaradhānaṃ, paṭipattiantaradhānanti. Tattha pariyattīti tīṇi piṭakāni. Paṭivedhoti saccapaṭivedho. Paṭipattīti paṭipadā.**  
> ‘There are indeed three disappearances,’ namely, the disappearance of learning, the disappearance of penetration, and the disappearance of practice. There, ‘learning’ is the three baskets. ‘Penetration’ is the penetration of the truths. ‘Practice’ is the path.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Uparipaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā, Ps-iii 313) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_03&page_number=80&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VMSr4bmHaSBoaSBhbnRhcmFkaMSBbsSBbmkgbsSBbWEgcGFyaXlhdHRpYW50YXJhZGjEgW5h4bmDLCBwYeG5rWl2ZWRoYWFudGFyYWRoxIFuYeG5gyw%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Ananubodhāti ñātapariññāvasena ananubujjhanā. Appaṭivedhāti tīraṇappahānapariññāvasena appaṭivijjhanā.**  
> ‘By not understanding’ means not knowing by way of comprehension through knowing. ‘By not penetrating’ means not penetrating by way of comprehension through investigating and abandoning.  
>  
> — Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā (Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, on DN 15) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_02&page_number=85&name=mah%C4%81vagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QW5hbnVib2RoxIF0aSDDscSBdGFwYXJpw7HDscSBdmFzZW5hIGFuYW51YnVqamhhbsSBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī, penetration of the four truths)
> **Tasmiṃ khaṇe cattāri saccāni ekapaṭivedheneva paṭivijjhati, ekābhisamayena abhisameti. Dukkhaṃ pariññāpaṭivedhena paṭivijjhati, samudayaṃ pahānapaṭivedhena, nirodhaṃ sacchikiriyāpaṭivedhena, maggaṃ bhāvanāpaṭivedhena.**  
> In that moment, he penetrates the four truths by a single penetration, he breaks through by a single realization. He penetrates suffering by the penetration of full comprehension, the origin by the penetration of abandonment, cessation by the penetration of realization, the path by the penetration of development.  
>  
> — [Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/) (Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā, Ps-i 421) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=76&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzbWnhuYMga2hh4bmHZSBjYXR0xIFyaSBzYWNjxIFuaSBla2FwYeG5rWl2ZWRoZW5ldmEgcGHhua1pdmlqamhhdGks)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Saddhammappakāsinī, the boat simile)
> **Yathā nāvā apubbaṃ acarimaṃ ekakkhaṇe cattāri kiccāni karoti, orimaṃ tīraṃ pajahati, sotaṃ chindati, bhaṇḍaṃ vahati, pārimaṃ tīraṃ appeti, evamevaṃ maggañāṇaṃ apubbaṃ acarimaṃ ekakkhaṇe cattāri saccāni abhisameti, dukkhaṃ pariññābhisamayena abhisameti, samudayaṃ pahānābhisamayena abhisameti, maggaṃ bhāvanābhisamayena abhisameti, nirodhaṃ sacchikiriyābhisamayena abhisameti.**  
> Just as a boat performs four tasks at the same time—it abandons the near shore, cuts the stream, carries the cargo, and fixes to the further shore—so too, the path knowledge breaks through the four truths at the same time, without priority or subsequence: it breaks through suffering by the penetration of full understanding; it breaks through its origin by the penetration of abandonment; it breaks through the path by the penetration of development; it breaks through its cessation by the penetration of realization.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/saddhammappakasini/) (Saddhammappakāsinī, Paṭis-a 505) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_18_01&page_number=114&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=WWF0aMSBIG7EgXbEgSBhcHViYmHhuYMgYWNhcmltYeG5gyBla2Fra2hh4bmHZSBjYXR0xIFyaQ%3D%3D)
