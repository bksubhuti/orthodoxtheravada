### Grammatical Analysis
**Sīlānussati**: [f.] recollection of virtue; mindfulness of one's own morality. Formed by *[sīla](/glossary/sila/)* (moral conduct/virtue) + *anussati* (recollection).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sīlānussati* is the recollection of one's own virtue, the fourth of the ten recollections (Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, sīla, ...; Vism-i 1186:1 opens "these ten recollections"; A-vi 35:3 gives the six: buddhānussati, dhammānussati, saṅghānussati, sīlānussati, cāgānussati, devatānussati). In the Mūla the noble disciple "recollects his own virtues" (*attano sīlāni anussarati*) as *akhaṇḍāni acchiddāni asabalāni akammāsāni bhujissāni viññuppasatthāni aparāmaṭṭhāni samādhisaṃvattanikāni* (unbroken, unpierced, unspotted, unblemished, liberating, praised by the wise, untarnished, conducive to concentration), and at that time his mind is not obsessed by greed, hatred or delusion but is straight, focused on virtue (A-vi 41:1-3).

The Visuddhimagga explains the practice and each term: one who wishes to develop it goes into solitude and recollects his own virtues by way of their special qualities of being unbroken and so forth (Vism-i 1356:1). Virtues are *akhaṇḍa* when, whether a householder's or one gone forth, not a single rule is broken at the beginning or the end, "not damaged like a garment torn at the edge"; *acchidda* when none is broken in the middle; *asabala* when two or three in succession are not broken, "not spotted like a cow" (Vism-i 1357:1-3). Because of the profundity of the qualities of virtue, or because the mind is intent on recollecting qualities of many kinds, the jhāna is only access (*upacārappattameva jhānaṃ*), not absorption (Vism-i 1358:4). Benefits: the bhikkhu has reverence for the training, sees danger in the slightest faults, acquires fullness of faith and has much happiness and gladness (Vism-i 1359:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahānāma Sutta)
> **“Puna caparaṃ, mahānāma, ariyasāvako attano sīlāni anussarati akhaṇḍāni acchiddāni asabalāni akammāsāni bhujissāni viññuppasatthāni aparāmaṭṭhāni samādhisaṃvattanikāni. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako sīlaṃ anussarati nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti sīlaṃ ārabbha.**  
> “Furthermore, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects their own virtues, which are unbroken, unpierced, unspotted, unblemished, liberating, praised by the wise, untarnished, and conducive to concentration. Mahānāma, at the time when a noble disciple recollects their virtue, at that time their mind is not obsessed by greed, not obsessed by hate, not obsessed by delusion; at that time their mind is simply straight, focused on virtue.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 6.10, Mahānāma Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_06&page_number=253&name=chakkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFB1bmEgY2FwYXJh4bmDLCBtYWjEgW7EgW1hLCBhcml5YXPEgXZha28gYXR0YW5v)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Sīlānussatiṃ bhāvetukāmena pana rahogatena paṭisallīnena “aho vata me sīlāni akhaṇḍāni acchiddāni asabalāni akammāsāni bhujissāni viññuppasatthāni aparāmaṭṭhāni samādhisaṃvattanikānī” ti (a. ni.6.10) evaṃ akhaṇḍatādiguṇavasena attano sīlāni anussaritabbāni.**  
> 158. One who wishes to develop the recollection of virtue, having gone into solitude and retreated, should recollect his own virtues by way of their special qualities of being unbroken and so forth, in this way: 'Oh, indeed, my virtues are unbroken, unpierced, unspotted, unblemished, liberating, praised by the wise, untarnished, and conducive to concentration!'  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-i 1356, Sīlānussati) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=215&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MTU4LiBTxKtsxIFudXNzYXRp4bmDIGJoxIF2ZXR1a8SBbWVuYSBwYW5hIHJhaG9nYXRlbmEgcGHhua1pc2FsbMSrbmVuYQ%3D%3D)

* **Canonical**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2, D-i 217:1, "2. Sāmaññaphalasuttaṃ") – the monk endowed with the noble aggregate of virtue experiences within himself a blameless happiness (*ajjhattaṃ anavajjasukhaṃ paṭisaṃvedeti*, D-i 308:4).
