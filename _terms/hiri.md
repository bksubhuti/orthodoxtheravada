### Grammatical Analysis
**Hiri**: [f.] moral shame; conscience. The Visuddhimagga derives it from the verb: "one is disgusted by bodily misconduct and so on, thus it is *hirī* (*kāyaduccaritādīhi hiriyatīti hirī*)"; this is a name for shame (*lajjā*) (Vism-ii 438:1-2; also As 1220:2-3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Hiri* is the moral shame that holds a person back from evil. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as that which causes one to feel shame at what should be felt shame towards, and to feel shame at the acquisition of bad, unwholesome states (Dhs 428:2). It is one of the beautiful mental factors (*[sobhana-cetasika](/glossary/sobhana-cetasika/)*), listed with *ottappa* among the nineteen beautiful universals that arise in every beautiful consciousness (Abhidh-s 90:1).

The Visuddhimagga gives its marks in the standard pattern: its characteristic is disgust at evil (*pāpato jigucchanalakkhaṇā*); its function is not doing evil in the manner of shame (*lajjākārena pāpānaṃ akaraṇarasā*); it is manifested as shrinking from evil (*pāpato saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānā*); and its proximate cause is respect for oneself (*attagārava*; for *ottappa* it is respect for others, *paragārava*). "By respecting oneself, one abandons evil through *hirī*, like a high-born lady; by respecting others, one abandons evil through *ottappa*, like a courtesan" (Vism-ii 438:5-11).

*Hiri* and its twin *[ottappa](/glossary/ottappa/)* are the two bright states that protect the world (AN 2.9). If they did not, no one would recognize a mother, a mother's sister, an aunt, a teacher's wife or the wives of revered ones, and the world would fall into promiscuity (A-ii 28:4-5). The Visuddhimagga says these two are to be seen as the guardians of the world (*lokapālakā*, Vism-ii 438:12).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, AN 2.9)
> **“Dveme, bhikkhave, dhammā sukkā lokaṃ pālenti. Katame dve? Hirī ca ottappañca. Ime kho, bhikkhave, dve sukkā dhammā lokaṃ na pāleyyuṃ, nayidha paññāyetha mātāti vā mātucchāti vā mātulānīti vā ācariyabhariyāti vā garūnaṃ dārāti vā.**  
> “Monks, these two bright qualities protect the world. What are the two? Shame and moral dread. If these two bright qualities, monks, did not protect the world, then here no one would recognize a mother, or a mother’s sister, or an aunt, or a teacher’s wife, or the wives of revered ones.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye hirī hoti? Yaṃ tasmiṃ samaye hirīyati hiriyitabbena hirīyati pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ samāpattiyā – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye hirī hoti.**  
> 38. What is moral shame at that time? Whatever causes one to be conscientious regarding what should be felt shame towards, and causes one to be conscientious regarding the acquisition of bad, unwholesome states at that time—this is moral shame at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Hirī, §38) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=23&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzguIEthdGFtxIEgdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGhpcsSrIGhvdGk%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Kāyaduccaritādīhi hiriyatīti hirī. Lajjāyetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tehiyeva ottappatīti ottappaṃ. Pāpato ubbegassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tattha pāpato jigucchanalakkhaṇā hirī. Uttāsanalakkhaṇaṃ ottappaṃ. Lajjākārena pāpānaṃ akaraṇarasā hirī. Uttāsākārena ottappaṃ. Vuttappakāreneva ca pāpato saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānā etā, attagāravaparagāravapadaṭṭhānā. Attānaṃ garuṃ katvā hiriyā pāpaṃ jahāti kulavadhū viya. Paraṃ garuṃ katvā ottappena pāpaṃ jahāti vesiyā viya. Ime ca pana dve dhammā lokapālakāti (a. ni.2.9) daṭṭhabbā.**  
> 466. One is disgusted by bodily misconduct and so on, thus it is moral shame (hirī). This is a name for shame. One dreads those same things, thus it is moral dread (ottappa). This is a name for the agitation caused by evil. Therein, moral shame has the characteristic of being disgusted by evil. Moral dread has the characteristic of dread. Moral shame has the function of not doing evil in the manner of shame. Moral dread in the manner of dread. In the very manner stated, they are manifested as shrinking away from evil, and they have respect for oneself and respect for others as their proximate causes. By respecting oneself, one abandons evil through moral shame, like a high-born lady. By respecting others, one abandons evil through moral dread, like a courtesan. Furthermore, these two things should be seen as the guardians of the world.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Saṅkhārakkhandhakathā, §466) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=94&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDY2LiBLxIF5YWR1Y2Nhcml0xIFkxKtoaSBoaXJpeWF0xKt0aSBoaXLEqy4%3D)
