### Grammatical Analysis
**Manokamma** (*manokammaṃ*): [nt.] Mental action, action of the mind. A compound of *mano* (mind) + *kamma* (action, deed). It is the third of the three kammas, after *[kāyakamma](/glossary/kayakamma/)* and *[vacīkamma](/glossary/vacikamma/)*. The Atthasālinī, in the Manokammadvārakathā, says the mind (*mano*) is of four kinds, by way of the sense sphere and so on.

### Orthodox Definition
*Manokamma* is the third kamma (MN 56: *kāyakammaṃ, vacīkammaṃ, manokammaṃ*). The Atthasālinī (Kammakathā) asks what kamma is and answers with the Buddha's words: *cetanāhaṃ, bhikkhave, kammaṃ vadāmi, cetayitvā kammaṃ karoti kāyena vācāya manasā* ("Monks, I say that volition is kamma. Having intended, one makes kamma by body, speech and mind").

The three unwholesome mental courses of action (*manasā asoceyyaṃ*), in the Cunda Sutta (AN 10.176):
1. **Abhijjhālu** – one who is covetous.
2. **Byāpannacitto** – one with a mind of ill will.
3. **[Micchādiṭṭhiko](/glossary/micchaditthi/)** – one who holds wrong view.

The three wholesome mental courses are *anabhijjhālu*, *abyāpannacitto* and *sammādiṭṭhiko* (AN 10.176).

The Atthasālinī says: *tividhaṃ manoduccaritaṃ akusalaṃ manokammameva hoti* (threefold mental misconduct is solely unwholesome mental action). It also says that unrestraint arising through the five sense-contact doors is solely unwholesome mental action, while unrestraint arising through the mind-contact door can become all three actions: unwholesome bodily or verbal action if it reaches agitation at the body or speech door, and unwholesome mental action if it does not reach agitation in either.

### Quote (Mūla: MN 56)
> **“Tīṇi kho ahaṃ, tapassi, kammāni paññapemi pāpassa kammassa kiriyāya pāpassa kammassa pavattiyā, seyyathidaṃ – kāyakammaṃ, vacīkammaṃ, manokamman” ti.**  
> “Verily, Tapassī, there are three modes of ‘action’ I declare, in doing and perpetrating evil deeds, namely: ‘action’ of body, of word, and of mind.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: AN 10.176)
> **“Kathañca, cunda, tividhaṃ manasā asoceyyaṃ hoti? Idha, cunda, ekacco abhijjhālu hoti. “Byāpannacitto hoti paduṭṭhamanasaṅkappo – “Micchādiṭṭhiko hoti viparītadassano – “Ime kho, cunda, dasa akusalakammapathā.**  
> “And how, Cunda, is one made impure in three ways by mental action? There is the case where a certain person is covetous. “He has a mind affected by ill will, with a corrupted mental intention: “He holds wrong view, with a distorted perspective: “These, Cunda, are the ten courses of unwholesome action.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā kusalā? Tīṇi kusalamūlāni – alobho, adoso, amoho; taṃsampayutto vedanākkhandho, saññākkhandho, saṅkhārakkhandho, viññāṇakkhandho; taṃsamuṭṭhānaṃ kāyakammaṃ, vacīkammaṃ, manokammaṃ – ime dhammā kusalā.**  
> 985. What mental phenomena are wholesome? Three wholesome roots – non-greed, non-hatred, non-delusion; the feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the formations aggregate, the consciousness aggregate, associated with them; and bodily actions, verbal actions, mental actions, arisen from them – these mental phenomena are wholesome.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Tikanikkhepa, Kusalatika §985) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=205&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTg1LiBLYXRhbWUgZGhhbW3EgSBrdXNhbMSBPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, Kammakathā)
> **Tīṇi hi kammāni – kāyakammaṃ vacīkammaṃ manokammanti. “Cetanāhaṃ, bhikkhave, kammaṃ vadāmi, cetayitvā kammaṃ karoti kāyena vācāya manasā”(a. ni.6.63; kathā.539).**  
> Indeed, there are three karmas: bodily karma, verbal karma, and mental karma. ‘Monks, I say that volition is kamma. Having intended, one makes kamma by way of body, speech, and mind.’  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Kammakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=130&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VMSr4bmHaSBoaSBrYW1txIFuaSDigJMga8SBeWFrYW1tYeG5gyB2YWPEq2thbW1h4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, Kammapathasaṃsandana)
> **Tividhaṃ kāyaduccaritaṃ akusalaṃ kāyakammameva hoti, catubbidhaṃ vacīduccaritaṃ akusalaṃ vacīkammameva hoti, tividhaṃ manoduccaritaṃ akusalaṃ manokammameva hoti.**  
> Threefold bodily misconduct is solely unwholesome bodily action; fourfold verbal misconduct is solely unwholesome verbal action; threefold mental misconduct is solely unwholesome mental action.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Upāli Sutta* (MN 56) – the Buddha tells Dīghatapassī that he declares three kammas: *kāyakammaṃ, vacīkammaṃ, manokammaṃ*.
* **Sutta**: *Cunda Sutta* (AN 10.176) – the ten unwholesome and ten wholesome courses of action, arranged as three by body, four by speech, three by mind.
* **Sutta**: *Sāleyyaka Sutta* (MN 41) – the same threefold, fourfold and threefold division, given as *adhammacariyā* and *dhammacariyā*.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Tikanikkhepa, Kusalatika §985) – *kāyakammaṃ, vacīkammaṃ, manokammaṃ* arisen from the wholesome roots.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* – Kāyakammadvārakathā, Vacīkammadvārakathā, Manokammadvārakathā and Kammakathā.
