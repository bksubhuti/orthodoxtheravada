### Grammatical Analysis
**Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā**: [f.] the explanation of the meaning of the Jātaka. Formed by *[jātaka](/glossary/jataka/)* (birth story) + *attha* (meaning) + *vaṇṇanā* (explanation). The text's own words for it are "*jātakassa atthavaṇṇanā*" and "*jātakassatthavaṇṇanaṃ*" (Ja-a-i 25:1, 29:1); in the other commentaries and in the Ṭīkās it is referred to as the *jātakaṭṭhakathā* (for example Ud-a 438:6, Ap-a 748:4, Cp-a 43:4, Vism-nid 551:1). The name *jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā* itself is not found in the TPR texts.

### Orthodox Definition
The *Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā* is the Aṭṭhakathā on the Jātaka. In its opening verses the author says that the Jātaka was chanted by the compilers of the Dhamma, and that, desiring the long endurance of the Buddha-lineage (*buddhavaṃsa*), he was asked by the Elder Atthadassī, by Buddhamitta of the Mahiṃsāsaka lineage and by Buddhadeva, "to illuminate the inconceivable power of the practices of the Great Being", and he says "I shall expound the commentary on that Jātaka (*jātakassatthavaṇṇanaṃ*), relying on the method of teaching (*vācanāmagga*) of the dwellers in the Mahāvihāra" (Ja-a-i 17:1-27:1).

It begins with the Nidānakathā (Ja-a-i 28), which it defines in three parts: the Remote Introduction (*dūrenidāna*), from the Great Being's aspiration at the feet of Dīpaṅkara until his rebirth in Tusita after passing away as Vessantara; the Not-Too-Remote Introduction (*avidūrenidāna*), from his passing away from Tusita until omniscience at the Bodhi-maṇḍa; and the Immediate Introduction (*santikenidāna*), obtained at each place where the Buddha dwelt (Ja-a-i 29:1, 30:2-4). In the individual stories it gives the story of the present (*paccuppannavatthu*) and the story of the past (*atītavatthu*) (for example Ja-a-i 1057:1-2).

On authorship, the Visuddhimagga-nidānakathā (a Ṭīkā) says: "They also say that the Jātaka commentary was composed by the Elder Ācariya Buddhaghosa, but no reason for this is seen here; rather it was composed relying on the path of recitation of the dwellers in the Mahāvihāra, at the request of the three elders Atthadassī, Buddhamitta (of the Mahiṃsāsaka school) and Buddhadeva" (Vism-nid 551:1-2).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Jātaka-aṭṭhakathā (opening verses))
> **Mahāpurisacariyānaṃ, ānubhāvaṃ acintiyaṃ; Tassa vijjotayantassa, jātakassatthavaṇṇanaṃ. Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ, vācanāmagganissitaṃ; Bhāsissaṃ bhāsato taṃ me, sādhu gaṇhantu sādhavoti.**  
> To illuminate the inconceivable power of the Great Man's practice, I shall expound the commentary on that Jātaka. Relying upon the method of teaching of the dwellers of the Mahāvihāra, I shall speak; may the virtuous ones receive it well.  
>  
> — [Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā](/glossary/jatakatthavannana/) (Jātaka-aṭṭhakathā, opening verses, Ja-a-i 17-27) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_zat_01&page_number=2&name=j%C4%81takaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=TWFoxIFwdXJpc2FjYXJpecSBbmHhuYMsIMSBbnViaMSBdmHhuYMgYWNpbnRpeWHhuYM7)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Jātaka-aṭṭhakathā (Nidānakathā))
> **Sā panāyaṃ jātakassa atthavaṇṇanā dūrenidānaṃ, avidūrenidānaṃ, santikenidānanti imāni tīṇi nidānāni dassetvā vaṇṇiyamānā ye naṃ suṇanti, tehi samudāgamato paṭṭhāya viññātattā yasmā suṭṭhu viññātā nāma hoti, tasmā taṃ tāni nidānāni dassetvā vaṇṇayissāma. Tattha ādito tāva tesaṃ nidānānaṃ paricchedo veditabbo. Dīpaṅkarapādamūlasmiñhi katābhinīhārassa mahāsattassa yāva vessantarattabhāvā cavitvā tusitapure nibbatti, tāva pavatto kathāmaggo dūrenidānaṃ nāma. Tusitabhavanato pana cavitvā yāva bodhimaṇḍe sabbaññutappatti, tāva pavatto kathāmaggo avidūrenidānaṃ nāma. Santikenidānaṃ pana tesu tesu ṭhānesu viharato tasmiṃ tasmiṃyeva ṭhāne labbhatīti.**  
> Now, this commentary on the Jātaka, being expounded after showing these three categories of introduction—the distant introduction, the intermediate introduction, and the immediate introduction—is called 'well-understood' because those who hear it know it well from its origin. Therefore, having shown those introductions, I shall expound the commentary. There, first of all, the division of those introductions should be understood. For the path of the narrative that proceeds from the Great Being's aspiration at the feet of Dīpaṅkara until his rebirth in the Tusita heaven after passing away from the existence as Vessantara is called the distant introduction. The path of the narrative that proceeds from his passing away from the Tusita heaven until his attainment of omniscience at the Bodhi-tree is called the intermediate introduction. The immediate introduction is found in each of those places where the Blessed One dwells.  
>  
> — [Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā](/glossary/jatakatthavannana/) (Jātaka-aṭṭhakathā, Nidānakathā, Ja-a-i 28-30) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_zat_01&page_number=2&name=j%C4%81takaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U8SBIHBhbsSBeWHhuYMgasSBdGFrYXNzYSBhdHRoYXZh4bmH4bmHYW7EgSBkxatyZW5pZMSBbmHhuYMsIGF2aWTFq3JlbmlkxIFuYeG5gyw%3D)


### Quote (Ṭīkā: Visuddhimagga-nidānakathā)
> **Jātakaṭṭhakathāpi ca ācariyabuddhaghosatthereneva katāti vadanti, kāraṇaṃ panettha na dissati. Sā pana atthadassittherena ca buddhamittattherena ca mahisāsakanikāyikena ca buddhadevattherenāti tīhi therehi abhiyācito mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vācanāmaggaṃ nissāya katā. Imissāpi nāmaviseso natthi.**  
> They also say that the Jātaka commentary was composed by the Elder Ācariya Buddhaghosa, but no evidence for this is seen here. Rather, it was composed by the Elder Atthadassin, the Elder Buddhamitta, who belonged to the Mahīśāsaka school, and the Elder Buddhadeva—those three elders requested, and it was composed relying on the path of recitation of the residents of the Great Monastery. There is also no special name for this.  
>  
> — Visuddhimagga-nidānakathā (Vism-nid 550-551, Jātakaṭṭhakathākaraṇaṃ)
