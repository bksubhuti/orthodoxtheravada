### Grammatical Analysis
**Piṇḍapāta**: [m.] Alms food; the dropping of lumps (of food). Formed by *piṇḍa* (lump, morsel) + *pāta* (dropping, falling). The Visuddhimagga explains it two ways: any food is called *piṇḍapāta* "because it has been dropped into a bhikkhu's bowl through his alms-gathering" (*piṇḍolyena patte patitattā*), or *piṇḍānaṃ pāto*, "the gathering of alms obtained here and there" (Vism-i 192).

### Orthodox Definition
*Piṇḍapāta* is almsfood, the second of the four resources (*nissaya*) explained at ordination: "going forth is on account of meals of scraps (*piṇḍiyālopabhojanaṃ nissāya pabbajjā*); in this respect effort is to be made by you for life" (Mahāvagga). The food is "the falling of lumps given by others into the bowl" (Vism-i 426).

The Mūla (MN 2) teaches that a monk takes almsfood only after wise reflection: "not for sport, not for indulgence, not for adornment, not for beautification, but only for the maintenance and sustenance of this body". The Visuddhimagga gives the going-for-alms conduct: he goes "with downcast eyes" (*okkhittacakkhu*, Vism-i 150), and lists the benefits of the almsfood-eater's practice (*piṇḍapātikaṅga*) as an independent livelihood, a purified livelihood, the abandoning of pride, "the prevention of craving for tastes" (*rasataṇhānivāraṇaṃ*), compassion for others and fulfilment of the Sekhiya training (Vism-i 471). In that practice fourteen kinds of invited meals (for the Saṅgha, by invitation, and so on) are not to be accepted (Vism-i 468).

Going on alms round is thus not only for physical sustenance but a training in humility and contentment, and it gives lay people the opportunity to make merit.

### Quote
> **‘‘Paṭisaṅkhā yoniso piṇḍapātaṃ paṭisevati – ‘neva davāya, na madāya, na maṇḍanāya, na vibhūsanāya, yāvadeva imassa kāyassa ṭhitiyā yāpanāya, vihiṃsūparatiyā, brahmacariyānuggahāya, iti purāṇañca vedanaṃ paṭihaṅkhāmi navañca vedanaṃ na uppādessāmi, yātrā ca me bhavissati anavajjatā ca phāsuvihāro ca’.**  
> ‘‘Reflecting wisely, he partakes of almsfood—not for sport, not for indulgence, not for adornment, not for beautification, but only for the maintenance and sustenance of this body, for the cessation of harm, for the furtherance of the holy life, reflecting: ‘Thus I shall ward off old painful feelings and shall not arouse new painful feelings, and there will be for me subsistence, blamelessness, and a comfortable abiding.’’’
> 
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 2, Sabbāsava Sutta)

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, the four resources)
> **“anujānāmi, bhikkhave, upasampādentena cattāro nissaye ācikkhituṃ – piṇḍiyālopabhojanaṃ nissāya pabbajjā, tattha te yāvajīvaṃ ussāho karaṇīyo;**  
> “Monks, I allow you, when you are ordaining, to explain four resources: that going forth is on account of meals of scraps; in this respect effort is to be made by you for life.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga I)
> **Piṇḍapātanti yaṃkiñci āhāraṃ. Yo hi koci āhāro bhikkhuno piṇḍolyena patte patitattā piṇḍapātoti vuccati. Piṇḍānaṃ vā pāto piṇḍapāto, tattha tattha laddhānaṃ bhikkhānaṃ sannipāto samūhoti vuttaṃ hoti.**  
> Almsfood (piṇḍapāta) means any kind of food. For any kind of food is called almsfood (piṇḍapāta) because it has been dropped into a bhikkhu's bowl through his alms-gathering. Alternatively, almsfood (piṇḍapāta) is the dropping of lumps; this means the gathering or collection of alms obtained here and there.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (I, paccayasannissitasīla) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=30&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=UGnhuYfhuI1hcMSBdGFudGkgeWHhuYNracOxY2kgxIFoxIFyYeG5gy4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga II)
> **Ayaṃ panānisaṃso ,“piṇḍiyālopabhojanaṃ nissāya pabbajjā” ti (a. ni.4.27; itivu.101) vacanato nissayānurūpapaṭipattisabbhāvo, dutiye ariyavaṃse patiṭṭhānaṃ, aparāyattavuttitā,“appāni ceva sulabhāni ca tāni ca anavajjānī” ti bhagavatā saṃvaṇṇitapaccayatā, kosajjanimmaddanatā, parisuddhājīvatā, sekhiyapaṭipattipūraṇaṃ, aparapositā, parānuggahakiriyā, mānappahānaṃ, rasataṇhānivāraṇaṃ, gaṇabhojanaparamparabhojanacārittasikkhāpadehi anāpattitā, appicchatādīnaṃ anulomavuttitā, sammāpaṭipattibrūhanaṃ, pacchimajanatānukampananti.**  
> This, indeed, is the benefit (ānisaṃsa): on account of the statement, ‘the going forth is in dependence on almsfood,’ there is the presence of practice in conformity with this support, establishment in the second lineage of the noble ones, an independent livelihood, a requisite praised by the Blessed One as ‘trifles, easily gained, and blameless,’ the crushing of laziness, a purified livelihood, the fulfillment of the training rules of deportment, not nourishing another, showing kindness to others, the abandoning of pride, the prevention of craving for tastes, freedom from offenses regarding eating in a group, eating in succession, and standard conduct, living in accordance with fewness of wishes, etc., the growth of right practice, and compassion for future generations.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (II, Piṇḍapātikaṅgakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=64&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=QXlh4bmDIHBhbsSBbmlzYeG5g3NvICzigJjigJggcGnhuYfhuI1pecSBbG9wYWJob2phbmHhuYMgbmlzc8SBeWEgcGFiYmFqasSB)

* **Vinaya**: *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* (Mahākhandhaka, Vin-iii 409) – the four *nissaya*, the first being "going forth on account of meals of scraps".
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Kasibhāradvāja Sutta* (Sn 1.4, "4. Kasibhāradvājasuttaṃ", Sn 250-) – the brahmin "saw the Blessed One standing for alms" (*piṇḍāya ṭhitaṃ*) and told him that he himself ploughs and sows before eating (Sn 252).
