### Grammatical Analysis
**Viññāṇadhātu**: [f.] consciousness element. Formed by *[viññāṇa](/glossary/vinnana/)* (consciousness) + *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element). The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā explains a compound of this kind (*manoviññāṇadhātu*) as consciousness that is an "element" in the sense of being without a living essence or soul (*nissattanijjīvaṭṭhena dhātu*).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta (MN 140) the Buddha says: "this person consists of six elements" (*chadhāturo ayaṃ puruso*): earth, water, fire, air, space and consciousness (*viññāṇadhātu*). After the five material elements he says "then there remains only consciousness, purified and bright" (*athāparaṃ viññāṇaṃyeva avasissati parisuddhaṃ pariyodātaṃ*), by which one cognizes pleasant, painful and neither-painful-nor-pleasant. The Papañcasūdanī explains: this teaching shows the meditation on the non-material by way of feeling, "purified" means free from imperfections (*nirupakkilesaṃ*) and "bright" means radiant (*pabhassaraṃ*).

The Vibhaṅga (Dhātuvibhaṅga, §172, §178) lists the same six, and defines the consciousness element as the six: eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, body- and mind-consciousness element (*cakkhuviññāṇadhātu ... manoviññāṇadhātu*). In the list of eighteen elements (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha §45, Abhidh-s 670) the mind element (*manodhātu*) is listed separately, so the commentary on the Dhātukathā speaks of "the seven consciousness elements" (*sattaviññāṇadhātu*): the five sense-consciousnesses, the mind element and the mind-consciousness element.

> **Tattha katamā viññāṇadhātu? Cakkhuviññāṇadhātu, sotaviññāṇadhātu, ghānaviññāṇadhātu, jivhāviññāṇadhātu, kāyaviññāṇadhātu, manoviññāṇadhātu – ayaṃ vuccati “viññāṇadhātu”.**  
> 178. Of these, what is the consciousness element (viññāṇadhātu)? The eye-consciousness element (cakkhuviññāṇadhātu), ear-consciousness element (sotaviññāṇadhātu), nose-consciousness element (ghānaviññāṇadhātu), tongue-consciousness element (jivhāviññāṇadhātu), body-consciousness element (kāyaviññāṇadhātu), mind-consciousness element (manoviññāṇadhātu) — this is called the 'consciousness element'.  
>

> **Athāparaṃ viññāṇaṃyeva avasissatīti ayampettha pāṭiyekko anusandhi. Yaṃ vā panetaṃ imassa bhikkhuno pathavīdhātuādīsu āgamaniyavipassanāvasena kammakārakaviññāṇaṃ, taṃ viññāṇadhātuvasena bhājetvā dassentopi imaṃ desanaṃ ārabhi. Parisuddhanti nirupakkilesaṃ. Pariyodātanti pabhassaraṃ.**  
> 354. “Then there remains only consciousness” indicates a separate sequence of inquiry. Or, to show the consciousness that acts as the agent of insight by way of the insight arriving at the elements—starting with the earth element—having divided that consciousness by way of the consciousness element, he began this teaching. “Purified” means free from influxes/stains. “Bright” means radiant.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta* (MN 140, M-iii 1134–1171) – the six elements (M-iii 1141) and "then there remains only consciousness, purified and bright" (M-iii 1151). Also the six elements in M-iii 255:3.
* **Abhidhamma**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)*, Dhātuvibhaṅga (Vibh 601–692; §172 the six elements, §178 the consciousness element). The Dhātukathā (Dhatuk) uses *viññāṇadhātu* 13 times in the Mūla; its commentary says "seven consciousness elements" (Dhatuk-a 25:10).
* **Commentary**: *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* (Ps-iii 893) on MN 140; Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Abhidh-s-t 1187).
