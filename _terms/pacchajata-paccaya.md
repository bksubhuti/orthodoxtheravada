### Grammatical Analysis
**Pacchājātapaccaya**: [m.] Post-nascence condition; Condition of arising later. Formed by *pacchā* (after, later, behind) + *jāta* (born, arisen) + *paccaya* (condition).

### Orthodox Definition
*Pacchājāta-paccaya* is the eleventh of the 24 Conditions (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, 44:1). It is the counterpart of the pre-nascence condition (*purejāta-paccaya*): there the condition arises first, here it arises later. The Mūla gives it in one line: later-arisen consciousness and mental factors (*pacchājātā cittacetasikā dhammā*) are a condition for this earlier-arisen body (*purejātassa imassa kāyassa*).

The Aṭṭhakathā puts it as: a formless phenomenon that helps by supporting (*upatthambhaka*) material phenomena that arose earlier. The Ṭīkā explains what this support is: the immaterial states that occur by way of the nutriment of mental volition (*manosañcetanāhāra*) support the material body. It is not said of volition as food in the literal sense, which is why the text takes the word *cetanā* and not just *āhārāsā*.

The traditional illustration is the young vulture (*gijjhapotaka*): the volition of appetite for food (*āhārāsācetanā*) supports the bodies of the vulture chicks, which are already born. In the same way later-arising mind and mental factors support the body that arose before them.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Pacchājātapaccayoti – pacchājātā cittacetasikā dhammā purejātassa imassa kāyassa pacchājātapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 11. Post-nascence condition: Post-nascent consciousness and mental factors are related to this pre-nascent material body by post-nascence condition.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Purejātānaṃ rūpadhammānaṃ upatthambhakaṭṭhena upakārako arūpadhammo pacchājātapaccayo , gijjhapotakasarīrānaṃ āhārāsācetanā viya. Tena vuttaṃ – “pacchājātā cittacetasikā dhammā purejātassa imassa kāyassa pacchājātapaccayena paccayo” ti.**  
> A formless phenomenon that helps in the sense of providing support (upatthambhakaṭṭhena) for material phenomena (rūpadhammānaṃ) that have arisen earlier is postnascence condition (pacchājātapaccayo), like the volition of appetite for food (āhārāsācetanā) for the bodies of vulture chicks. Because of that, it was said: “Post-arising mind and mental factors (pacchājātā cittacetasikā dhammā) are a condition for this earlier-arisen body (purejātassa imassa kāyassa) by way of postnascence condition (pacchājātapaccayena paccayo).”  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā (on the pacchājātapaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=349&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UHVyZWrEgXTEgW5h4bmDIHLFq3BhZGhhbW3EgW5h4bmDIHVwYXR0aGFtYmhha2Hhua3hua1oZW5hIHVwYWvEgXJha28gYXLFq3BhZGhhbW1vIHBhY2NoxIFqxIF0YXBhY2NheW8%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Paṭṭhānapakaraṇa-mūlaṭīkā)
> **Gijjhapotakasarīrānaṃ āhārāsācetanā viyāti etena manosañcetanāhāravasena pavattamānehi arūpadhammehi rūpakāyassa upatthambhitabhāvaṃ dasseti. Teneva “āhārāsā viyā” ti avatvā cetanāgahaṇaṃ karoti.**  
> 'Like the volition of nutriment for the bodies of vulture chicks' — by this, he shows the state of support for the material body by immaterial phenomena that are occurring by way of the nutriment of mental volition. Because of that, he does not say 'like nutriment,' but rather takes the term 'volition'.  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānapakaraṇa-mūlaṭīkā (on the pacchājātapaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_bi_03&page_number=172&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87am%C5%ABla%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=R2lqamhhcG90YWthc2FyxKtyxIFuYeG5gyDEgWjEgXLEgXPEgWNldGFuxIEgdml5xIF0aSBldGVuYSBtYW5vc2HDsWNldGFuxIFoxIFyYXZhc2VuYSBwYXZhdHRhbcSBbmVoaQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* – the list of the 24 conditions (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, pacchājātapaccayo is the eleventh) and the definition "11. Pacchājātapaccayoti – pacchājātā cittacetasikā dhammā purejātassa imassa kāyassa pacchājātapaccayena paccayo" (Paṭṭh-i 44:1).
* **Commentary**: *Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭṭh-a 1931:1-3) and the *Visuddhimagga* (XVII, 604, Vism-ii 933:1) give the same definition with the vulture-chick simile; the *Paṭṭhānapakaraṇa-mūlaṭīkā* (Paṭṭh-mt 1083) explains it by *manosañcetanāhāra*. The *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha* tradition repeats it in the Paccayasaṅgaha (quoted in the Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā, Abhidh-s-t 1666:2-3).
