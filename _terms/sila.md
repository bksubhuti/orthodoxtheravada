### Grammatical Analysis
**Sīla**: [nt.] virtue; morality; ethical conduct; discipline. The *Visuddhimagga* explains it as *sīlana*: either "coordinating" (*samādhāna*), the non-dispersal of bodily action and the rest through virtue, or "upholding" (*upadhāraṇa*), being the basis (*ādhāra*) for wholesome states by serving as their foundation, and says that those who know etymology admit only these two meanings. Others explain the word as "head" (*sira*) or "cool" (*sītala*) (Vism-i 69).

### Orthodox Definition
In the *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* the question "What is virtue?" (*kiṃ sīlaṃ*) is answered: volition is virtue (*cetanā sīlaṃ*), the mental concomitant is virtue (*cetasikaṃ sīlaṃ*), restraint is virtue (*saṃvaro sīlaṃ*), non-transgression is virtue (*avītikkamo sīlaṃ*), and there are three kinds: wholesome, unwholesome and indeterminate (Paṭis 240–241). The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (I, Sīlaniddesa) explains these: virtue as volition is the volition of one who abstains from killing and so on, or fulfils the practice of duties, and also the seven volitions of the courses of action of one who gives up killing and so on; virtue as concomitant is the abstinence (*virati*) of one who abstains, and also the states of non-covetousness, non-ill will and right view; virtue as restraint is fivefold: restraint of the Pātimokkha, of mindfulness, of knowledge, of patience and of energy (Vism-i 64).

The *Visuddhimagga* gives the full definition by characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: *composure* (*sīlana*) is its characteristic, even when analysed in many ways, as visibility is the characteristic of the visible object; its function (*rasa*) is destroying bad conduct (*dussīlyaviddhaṃsana*) and, as achievement, blamelessness (*anavajja*); it is manifested as purity (*soceyya*), of body, speech and mind; and its proximate cause (*padaṭṭhāna*) is conscience and prudence (*hirottappa*), for when they exist, virtue arises and persists (Vism-i 71–79).

In the Abhidhamma analysis, the right speech, right action and right livelihood, "these three are called abstinences (*viratiyo*)" (Abhidh-s 91): the abstinence from the four verbal misconducts is right speech, and so on (Dhs 781 for the supramundane consciousness).

For different groups, virtue is set out in different training rules. The Mūla gives the Ten Training Rules (*dasasikkhāpada*, Khp 2) that the novice (*sāmaṇera*) undertakes, beginning with abstaining from killing living beings (Khp 16; *sāmaṇerānaṃ dasa sikkhāpadāni*, Vin-v 2131), and the Uposatha complete in eight factors (*aṭṭhaṅgasamannāgato uposatho*, AN 8.41), which a noble disciple observes. For the bhikkhu, the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha lists the fourfold purity of virtue (*catupārisuddhisīla*): restraint by the Pātimokkha, restraint of the faculties, purity of livelihood, and virtue connected with the requisites (*pātimokkhasaṃvarasīlaṃ indriyasaṃvarasīlaṃ ājīvapārisuddhisīlaṃ paccayasannissitasīlaṃ*, Abhidh-s 818); the *Visuddhimagga* illustrates the first two with the Mūla formulas ("pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto viharati …", "cakkhunā rūpaṃ disvā na nimittaggāhī hoti …", Vism-i 139).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Kiṃ sīlaṃ? Kiṃ sīlanti cetanā sīlaṃ, cetasikaṃ sīlaṃ, saṃvaro sīlaṃ, avītikkamo sīlaṃ. Kati sīlānīti tīṇi sīlāni – kusalasīlaṃ, akusalasīlaṃ, abyākatasīlaṃ.**  
> 39. What is virtue? What is virtue? There is virtue as volition, virtue as consciousness concomitant, virtue as restraint, and virtue as non-transgression. How many kinds of virtue are there? There are three kinds of virtue: wholesome virtue, unwholesome virtue, and indeterminate virtue.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Sīlakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=43&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzkuIEtp4bmDIHPEq2xh4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Idha, mahārāja, bhikkhu pāṇātipātaṃ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti.**  
> Herein, great king, having abandoned the destruction of life, the bhikkhu abstains from the destruction of life.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 2, Sāmaññaphala Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_01&page_number=59&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SWRoYSwgbWFoxIFyxIFqYSwgYmhpa2todSBwxIHhuYfEgXRpcMSBdGHhuYMgcGFoxIF5YSBwxIHhuYfEgXRpcMSBdMSB)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the meaning)
> **Avasesesu kenaṭṭhena sīlanti sīlanaṭṭhena sīlaṃ. Kimidaṃ sīlanaṃ nāma. Samādhānaṃ vā, kāyakammādīnaṃ susīlyavasena avippakiṇṇatāti attho. Upadhāraṇaṃ vā, kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ patiṭṭhānavasena ādhārabhāvoti attho.**  
> Among the remaining questions: In what sense is it virtue? (kenaṭṭhena sīlaṃ) It is virtue in the sense of composure (sīlana). What is this composure? Either coordinating, meaning the non-dispersal of bodily action and so on through virtue; Or upholding, meaning the state of being a basis for wholesome states by serving as their foundation.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (I, Sīlaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=8&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Ny4gQXZhc2VzZXN1IGtlbmHhua3hua1oZW5hIHPEq2xhbnRpIHPEq2xhbmHhua3hua1oZW5hIHPEq2xh4bmDLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, lakkhaṇa-rasa-paccupaṭṭhāna-padaṭṭhāna)
> **Tasmā idaṃ sīlaṃ nāma kiccaṭṭhena rasena dussīlyaviddhaṃsanarasaṃ, sampattiatthena rasena anavajjarasanti veditabbaṃ. Tayidaṃ sīlaṃ kāyasoceyyaṃ vacīsoceyyaṃ manosoceyyanti (a. ni.3.121) evaṃ vuttasoceyyapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, soceyyabhāvena paccupaṭṭhāti gahaṇabhāvaṃ gacchati. Hirottappañca panassa viññūhi padaṭṭhānanti vaṇṇitaṃ, āsannakāraṇanti attho. Hirottappe hi sati sīlaṃ uppajjati ceva tiṭṭhati ca.**  
> Therefore, this virtue should be understood to have the function of destroying bad conduct as its function in the sense of action, and a blameless function as its function in the sense of achievement. This virtue is manifested as the kinds of purity described as: 'purity of body, purity of speech, and purity of mind'; it is manifested, and comes to be apprehended, as a state of purity. But conscience and prudence are described by the wise as its proximate cause, meaning its immediate cause. For when conscience and prudence exist, virtue arises and persists;  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (I, Sīlaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=9&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzbcSBIGlkYeG5gyBzxKtsYeG5gyBuxIFtYSBraWNjYeG5reG5rWhlbmEgcmFzZW5h)
