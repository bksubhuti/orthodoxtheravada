### Grammatical Analysis
**Tejodhātu**: [f.] fire element; element of heat; temperature. Formed by *tejo* (fire, heat, flame, thermal energy) + *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element).

### Orthodox Definition
*Tejodhātu* is the third of the four great physical elements (*mahābhūta*). It is the material element of **heat and maturation**.

The Aṭṭhakathā gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: *tejodhātu uṇhattalakkhaṇā, paripācanarasā, maddavānuppadānapaccupaṭṭhānā* (heat is its characteristic, maturing its function, and the supplying of softness its manifestation; As 2135:8, Vism-i 2172:3). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as *tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ*, internal or external, clung-to or not clung-to (Dhs §969). The Visuddhimagga, speaking of temperature (*utu*), says it is the fire element, of two kinds: hot temperature and cold temperature (*uṇhautu sītautu*; Vism-ii 1515:2).

In the Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta (MN 140, first Quote) the internal fire element is shown in four ways within the body:
1. That by which one is warmed (*yena ca santappati*).
2. That by which one ages (*yena ca jīrīyati*).
3. That by which one is consumed (*yena ca pariḍayhati*).
4. That by which what is eaten, drunk, chewed and tasted is properly digested (*yena ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ sammā pariṇāmaṃ gacchati*).

### Quote (Mūla: MN 140)
> **“Katamā ca, bhikkhu, tejodhātu? Tejodhātu siyā ajjhattikā siyā bāhirā. Katamā ca, bhikkhu, ajjhattikā tejodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ tejo tejogataṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – yena ca santappati, yena ca jīrīyati, yena ca pariḍayhati, yena ca asitapītakhāyitasāyitaṃ sammā pariṇāmaṃ gacchati, yaṃ vā panaññampi kiñci ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ tejo tejogataṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhu, ajjhattikā tejodhātu. Yā ceva kho pana ajjhattikā tejodhātu yā ca bāhirā tejodhātu tejodhāturevesā. ‘ Taṃ netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā ’ ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya daṭṭhabbaṃ. Evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya disvā tejodhātuyā nibbindati, tejodhātuyā cittaṃ virājeti.**  
> “What, bhikkhu, is the fire element? The fire element may be either internal or external. What is the internal fire element? Whatever internally, belonging to oneself, is fire, fiery, and clung-to, that is, that by which one is warmed, ages, and is consumed, and that by which what is eaten, drunk, consumed, and tasted gets completely digested, or whatever else internally, belonging to oneself, is fire, fiery, and clung-to: this is called the internal fire element. Now both the internal fire element and the external fire element are simply fire element. And that should be seen as it actually is with proper wisdom thus: ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self.’ When one sees it thus as it actually is with proper wisdom, one becomes disenchanted with the fire element and makes the mind dispassionate towards the fire element.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 140, Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=284&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzUxLuKAmOKAmCBLYXRhbcSBIGNhLCBiaGlra2h1LCB0ZWpvZGjEgXR1Pw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ tejodhātu? Yaṃ tejo tejogataṃ usmā usmāgataṃ usumaṃ usumagataṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā upādiṇṇaṃ vā anupādiṇṇaṃ vā – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ tejodhātu.**  
> 969. Which form is the fire element? Whatever is fire, fiery, heat, in a state of heat, intense heat, in a state of intense heat, whether internal or external, whether grasped by kamma or ungrasped by kamma – this is that form which is the fire element.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§969, Tejodhātu) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=202&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTY5LiBLYXRhbWHhuYMgdGHhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgdGVqb2RoxIF0dT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Lakkhaṇāditoti pathavīdhātu kiṃ lakkhaṇā, kiṃ rasā, kiṃ paccupaṭṭhānāti evaṃ catassopi dhātuyo āvajjetvā pathavīdhātu kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā, patiṭṭhānarasā, sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā. Tejodhātu uṇhattalakkhaṇā, paripācanarasā, maddavānuppadānapaccupaṭṭhānā.**  
> 350. Regarding by characteristic and so on (lakkhaṇādito): having reflected upon the four elements thus: 'What is the characteristic of the earth element? What is its function? What is its manifestation?'—the earth element (pathavīdhātu) has the characteristic of hardness, the function of acting as a foundation, and is manifested as receiving. The fire element (tejodhātu) has the characteristic of heat, the function of maturing, and is manifested as imparting softness.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XI, Catudhātuvavatthānabhāvanā, lakkhaṇādito) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=362&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MzUwLiBMYWtraGHhuYfEgWRpdG90aSBwYXRoYXbEq2RoxIF0dSBraeG5gyBsYWtraGHhuYfEgSwga2nhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Tattha kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā pathavīdhātu , patiṭṭhānarasā, sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā. Tejodhātu uṇhattalakkhaṇā, paripācanarasā, maddavānuppadānapaccupaṭṭhānā.**  
> Therein, the earth element (pathavīdhātu) has the characteristic of solidity, the function of providing a foundation, and the manifestation of receiving (or supporting associated phenomena). The fire element (tejodhātu) has the characteristic of heat, the function of maturation, and the manifestation of producing softness.  
>  
> — Atthasālinī (Dhs-a, on §647, Phoṭṭhabbāyatana) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=368&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGtha2toYeG4t2F0dGFsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBwYXRoYXbEq2RoxIF0dSAsIHBhdGnhua3hua1oxIFuYXJhc8SBLCBzYW1wYeG5rWljY2hhbmFwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLg%3D%3D)
