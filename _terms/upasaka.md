### Grammatical Analysis
**Upāsaka**: [m.] male lay follower (f. *upāsikā*). From *upāsati*, "he serves, sits near, attends upon": the Aṭṭhakathā explains "so hi buddhaṃ upāsatīti upāsako", "he serves the Buddha, therefore he is a lay follower", and likewise the Dhamma and the Saṅgha (Mp-ii 104:2-4; Sv-i 1017:1-2).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla the Buddha tells Mahānāma: "When one has gone for refuge to the Buddha, has gone for refuge to the Dhamma, has gone for refuge to the Saṅgha, it is in this respect that one is a male lay follower" (AN 8.25, A-viii 298:5-7; the same question and answer is in S-v 2266, CST §1033). The Aṭṭhakathā put it as: "ko upāsako? Yo koci saraṇagato gahaṭṭho", any householder who has gone for refuge (Sv-i 1016:1; Mp-ii 102:1). No complex ritual is mentioned in these texts; the refuges are what make one an *upāsaka*, and the five abstinences are his virtue: the lay follower is "virtuous" (*sīlavā*) when he abstains from killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, false speech and intoxicants (A-viii 299:2-3), and the commentary answers "kimassa sīlaṃ? Pañca veramaṇiyo" (Sv-i 1018:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā add his livelihood, abandoning the five wrong trades and living by what is right (Sv-i 1019:1); the five trades are trade in weapons, living beings, meat, intoxicants and poison, which a lay follower should not engage in (AN 5.177, quoted at Mp-ii 108:3-4). The Caṇḍāla Sutta contrasts the "outcaste among lay followers" (no faith, immoral, eager for protective charms and ceremonies, trusting ceremonies and not kamma, seeking those worthy of offerings outside this teaching and giving there first) with the "jewel, lotus and white lotus among lay followers", who has faith, virtue, trusts kamma, does not seek recipients of gifts outside this teaching, and makes offerings here first (AN 5.175, A-v 1186-1187). In the Mahānāma Sutta the Buddha also describes the lay follower who practises for his own benefit and that of others (A-viii 300-301).

The lay life does not bar the noble attainments: the Buddha names laymen and laywomen as once-returners and stream-enterers, and many laymen of Nādika as non-returners (DN 16, D-ii 387:3-7).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, what makes a lay follower)
> **“kittāvatā nu kho, bhante, upāsako hotī” ti? “Yato kho, mahānāma, buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti, dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti, saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gato hoti; ettāvatā kho, mahānāma, upāsako hotī” ti. “Kittāvatā pana, bhante, upāsako sīlavā hotī” ti? “Yato kho, mahānāma, upāsako pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti, adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti, kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti, musāvādā paṭivirato hoti, surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā paṭivirato hoti; ettāvatā kho, mahānāma, upāsako sīlavā hotī” ti.**  
> “In what way, venerable sir, is one a male lay follower?” “When indeed, Mahānāma, one has gone for refuge to the Buddha, has gone for refuge to the Dhamma, has gone for refuge to the Saṅgha; It is in this respect, Mahānāma, that one is a male lay follower.” “But in what way, venerable sir, is a male lay follower virtuous?” “When indeed, Mahānāma, a male lay follower abstains from killing living beings, he abstains from taking what is not given, he abstains from sexual misconduct, he abstains from false speech, he abstains from liquor, wine, and intoxicants, the basis for heedlessness; It is in this respect, Mahānāma, that a male lay follower is virtuous.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, the jewel among lay followers)
> **“Pañcahi, bhikkhave, dhammehi samannāgato upāsako upāsakaratanañca hoti upāsakapadumañca upāsakapuṇḍarīkañca. Katamehi pañcahi? Saddho hoti; sīlavā hoti; akotūhalamaṅgaliko hoti, kammaṃ pacceti no maṅgalaṃ; na ito bahiddhā dakkhiṇeyyaṃ gavesati; idha ca pubbakāraṃ karoti.**  
> Monks, a male lay follower possessed of these five qualities is a jewel among male lay followers, a lotus among male lay followers, and a white lotus among male lay followers. Which five? He is endowed with faith. He is virtuous. He is not eager for protective charms & ceremonies; he trusts kamma, not protective charms & ceremonies. He does not search for recipients of his offerings outside this teaching. And here he makes offerings first.  
>  
> — *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (AN 5.175, Caṇḍāla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=181&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFBhw7FjYWhpLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGRoYW1tZWhpIHNhbWFubsSBZ2F0byB1cMSBc2Frbw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Tattha ko upāsakoti yo koci saraṇagato gahaṭṭho. Kasmā upāsakoti ratanattayaṃ upāsanato. So hi buddhaṃ upāsatīti upāsako, tathā dhammaṃ saṃghaṃ. Kimassa sīlanti pañca veramaṇiyo. Ko ājīvoti pañca micchāvaṇijjā pahāya dhammena samena jīvitakappanaṃ.**  
> Among these, regarding ‘Who is a lay follower?’ any householder who has gone for refuge. Regarding ‘Why a lay follower?’ serving the Triple Gem. For they serve the Buddha, and thus are a lay follower; likewise for the Dhamma and the Saṅgha. Regarding ‘What is their virtue?’ the five abstinences. Regarding ‘What is their livelihood?’ abandoning the five wrong trades and maintaining life by law and right.  
>  
> — *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (upāsakavidhikosalla) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=209&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGtvIHVwxIFzYWtvdGkgeW8ga29jaSBzYXJh4bmHYWdhdG8%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahānāma Sutta* (AN 8.25, A-viii 297-301, "5. Mahānāmasuttaṃ"; parallel in S-v 2265-2267, CST §1033) – what makes one a lay follower, virtuous, and so on.
* **Sutta**: *Caṇḍāla Sutta* (AN 5.175, A-v 1185-1187) – the outcaste and the jewel among lay followers.
* **Sutta**: *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* (DN 16, D-ii 387) – lay followers who attained stream-entry, once-return and non-return.
* **Commentary**: *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (Sv-i 1015-1021) and *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-ii 101-112) – who an upāsaka is, why so called, his virtue, livelihood, ruin and prosperity, with the five wrong trades (AN 5.177).
