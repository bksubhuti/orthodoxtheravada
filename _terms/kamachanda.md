### Grammatical Analysis
**Kāmacchanda**: [m.] sensual desire; excitement for sensuality. Formed by *kāma* (sensual pleasure, objects of taste, sight, touch) + *chanda* (desire, impulse, will-to-do). Signifies the active yearning for sensuous experience.

### Orthodox Definition
*Kāmacchanda* is the first of the five mental hindrances (*[nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*). Doctrinally rooted in the unwholesome root of greed (*[lobha](/glossary/lobha/)-cetasika*), it represents the mind's constant drifting away from the meditation object to feast upon pleasant visual sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and physical touches.

The commentaries note that kāmacchanda acts like a sticky glue, preventing the mind from entering one-pointed unification. In the *[Satipaṭṭhāna](/glossary/satipatthana/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* commentary, six specific practical methods are detailed to overcome this hindrance:
1. Learning how to meditate on an unbeautiful object (*asubha-nimitta*).
2. Devoting oneself to the actual meditation on bodily repulsiveness (*[asubhabhāvanā](/glossary/asubhabhavana/)*).
3. Guarding the doors of the sensory faculties (*indriya-saṃvara*).
4. Moderation in eating (*bhojane mattaññutā*).
5. Cultivating noble friendship (*kalyāṇamittatā*).
6. Profitable conversation that dispels sensuality.

It is temporarily suppressed by the arising of the first [jhāna](/glossary/jhana/) factor of one-pointedness (*[ekaggatā](/glossary/ekaggata/)*) and completely uprooted at the stage of Non-Returner (*[Anāgāmī](/glossary/anagami/)*).

### Quote
> **“Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu santaṃ vā ajjhattaṃ kāmacchandaṃ ‘ atthi me ajjhattaṃ kāmacchando ’ ti pajānāti, asantaṃ vā ajjhattaṃ kāmacchandaṃ ‘ natthi me ajjhattaṃ kāmacchando ’ ti pajānāti; yathā ca anuppannassa kāmacchandassa uppādo hoti tañca pajānāti, yathā ca uppannassa kāmacchandassa pahānaṃ hoti tañca pajānāti, yathā ca pahīnassa kāmacchandassa āyatiṃ anuppādo hoti tañca pajānāti.**  
> “Here, mendicants, when sensual desire is present within oneself, a bhikkhu understands: ‘Sensual desire is present within me’; or when sensual desire is not present within oneself, he understands: ‘Sensual desire is not present within me’; he understands how unarisen sensual desire comes to arise, how arisen sensual desire is abandoned, and how abandoned sensual desire does not arise again in the future.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 10, Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta, Nīvaraṇapabba) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=77&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIElkaGEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgYmhpa2todSBzYW50YeG5gyB2xIE%3D)
