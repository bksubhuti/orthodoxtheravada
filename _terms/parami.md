### Grammatical Analysis
**Pāramī**: [f.] perfection. The Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā derives it from *parama* (supreme): the Bodhisatta is *parama* because of his supreme state through the special qualities of giving, virtue and the rest, and “tesaṃ bhāvo kammaṃ vā pāramī, dānādikiriyā”, his state or action is *pāramī*, namely the acts of giving and so on (Cp-a 1896:2). It also gives a sequence of word-analyses with *pāra* (the further shore, Nibbāna): *pāre vā nibbāne majjati sujjhati* and so on (Cp-a 1896:4-8). The abstract form is *pāramitā*.

### Orthodox Definition
The Aṭṭhakathā on the Cariyāpiṭaka, made by the Teacher Dhammapāla, resident of the Badaratittha monastery (Cp-a 2124:1-2125:1), defines the pāramīs as the qualities beginning with giving (*dānādayo guṇā*) that are not struck by craving, conceit or views (*taṇhāmānadiṭṭhīhi anupahatā*) and are held by compassion and skill in means (*karuṇūpāyakosalapariggahitā*) (Cp-a 1895:2). They are the practice of the Bodhisatta, who fulfils them for the welfare of the many (Sv-ii 978:1).

The Mūla names the ten in the Buddhavaṃsa, where the Buddha is asked “Dasa pāramī tayā dhīra, kīdisī lokanāyaka” (Bv 237:1) and lists them: *dānaṃ, sīlaṃ, nekkhammaṃ, paññā, vīriyaṃ, khanti, saccaṃ, adhiṭṭhānaṃ, mettā, upekkhā* (Bv 234:1-235:1; Cp-a 1900:6 quotes the same ten as *buddhakārakā dhammā*). The ten pāramīs are:
1. *[Dāna](/glossary/dana/)* (Generosity)
2. *[Sīla](/glossary/sila/)* (Virtue/Morality)
3. *[Nekkhamma](/glossary/nekkhamma/)* (Renunciation)
4. *[Paññā](/glossary/panna/)* (Wisdom)
5. *[Viriya](/glossary/viriya/)* (Energy/Effort)
6. *[Khanti](/glossary/khanti/)* (Patience/Forbearance)
7. *[Sacca](/glossary/sacca/)* (Truthfulness)
8. *[Adhiṭṭhāna](/glossary/adhitthana/)* (Determination/Resolve)
9. *[Mettā](/glossary/metta/)* (Loving-kindness)
10. *[Upekkhā](/glossary/upekkha/)* (Equanimity)

The Aṭṭhakathā explains the order (Cp-a 1904-1907: each is held by the next, for example giving is held by virtue, virtue by renunciation) and gives each its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause. For giving: its characteristic is relinquishing (*pariccāgalakkhaṇā*), its function is destroying greed in the gift, its manifestation is non-attachment, its proximate cause is the thing to be given up (Cp-a 1910:1).

The pāramīs are counted at three levels: there are ten *pāramiyo*, ten *upapāramiyo* and ten *paramatthapāramiyo*, thirty in all (*samattiṃsa pāramiyo*) (Cp-a 2028:2). For giving, the giving of external goods is *pāramī*, the giving of one's limbs is *upapāramī* and the giving of one's life is *paramatthapāramī*, and the same applies to the others (Bv-a 640:1-3). The Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā also records another explanation of the three by stages: *pāramiyo* while being fulfilled, *upapāramiyo* on the Bodhisatta's ground, and *paramatthapāramiyo* in full on the Buddha's ground (Cp-a 2028:4).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tatridaṃ vissajjanaṃ – kā panetā pāramiyoti? Taṇhāmānadiṭṭhīhi anupahatā karuṇūpāyakosallapariggahitā dānādayo guṇā pāramiyo. Kenaṭṭhena pāramiyoti? Dānasīlādiguṇavisesayogena sattuttamatāya paramā mahāsattā bodhisattā, tesaṃ bhāvo kammaṃ vā pāramī, dānādikiriyā. Atha vā paratīti paramo, dānādiguṇānaṃ pūrako pālako cāti bodhisatto, paramassa ayaṃ, paramassa vā bhāvo, kammaṃ vā pāramī, dānādikiriyāva.**  
> Herein is the answer: “What, then, are these perfections?” The qualities of giving and so on, unimpaired by craving, conceit, and views, and encompassed by compassion and skill in means, are perfections. “In what sense are they perfections?” Through the combination of special qualities like giving and virtue, the Bodhisattas, being Great Beings, are supreme due to their preeminence among beings. Their state or action is a perfection, meaning activities like giving and so on. Alternatively, “supreme” (paramo) means one who transcends. And a Bodhisatta is supreme because he fulfills and protects qualities like giving and so on. This (perfection) belongs to the supreme one, or the state or action of the supreme one is a perfection, namely, activities like giving and so on.  
>  
> — [Cariyāpiṭaka](/glossary/cariyapitaka/)-aṭṭhakathā (Pāramīkathā, what the pāramīs are and why so called) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_13&page_number=269&name=cariy%C4%81pi%E1%B9%ADakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0cmlkYeG5gyB2aXNzYWpqYW5h4bmDIOKAkyBrxIEgcGFuZXTEgSBwxIFyYW1peW90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **“kati nu kho, bhante, buddhakārakā dhammā? Dasa kho, sāriputta, buddhakārakā dhammā. Katame dasa? Dānaṃ kho, sāriputta, buddhakārako dhammo, sīlaṃ, nekkhammaṃ, paññā, vīriyaṃ, khanti, saccaṃ, adhiṭṭhānaṃ, mettā, upekkhā buddhakārako dhammo. Ime kho, sāriputta, dasa buddhakārakā dhammā” ti.**

What ten? “Indeed, Sāriputta, the qualities that make one a Buddha are giving, virtue, renunciation, wisdom, energy, patience, truth, determination, loving-kindness, and equanimity. These, Sāriputta, are the ten qualities that make one a Buddha.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tasmā pariccāgalakkhaṇā dānapāramī , deyyadhamme lobhaviddhaṃsanarasā, anāsattipaccupaṭṭhānā, bhavavibhavasampattipaccupaṭṭhānā vā, pariccajitabbavatthupadaṭṭhānā.**  
> Therefore, the perfection of giving (dānapāramī) has: (1) renunciation as its characteristic; (2) the function of destroying greed regarding the object to be given; (3) non-attachment as its manifestation, or the manifestation of prosperity and success in existence; and (4) the object to be sacrificed as its proximate cause.  
>  
> — [Cariyāpiṭaka](/glossary/cariyapitaka/)-aṭṭhakathā (lakkhaṇa-rasa-paccupaṭṭhāna-padaṭṭhāna of dānapāramī) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_13&page_number=273&name=cariy%C4%81pi%E1%B9%ADakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGFzbcSBIHBhcmljY8SBZ2FsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBkxIFuYXDEgXJhbcSrICwgZGV5eWFkaGFtbWUgbG9iaGF2aWRkaGHhuYNzYW5hcmFzxIEs)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Ko vibhāgoti? Dasa pāramiyo, dasa upapāramiyo, dasa paramatthapāramiyoti samattiṃsapāramiyo. Tattha katābhinīhārassa bodhisattassa parahitakaraṇābhininnāsayapayogassa kaṇhadhammavokiṇṇā sukkadhammā pāramiyo eva, tehi avokiṇṇā sukkadhammā upapāramiyo, akaṇhaasukkā paramatthapāramiyoti keci.**  
> “What is the division?” The perfections are thirty-fold: ten perfections, ten secondary perfections, and ten highest perfections. There, some say that for a Bodhisatta who has made aspirations and whose intention and application are dedicated to the welfare of others, the wholesome qualities mixed with unwholesome qualities are indeed the perfections; the wholesome qualities not mixed with them are the secondary perfections; and those that are neither dark nor bright are the highest perfections.  
>  
> — [Cariyāpiṭaka](/glossary/cariyapitaka/)-aṭṭhakathā (the thirty pāramīs) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_13&page_number=312&name=cariy%C4%81pi%E1%B9%ADakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S28gdmliaMSBZ290aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tattha dānapāramiyaṃ tāva bāhirabhaṇḍapariccāgo pāramī nāma, aṅgapariccāgo upapāramī nāma, jīvitapariccāgo paramatthapāramī nāmāti. Esa nayo sesapāramīsupi. Evaṃ dasa pāramiyo dasa upapāramiyo dasa paramatthapāramiyoti samattiṃsa pāramiyo honti.**  
> Therein, first of all, in the perfection of giving: the sacrifice of external goods is called a perfection (pāramī), the sacrifice of limbs is called a secondary perfection (upapāramī), and the sacrifice of life is called a supreme perfection (paramatthapāramī). This method applies also to the remaining perfections. In this way, there are thirty perfections: ten perfections, ten secondary perfections, and ten supreme perfections.  
>  
> — Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā (Madhuratthavilāsinī, on Bv 77) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_12&page_number=80&name=buddhava%E1%B9%83saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGTEgW5hcMSBcmFtaXlh4bmDIHTEgXZhIGLEgWhpcmFiaGHhuYfhuI1hcGFyaWNjxIFnbyBwxIFyYW3EqyBuxIFtYSw%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Buddhavaṃsa](/glossary/buddhavamsa/)* (Bv 234:1-238:1, Ratanacaṅkamanakaṇḍa 76-77, the ten pāramīs and the question about *upapāramī* and *paramatthapāramī*; Bv 611:1, Sumedhapatthanākathā 116, “paṭhamaṃ dānapāramiṃ”). The *[Cariyāpiṭaka](/glossary/cariyapitaka/)* narrates the Bodhisatta's lives in the practice of the pāramīs; it uses the word, for example, at Cp 462:1 (“ete dānassa pāramī”).
* **Commentary**: *Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā* (Cp-a 1895-1910, 2028) – definition, derivation, number, order, characteristics and the thirty pāramīs; *Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā* (Bv-a 640) – the three grades; *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (Sv-ii 978:1) – the ten pāramīs fulfilled for four asaṅkheyyas and a hundred thousand kappas.
