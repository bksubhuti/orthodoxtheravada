### Grammatical Analysis
**Taṇhā**: [f.] craving; thirst; insatiable desire. The Aṭṭhakathā explains it as craving in the sense of craving (*taṇhāyanaṭṭhena*), or because it trembles (*tasati*) for sense-objects such as forms (It-a 961:1).

### Orthodox Definition
In the structure of the Four Noble Truths, *taṇhā* is explicitly identified as the Second Noble Truth: the origin of suffering (*[dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)-[samudaya](/glossary/samudaya/)*). It is the driving force that fuels the engine of rebirth and binds sentient beings to the wheel of [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). Doctrinally, the Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists *kāmataṇhā*, *bhavataṇhā* and *vibhavataṇhā* among the terms of *lobha*, the unwholesome root of greed (Dhs §1236).

The Buddha systematically categorized taṇhā into three fundamental modes:
1. *Kāma-taṇhā*: Sensual craving; the Aṭṭhakathā says it is lust for the five cords of sensual pleasure (It-a 961:3).
2. *Bhava-taṇhā*: Craving for existence or becoming; the Aṭṭhakathā says it is lust for existence in the form and formless realms, the delight in jhāna, and lust associated with the eternalist view (*sassatadiṭṭhisahagato rāgo*; It-a 961:4).
3. *Vibhava-taṇhā*: Craving for non-existence, which the Aṭṭhakathā says is lust associated with the annihilationist view (*ucchedadiṭṭhisahagato rāgo*; It-a 961:5).

In the Visuddhimagga's analysis of dependent origination, *taṇhā* has the characteristic of being a cause (*hetulakkhaṇā*), its function is delight (*abhinandanarasā*), it is manifested as insatiability (*atittabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānā*), and its proximate cause is feeling (*vedanāpadaṭṭhānā*); the next link, clinging (*upādāna*), has craving as its proximate cause (Vism XVII, Vism-ii 899:10-11). It is therefore not an ultimate beginning but arises conditioned by feeling (*vedanā*).
