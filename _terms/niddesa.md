### Grammatical Analysis
**Niddesa**: [m.] Exposition; description; analytic explanation. Derived from *niddisati* (to point out, to explain, to define). The Pāḷi title has two parts: *Mahāniddesa* (“great exposition”) and *Cūḷaniddesa* (“minor exposition”). The Aṭṭhakathā explains *mahāniddesa* as “mahā ca so niddeso cā ti”, great because it is spoken as foremost (*atiaggaṃ katvā kathitattā*), like the great ocean and the great earth (Nidd-a-i 56:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Niddesa* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya (“Khuddakanikāye”, Nidd-i 2:1) and is a canonical commentary on verses of the *[Suttanipāta](/glossary/suttanipata/)*. The *Mahāniddesa* (Nidd-i) takes the Aṭṭhakavagga, beginning with “1. Kāmasuttaniddeso” (Nidd-i 5:1); the *Cūḷaniddesa* (Nidd-ii) takes the Pārāyanavagga, with its Vatthugāthā, and the Khaggavisāṇasutta (Nidd-ii 4:1, 2240:1). It quotes each verse and then explains it word by word with strings of synonyms and classifications, for example “kāmā” as *vatthukāmā* and *kilesakāmā* (Nidd-i 9:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā (the Saddhammapajjotikā of the Elder Upasena) attributes it to the Elder Sāriputta: “Sāriputto mahāpañño … dhammacakkaṃ vibhājetvā, mahāniddesamabravi” (Nidd-a-i 13:1-14:1). It says the text was among the two thousand units of teaching which Ānanda, the treasurer of the Dhamma, received from the monks (Nidd-a-i 61:1), and that it has two sections, the Aṭṭhakavagga and the Pārāyanavagga with the Khaggavisāṇasutta, sixteen suttas in each section and thirty-three suttas in all (Nidd-a-i 61:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāniddesa)
> **Kāmaṃ kāmayamānassa , tassa ce taṃ samijjhati ; Addhā pītimano hoti , laddhā macco yadicchati . Kāmaṃ kāmayamānassāti kāmāti uddānato dve kāmā – vatthukāmā ca kilesakāmā ca. Katame vatthukāmā ?**  
> “If a mortal desires sensual pleasure, if that desire succeeds for him, he becomes truly joyful-minded upon obtaining what he desires.” “If a mortal desires sensual pleasure” — here, by “sensual pleasures” (kāmā), there are two kinds of desire when summarized: desire for objects (vatthukāmā) and desire as defilements (kilesakāmā). What is the desire for objects?  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Sāriputto mahāpañño, satthukappo jinatrajo; Dhammacakkaṃ vibhājetvā, mahāniddesamabravi;**  
> Sāriputta, of great wisdom, fit to be compared with the Teacher, the Victor's heir, Analyzed the Dhamma-wheel and spoke the Mahāniddesa.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Evaṃ vuttappakāraṃ byañjanañca atthañca dassetvā idāni yo atiaggaṃ katvā kathitattā mahāsamuddamahāpathavī viya mahā ca so niddeso cāti mahāniddeso, taṃ mahāniddesaṃ vaṇṇayissāmi.**  
> Having shown the expression and meaning in the manner spoken, I shall now explain that 'Great Exposition' (Mahāniddesa), which is called 'great' because it is spoken as being foremost, just like the great ocean and the great earth.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tassa dve vaggā aṭṭhakavaggo pārāyanavaggo khaggavisāṇasuttañca, ekekasmiṃ vagge soḷasa soḷasa katvā khaggavisāṇasuttañcāti tettiṃsa suttāni kāmasuttādikhaggavisāṇasuttapariyosānāni. Evaṃ anekadhā vavatthāpitassa imassa mahāniddesassa anupubbapadatthavaṇṇanaṃ karissāmi. Ayañhi mahāniddeso pāṭhato atthato ca uddisantena niddisantena ca sakkaccaṃ uddisitabbo niddisitabbo ca, uggaṇhantenāpi sakkaccaṃ uggaṇhitabbo dhāretabbo ca.**  
> Its two chapters are the Aṭṭhakavagga and the Pārāyanavagga, and the Khaggavisāṇasutta. Counting sixteen in each chapter, there are thirty-three discourses including the Khaggavisāṇasutta, beginning with the Kāmasutta and ending with the Khaggavisāṇasutta. I shall now provide an explanation of the word-meanings of this Great Exposition, which has been established in such various ways, in sequential order. This Great Exposition should be carefully recited and explained—both by way of letter and by way of meaning—by one reciting it or explaining it, and it should be carefully learned and remembered by one studying it.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Mahāniddesa* (Nidd-i), the exposition of the Aṭṭhakavagga, and *Cūḷaniddesa* (Nidd-ii), the exposition of the Pārāyanavagga and the Khaggavisāṇasutta.
* **Commentary**: *[Saddhammapajjotikā](/glossary/saddhammapajjotika/)* (Nidd-a-i, Nidd-a-ii) – the Aṭṭhakathā on the Niddesa, by the Elder Upasena (colophons Nidd-a-i 1069:1, Nidd-a-ii 719:1; account of its writing Nidd-a-ii 726:1-739:1).
