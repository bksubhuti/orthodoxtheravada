### Grammatical Analysis
**Jetavana**: [nt.] Jeta's Grove. Formed by *Jeta* (Prince Jeta, the original owner) + *vana* (forest, grove).

### Orthodox Definition
*Jetavana* (formally *Jetavane Anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme*) is the most historically significant and frequently mentioned monastery in the Pali Canon. Located near the city of Sāvatthī in the kingdom of Kosala, it was purchased by the great patron [Anāthapiṇḍika](/glossary/anathapindika/) at an astronomical price and donated to the Buddha.

After his first twenty rains retreats without a fixed residence, the Buddha spent twenty-five rains retreats (*[vassa](/glossary/vassa/)*) based at Sāvatthī, using Jetavana and the Pubbārāma as his two regular residences (Papañcasūdanī, Ps-i 2083). Because of this, a great many of the Buddha's discourses (especially in the *Saṃyutta* and *Aṅguttara* Nikāyas) begin with the phrase: "At one time the Buddha was staying at Sāvatthī in Jeta's Grove, [Anāthapiṇḍika](/glossary/anathapindika/)'s Park." 

It served as the administrative and spiritual epicenter of the Theravāda dispensation, containing the Buddha's personal scented chamber (*Gandhakuṭi*) and housing thousands of monastics.

### Quote
> **Addasā kho anāthapiṇḍiko gahapati jetassa kumārassa uyyānaṃ – gāmato neva atidūre na accāsanne, gamanāgamanasampannaṃ, atthikānaṃ atthikānaṃ manussānaṃ abhikkamanīyaṃ, divā appākiṇṇaṃ, rattiṃ appasaddaṃ, appanigghosaṃ, vijanavātaṃ, manussarāhasseyyakaṃ, paṭisallānasāruppaṃ. Disvāna yena jeto kumāro tenupasaṅkami, upasaṅkamitvā jetaṃ kumāraṃ etadavoca – “dehi me, ayyaputta, uyyānaṃ ārāmaṃ kātun” ti. “Adeyyo, gahapati, ārāmo api koṭisantharenā” ti. “Gahito, ayyaputta, ārāmo” ti.**  
> The householder Anāthapiṇḍika saw the park of Prince Jeta, which was neither too far from the village nor too near, possessed of a means for going and returning, accessible to all people who wished to visit, during the day not disturbed by people, at night quiet, still, free from disturbances, suitable for human seclusion, and suitable for seclusion. Having seen it, he approached where Prince Jeta was, and after approaching, he said to Prince Jeta: “My lord, give me the park to make a monastery.” “The park is not to be given away, householder, even for a layer of ten million coins.” “It is bought, my lord!”  
>
