### Grammatical Analysis
**Pamāda**: [m.] heedlessness; negligence; carelessness; spiritual drifting. From prefix *pa* (forth) + root *mad* (to be intoxicated, neglect). Meaning: to be completely given over to intoxication and neglect.

### Orthodox Definition
*Pamāda* is the absolute antithesis of the Buddhist path. Included among the minor defilements (*[upakkilesa](/glossary/upakkilesa/)*), it represents the catastrophic relaxation of mindfulness and energy. 

A person afflicted by *pamāda* allows their mind to drift aimlessly among the five cords of sensual pleasure, fails to guard the sense doors, and continuously procrastinates their meditation practice. The Vibhaṅga defines it as the relinquishing of the mind (*cittassa vossaggo*) towards misconduct of body, speech and mind or the five cords of sensual pleasure, and the lack of diligent practice in developing wholesome states (Vibh 2553:2). The Papañcasūdanī gives its characteristic as letting the mind go in the five cords of sensual pleasure (*pañcasu kāmaguṇesu cittavossaggalakkhaṇo*), its function as not restraining that letting go (*vossaggānuppadānaraso*) and its manifestation as the absence of mindfulness (*sativippavāsapaccupaṭṭhāno*) (Ps-i 565:2).

The Buddha equated heedlessness directly with death. A person who is heedless is practically a walking corpse, as they are completely vulnerable to the forces of Māra and have surrendered all agency over their future karmic destination.

### Quote
> **Tattha katamo pamādo? Kāyaduccarite vā vacīduccarite vā manoduccarite vā pañcasu vā kāmaguṇesu cittassa vossaggo vossaggānuppadānaṃ kusalānaṃ vā dhammānaṃ bhāvanāya asakkaccakiriyatā asātaccakiriyatā anaṭṭhitakiriyatā olīnavuttitā nikkhittachandatā nikkhittadhuratā anāsevanā abhāvanā abahulīkammaṃ anadhiṭṭhānaṃ ananuyogo pamādo, yo evarūpo pamādo pamajjanā pamajjitattaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati “pamādo”.**  
> 846. Which is negligence (pamādo) there? The relinquishment of the mind or the repeated abandonment of the mind in bodily misconduct, verbal misconduct, mental misconduct, or in the five cords of sensual pleasure; the lack of diligent performance in the development of wholesome states; the lack of persistent practice; not acting firmly; having a shrinking attitude; putting aside desire; putting aside the burden; non-cultivation; non-development; non-frequent practice; the lack of resolution; non-pursuit—whatever such negligence, heedlessness, or the state of being heedless there is—this is called negligence (pamādo).  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)* ([Appamāda](/glossary/appamada/) Vagga, v. 21) – "Heedlessness is the path to death; the heedless are as if already dead."
* **Sutta**: A Saṃyutta Nikāya sutta (para "128.", S-i 1498:1-1501:3) in which the Buddha tells King Pasenadi that heedfulness (*appamādo*) is the one thing that secures both the benefit in this life and the benefit in the next.
* **Sutta**: A Saṃyutta Nikāya sutta at the Buddha's parinibbāna (para "186.", S-i 2345:1-4) – "vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādethā" (his last words).
* **Abhidhamma**: *Vibhaṅga* (Khuddakavatthuvibhaṅga, Vibh §846, Vibh 2553:2).
* **Commentary**: *Papañcasūdanī* on the Vatthasutta (Ps-i 565:2, 566:12: the intoxicated one "falls into heedlessness among the five cords of sensual pleasure"); and the *Dhammapada-Aṭṭhakathā* on Dhp 21 (narrative stories; not checked in detail).
