### Grammatical Analysis
**Tisaraṇa**: [nt.] The Three Refuges; Triple Refuge. Formed by *ti* (three) + *saraṇa* (refuge, shelter, protection). The Aṭṭhakathā derives *saraṇa* from *hiṃsati* (it strikes down): for those who have gone for refuge, through that very going it strikes down, dissipates and removes fear, terror, suffering, a bad destination and agitation (*hiṃsatīti saraṇaṃ*, Pj-i 62:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Tisaraṇa* (also *saraṇattayaṃ*) is the going for refuge to the **Buddha**, the **[Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/)** and the **[Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)**, in the canonical formula *Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, Dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, Saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi* (Khp 5:1-7:1), repeated a second and third time (Khp 8:1-10:1). In the Vinaya it is the way a person becomes a lay follower, "gone for refuge" (*saraṇaṃ gato*): the merchants Tapussa and Bhallika went for refuge to the Buddha and the Dhamma and were the first lay followers using the two-word formula (*dvevācikā*, Vin-iii 41:17-18), and Yasa's father, the merchant householder (*seṭṭhi gahapati*), was the first lay follower using the three-word formula (*paṭhamaṃ upāsako ahosi tevāciko*, Vin-iii 160:17-19). The same refuges are the first step of the going forth (Vin-iii 598:18-23).

The Paramatthajotikā defines the going for refuge as a state of mind (*cittuppādo*), arisen through faith and respect (*tappasādataggarutāhi*) towards the Three Jewels, in which defilements are struck down (Pj-i 62:4). It explains the Buddha as one who personally awakened to the truths and attained omniscience and mastery of the powers (Pj-i 55:1).

On breaking and defilement: the Aṭṭhakathā says the break of the going for refuge is of two kinds, blameworthy and blameless. The blameless one is at death; the blameworthy one is conduct towards another teacher, or conduct contrary to what the Teacher taught; both occur only for ordinary people (*puthujjanā*). Their refuge becomes defiled (*saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ*) through not knowing, doubting or wrong knowledge regarding the Buddha's qualities, or through disrespect and so on (Pj-i 65:1-4). Those who have gone for refuge with the defilements of the going cut off will not go to a lower realm (Pj-i 69:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Khuddakapāṭha)
> **Saraṇattayaṃ Buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi; Dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi; Saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi.**  
> The Three Refuges. I go for refuge to the Buddha. I go for refuge to the Dhamma. I go for refuge to the Saṅgha.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya)
> **“abhikkantaṃ, bhante, abhikkantaṃ, bhante, seyyathāpi, bhante, nikkujjitaṃ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṃ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṃ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṃ dhāreyya – cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantīti – evamevaṃ bhagavatā anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Esāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, dhammañca, bhikkhusaṅghañca. Upāsakaṃ maṃ bhagavā dhāretu ajjatagge pāṇupetaṃ saraṇaṃ gatan” ti. Sova loke paṭhamaṃ upāsako ahosi tevāciko.**  
> ‘Magnificent, Lord! Magnificent, Lord! Just as one might set upright what has been upset, or might reveal what was hidden, or might point out the way to one who was lost, or might bring a lamp into the darkness thinking: “Those with eyes may see forms,” even so has the Dhamma been declared in many ways by the Blessed One.’ ‘I go to the Blessed One for refuge, and to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha of bhikkhus.’ ‘Let the Blessed One accept me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forth as long as life endures.’ Thus he came to be the first lay follower in the world using the three-word formula.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthajotikā)
> **Idāni saraṇagamanādīsu hiṃsatīti saraṇaṃ, saraṇagatānaṃ teneva saraṇagamanena bhayaṃ santāsaṃ dukkhaṃ duggatiṃ parikkilesaṃ hiṃsati vidhamati nīharati nirodhetīti attho. Tappasādataggarutāhi vihataviddhaṃsitakileso tapparāyaṇatākārappavatto aparappaccayo vā cittuppādo saraṇagamanaṃ.**  
> Now, among regarding going for refuge, it is called a ‘refuge’ (saraṇa) because it strikes down; the meaning is that for those who have gone for refuge, through that very act of going for refuge, it strikes down, dissipates, removes, and suppresses fear, terror, suffering, a bad destination, and repeated agitation. The going for refuge is a state of mind—whether independent of others or dependent on others—that arises in the mode of being directed toward those, in which defilements are struck down and destroyed through faith and respect for them.  
>  
> — [Paramatthajotikā](/glossary/paramatthajotika/) (Pj-i 62) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_01&page_number=6&name=khuddakap%C4%81%E1%B9%ADhaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SWTEgW5pIHNhcmHhuYdhZ2FtYW7EgWTEq3N1IGhp4bmDc2F0xKt0aSBzYXJh4bmHYeG5gywgc2FyYeG5h2FnYXTEgW5h4bmDIHRlbmV2YQ%3D%3D)
