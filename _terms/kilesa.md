### Grammatical Analysis
**Kilesa**: [m.] defilement. The commentary explains: "they are called *kilesā* because they soil (*kilissanti*) and torment (*upatāpenti*); they are the ten beginning with lust (*rāgādayo dasa*)" (Bv-a 1007:6).

### Orthodox Definition
*Kilesas* are the defilements that soil and torment the mind. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī (Kilesagocchaka) answers "What are the defilements?" with ten defilement-bases (*dasa kilesavatthūni*) (Dhs 2028:1-2; also Vibh 2483:2):

1. *[Lobha](/glossary/lobha/)* (greed)
2. *[Dosa](/glossary/dosa/)* (hatred)
3. *[Moha](/glossary/moha/)* (delusion)
4. *[Māna](/glossary/mana/)* (conceit)
5. *[Diṭṭhi](/glossary/ditthi/)* (views)
6. *[Vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)* (doubt)
7. *Thina* (sloth)
8. *[Uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)* (restlessness)
9. *[Ahirika](/glossary/ahirika/)* (shamelessness)
10. *[Anottappa](/glossary/anottappa/)* (lack of moral dread)

The Atthasālinī explains that in the Kilesagocchaka the defilements themselves are the "defilement-bases" (*kilesā eva kilesavatthūni*), because beings whose taints are not extinguished dwell established in greed and the rest (As 2331:1-3). It also says that greed, when it arrives at the Cluster of Defilements, is called a defilement (*kilesagocchakaṃ patvā lobho kileso nāma hoti*, As 2288:7).

In the suttas the Buddha says that the mind is luminous (*pabhassaraṃ*) but defiled by adventitious defilements (*āgantukehi upakkilesehi upakkiliṭṭhaṃ*, AN 1.49, A-i 62:1-2); see *[upakkilesa](/glossary/upakkilesa/)* and *[anusaya](/glossary/anusaya/)*.

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā kilesā? Dasa kilesavatthūni – lobho, doso, moho, māno, diṭṭhi, vicikicchā, thinaṃ, uddhaccaṃ, ahirīkaṃ, anottappaṃ.**  
> 1235. What mental phenomena are defilements? There are ten defilement-bases: greed, hatred, delusion, conceit, view, doubt, mental inertia, restlessness, shamelessness, and lack of fear of wrongdoing.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Kilesagocchaka, §1235) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=243&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTIzNS4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEga2lsZXPEgT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā, definition)
> **Kileseti kilissanti upatāpentīti kilesā, rāgādayo dasa.**  
> Defilements (kilese) are called defilements because they soil (kilissanti) and cause suffering (upatāpenti); they are the ten, such as desire (rāgādayo dasa).  
>  
> — Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā (Madhuratthavilāsinī) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_12&page_number=114&name=buddhava%E1%B9%83saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2lsZXNldGkga2lsaXNzYW50aSB1cGF0xIFwZW50xKt0aSBraWxlc8SBLCByxIFnxIFkYXlvIGRhc2Eu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, Kilesagocchaka)
> **Kilesagocchake kilesā eva kilesavatthūni. Vasanti vā ettha akhīṇāsavā sattā lobhādīsu patiṭṭhitattāti ‘ vatthūni ’. Kilesā ca te tappatiṭṭhānaṃ sattānaṃ vatthūni cāti ‘ kilesavatthūni ’.**  
> 1235. In the cluster of defilements, the defilements themselves are defilement-bases. Or, because beings whose taints have not been extinguished dwell here, being established in greed and so on, therefore they are called ‘bases’. And because they are defilements, and they are also the bases for beings who are established in them, thus they are called ‘defilement-bases’.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, §1235) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=416&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MTIzNS4gS2lsZXNhZ29jY2hha2Uga2lsZXPEgSBldmEga2lsZXNhdmF0dGjFq25pLg%3D%3D)
