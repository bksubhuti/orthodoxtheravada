### Grammatical Analysis
**Muñcitukamyatāñāṇa**: [nt.] knowledge of the desire for deliverance. The Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā explains: "he desires, wants to release, to let go (*muñcituṃ cajituṃ kāmeti icchatīti*), so he is *muñcitukāmo*; the state of one who wants to release is *muñcitukamyatā*" (Paṭis-a 126:1). With *ñāṇa* (knowledge).

### Orthodox Definition
*Muñcitukamyatāñāṇa* is one of the insight knowledges, arising after the knowledge of disenchantment (*nibbidāñāṇa*). In the Paṭisambhidāmagga, in the section on the knowledge of equanimity about formations, the wisdom of "desire for deliverance, reflecting and composure" (*muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā*) is said, for arising, occurrence, sign, accumulation and so on, to be knowledge of equanimity about formations (Paṭis 332:1-2).

The Visuddhimagga explains how it arises: by the knowledge of disenchantment, the mind of this clansman, becoming disenchanted and dissatisfied, does not cling to even one formation in any existence, womb, destination, station of consciousness or abode of beings; it wants to be freed and to escape from all formations. Like a fish in a net, a frog in a snake's mouth, a wild fowl in a cage, a deer in a strong snare, a snake in a snake-charmer's hand, an elephant in a great bog, a nāga king in a supaṇṇa's mouth, the moon in Rāhu's mouth, or a man surrounded by enemies, who want to be freed and escape from each, so the yogi's mind wants to be freed from all formations. Then, in one whose attachment to all formations has vanished, the knowledge of desire for deliverance arises (Vism-ii 1751:1-4). The Paṭisambhidāmagga commentary gives the same account (Paṭis-a 127:1, 127:4).

Uppādaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ, pavattaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ, nimittaṃ muñcitukamyatā … pe … āyūhanaṃ muñcitukamyatā … paṭisandhiṃ muñcitukamyatā … gatiṃ muñcitukamyatā … nibbattiṃ muñcitukamyatā … upapattiṃ muñcitukamyatā … jātiṃ muñcitukamyatā … jaraṃ muñcitukamyatā … byādhiṃ muñcitukamyatā … maraṇaṃ muñcitukamyatā … sokaṃ muñcitukamyatā … paridevaṃ muñcitukamyatā … pe … upāyāsaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ.**  
> 54. How is it that wisdom of desire for deliverance, of reflecting and of composure, is knowledge of equanimity about formations? Wisdom of desire for deliverance from arising, of reflecting and of composure, is knowledge of equanimity about formations; wisdom of desire for deliverance from occurrence, of reflecting and of composure, is knowledge of equanimity about formations; wisdom of desire for deliverance from the sign … pe … from accumulation … from rebirth-linking … from destination … from production … from reappearing … from birth … from old age … from sickness … from death … from grief … from lamentation … pe … from despair, of reflecting and of composure, is knowledge of equanimity about formations.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇanti muñcituṃ cajituṃ kāmeti icchatīti muñcitukāmo, muñcitukāmassa bhāvo muñcitukamyatā.**  
> 9. In 'the wisdom that is the knowledge of desire for deliverance, reflection, and equanimity regarding formations,' desiring, wanting to release, is 'desire for deliverance,' and the state of one wanting to release is 'desire for deliverance' (muñcitukamyatā).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, Ch. XXI)
> **Muñcitukamyatāñāṇakathā 756. Iminā pana nibbidāñāṇena imassa kulaputtassa nibbindantassa ukkaṇṭhantassa anabhiramantassa sabbabhavayonigativiññāṇaṭṭhitisattāvāsagatesu sabhedakesu saṅkhāresu ekasaṅkhārepi cittaṃ na sajjati, na laggati, na bajjhati, sabbasmā saṅkhāragatā muccitukāmaṃ nissaritukāmaṃ hoti. Yathā kiṃ? Yathā nāma jālabbhantaragato maccho, sappamukhagato maṇḍūko, pañjarapakkhitto vanakukkuṭo, daḷhapāsavasagato migo, ahituṇḍikahatthagato sappo, mahāpaṅkapakkhando kuñjaro, supaṇṇamukhagato nāgarājā, rāhumukhappaviṭṭho cando, sapattaparivārito purisoti evamādayo tato tato muccitukāmā nissaritukāmāva honti, evaṃ tassa yogino cittaṃ sabbasmā saṅkhāragatā muccitukāmaṃ nissaritukāmaṃ hoti. Athassa evaṃ sabbasaṅkhāresu vigatālayassa sabbasmā saṅkhāragatā muccitukāmassa uppajjati muñcitukamyatā ñāṇanti.**  
> Explanation of the Knowledge of Desire for Deliverance 756. Now, for this clansman who, through this knowledge of disenchantment, is becoming disenchanted, dissatisfied, and without delight in the manifold categories of formations belonging to any becoming, generation, destiny, station, or abode—his mind no longer gets caught up (na sajjati) in, cleaves to, or is bound (na bajjhati) by even a single formation; he is desiring to be freed (muccitukāmaṃ) and desiring to escape from the whole category of formations. Just like what? Just as a fish in a net, a frog in a snake’s mouth, a wild fowl (vanakukkuṭo) in a cage, a deer caught in a strong snare, a snake in a snake-charmer’s hands, an elephant in a great bog, a naga king in a supaṇṇa's mouth, the moon inside Rāhu’s mouth, or a man surrounded by enemies—just as such beings are desiring to be freed and desiring to escape from their respective situations, just so this meditator’s mind is desiring to be freed and desiring to escape from the whole category of formations. Then, in one whose attachment to all formations has thus vanished and who wishes to be freed from the whole category of formations, the knowledge of desire for deliverance arises.  
>
