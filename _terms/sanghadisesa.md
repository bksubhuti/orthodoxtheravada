### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṅghādisesa**: [m.] An offense requiring the Saṅgha at the beginning and in the rest of the procedure. Formed by *saṅgha* (the order) + *ādi* (beginning) + *sesa* (the rest). The Samantapāsādikā gives the analysis: *saṅgho ādimhi ceva sese ca icchitabbo assāti saṅghādiseso*, "a saṅghādisesa is that for which the Saṅgha is required at the beginning and in the rest": at the beginning to give probation (*parivāsa*), in the rest to give *mānatta* (with or without pulling back to the beginning, *mūlāya paṭikassana*), and at the end for rehabilitation (*abbhāna*) (Sp-i 2045).

### Orthodox Definition
*Saṅghādisesa* is the second of the classes of offence listed in the Vinaya, after *[Pārājika](/glossary/parajika/)* (Vin-v 1511: *pārājikāpatti, saṅghādisesāpatti, pācittiyāpatti, pāṭidesanīyāpatti, dukkaṭāpatti*). There are **thirteen** saṅghādisesa rules for monks (nine are offences at once, *paṭhamāpattikā*, and four after the third announcement, *yāvatatiyakā*; Vin-i 1841, Dvem-bhk 61), and **seventeen** for nuns (Dvem-bhni 598). Unlike a Pārājika, it can be cleared.

The Vinaya's own definition (Vin-i 1832) is: *saṅghova tassā āpattiyā parivāsaṃ deti, mūlāya paṭikassati, mānattaṃ deti, abbheti; na sambahulā, na ekapuggalo*, "the Saṅgha itself gives probation for that offence, pulls back to the beginning, gives mānatta, and rehabilitates; not many monks, not an individual; therefore it is called saṅghādisesa".

The rules of the monks begin with the intentional emission of semen (*sukkavissaṭṭhi*, the first rule, Vin-i 1029), go through bodily contact with a woman (*kāyasaṃsagga*, the second, Vin-i 1173), acting as a go-between (*sañcaritta*, the fifth, Vin-i 1458) and building a hut (*kuṭikāra*, the sixth, Vin-i 1563), to causing a schism in the Saṅgha (*saṅghabheda*, the tenth, Vin-i 1750) and the thirteenth, the corrupter of families (*kuladūsaka*, Vin-i 1840).

The procedure is given in the closing formula of the Pātimokkha section (Vin-i 1841):
1. The monk who commits any one of them and knowingly conceals it must undergo probation (*parivāsa*) for as many days as he concealed it, "against his will" (*akāmā*). The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī says this means "not by his own wish, but without his own wish he must undertake the probation", and that the probation is threefold: concealed-probation (*paṭicchannaparivāsa*), purification-probation (*suddhantaparivāsa*) and composite-probation (*samodhānaparivāsa*) (Kkh 1428).
2. After the probation he must undertake *mānatta* for a further six nights (*chārattaṃ*).
3. When the *mānatta* is completed he is rehabilitated (*abbhetabbo*) in a Saṅgha of twenty bhikkhus (*vīsatigaṇo*). If the Saṅgha is even one short of twenty, he is not rehabilitated, and those bhikkhus are blameworthy.

### Quote (Mūla: Suttavibhaṅga, the definition)
> **Saṅghādisesoti saṅghova tassā āpattiyā parivāsaṃ deti, mūlāya paṭikassati, mānattaṃ deti, abbheti; na sambahulā, na ekapuggalo. Tena vuccati – ‘ saṅghādiseso ’ ti, tasseva āpattinikāyassa nāmakammaṃ adhivacanaṃ.**  
> Saṅghādisesa offense: the Sangha itself gives probation for that offense, pulls back to the beginning, gives mānatta, rehabilitates; not many monks, not an individual. Therefore it is called – ‘saṅghādisesa offense’ – this is the name, the designation for that class of offense.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Pātimokkha formula)
> **Uddiṭṭhā kho, āyasmanto, terasa saṅghādisesā dhammā, nava paṭhamāpattikā, cattāro yāvatatiyakā. Yesaṃ bhikkhu aññataraṃ vā aññataraṃ vā āpajjitvā yāvatīhaṃ jānaṃ paṭicchādeti tāvatīhaṃ tena bhikkhunā akāmā parivatthabbaṃ. Parivutthaparivāsena bhikkhunā uttari chārattaṃ bhikkhumānattāya paṭipajjitabbaṃ. Ciṇṇamānatto bhikkhu yattha siyā vīsatigaṇo bhikkhusaṅgho tattha so bhikkhu abbhetabbo. Ekenapi ce ūno vīsatigaṇo bhikkhusaṅgho taṃ bhikkhuṃ abbheyya, so ca bhikkhu anabbhito, te ca bhikkhū gārayhā, ayaṃ tattha sāmīci.**  
> 442. It has been proclaimed, venerable sirs, the thirteen saṅghādisesa phenomena: nine are immediate offenses, four are offenses after the third announcement. If a bhikkhu commits any one of them, and knowingly conceals it for as many days as he knows, for that many days he must undergo probation. After completing the probation, the bhikkhu must undertake the trial period for a further six days. When the mānatta is completed, if there is a Sangha of twenty bhikkhus, that bhikkhu should be rehabilitated. If the Sangha has even one less than twenty bhikkhus and rehabilitates that bhikkhu, that bhikkhu is not rehabilitated, and those bhikkhus are blameworthy. This is the proper procedure.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Vacanattho panettha saṅgho ādimhi ceva sese ca icchitabbo assāti saṅghādiseso. Kiṃ vuttaṃ hoti? Imaṃ āpattiṃ āpajjitvā vuṭṭhātukāmassa yaṃ taṃ āpattivuṭṭhānaṃ, tassa ādimhi ceva parivāsadānatthāya ādito sese ca majjhe mānattadānatthāya mūlāya paṭikassanena vā saha mānattadānatthāya avasāne abbhānatthāya saṅgho icchitabbo. Na hettha ekampi kammaṃ vinā saṅghena sakkā kātunti saṅgho ādimhi ceva sese ca icchitabbo assāti saṅghādisesoti.**  
> But the meaning of the word here is: a saṅghādisesa is that for which the Saṅgha is required at the beginning and in the rest. What is meant by this? For one who has committed this offense and wishes to rehabilitate himself, in whatever is the rehabilitation from the offense, the Saṅgha is required at the beginning for the purpose of giving probation, and in the rest after the beginning: in the middle for the purpose of giving the mānatta discipline—or together with pulling back to the beginning for the purpose of giving the mānatta—and at the end for the purpose of rehabilitation. For herein, not even a single formal act can be done without the Saṅgha; thus, it is a saṅghādisesa because the Saṅgha is required at the beginning and in the rest.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Tāvatīhanti tattakāni ahāni, paṭicchāditadivasato paṭṭhāya yāva ārocitadivaso, tāva divasapakkhamāsasaṃvaccharavasena yattako kālo atikkanto, tattakaṃ kālanti attho. Akāmā parivatthabbanti na kāmena na vasena, atha kho akāmena avasena parivāsaṃ samādāya vatthabbaṃ. Tattha paṭicchannaparivāso suddhantaparivāso samodhānaparivāso cāti tividho parivāso.**  
> 'For as many days'—that many days in duration, starting from the day it was concealed up to the day it was reported or made manifest; however many days, fortnights, months, or years have passed, that is the duration. 'Must undergo probation even without prior intention' means he must not undergo probation on his own terms, but rather, without his own preference, he must undertake the probation and dwell (therein). Here, probation is threefold: concealed-probation, purification-probation, and composite-probation.  
>
