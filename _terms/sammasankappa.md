### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammāsaṅkappa**: [m.] Right Intention; Right Resolve; Correct Thought. Formed by *sammā* (correctly) + *saṅkappa* (thought, purpose, mental application). Doctrinally it is the mental factor of applied thought (*[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)-cetasika*): the Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it with the words *takko vitakko saṅkappo … abhiniropanā* (Dhs §298), as a path factor.

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammāsaṅkappa* is the second limb of the Noble Eightfold Path, completing the wisdom division (*[paññā](/glossary/panna/)-sikkhā*). While Right View provides the correct cognitive map of reality, Right Intention is the directing of the mind onto its object (*abhiniropana*), so that it moves along that map.

The Buddha defined Right Intention as **three wholesome thoughts**:
1. *[Nekkhamma](/glossary/nekkhamma/)-saṅkappa* (Thought of Renunciation): Resolving to step away from the trapping pull of sensuality. The opposite of sensual thought (*kāmavitakka*).
2. *Abyāpāda-saṅkappa* (Thought of Non-Ill-Will): Resolving to maintain thoughts of universal kindness. The opposite of ill-will thought (*byāpādavitakka*).
3. *Avihiṃsā-saṅkappa* (Thought of Harmlessness): Resolving not to harm beings. The opposite of harmful thought (*vihiṃsāvitakka*).

The Aṭṭhakathā gives its characteristic as *sammā-abhiniropana* ("applying the mind correctly", It-a 637). It explains *sammāsaṅkappo* as the intention (*saṅkappo*) that, through truthfulness (*yāthāvatā*) and its power to lead out (*niyyānikatā*), has reached wholesomeness and is praised (As 1311). The Visuddhimagga describes it for the one who has right view as the mind's directing onto the state of Nibbāna (*cetaso nibbānapadābhiniropanaṃ*), which strikes down wrong intention (Vism-ii 785).

### Quote
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo? Nekkhammasaṅkappo abyāpādasaṅkappo avihiṃsāsaṅkappo, ayaṃ vuccati bhikkhave, sammāsaṅkappo.**  
> “Now what, monks, is right thought? The thought of renunciation, the thought of good-will, the thought of non-violence. This, monks, is called right thought.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=249&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYW1txIFzYeG5hWthcHBvPw%3D%3D)

> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye sammāsaṅkappo hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā sammāsaṅkappo maggaṅgaṃ maggapariyāpannaṃ – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye sammāsaṅkappo hoti.**  
> 298. What is right intention at that time? Whatever thought, applied thought, intention, effecting of an aim, strong effecting of an aim, directing the mind onto, right intention, being a path factor, included in the path exists at that time—this is right intention at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§298, Abhidhamma) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=76&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mjk4LiBLYXRhbW8gdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIHNhbW3EgXNh4bmFa2FwcG8gaG90aT8%3D)

> **Ārammaṇe cittaṃ abhiniropeti patiṭṭhāpetīti cetaso abhiniropanā. Yāthāvatāya niyyānikatāya ca kusalabhāvappatto pasattho saṅkappoti sammāsaṅkappo.**  
> Directing the mind onto (cetaso abhiniropanā) means applying the mind (cittaṃ) to the object (ārammaṇaṃ) and establishing it. Right intention (sammāsaṅkappo) is the intention (saṅkappo) that, due to its truthfulness and its liberating nature, has attained the state of wholesomeness and is praised (pasattho).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=187&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=xIByYW1tYeG5h2UgY2l0dGHhuYMgYWJoaW5pcm9wZXRpIHBhdGnhua3hua1oxIFwZXTEq3RpIGNldGFzbyBhYmhpbmlyb3BhbsSBLg%3D%3D)

> **“Sammādassanalakkhaṇā sammādiṭṭhi, sammāabhiniropanalakkhaṇo sammāsaṅkappo, sammāpariggahaṇalakkhaṇā sammāvācā, sammāsamuṭṭhāpanalakkhaṇo sammākammanto, sammāvodānalakkhaṇo sammāājīvo, sammāpaggahalakkhaṇo sammāvāyāmo, sammāupaṭṭhānalakkhaṇā sammāsati, sammāavikkhepalakkhaṇo sammāsamādhī” ti evaṃ visesalakkhaṇato.**  
> ‘Right view has the characteristic of seeing correctly; right intention has the characteristic of applying the mind correctly; right speech has the characteristic of taking up correctly; right action has the characteristic of establishing correctly; right livelihood has the characteristic of purifying correctly; right effort has the characteristic of exerting correctly; right mindfulness has the characteristic of establishing correctly; right concentration has the characteristic of non-distraction;’ thus, by the specific characteristic (visesalakkhaṇato).  
>  
> — Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā (Aṭṭhakathā, on the eightfold path) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=125&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNhbW3EgWRhc3NhbmFsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBzYW1txIFkaeG5reG5rWhpLCBzYW1txIFhYmhpbmlyb3BhbmFsYWtraGHhuYdvIHNhbW3EgXNh4bmFa2FwcG8sIHNhbW3EgXBhcmlnZ2FoYeG5h2FsYWtraGHhuYfEgQ%3D%3D)

> **Tathā sampannadiṭṭhino taṃsampayuttaṃ micchāsaṅkappanighātakaṃ cetaso nibbānapadābhiniropanaṃ sammāsaṅkappo.**  
> For one whose view is thus perfected, that which is associated with it, which strikes down wrong thought and applies the mind to the state of Nibbāna, is right thought (sammāsaṅkappo).  
>
