### Grammatical Analysis
**Sotāpanna**: [m.; f.] a stream-enterer. Formed by *sota* (stream, current) + *āpanna* (entered, gone into). The Mūla identifies the stream: "the stream is simply this noble eightfold path" (*ayameva hi, bhante, ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo soto*, S-v 2042); the Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā explains "*sotāpanno* means one who has entered the stream (*sotaṃ āpanno*); *soto* is a designation for the path; *sotāpanno* is the individual who possesses it" (Ps-i 824).

### Orthodox Definition
A *Sotāpanna* is the first of the noble persons (*ariyapuggala*). The Mūla says who he is in two ways: as one "who, through the destruction of three fetters (*tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā*), is a stream-enterer, not liable to the lower states (*avinipātadhammo*), assured (*niyato*), with full awakening as his destination (*sambodhiparāyaṇo*)" (A-iv 896); and as one who "is possessed of this noble eightfold path" (S-v 2042). The three fetters are named by the Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā as identity view, doubt and attachment to rules and vows (*sakkāyadiṭṭhivicikicchāsīlabbataparāmāsa*, Ps-i 824):
1. *Sakkāya-diṭṭhi*: identity view.
2. *[Vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)*: doubt.
3. *Sīlabbata-parāmāsa*: clinging to rules and observances.

The Ratana Sutta says that with his "attainment of vision" (*dassanasampadā*) three things are abandoned: identity view, doubt and any clinging to rules and observances; he is "freed from the four states of woe" and "incapable of committing the six major wrongdoings" (Sn 796–799, Khp 10–11). The Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā explains the four states of woe as hell, the animal realm, the realm of ghosts and the asura host, from which he is free even while taking up to seven more existences, and the six major wrongdoings (*abhiṭhānāni*, coarse acts) as killing one's mother, killing one's father, killing an arahant, shedding the blood of a Tathāgata, causing a schism in the Saṅgha, and professing another teacher (Pj-i 885–886).

He has unshakeable confidence (*aveccappasāda*) in the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha, and is "perfect in virtue loved by the noble ones" (*ariyakantehi sīlehi*): these are the four factors of stream-entry (A-ix 221–224). The Puggalapaññatti names three kinds of stream-enterer, the *sattakkhattuparamo* ("seven times at most"), the *kolaṅkolo* ("from family to family") and the *ekabījī* ("one seed"): the first "runs on and transmigrates seven times among devas and humans and makes an end of suffering" (Pp 221–222).

### Quote (Mūla: Suttanipāta, the Ratana Sutta)
> **Sahāvassa dassanasampadāya, tayassu dhammā jahitā bhavanti; Sakkāyadiṭṭhi vicikicchitañca, sīlabbataṃ vāpi yadatthi kiñci. 234. Catūhapāyehi ca vippamutto, chaccābhiṭhānāni bhabba kātuṃ; Idampi saṅghe ratanaṃ paṇītaṃ, etena saccena suvatthi hotu.**  
> Along with their insight-attainment, these three things are discarded utterly: embodiment-view and doubt, and clinging to vain rites and empty vows, whatever there may be. Escaped that one from all four evil states, and of the six great sins incapable. Yea, in the Saṅgha shines this glorious jewel: By virtue of this truth, may blessing be!  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, the stream)
> **‘“Soto, soto ’ ti hidaṃ, sāriputta, vuccati. Katamo nu kho, sāriputta, soto” ti? “Ayameva hi, bhante, ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo soto, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati, sammāsamādhī” ti.**  
> “Sāriputta, they speak of ‘the stream’. What, Sāriputta, is the stream?” “Venerable sir, the stream is simply this noble eight-factored path, that is to say: right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the formula)
> **Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā sotāpanno hoti avinipātadhammo niyato sambodhiparāyaṇo.**  
> Here, monks, a bhikkhu, after having completely destroyed the Three Bonds, is a stream-enterer, not subject to downfall, assured of supreme Awakening.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Puggalapaññatti)
> **Katamo ca puggalo sattakkhattuparamo? Idhekacco puggalo tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā sotāpanno hoti avinipātadhammo niyato sambodhiparāyano. So sattakkhattuṃ deve ca mānuse ca sandhāvitvā saṃsaritvā dukkhassantaṃ karoti – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo “sattakkhattuparamo”.**  
> 31. What sort of person is he who undergoes re-birth (not more than) seven times? Here a person, having completely destroyed the three fetters, becomes a “stream-enterer”; he is no more liable to fall into a woeful state, but is assured, and has supreme awakening as his final end. He running on and transmigrating seven times amongst devas and men makes an end of suffering. Such a person is said to be one not undergoing re-birth more than seven times.  
>  
> — [Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/) (Tikaniddesa, §31) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_04&page_number=118&name=puggalapa%C3%B1%C3%B1attip%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzEuIEthdGFtbyBjYSBwdWdnYWxvIHNhdHRha2toYXR0dXBhcmFtbz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Navamavāre tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānanti sakkāyadiṭṭhivicikicchāsīlabbataparāmāsasaṅkhātānaṃ tiṇṇaṃ bandhanānaṃ. Tāni hi saṃyojenti khandhagatibhavādīhi khandhagatibhavādayo, kammaṃ vā phalena, tasmā saṃyojanānīti vuccanti, bandhanānīti attho. Parikkhayāti parikkhayena. Sotāpannoti sotaṃ āpanno. Sototi ca maggassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Sotāpannoti taṃsamaṅgipuggalassa.**  
> 67. In the ninth section, 'the three fetters' (tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ) refers to the three bonds known as identity view, doubt, and attachment to rules and vows. For they fetter the aggregates, destinations, and existences to other aggregates, destinations, and existences; or they fetter deeds to their result. Therefore they are called 'fetters' (saṃyojana), meaning bonds. 'Destruction' (parikkhayā) means by destruction. 'Stream-enterer' (sotāpanno) means one who has entered the stream. This is a designation for the path. 'Stream-enterer' is a designation for the individual associated with that.  
>  
> — *Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=166&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=NjcuIE5hdmFtYXbEgXJlIHRp4bmH4bmHYeG5gyBzYeG5g3lvamFuxIFuYW50aSBzYWtrxIF5YWRp4bmt4bmtaGl2aWNpa2ljY2jEgXPEq2xhYmJhdGFwYXLEgW3EgXNhc2HhuYVraMSBdMSBbmHhuYMgdGnhuYfhuYdh4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Evamassa vipākavaṭṭappahānaṃ dassetvā idāni yamassa vipākavaṭṭassa mūlabhūtaṃ kammavaṭṭaṃ, tassāpi pahānaṃ dassento āha “chaccābhiṭhānāni abhabba kātun”ti. Tattha abhiṭhānānīti oḷārikaṭṭhānāni, tāni esa cha abhabbo kātuṃ. Tāni ca “aṭṭhānametaṃ, bhikkhave, anavakāso, yaṃ diṭṭhisampanno puggalo mātaraṃ jīvitā voropeyyā” tiādinā (a. ni.1.271; ma. ni.3.128; vibha.809) nayena ekakanipāte vuttāni mātughātapitughātaarahantaghātalohituppādasaṅghabhedaaññasatthāruddesakammānīti veditabbāni.**  
> Having thus shown the abandonment of the resultant-cycle for him, he now explained the abandonment of the kamma-cycle, which is the root of that resultant-cycle, by saying: “and therefore, incapable of committing the six major wrongdoings.” Regarding that, “major wrongdoings” (abhiṭhānāni) means: coarse actions; he is incapable of performing these six. And these should be understood as the deeds—killing one's mother, killing one's father, killing an arahant, shedding the blood of a Tathāgata, causing a schism in the Saṅgha, and identifying another teacher—which are stated in the Single-section by the method: “It is impossible, monks, it cannot happen, that a noble disciple should deprive his mother of life.”  
>
