### Grammatical Analysis
**Vīriyapāramī**: [f.] The Perfection of Energy. Formed by *[vīriya](/glossary/viriya/)* (energy, vigour, effort) + *[pāramī](/glossary/parami/)* (perfection). The Tipiṭaka spells it *vīriyapāramī* (also *vīriyapāramitā*, *vīriyapāramiṃ*); the form *viriyapāramī* does not occur in the texts. The Mūla has *pañcamaṃ vīriyapāramiṃ*, "the fifth perfection of energy" (Bv verse 136).

### Orthodox Definition
*[Vīriya](/glossary/viriya/)-[pāramī](/glossary/parami/)* is the fifth of the ten perfections (Bv 671:1; the order dāna, sīla, nekkhamma, paññā, vīriya, … is given in Cp-a 1910:1-11).

The Aṭṭhakathā gives its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: *ussāhalakkhaṇā vīriyapāramī, upatthambhanarasā, asaṃsīdanapaccupaṭṭhānā, vīriyārambhavatthupadaṭṭhānā, saṃvegapadaṭṭhānā vā*, that is, it has the characteristic of exertion, the function of upholding, the manifestation of not sinking down, and as proximate cause the grounds for arousing energy, or a sense of urgency (Cp-a 1910:6). Elsewhere it is defined as bodily and mental effort for the welfare of others, grasped through compassion and skill in means (*karuṇūpāyakosallapariggahito kāyacittehi parahitārambho vīriyapāramitā*; Cp-a 1909:3). The Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā says the Bodhisatta fulfilled it in countless existences, as King Mahāsīlava, Prince Pañcāvudha, the Great Vānarinda and others, and without doubt as Mahājanaka (Cp-a 1856:3-4).

In the Mahājanaka example the Bodhisatta, in the middle of the ocean where all the others perished, did not see the shore, yet kept the mind unchanged: *cittassa aññathā natthi, esā me vīriyapāramī* (Cp-a 1857-1858; As 792:1-793:1). The Aṭṭhakathā on the Jātaka says his body was afflicted by the salt water for seven days (Ja-a-vi 555:3), and the Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā says his perfection of energy, as he crossed the great ocean, became an ultimate perfection (*paramatthapāramī*; Cp-a 1859:1).

The Buddha himself says of his striving: "Gladly would my skin, sinews and bones remain, let the flesh and blood in my body dry up; there will be no rest from energy until I have attained what can be attained by a man's strength, energy and endeavour" (AN 2.5, A-ii 19:3, second Quote).

The Abhidhamma defines the underlying mental factor, *vīriya* (*vīriyārambho nikkamo parakkamo uyyāmo vāyāmo ussāho …*, Dhs §13, Dhs 403:2). The Aṭṭhakathā on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī also places the Buddha's search for the Dhamma across four asaṅkheyyas and a hundred thousand aeons (As 111:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: lakkhaṇa, rasa, paccupaṭṭhāna, padaṭṭhāna)
> **Ussāhalakkhaṇā vīriyapāramī , upatthambhanarasā, asaṃsīdanapaccupaṭṭhānā, vīriyārambhavatthupadaṭṭhānā, saṃvegapadaṭṭhānā vā.**  
> The perfection of energy (vīriyapāramī) has: (1) effort as its characteristic; (2) the function of supporting; (3) non-lapsing as its manifestation; and (4) the object of arousing energy, or a sense of urgency, as its proximate cause.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: the Mahājanaka example)
> **Vīriyaṃ katvāna uttamanti sammāsambodhiṃ pāpetuṃ samatthatāya uttamaṃ padhānaṃ vīriyanti vividhampi vīriyapāramiṃ katvā uppādetvā. Tattha mahāsīlavarājakāle (jā.1.1.51) pañcāvudhakumārakāle (jā.1.1.55) mahāvānarindakāleti (jā.1.1.57) evamādīsu vīriyapāramiyā pūritattabhāvānaṃ parimāṇaṃ nāma natthi. Ekantena panassa mahājanakakāle – “Atīradassī jalamajjhe, hatā sabbeva mānusā; Cittassa aññathā natthi, esā me vīriyapāramī” ti.(dha. sa. aṭṭha. nidānakathā; jā. aṭṭha.1. dūrenidānakathā; apa. aṭṭha.1. dūrenidānakathā)–**  
> Having performed the highest effort (vīriyaṃ katvāna uttamaṃ) means: due to its ability to bring about supreme full enlightenment, having performed and aroused the highest striving, which is energy, as well as various kinds of the perfection of energy. There is no measure of the existences in which the perfection of energy was fulfilled in eras such as that of King Mahāsīlava, Prince Pañcāvudha, and Great Vānarinda, and so on. But certainly, in his (life as) Mahājanaka:– “Not seeing the near shore in the middle of the water, all the humans were destroyed; there was no other state in my mind: this is my perfection of energy.”  
>  
> — Cariyāpiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā (Pāramīs, on vīriyapāramī) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_13&page_number=265&name=cariy%C4%81pi%E1%B9%ADakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VsSrcml5YeG5gyBrYXR2xIFuYSB1dHRhbWFudGkgc2FtbcSBc2FtYm9kaGnhuYMgcMSBcGV0deG5gyBzYW1hdHRoYXTEgXlh)
