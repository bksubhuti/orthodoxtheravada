### Grammatical Analysis
**Visākhā**: [f.] a woman's name. The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says: *jātakāle tassā visākhāti nāmaṃ akaṃsu*, "at the time of her birth they gave her the name Visākhā" (Sv-iii 176:5). She is called *Migāramātā*, "Migāra's Mother", because the financier Migāra placed her in the position of a mother (*mātuṭṭhāne ṭhapesi*), as the commentaries say (Sv-iii 177:2-3; Mp-ii 138:3).

### Orthodox Definition
Visākhā was the foremost of the female lay disciples who give: in the Etadagga the Buddha says "of those who are givers is Visākhā Migāramātā" (AN 1, ¶259). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī gives her story. In an earlier life, a hundred thousand aeons ago, she invited the Buddha Padumuttara, made an offering to a hundred thousand monks, and aspired to be the chief female attendant of a future Buddha (Sv-iii 176:3). She was born in Bhaddiya, in the house of the treasurer Dhanañjaya, and when the Buddha came to Bhaddiya she went out to meet him with five hundred maidens and "upon seeing him for the first time became a stream-enterer" (Sv-iii 176:4-6). She went to Sāvatthī to the house of Puṇṇavaḍḍhana, son of the treasurer Migāra, and Migāra placed her in the position of a mother. Her father had given her a great ornament called Mahālatāpiḷandhana; later she gave that ornament up (*vissajjetvā*) and with nine crores built a palace (*pāsāda*) for the Buddha (Sv-iii 177:1-9).

The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says there was no other woman in the Dispensation who gave away wealth like Visākhā; she had the Pubbārāma built in the east of Sāvatthī (Sv-iii 178:1, 178:3). The Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā says the Buddha stayed six rains residences in the Pubbārāma, built by Visākhā at a cost of twenty-seven crores (Dhp-a 46:2).

In the Vinaya (Mahāvagga, Cīvarakkhandhaka, the Story of Visākhā) she invites the Buddha, serves the Saṅgha, and says "I ask eight boons of the Blessed One" (*aṭṭhāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ varāni yācāmi*); the Buddha says that Tathāgatas are beyond granting boons, and she replies that they are allowable and blameless. She asks to give to the Saṅgha for life: rains cloths (*vassikasāṭikā*), food for incoming monks (*āgantukabhatta*), food for outgoing monks (*gamikabhatta*), food for the sick (*gilānabhatta*), food for those who tend the sick (*gilānupaṭṭhākabhatta*), medicine for the sick (*gilānabhesajja*), a constant supply of conjey (*dhuvayāgu*), and, to the Order of nuns, bathing cloths (*udakasāṭikā*). She then gives the benefit of each; for example, the first because monks were seen bathing naked in the rain and taken for naked ascetics, and the last because the nuns were mocked while bathing naked at the same ford as the prostitutes (Vin-iii 2151, 2157). The Visākhā Sutta (AN 8.43) is the Buddha's teaching to her on the Uposatha with eight factors.

### Quote
> **“aṭṭhāhaṃ, bhante, bhagavantaṃ varāni yācāmī” ti. “Atikkantavarā kho, visākhe, tathāgatā” ti. “Yāni ca, bhante, kappiyāni yāni ca anavajjānī” ti. “Icchāmahaṃ, bhante, saṅghassa yāvajīvaṃ vassikasāṭikaṃ dātuṃ, āgantukabhattaṃ dātuṃ, gamikabhattaṃ dātuṃ, gilānabhattaṃ dātuṃ, gilānupaṭṭhākabhattaṃ dātuṃ, gilānabhesajjaṃ dātuṃ, dhuvayāguṃ dātuṃ, bhikkhunisaṅghassa udakasāṭikaṃ dātun” ti.**  
> “I ask eight boons of the Blessed One.” “Truth-finders are beyond granting boons, Visākhā.” “They are those which are allowable and those which are blameless, Blessed One.” “Lord, I want to give for life to the Order cloths for the rains, to give food for those coming in, to give food for those going out, to give food for the sick, to give food for those who tend the sick, to give medicine for the sick, to give a constant supply of conjey, to give bathing-cloths for the Order of nuns.”  
>

> **Jātakāle tassā visākhāti nāmaṃ akaṃsu. Sā yadā bhagavā bhaddiyanagaraṃ āgamāsi, tadā pañcadāsisatehi saddhiṃ bhagavato paccuggamanaṃ katvā paṭhamadassanamhiyeva sotāpannā ahosi. Aparabhāge sāvatthiyaṃ migāraseṭṭhiputtassa puṇṇavaḍḍhanakumārassa gehaṃ gatā. Tattha naṃ migāraseṭṭhi mātuṭṭhāne ṭhapesi. Tasmā migāramātāti vuccati.**  
> At the time of her birth, they gave her the name Visākhā. When the Blessed One came to the city of Bhaddiya, she went out to meet the Blessed One together with five hundred maidens, and upon seeing him for the first time, she became a stream-enterer. After a time, she went to the house of Puṇṇavaḍḍhana, son of the treasurer Migāra, in Sāvatthī. There, the treasurer Migāra placed her in the position of a mother. Therefore, she is called Migāra's Mother.  
>

> **Mātugāmattabhāve ṭhitāya visākhāya viya aññissā buddhasāsane dhanapariccāgo nāma natthi, purisattabhāve ṭhitassa anāthapiṇḍikassa viya aññassāti. Visākhā sāvatthiyā pācīnabhāge uttaradeviyā vihārasadise ṭhāne pubbārāmaṃ nāma kāresi.**  
> Just as there is no other female lay follower who donated wealth in the Buddha's Dispensation like Visākhā while she was in the female state, there is no one else like Anāthapiṇḍika while he was in the male state. Visākhā had the Eastern Park Monastery built in the eastern part of Sāvatthī, in a place like the monastery of Queen Uttarā.
