### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭiccasamuppāda**: [m.] Dependent Origination; Conditioned Genesis. The Visuddhimagga explains the compound: it is *paṭicca*, because it is to be understood (*patīyamāno*) and the wise are fit to accept it (*paccetuṃ arahanti naṃ paṇḍitā*), for it leads to welfare and happiness; and *samuppāda*, because arising it arises together and rightly (*saha sammā ca uppajjati*), not one by one and not without cause (“na ekekato, nāpi ahetuto”): thus *paṭicco ca so samuppādo cāti paṭiccasamuppādo* (Vism XVII 577, Vism-ii 850:2-4). The Visuddhimagga rejects the view that the term means mere arising (*uppādamatta*): there is no sutta for it and it contradicts the suttas (Vism XVII 574).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paṭiccasamuppāda* is what the Buddha attended to, in forward and reverse order (*anulomapaṭiloma*), in the first watch of the night at the foot of the Bodhi tree after his awakening (Vin-iii 6:1-4). The Buddha himself defines it in the Paṭiccasamuppāda Sutta: “Katamo ca, bhikkhave, paṭiccasamuppādo? Avijjāpaccayā, bhikkhave, saṅkhārā …”, the twelve links up to the origin of the whole mass of suffering, followed by the cessation order (SN 12.1, S-ii 8:1-14, 9:1-12). The Vibhaṅga gives the same formula and then defines each link (Vibh 905-907). The Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha states that it is to be understood by its three periods (*tayo addhā*), twelve factors (*dvādasaṅgāni*), twenty modes (*vīsatākārā*), three links (*tisandhi*), four groups (*catusaṅkhepā*), three rounds (*tīṇi vaṭṭāni*) and two roots (*dve mūlāni*) (Abh-s 693:1).

The standard formula consists of **twelve interlinked causal factors**:
1. Ignorance (*[avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)*)
2. Volitional Formations (*[saṅkhāra](/glossary/sankhara/)*)
3. Consciousness (*[viññāṇa](/glossary/vinnana/)*)
4. Mentality-Materiality (*nāmarūpa*)
5. Six Sense Bases (*[saḷāyatana](/glossary/salayatana/)*)
6. Contact (*[phassa](/glossary/phassa/)*)
7. Feeling (*[vedanā](/glossary/vedana/)*)
8. Craving (*[taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/)*)
9. Clinging (*[upādāna](/glossary/upadana/)*)
10. Becoming (*[bhava](/glossary/bhava/)*)
11. Birth (*[jāti](/glossary/jati/)*)
12. Aging and Death (*[jarāmaraṇa](/glossary/jaramarana/)*)

The Visuddhimagga, on the sutta's wording “jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ … idappaccayatā”, says that the conditioning states themselves are called *paṭiccasamuppāda* (“paccayadhammāva paṭiccasamuppādoti vuttā”), and gives its mark: its characteristic is conditionality (*paccayalakkhaṇo*), its function is the continuity of suffering (*dukkhānubandhanaraso*) and its manifestation is the wrong path (*kummaggapaccupaṭṭhāno*) (Vism XVII 572, Vism-ii 838:1-2).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka)
> **Tena samayena buddho bhagavā uruvelāyaṃ viharati najjā nerañjarāya tīre bodhirukkhamūle paṭhamābhisambuddho. Atha kho bhagavā bodhirukkhamūle sattāhaṃ ekapallaṅkena nisīdi vimuttisukhapaṭisaṃvedī. Atha kho bhagavā rattiyā paṭhamaṃ yāmaṃ paṭiccasamuppādaṃ anulomapaṭilomaṃ manasākāsi – “avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā, saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṃ, viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṃ, nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṃ, saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā, vedanāpaccayā taṇhā, taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṃ, upādānapaccayā bhavo, bhavapaccayā jāti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti – evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo hoti.**  
> At one time the awakened one, the Lord, having just attained supreme awakening, was dwelling at Uruvelā on the bank of the river Nerañjarā at the foot of the Tree of Awakening. Then the Lord sat cross-legged in one posture for seven days at the foot of the Tree of Awakening, experiencing the bliss of freedom. Then the Lord, during the first watch of the night, paid attention to Dependent Origination in forward and reverse order: “With ignorance as condition, volitional formations arise; with volitional formations as condition, consciousness; with consciousness as condition, name-and-form; with name-and-form as condition, the six sense bases; with the six sense bases as condition, contact; with contact as condition, feeling; with feeling as condition, craving; with craving as condition, clinging; with clinging as condition, existence; with existence as condition, birth; with birth as condition, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair come to be. Such is the origin of this whole mass of suffering.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, paṭiccasamuppādo? Avijjāpaccayā, bhikkhave, saṅkhārā; saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṃ; viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṃ; nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṃ; saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso; phassapaccayā vedanā; vedanāpaccayā taṇhā; taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṃ; upādānapaccayā bhavo; bhavapaccayā jāti; jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti. Evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo hoti. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, paṭiccasamuppādo.**  
> And what, monks, is dependent origination? With ignorance as condition, volitional formations come to be; with volitional formations as condition, consciousness; with consciousness as condition, name-and-form; with name-and-form as condition, the six sense bases; with the six sense bases as condition, contact; with contact as condition, feeling; with feeling as condition, craving; with craving as condition, clinging; with clinging as condition, existence; with existence as condition, birth; with birth as condition, aging-and-death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair come to be. Such is the origin of this whole mass of suffering. This, monks, is called dependent origination.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 12.1, Paṭiccasamuppādasutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=243&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBwYeG5rWljY2FzYW11cHDEgWRvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā, saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṃ, viññāṇapaccayā nāmarūpaṃ, nāmarūpapaccayā saḷāyatanaṃ, saḷāyatanapaccayā phasso, phassapaccayā vedanā, vedanāpaccayā taṇhā, taṇhāpaccayā upādānaṃ, upādānapaccayā bhavo, bhavapaccayā jāti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsā sambhavanti. Evametassa kevalassa dukkhakkhandhassa samudayo hoti.**  
> With ignorance as condition, volitional formations; with volitional formations as condition, consciousness; with consciousness as condition, name-and-form; conditioned by name-and-form, the six sense bases; conditioned by the six sense bases, contact; conditioned by contact, feeling; conditioned by feeling, craving; conditioned by craving, clinging; with clinging as condition, existence; with existence as condition, birth; with birth as condition, old age and death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, displeasure, and despair come to be. Thus is the origin of this whole mass of suffering.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Evaṃ paṭiccasamuppādaṃ desentena tathatādīhi vevacanehi paccayadhammāva paṭiccasamuppādoti vuttā. Tasmā jarāmaraṇādīnaṃ dhammānaṃ paccayalakkhaṇo paṭiccasamuppādo, dukkhānubandhanaraso, kummaggapaccupaṭṭhānoti veditabbo.**  
> 572. Thus, for the one who teaches dependent-arising using synonyms such as 'reality' and so on, it is the conditioning factors themselves that are stated as dependent origination. Therefore, dependent origination should be understood as having the characteristic of being the conditions for states beginning with old age and death, having the function of continuing suffering, and being manifested as the wrong path.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, 572) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=149&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NTcyLiBFdmHhuYMgcGHhua1pY2Nhc2FtdXBwxIFkYeG5gyBkZXNlbnRlbmEgdGF0aGF0xIFkxKtoaSB2ZXZhY2FuZWhp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Yo hi ayaṃ paccayatāya pavatto dhammasamūho, tattha paṭiccasamuppādoti idaṃ vacanaṃ dvidhā icchanti. So hi yasmā patīyamāno hitāya sukhāya ca saṃvattati, tasmā paccetumarahanti naṃ paṇḍitāti paṭicco. Uppajjamāno ca saha sammā ca uppajjati, na ekekato, nāpi ahetutoti samuppādo. Evaṃ paṭicco ca so samuppādo cāti paṭiccasamuppādo.**  
> For this term 'dependent origination' is intended in two ways regarding this totality of states arisen through conditionality. For since that, when apprehended, leads to welfare and happiness, the wise consider it worthy to be approached (paccetuṃ); thus it is 'dependent' (paṭicco). And when it arises, it arises together (saha) and rightly (sammā), not individually or without a cause; thus it is 'origination' (samuppādo). Thus, it is 'dependent' (paṭicco) and it is 'origination' (samuppādo); therefore it is 'dependent origination' (paṭiccasamuppādo).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, 577, the word paṭiccasamuppāda) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=151&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=WW8gaGkgYXlh4bmDIHBhY2NheWF0xIF5YSBwYXZhdHRvIGRoYW1tYXNhbcWraG8s)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Keci pana paṭicca sammā ca titthiyaparikappitapakatipurisādikāraṇanirapekkho uppādo paṭiccasamuppādoti evaṃ uppādamattaṃ paṭiccasamuppādoti vadanti, taṃ na yujjati.**  
> 574. But some say that dependent origination is a 'right' (sammā) 'arising' (uppāda) 'having depended' (paṭicca)—one that is independent of such causes imagined by sectarians as Primordial Essence, the World Soul, and so on. Thus they claim that dependent origination is mere arising; but that is not suitable.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, 574) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=149&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NTc0LiBLZWNpIHBhbmEgcGHhua1pY2NhIHNhbW3EgSBjYQ%3D%3D)
