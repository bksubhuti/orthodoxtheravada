### Grammatical Analysis
**Kaṭhina** (written *kathina* in the Vinaya texts): [nt.] the kathina cloth, and the making up of that cloth (*kathinatthāro*). The Vinayālaṅkāra-ṭīkā asks, "in what sense is it called kathina?" and answers: in the sense of stability (*thiraṭṭhena*), because it is able to keep the five benefits within (*pañcānisaṃse antokaraṇasamatthatāya*); or in the sense of gathering (*saṅgaṇhanaṭṭhena*), because it gathers the five benefits and does not let them go elsewhere (Vin-alaṅke 1428:1-10). The ṭīkā teachers explain the word merely as an unformed stem (*anipphannapāṭipadika*), without analysing root and suffix (Vin-alaṅke 1429:2).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Kaṭhina* is a Vinaya procedure for robe cloth, allowed by the Buddha for monks who have completed the rains. In the Kaṭhinakkhandhaka (Mahāvagga) thirty monks of Pāvā, all forest-dwellers, almsmen, rag-robe wearers and wearers of the three robes, spent the rains at Sāketa because they could not reach Sāvatthī in time, and came to the Buddha at Jetavana with drenched robes. The Buddha then said: "I allow you, monks, to make up the kathina when you have completed the rains. When the kathina is made up, five things will be allowable to you: going without having asked, walking without the three robes, a group-meal, as many robes as you require, and whatever robe-material accrues there" (Vin-iii 1813:1-20).

The procedure: the Saṅgha formally gives the kathina cloth to one monk by a motion (*ñatti*) and a resolution: "this kathina cloth has been received by the Saṅgha; the Saṅgha gives this kathina cloth to the monk so-and-so to make up the kathina" (Vin-iii 1814:1-4; 1815:1-5). The Samantapāsādikā says that the monk who makes it up must know the procedure: the cloth should be washed well, and sewn together with many monks on that same day, dyed, and marked with the kappabindu, and then made up (Sp-iii 709:1-2). He says the words "I make up the kathina with this saṅghāṭi," and then approaches the Saṅgha and declares that the kathina has been made up; the others rejoice (Sp-iii 712:1-3). The Samantapāsādikā explains the five allowances one by one, and that they last as long as the kathina is not revoked (Sp-iii 706:1-6). It also says that, at the least, five persons are needed, and that those who entered the rains in the first period and invited on the first Invitation get it (Sp-iii 707:2-3).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, Kathinakkhandhaka)
> **Atha kho bhagavā etasmiṃ nidāne etasmiṃ pakaraṇe dhammiṃ kathaṃ katvā bhikkhū āmantesi – “anujānāmi, bhikkhave, vassaṃvuṭṭhānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ kathinaṃ attharituṃ. Atthatakathinānaṃ vo, bhikkhave, pañca kappissanti – anāmantacāro, asamādānacāro, gaṇabhojanaṃ, yāvadatthacīvaraṃ, yo ca tattha cīvaruppādo so nesaṃ bhavissatīti. Atthatakathinānaṃ vo, bhikkhave, imāni pañca kappissanti.**  
> Then the Blessed One, on this occasion, having given a talk on the Dhamma, addressed the monks, saying: “I allow you, monks, to make up kathina-cloth when monks have completed the rains. Five things will be allowable to you, monks, when the kathina-cloths have been made up: going without having asked for permission, walking not taking the three robes, a group-meal, as many robes as you require, and whatever robe-material accrues there, that will be for them. These five things will be allowable to you, monks, when the kathina-cloths have been made up.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Kathinatthārakenāpi dhammena samena uppannaṃ kathinaṃ attharantena vattaṃ jānitabbaṃ. Tantavāyagehato hi ābhatasantāneneva khalimakkhitasāṭako na vaṭṭati, malīnasāṭakopi na vaṭṭati, tasmā kathinatthārasāṭakaṃ labhitvā suṭṭhu dhovitvā sūciādīni cīvarakammūpakaraṇāni sajjetvā bahūhi bhikkhūhi saddhiṃ tadaheva sibbitvā niṭṭhitasūcikammaṃ rajitvā kappabinduṃ datvā kathinaṃ attharitabbaṃ.**  
> The bhikkhus who are making up the kathina-cloth, making it up according to Dhamma, should know the code of conduct. Indeed, cloth brought from the weaver's house just as it is, stiff with starch, is not allowable; dirty cloth is also not allowable; therefore, having obtained the kathina-cloth, having washed it well, having prepared the requisites for robe-making such as needles, one should make it up together with many bhikkhus on that very day, have it dyed, give the kathina-mark, and make up the kathina.  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Vinayālaṅkāra-ṭīkā, the meaning of kathina)
> **Kenaṭṭhena kathinanti? Thiraṭṭhena. Kasmā thiranti? Anāmantacāraasamādānacāragaṇabhojanayāvadatthacīvarayocatatthacīvaruppādasaṅkhāte pañcānisaṃse antokaraṇasamatthatāya. Vuttañhi sāratthadīpaniyaṃ (sārattha. ṭī. mahāvagga 3.306)“pañcānisaṃse antokaraṇasamatthatāya thiranti attho” ti, tathā vimativinodaniyaṃ (vi. vi. ṭī. mahāvagga 2.306) vajirabuddhiṭīkāyañca (vajira. ṭī. mahāvagga 306).**  
> When asked, “In what sense is it called the Kathina?”, One should answer, “In the sense of stability (thira).” If one should ask, “Why is it stable?” It is stable because of its ability to restrict one within the monastery regarding the five benefits: going without notice, going without taking the set, eating in groups, having as many robes as one wishes, and the arising of robes that occurs there. For this is stated in the Sāratthadīpanī: “By its ability to restrict one within the monastery regarding the five benefits, it is stable—this is the meaning,” and it is similarly stated in the Vimativinodanī and the Vajirabuddhi-ṭīkā.  
>
