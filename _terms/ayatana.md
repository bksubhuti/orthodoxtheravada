### Grammatical Analysis
**Āyatana**: [nt.] sense base; sphere; gateway; meeting place; source. Traditionally derived from *āya* (turning, extending, coming in) + root *tan* (to stretch, expand), meaning the place where experiential inputs expand and pool together.

### Orthodox Definition
*Āyatana* is a critical taxonomy used by the Buddha to dissect experience and collapse the illusion of a centralized soul or ego. The framework maps the sensory grid into **twelve distinct bases**, split into six internal gates and six external objective fields.

The Internal Bases (*ajjhattika-āyatana*) are: Eye (*cakkhu*), Ear (*sota*), Nose (*ghāna*), Tongue (*jivhā*), Body (*kāya*), and Mind (*mano*).
The External Bases (*bāhira-āyatana*) are: Visible Forms (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*), Sounds (*sadda*), Odors (*gandha*), Tastes (*rasa*), Tangible Objects (*phoṭṭhabba*), and Mental Objects (*[dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/)*).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* explains *āyatana* in general as "actuating" (*āyatana*), "extending the origins" (*āyānaṃ tananato*) and "leading on what is extended" (*āyatassa nayanato*): the mind and mental factors at each door arise and exert themselves through their own functions (Vism-ii 545:1-4). It adds the senses of residence, source, gathering place, birth-place and cause (Vism-ii 546:1).
