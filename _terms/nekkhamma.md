### Grammatical Analysis
**Nekkhamma**: [nt.] renunciation; giving up worldly life; freedom from sensuality. The commentaries explain it from *nikkhanta* ("gone out"), as in *nikkhantattā nekkhammapāramī* (Cp-a 205:1; Bv-a 651:1: leaving the kingdom without attachment). The Mūla defines it as the escape from sensual desires: *kāmānametaṃ nissaraṇaṃ yadidaṃ nekkhammaṃ* (It 656:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Nekkhamma* represents the third of the ten perfections (*[pāramī](/glossary/parami/)*) and forms the explicit definition of the second limb of the Noble Eightfold Path: Right Intention (*[Sammāsaṅkappa](/glossary/sammasankappa/)*), specifically as *Nekkhamma-saṅkappa* (the intention of renunciation).

Doctrinally, nekkhamma is not merely the physical act of shaving the head and donning robes (*[pabbajjā](/glossary/pabbajja/)*); the Itivuttaka commentary quotes: "Pabbajjā paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ, nibbānañca vipassanā, sabbepi kusalā dhammā, nekkhammanti pavuccare" (It-a 1630:1-1631:1), "going forth, the first jhāna, nibbāna and insight: all wholesome states are called nekkhamma". It is fundamentally a psychological condition—the active turning away of the mind from the trapping pull of sensuality, worldly ambition, and domestic entanglements. It is driven by the wholesome mental factor of non-greed (*alobha-cetasika*).

Nekkhamma is the first of the three "elements of escape" (*nissaraṇiyā dhātuyo*), followed by the formless and by cessation (It 656:3).

### Quote
> **“Tisso imā, bhikkhave, nissaraṇiyā dhātuyo. Katamā tisso? Kāmānametaṃ nissaraṇaṃ yadidaṃ nekkhammaṃ, rūpānametaṃ nissaraṇaṃ yadidaṃ āruppaṃ, yaṃ kho pana kiñci bhūtaṃ saṅkhataṃ paṭiccasamuppannaṃ nirodho tassa nissaraṇaṃ – imā kho, bhikkhave, tisso nissaraṇiyā dhātuyo” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these three elements of escape. Which three? The escape from sensual desires, that is, renunciation; the escape from form, that is, the formless; and the escape from whatever has come to be, from whatever is conditioned and dependently arisen, that is, cessation. These, monks, are the three elements of escape.”  
>  
> — [Itivuttaka](/glossary/itivuttaka/) (Iti 72, Nissaraṇiya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_04&page_number=237&name=itivuttakap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFRpc3NvIGltxIEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgbmlzc2FyYeG5h2l5xIEgW25pc3PEgXJh4bmHxKt5xIE%3D)
