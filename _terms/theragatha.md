### Grammatical Analysis
**Theragāthā**: [f.] Verses of the Elder Monks. Formed by *thera* (elder, senior monk) + *gāthā* (verse, poem).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Theragāthā* is the eighth book of the Khuddaka Nikāya (order of Pj-i 24:5: Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Suttanipāta, Vimānavatthu, Petavatthu, Theragāthā, Therīgāthā, ...). Its closing verses give the count: "a thousand verses, three hundred and sixty" and "two hundred and sixty Elders, and four" (*sahassaṃ honti tā gāthā, tīṇi saṭṭhisatāni ca; therā ca dve satā saṭṭhi, cattāro ca*), the Buddha's sons "free from the taints" (*buddhaputtā anāsavā*) (Th 4665-4668).

The same colophon calls them "lion's roars" (*sīhanādaṃ naditvāna*): declarations of final victory over [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). Some poems long for solitude, as in the quoted verses of Tālapuṭa, who as a monk addresses his own mind ("So you used to urge me, mind", Th 4130:1); others praise the forest (the peacock's cry in the mountain cave, Th 1106). Their authors came from very different lives: Aṅgulimāla was a killer of men (Th-a-ii 3996) and Tālapuṭa a famous dancer, the "dancing headman" Naṭagāmaṇi (Th-a-ii 5581:3).

The commentary on these verses is the *Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā*. Its author is Ācariya Dhammapāla, as the Ṭīkā says ("ācariyadhammapālattherena theragāthāsaṃvaṇṇanāya", Mp-t-i 702:4), and it belongs to the series of his commentaries on the Khuddaka Nikāya called *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* ("Elucidation of the Ultimate Meaning"; so named at Thī-a 4534 and in Mp-t-i 922:12). Its introduction says it relies on the method of the ancient commentaries and the tradition of the Mahāvihāra dwellers (Th-a-i 25-27), and each section opens with the question "Kā uppatti?" ("What was its origin?", Th-a-ii 5581:2), giving the elder's past aspirations and the occasion of his verses.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Aṅgulimālattheragāthā* (Th 3336, "8. Aṅgulimālattheragāthā"; Th-a-ii 3993) – the verses of the former killer Aṅgulimāla; in verse 867 the Buddha answers him, "I have laid aside the rod towards all beings".
* **Canonical**: *Tālapuṭattheragāthā* (Th 4052, "1. Tālapuṭattheragāthā" in the Fifties Section; Th-a-ii 5580) – a long poem beginning "Kadā nuhaṃ" (verses 1094 ff.), in which the elder addresses his mind and longs for the forest.
* **Commentary**: *Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā* (by Dhammapāla, Th-a-i and Th-a-ii; part of the *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* series) – gives the origin story (*uppatti*) of each elder's verses.
