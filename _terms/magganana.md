### Grammatical Analysis
**Maggañāṇa**: [nt.] path knowledge. A compound of *magga* (path) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Paṭisambhidāmagga has a chapter "Maggañāṇaniddesa" and gives its definition: "understanding of emergence and turning away from both is knowledge in the path" (*dubhato vuṭṭhānavivaṭṭane paññā magge ñāṇaṃ*, Paṭis 380:4).

### Orthodox Definition
*Maggañāṇa* is the knowledge of the supramundane path. The Paṭisambhidāmagga explains it by the moment of the stream-entry path: right view, in the sense of seeing, emerges from wrong view, from the defilements and aggregates that follow it, and from all external signs; right speech, in the sense of embracing, emerges from wrong speech, and so on for each path factor (Paṭis 380:2; 381:1). The commentary on this chapter says that "emerges from wrong view" means that, by abandoning the underlying tendency to view by way of complete cutting off, it emerges from the sixty-two wrong views (Paṭis-a 1115:1).

The Paṭisambhidāmagga commentary says that at the time of the penetration of the truths, in one moment, path knowledge performs four functions: full comprehension (*pariññā*), abandonment (*pahāna*), realization (*sacchikiriyā*) and development (*bhāvanā*). It illustrates with the boat that, in one moment, leaves the near shore, cuts the current, carries its cargo and reaches the far shore. Taking Nibbāna (cessation) as object, path knowledge sees all four truths by way of function: it comprehends suffering, abandons the origin, develops the path and realizes cessation (Paṭis-a 1250:3-10).

The Visuddhimagga (Ch. XXII) says that the abandoning of the fetters and the like by the knowledge of the noble path, in such a way that they do not arise again, like a tree struck by a thunderbolt, is *samucchedappahāna*, abandoning by destruction (Vism-ii 2040:1-3).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, Maggañāṇaniddesa)
> **Kathaṃ dubhato vuṭṭhānavivaṭṭane paññā magge ñāṇaṃ? Sotāpattimaggakkhaṇe dassanaṭṭhena sammādiṭṭhi micchādiṭṭhiyā vuṭṭhāti, tadanuvattakakilesehi ca khandhehi ca vuṭṭhāti, bahiddhā ca sabbanimittehi vuṭṭhāti. Tena vuccati – “dubhato vuṭṭhānavivaṭṭane paññā magge ñāṇaṃ”.**  
> 61. How is it that understanding of emergence and turning away from both is path knowledge? At the moment of the path of stream-entry, right view in the sense of seeing emerges from wrong view, and it emerges from defilements and from aggregates that occur consequent upon them, and externally it emerges from all signs. Hence it is said: “Understanding of emergence and turning away from both is path knowledge.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā (Saddhammappakāsinī))
> **Saccābhisamayakālasmiñhi maggañāṇassa ekakkhaṇe pariññā, pahānaṃ, sacchikiriyā, bhāvanāti cattāri kiccāni honti. Yathā nāvā apubbaṃ acarimaṃ ekakkhaṇe cattāri kiccāni karoti, orimaṃ tīraṃ pajahati, sotaṃ chindati, bhaṇḍaṃ vahati, pārimaṃ tīraṃ appeti, evameva maggañāṇaṃ apubbaṃ acarimaṃ ekakkhaṇe cattāri saccāni abhisameti, dukkhaṃ pariññābhisamayena abhisameti, samudayaṃ pahānābhisamayena abhisameti, maggaṃ bhāvanābhisamayena abhisameti, nirodhaṃ sacchikiriyābhisamayena abhisameti. Kiṃ vuttaṃ hoti? Nirodhaṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā kiccavasena cattāripi saccāni pāpuṇāti passati paṭivijjhatīti. Yathā orimaṃ tīraṃ pajahati, evaṃ maggañāṇaṃ dukkhaṃ parijānāti. Yathā sotaṃ chindati, evaṃ samudayaṃ pajahati. Yathā bhaṇḍaṃ vahati, evaṃ sahajātādipaccayatāya maggaṃ bhāveti. Yathā pārimaṃ tīraṃ appeti, evaṃ pārimatīrabhūtaṃ nirodhaṃ sacchikarotīti evaṃ upamāsaṃsandanaṃ veditabbaṃ.**  
> For at the time of the convergence of truths, there are four duties in the one moment of path-wisdom: full understanding, abandonment, realization, and development. Just as a boat performs four duties in one moment, without priority or subsequence—it leaves the near shore, cuts the stream, carries the cargo, and reaches the further shore—so too, path-wisdom converges on the four truths in one moment, without priority or subsequence: it converges on suffering by the convergence of full understanding, it converges on the origin by the convergence of abandonment, it converges on the path by the convergence of development, and it converges on the cessation by the convergence of realization. What is meant by this? Having made cessation the object, by way of function, one attains, sees, and penetrates all four truths. Just as it leaves the near shore, path-wisdom fully understands suffering. Just as it cuts the stream, path-wisdom abandons the origin. Just as it carries the cargo, path-wisdom develops the path by way of conditionality such as co-arising. Just as it reaches the further shore, path-wisdom realizes cessation, which is the further shore; this simile comparison should be understood in this way.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, Ch. XXII)
> **Yaṃ pana asanivicakkābhihatassa rukkhassa viya ariyamaggañāṇena saṃyojanādīnaṃ dhammānaṃ yathā na puna pavatti, evaṃ pahānaṃ, idaṃ samucchedappahānaṃ nāma. Yaṃ sandhāya vuttaṃ “samucchedappahānañca lokuttaraṃ khayagāmimaggaṃ bhāvayato” ti (paṭi. ma.1.24). Iti imesu tīsu pahānesu samucchedappahānameva idha adhippetaṃ.**  
> 851. But the abandoning of states such as the fetters by the knowledge of the noble path in such a way that they do not arise again—like a tree struck by a thunderbolt—this is called abandoning by destruction (samucchedappahāna). Referring to this, it was said: “And the abandoning by destruction for one who develops the supramundane path leading to destruction.” Thus, among these three kinds of abandoning, only abandoning by destruction is intended here.  
>
