### Grammatical Analysis
**Amoha**: [m.] Non-delusion; undeludedness; wisdom. Formed by negative prefix *a* (not) + *moha* (delusion, ignorance, blindness).

### Orthodox Definition
*Amoha* is the third and most powerful of the Wholesome Roots (*[kusala-mūla](/glossary/kusala-mula/)*). In [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) terminology, *Amoha* is the mental factor of wisdom (*[paññā](/glossary/panna/)-cetasika*; As 1276:1). 

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as knowledge: "Dukkhe ñāṇaṃ, dukkhasamudaye ñāṇaṃ, dukkhanirodhe ñāṇaṃ, dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ñāṇaṃ, pubbante ñāṇaṃ, aparante ñāṇaṃ … yā evarūpā paññā pajānanā … paññindriyaṃ paññābalaṃ … amoho kusalamūlaṃ", knowledge of the four truths, of the past, the future and dependent origination (Dhs 1834:2; in the first wholesome citta, Dhs 424:2). The Atthasālinī says that paññā, "when it reaches the roots", is called *amoha* (*mūlāni patvā amohoti*, As 1276:1). It gives paññā the characteristic of penetrating the true nature (*yathāsabhāvapaṭivedhalakkhaṇā*), or of unerring penetration like an arrow shot by a skilled archer (*kusalissāsakhittausupaṭivedho viya*); the function of illuminating the object, like a lamp (*visayobhāsarasā padīpo viya*); and the manifestation of non-bewilderment, like a guide in the forest (*asammohapaccupaṭṭhānā araññagatasudesako viya*) (As 1212:1-4).

### Quote
> **“Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, kusalamūlāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Alobho kusalamūlaṃ, adoso kusalamūlaṃ, amoho kusalamūlaṃ. “Yadapi, bhikkhave, amoho tadapi kusalamūlaṃ; yadapi amūḷho abhisaṅkharoti kāyena vācāya manasā tadapi kusalaṃ; yadapi amūḷho mohena anabhibhūto apariyādinnacitto na parassa asatā dukkhaṃ uppādayati vadhena vā bandhanena vā jāniyā vā garahāya vā pabbājanāya vā balavamhi balattho itipi tadapi kusalaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these three roots of the wholesome. What are these three? Non-greed is a root of the wholesome, non-hatred is a root of the wholesome, non-delusion is a root of the wholesome. “Monks, non-delusion itself is a root of the wholesome; whatever a person not deluded generates by means of body, speech, or mind, that too is wholesome; whatever suffering a person not deluded—his mind not overcome by delusion, his mind not obsessed by it—does not wrongly inflict on another person through beating, imprisonment, confiscation, placing blame, or banishment, thinking, ‘I have power. I want power,’ that too is wholesome.  
>

Dukkhe ñāṇaṃ, dukkhasamudaye ñāṇaṃ, dukkhanirodhe ñāṇaṃ, dukkhanirodhagāminiyā paṭipadāya ñāṇaṃ, pubbante ñāṇaṃ, aparante ñāṇaṃ, pubbantāparante ñāṇaṃ, idappaccayatā paṭiccasamuppannesu dhammesu ñāṇaṃ, yā evarūpā paññā pajānanā vicayo pavicayo dhammavicayo sallakkhaṇā upalakkhaṇā paccupalakkhaṇā paṇḍiccaṃ kosallaṃ nepuññaṃ vebhabyā cintā upaparikkhā bhūrī medhā pariṇāyikā vipassanā sampajaññaṃ patodo paññā paññindriyaṃ paññābalaṃ paññāsatthaṃ paññāpāsādo paññāāloko paññāobhāso paññāpajjoto paññāratanaṃ amoho dhammavicayo sammādiṭṭhi – ayaṃ vuccati amoho.**

Knowledge of suffering, knowledge of the origin of suffering, knowledge of the cessation of suffering, knowledge of the path leading to the cessation of suffering, knowledge of the past, knowledge of the future, knowledge of the past and future, knowledge of the conditionality of this (idappaccayatā) and of dependently arisen phenomena (paṭiccasamuppannesu dhammesu), whatever such wisdom, understanding, investigation, analysis, analysis of phenomena, discernment, observation, scrutiny, discernment, skill, cleverness, elucidation, thought, scrutiny, breadth, wit, guidance, insight, awareness, goad, wisdom, the faculty of wisdom, the strength of wisdom, the weapon of wisdom, the palace of wisdom, the light of wisdom, the illumination of wisdom, the brightness of wisdom, the jewel of wisdom, non-delusion, analysis of phenomena, right view — this is called non-delusion.  
>

### Quote
> **Aparo nayo – yathāsabhāvapaṭivedhalakkhaṇā paññā; akkhalitapaṭivedhalakkhaṇā vā kusalissāsakhittausupaṭivedho viya. Visayobhāsarasā padīpo viya. Asammohapaccupaṭṭhānā araññagatasudesako viya.**  
> Another method: wisdom has the characteristic of penetrating according to reality; or it has the characteristic of unfaltering penetration, like the penetration of an arrow shot by a skillful archer. Its function is illuminating the object, like a lamp. It is manifested as non-confusion, like a good guide in a forest.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on paññā, the same as amoha) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=166&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBhcm8gbmF5byDigJMgeWF0aMSBc2FiaMSBdmFwYeG5rWl2ZWRoYWxha2toYeG5h8SBIHBhw7HDscSBOw%3D%3D)

### Quote
> **Paññā pana indriyāni patvā paññindriyaṃ hotīti vuttā, maggaṅgāni patvā sammādiṭṭhīti, balāni patvā paññābalanti, mūlāni patvā amohoti, kammapathaṃ patvā sammādiṭṭhīti, piṭṭhidukaṃ patvā sampajaññaṃ vipassanāti.**

>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Akusalamūla Sutta* (AN 3; heading "9. Akusalamūlasuttaṃ", A-iii 720:1) – the three roots of the wholesome (A-iii 729:1-3); "Yadapi, bhikkhave, amoho tadapi kusalamūlaṃ" (A-iii 732:1); the undeluded one, his mind not overcome by delusion, does not wrongly inflict suffering on another (A-iii 732:3).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Dhs 1834:2, §1063; Dhs 424:2, §34) – amoha defined as ñāṇa and paññā, including *paññindriyaṃ*, *dhammavicayo* and *sammādiṭṭhi* in its synonyms.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 1212:1-4 and 1276:1) – the characteristic of wisdom with the archer, lamp and forest-guide similes; paññā as amoha among the roots; and As 1233:9, which refers to this earlier exposition.
