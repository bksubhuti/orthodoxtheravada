### Grammatical Analysis
**Pabbajjā**: [f.] Going forth; leaving home; lower ordination. Formed by prefix *pa* (forth, out) + root *vaj* (to go). Literally "going forth" from the household life into homelessness.

### Orthodox Definition
*Pabbajjā* is the formal act of renunciation that initiates an individual into the Buddhist monastic order (*[Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)*). 

In the canonical texts, it is repeatedly described by the formula: "Cutting off hair and beard, donning ochre robes, and going forth from the home life into homelessness." Mechanically, within the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/), *Pabbajjā* constitutes the "lower ordination," making a layperson a Novice Monk (*[Sāmaṇera](/glossary/samanera/)*) or Novice Nun (*[Sāmaṇerī](/glossary/samaneri/)*). The candidate formally requests admission from a preceptor (*upajjhāya*), takes the Three Refuges (*[Tisarana](/glossary/tisarana/)*), and undertakes the Ten Precepts.

It is distinct from *[Upasampadā](/glossary/upasampada/)* (higher ordination), which makes one a fully-fledged [Bhikkhu](/glossary/bhikkhu/) or [Bhikkhunī](/glossary/bhikkhuni/) subject to the complete [Pātimokkha](/glossary/patimokkha/) rules. To take *Pabbajjā*, a candidate must generally be at least 15 years old, free from certain diseases, and have parental consent.

### Quote
> **Pabbajjaṁ kittayissāmi**  
> "I shall describe the Going Forth."  
> 
> **Yathā pabbaji cakkhumā**  
> "How the Visionary One (the Buddha) went forth."  
> 
> **Yathā vīmaṁsamāno so**  
> "How he, reflecting and examining,"  
> 
> **Pabbajjaṁ samarocayi**  
> "came to delight in the life of renunciation."  
> 
> **Sambādhoyaṁ gharāvāso**  
> "This household life is cramped and confined."  
> 
> **Rajassāyatanaṁ iti**  
> "It is a dwelling-place of dust (defilements)."  
> 
> **Abbhokāsova pabbajjā**  
> "The going forth is like the open sky."  
> 
> **Iti disvāna pabbaji**  
> "Seeing thus, he went forth."  
> 
> **Pabbajitvāna kāyena**  
> "Having gone forth, in bodily conduct,"  
> 
> **Pāpakammaṁ vivajjayi**  
> "he avoided evil actions."  
> 
> **Vacīduccaritaṁ hitvā**  
> "Having abandoned wrong speech,"  
> 
> **Ājīvaṁ parisodhayi**  
> "he purified his livelihood."  
> 
> — *Pabbajjāsutta* (Snp 3.1, vv. 1-3)
> 
> **Taṁ kiṁ maññasi, mahārāja?**  
> "What do you think, Great King?"  
> 
> **Idha te assa puriso dāso kammakāro pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṅkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī mukhullokako.**  
> "Suppose you had a man who was a servant and worker--one who rose before you and went to bed after you, who carried out your commands, acted pleasingly, spoke kindly, and watched your face for instructions."  
> 
> **Tassa evamassa: 'acchariyaṁ, vata bho, abbhutaṁ, vata bho, puññānaṁ gati, puññānaṁ vipāko.**  
> "And it might occur to him: 'Wonderful indeed! Amazing indeed is the course of merit and the result of merit!'"  
> 
> **Ayañhi rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto manusso; ahampi manusso.**  
> "This King Ajātasattu of Magadha, son of the Vedehī queen, is a human being; I too am a human being."  
> 
> **Ayañhi rājā māgadho ajātasattu vedehiputto pañcahi kāmaguṇehi samappito samaṅgībhūto paricāreti, devo maññe.**  
> "Yet this King Ajātasattu enjoys and is endowed with the five strands of sensual pleasure, almost as if he were a god."  
> 
> **Ahaṁ panamhissa dāso kammakāro pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṅkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī mukhullokako.**  
> "But I am merely his servant and worker, rising before him, retiring after him, carrying out his commands, acting pleasingly, speaking kindly, and watching his face for instructions."  
> 
> **So vatassāhaṁ puññāni kareyyaṁ.**  
> "'What if I were to perform meritorious deeds?'"  
> 
> **Yannūnāhaṁ kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajeyyan'ti.**  
> "'Suppose I were to shave off my hair and beard, put on the saffron robes, and go forth from the household life into homelessness.'"  
> 
> **So aparena samayena kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajeyya.**  
> "After some time, he shaves off his hair and beard, dons the saffron robes, and goes forth from the household life into homelessness."  
> 
> **So evaṁ pabbajito samāno kāyena saṁvuto vihareyya, vācāya saṁvuto vihareyya, manasā saṁvuto vihareyya, ghāsacchādanaparamatāya santuṭṭho, abhirato paviveke.**  
> "Having thus gone forth, he dwells restrained in body, restrained in speech, and restrained in mind, content with mere food and clothing, delighting in seclusion."  
> 
> **Tañce te purisā evamāroceyyuṁ: 'yagghe, deva, jāneyyāsi, yo te so puriso dāso kammakāro pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṅkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī mukhullokako.**  
> "And suppose your men were to report to you: 'Your Majesty, that servant and worker of yours--the one who rose before you, retired after you, carried out your commands, acted pleasingly, spoke kindly, and watched your face for instructions--"  
> 
> **So, deva, kesamassuṁ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajito.**  
> "'has shaved off his hair and beard, donned the saffron robes, and gone forth from the household life into homelessness.'"  
> 
> **So evaṁ pabbajito samāno kāyena saṁvuto viharati, vācāya saṁvuto viharati, manasā saṁvuto viharati, ghāsacchādanaparamatāya santuṭṭho, abhirato paviveke'ti.**  
> "Having thus gone forth, he now lives restrained in body, speech, and mind, content with mere food and clothing, delighting in seclusion."  
> 
> **Api nu tvaṁ evaṁ vadeyyāsi: 'etu me, bho, so puriso, punadeva hotu dāso kammakāro pubbuṭṭhāyī pacchānipātī kiṅkārapaṭissāvī manāpacārī piyavādī mukhullokako'ti?**  
> "Would you then say: 'Let that man come back to me and once again be my servant and worker, rising before me and retiring after me, carrying out my commands and serving me as before'?"  
> 
> **No hetaṁ, bhante.**  
> "Certainly not, Bhante."  
> 
> **Atha kho naṁ mayameva abhivādeyyāmapi, paccuṭṭheyyāmapi, āsanenapi nimanteyyāma, abhinimanteyyāmapi naṁ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārehi, dhammikampissa rakkhāvaraṇaguttiṁ saṁvidaheyyāmā.**  
> "Rather, we ourselves would pay homage to him, rise up in respect for him, offer him a seat, invite him to accept robes, almsfood, lodging, and medicines for the sick, and arrange lawful protection, security, and support for him."  
> 
> **Taṁ kiṁ maññasi, mahārāja, yadi evaṁ sante hoti vā sandiṭṭhikaṁ sāmaññaphalaṁ no vā?**  
> "What do you think, Great King? If this is so, is there a visible fruit of the contemplative life in this very life, or not?"  
> 
> **Addhā kho, bhante, evaṁ sante hoti sandiṭṭhikaṁ sāmaññaphalaṁ.**  
> "Indeed, Bhante, in such a case there is a visible fruit of the contemplative life in this very life."  
> 
> **Idaṁ kho te, mahārāja, mayā paṭhamaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sandiṭṭhikaṁ sāmaññaphalaṁ paññattan.**  
> "This, Great King, is the first visible fruit of the contemplative life in this very life that I have explained to you."  
> 
> — *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2.35-36)
> 
> **Ye parato "na bhikkhave pañcahi ābādhehi phuṭṭho pabbājetabbo"tiādiṃ katvā yāva "na andhamūgabadhiro pabbājetabbo"ti evaṃ paṭikkhittā puggalā, te vajjetvā pabbajjādosavirahito puggalo pabbājetabbo. Sopi ca mātāpitūhi anuññātoyeva.**  
> "Avoiding those persons who are later prohibited, beginning with 'Monks, one afflicted with the five diseases should not be given the going-forth' down to 'one blind, mute, and deaf should not be given the going-forth,' a person free from the faults obstructing going forth may be given the going-forth. And he too must have been permitted by his mother and father."  
> 
> **Pabbajjā hi saraṇagamaneheva siddhā, sikkhāpadāni pana kevalaṃ sikkhāparipūraṇatthaṃ jānitabbāni.**  
> "For the going-forth is accomplished by the going for refuge; the training rules, however, are to be known simply for the fulfillment of the training."  
> 
> — *Samantapāsādikā* (Mahākhandhakavaṇṇanā, Pabbajjākathā)
