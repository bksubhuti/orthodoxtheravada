### Grammatical Analysis
**Pabbajjā**: [f.] Going forth; leaving home. From *pabbajati*, "goes forth" (prefix *pa* + *vaj*, "to go"); the grammar text Payogasiddhi has *pabbajanaṃ pabbajjā*, "going forth is *pabbajjā*". The Apadāna-aṭṭhakathā glosses it: *Pabbajjāti anagāriyabhāvo*, "going forth means the state of homelessness" (Ap-a 1211:15); the canonical phrase is *agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajati*, "goes forth from home into homelessness".

### Orthodox Definition
*Pabbajjā* is the going forth from the household life into the Buddha's discipline. The Mūla gives the stock formula: one who has faith reflects that "the household life is crowded, a path of dust; going forth is like the open air", and decides to "shave off my hair and beard, put on saffron robes, and go forth from home to homelessness" (DN 2; the Pabbajjā Sutta, Sn 3.1, says the same in verse).

In the Vinaya (Mahāvagga) the going forth was first allowed together with the higher ordination by the three goings for refuge (Vin-iii 186): the candidate has hair and beard shaved, puts on the yellow robe, salutes the monks' feet, sits on his haunches with joined palms and says the three refuges three times. The *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* explains that the higher ordination by the three refuges was later rejected in favour of the formal act with a motion and three announcements (*ñatticatutthakamma*), while the going forth was neither rejected nor allowed again; the Buddha then re-allowed the novice's going forth (*sāmaṇerapabbajjā*) through the three refuges (Sp-iii 255). The novice trains in the ten training rules (*dasa sikkhāpadāni*) the Buddha allowed for novices (Vin-iii 603). It is distinct from *upasampadā*, the higher ordination.

The Mahāvagga also lays down conditions: a boy under fifteen is not to be let go forth, except one who is able to scare away crows (Vin-iii 564-565); a child without the parents' consent is not to be let go forth (Vin-iii 599); and one afflicted with any of the five diseases is not to be let go forth (Vin-iii 529). Whoever lets such a person go forth incurs an offence of wrong-doing (*dukkaṭa*).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **So tena saddhāpaṭilābhena samannāgato iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ sambādho gharāvāso rajopatho, abbhokāso pabbajjā. Nayidaṃ sukaraṃ agāraṃ ajjhāvasatā ekantaparipuṇṇaṃ ekantaparisuddhaṃ saṅkhalikhitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ carituṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ kesamassuṃ ohāretvā kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādetvā agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajeyyan ’ ti.**  
> Endowed with such faith, he reflects: ‘The household life is crowded, a path of dust. Going forth is like the open air. It is not easy for one dwelling at home to lead the perfectly complete, perfectly purified holy life, bright as a polished conch. Let me then shave off my hair and beard, put on saffron robes, and go forth from home to homelessness.’  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Suttanipāta)
> **Pabbajjaṃ kittayissāmi, yathā pabbaji cakkhumā; 408. Sambādhoyaṃ gharāvāso, rajassāyatanaṃ iti; Abbhokāsova pabbajjā, iti disvāna pabbaji.**  
> Now I will proclaim the Going Forth, how he, the one with vision, went forth; how he, having investigated, proclaimed the Going Forth. “The household life is crowded, a sphere of dust; thus, the going forth is like the open air; seeing this, he went forth.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, the going forth by the three refuges)
> **Paṭhamaṃ kesamassuṃ ohārāpetvā, kāsāyāni vatthāni acchādāpetvā, ekaṃsaṃ uttarāsaṅgaṃ kārāpetvā, bhikkhūnaṃ pāde vandāpetvā, ukkuṭikaṃ nisīdāpetvā, añjaliṃ paggaṇhāpetvā, evaṃ vadehīti vattabbo – buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi; dutiyampi buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, dutiyampi dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, dutiyampi saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi; tatiyampi buddhaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, tatiyampi dhammaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmi, tatiyampi saṅghaṃ saraṇaṃ gacchāmī” ti. “Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, imehi tīhi saraṇagamanehi pabbajjaṃ upasampadan” ti.**  
> First, having made them shave off hair and beard, having made them put on yellow robes, having made them arrange an upper robe over one shoulder, having made them honour the monks’ feet, having made them sit down on their haunches, having made them salute with joined palms, they should be told: ‘Speak thus: “I go to the Buddha for refuge, I go to the Dhamma for refuge, I go to the Saṅgha for refuge. And a second time I go to the Buddha for refuge, a second time I go to the Dhamma for refuge, a second time I go to the Saṅgha for refuge; And a third time I go to the Buddha for refuge, a third time I go to the Dhamma for refuge, a third time I go to the Saṅgha for refuge.”’ ‘I allow, monks, the going forth and the ordination by these three goings for refuge.’  
>  
> — [Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/) (Pabbajjūpasampadākathā, 34) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=29&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=UGHhua1oYW1h4bmDIGtlc2FtYXNzdeG5gyBvaMSBcsSBcGV0dsSBIFtvaMSBcmV0dsSBIChrYS4pXSwga8SBc8SBecSBbmk%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, the ten training rules of the novice)
> **Bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ … pe … anujānāmi, bhikkhave, sāmaṇerānaṃ dasa sikkhāpadāni, tesu ca sāmaṇerehi sikkhituṃ – pāṇātipātā veramaṇī, adinnādānā veramaṇī, abrahmacariyā veramaṇī, musāvādā veramaṇī, surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇī, vikālabhojanā veramaṇī, naccagītavāditavisūkadassanā veramaṇī, mālāgandhavilepanadhāraṇamaṇḍanavibhūsanaṭṭhānā veramaṇī, uccāsayanamahāsayanā veramaṇī, jātarūparajatapaṭiggahaṇā veramaṇī.**  
> They told this matter to the Blessed One … and he said: “I allow, bhikkhus, ten training rules for novices, and novices are to train in these: refraining from killing living creatures, refraining from taking what has not been given, refraining from uncelibacy, refraining from false speech, refraining from taking distilled and fermented drinks that cause heedlessness, refraining from eating at the wrong time, refraining from dancing, singing, music, and watching shows, refraining from wearing garlands, using perfumes, and beautifying the body with cosmetics, refraining from high and luxurious beds, refraining from accepting gold and silver.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, the age of the one who goes forth)
> **Na, bhikkhave, ūnapannarasavasso dārako pabbājetabbo. Yo pabbājeyya, āpatti dukkaṭassāti. “bhagavatā paññattaṃ ‘ na ūnapannarasavasso dārako pabbājetabbo ’ ti. Ime ca dārakā ūnapannarasavassā. Kena nu kho upāyena ime dārakā na vinasseyyun” ti? Atha kho āyasmā ānando bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesi. Ussahanti pana te, ānanda, dārakā kāke uḍḍāpetunti? “anujānāmi, bhikkhave, ūnapannarasavassaṃ dārakaṃ kākuḍḍepakaṃ pabbājetun” ti.**  
> “Monks, a boy of less than fifteen years of age should not be let go forth. Whoever should let (one such) go forth, there is an offence of wrong-doing.” “It is laid down by the Blessed One: ‘A boy of less than fifteen years of age should not be let go forth.’ And these boys are less than fifteen years of age. By what means might these boys not be lost?” Then the Venerable Ānanda told this matter to the Blessed One. “But are these boys able, Ānanda, to scare crows?” “I allow you, bhikkhus, to let a youth of less than fifteen years of age who is a scarer of crows go forth.”  
>  
> — [Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/) (Ahivātakarogavatthu, 100) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=110&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=TmEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgxatuYXBhbm5hcmFzYXZhc3NvIGTEgXJha28gcGFiYsSBamV0YWJiby4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Idāni yā sā bhagavatā bārāṇasiyaṃ tīhi saraṇagamanehi pabbajjā ca upasampadā ca anuññātā, tato yasmā upasampadaṃ paṭikkhipitvā garubhāve ṭhapetvā ñatticatutthena kammena upasampadā anuññātā, pabbajjā pana neva paṭikkhittā, na puna anuññātā, tasmā anāgate bhikkhūnaṃ vimati uppajjissati – “ayaṃ pabbajjā nāma pubbe upasampadāsadisā, kiṃ nu kho idānipi upasampadā viya kammavācāya eva kattabbā, udāhu saraṇagamanehī” ti. Imañca panatthaṃ viditvā bhagavā puna tīhi saraṇagamanehi sāmaṇerapabbajjaṃ anujānitukāmo, tasmā dhammasenāpati taṃ bhagavato ajjhāsayaṃ viditvā bhagavantaṃ puna pabbajjaṃ anujānāpetukāmo āha –**  
> Now, since the going forth and ordination allowed by the Blessed One at Benares through the three goings for refuge were later rejected in favor of the higher ordination allowed by a formal act consisting of a motion and three announcements, but the going forth was neither rejected nor explicitly re-allowed, uncertainty would arise among monks in the future— “Is this going forth the same as the former ordination?
