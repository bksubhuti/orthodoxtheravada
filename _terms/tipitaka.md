### Grammatical Analysis
**Tipiṭaka**: [nt.] The Three Baskets; the Pāḷi Canon. Formed by *ti* (three) + *piṭaka* (basket; collection). The word *tipiṭaka* does not occur in the Mūla (0 hits); the Aṭṭhakathā use it (109 hits) and speak of "tīṇi piṭakāni", the three baskets (As 151:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Tipiṭaka* is the word of the Buddha classified by basket. The Atthasālinī, speaking of all that the Buddha taught between his first and last words, says: "classified by basket (*piṭakato*) it is the three baskets, by collection (*nikāyato*) the five collections, by factor (*aṅgato*) the nine factors, by mass of Dhamma (*dhammakkhandhato*) the eighty-four thousand masses of Dhamma" (As 151:1). The three are:
1. **[Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) Piṭaka (the Basket of Discipline):** the two Pātimokkhas, the two Vibhaṅgas, the twenty-two Khandhakas and the sixteen Parivāra sections (As 151:4).
2. **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/) Piṭaka, the Suttanta Piṭaka (the Basket of Discourses):** the five Nikāyas: Dīgha with 34 suttas, Majjhima with 152, Saṃyutta with 7,762, Aṅguttara with 9,557, and the Khuddaka Nikāya of fifteen divisions (As 151:5-9).
3. **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka (the Basket of Ultimate Doctrine):** the seven treatises beginning with the Dhammasaṅgaṇī (As 151:10).

In the Mūla, the first recitation took place at Rājagaha soon after the Buddha's passing, when the Elder Mahākassapa had five hundred bhikkhus appointed "to recite the Dhamma and the Vinaya" there (Vin-iv 2685:2; Cūḷavagga XI, the "Pañcasatikakkhandhaka", the Five Hundred). For orthodox *[Mahāvihāra](/glossary/maha-vihara/)* practitioners the canon is the measure of what is the Buddha's word. The Buddha himself gave the test in the Four Great Referrals: what a monk claims to have heard is "laid alongside the Suttas and compared with the Vinaya"; if it fits neither, "this is not the Blessed One's word" and it is to be rejected (DN 16, D-ii 486:4-5).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, the first council)
> **Atha kho āyasmā mahākassapo saṅghaṃ ñāpesi – 438.“Suṇātu me, āvuso, saṅgho. Yadi saṅghassa pattakallaṃ, saṅgho imāni pañca bhikkhusatāni sammanneyya – rājagahe vassaṃ vasantāni dhammañca vinayañca saṅgāyituṃ, na aññehi bhikkhūhi rājagahe vassaṃ vasitabbanti.**  
> Then the venerable Mahākassapa informed the Saṅgha: 438. “Let the Saṅgha listen to me, friends. If it seems right to the Saṅgha, the Saṅgha should appoint these five hundred bhikkhus to spend the rains in Rājagaha to recite the Dhamma and the Vinaya, and no other bhikkhus should spend the rains in Rājagaha.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha, the Great Referrals)
> **“Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ sammukhā metaṃ, āvuso, bhagavato sutaṃ sammukhā paṭiggahitaṃ, ayaṃ dhammo ayaṃ vinayo idaṃ satthusāsanan ’ ti. Tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno bhāsitaṃ neva abhinanditabbaṃ nappaṭikkositabbaṃ. Anabhinanditvā appaṭikkositvā tāni padabyañjanāni sādhukaṃ uggahetvā sutte osāretabbāni, vinaye sandassetabbāni.**  
> “Here, monks, a monk might speak like this: ‘I have heard this directly from the Gracious One, friends, directly I learned it: “This is the Teaching, this is the Discipline, this is the Teacher’s Dispensation.”’ That monk’s speech, monks, is not to be rejoiced over, not to be scorned at. Without having rejoiced over it, without having scorned it, after learning those words and syllables well, they should be laid alongside the Discourses (sutta), they should be compared with the Discipline (vinaya).  
>  
> — *[Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/)* (DN 16, Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, Catumahāpadesakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=103&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTg4LuKAmOKAmCBJZGhhLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGJoaWtraHUgZXZh4bmDIHZhZGV5eWE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the three baskets)
> **Taṃ sabbampi saṅgayhamānaṃ piṭakato tīṇi piṭakāni honti, nikāyato pañca nikāyā , aṅgato navaṅgāni , dhammakkhandhato caturāsītidhammakkhandhasahassāni. Kathaṃ? Sabbampi hetaṃ piṭakato vinayapiṭakaṃ suttantapiṭakaṃ abhidhammapiṭakanti tippabhedameva hoti. Tattha ubhayāni pātimokkhāni dve vibhaṅgā dvāvīsati khandhakā soḷasa parivārāti idaṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ nāma.**  
> When all of that is classified by basket, it comprises the Three Baskets (tīṇi piṭakāni); by collection, the five collections (pañca nikāyā); by factor, the nine factors (navaṅgāni); and by mass of Dhamma, the eighty-four thousand masses of Dhamma (caturāsītidhammakkhandhasahassāni). How so? Indeed, all of that, when classified by basket, is of just three divisions: the Vinaya Piṭaka, the Suttanta Piṭaka, and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka. Therein, both monastic codes (pātimokkhāni), the two Expositions (vibhaṅgā), the twenty-two Chapters (khandhakā), and the sixteen Parivāra sections comprise the Vinaya Piṭaka.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Nidānakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGHhuYMgc2FiYmFtcGkgc2HhuYVnYXloYW3EgW5h4bmDIHBp4bmtYWthdG8gdMSr4bmHaSBwaeG5rWFrxIFuaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, Sutta and Abhidhamma)
> **Khuddakapāṭhadhammapadaudānaitivuttakasuttanipātavimānavatthupetavatthutheragāthātherīgāthājātakaniddesapaṭisambhidāapadānabuddhavaṃsacariyāpiṭakavasena pannarasappabhedo khuddakanikāyoti idaṃ suttantapiṭakaṃ nāma. Dhammasaṅgaṇīādīni satta pakaraṇāni abhidhammapiṭakaṃ nāma.**  
> The collection consisting of fifteen divisions—the Khuddakapāṭha, Dhammapada, Udāna, Itivuttaka, Sutta Nipāta, Vimānavatthu, Petavatthu, Theragāthā, Therīgāthā, Jātaka, Niddesa, Paṭisambhidāmagga, Apadāna, Buddhavaṃsa, and Cariyāpiṭaka—is the Khuddaka Nikāya; this is what is called the Suttanta Piṭaka. The seven treatises beginning with the Dhammasaṅgaṇī are what is called the Abhidhamma Piṭaka.  
>
