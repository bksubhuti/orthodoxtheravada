### Grammatical Analysis
**Ādīnavañāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of danger; knowledge of disadvantage. Formed by *ādīnava* (danger, fault, disadvantage) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge).

### Orthodox Definition
Flowing directly from the knowledge of fearfulness, *Ādīnavañāṇa* is the realization that because all conditioned things are continuously collapsing, they are fundamentally dangerous, utterly devoid of satisfaction, and deeply flawed. 

The Visuddhimagga says that all formations then appear as a great mass of danger, tasteless and unenjoyable (Vism-ii 1719:3), and compares the meditator to a fearful man who sees only danger in a beast-infested forest, a burning house and so on (Vism-ii 1720:2-3). The Paṭisambhidāmagga presents the knowledge as seeing "arising is terror", "occurrence is terror" and so on (Paṭis 310:2-4), and sets against it the knowledge of the peaceful state: "non-arising is safety" (Paṭis 311:1), where [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/) is seen as secure.

### Quote
> **Kathaṃ bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā ādīnave ñāṇaṃ? Uppādo bhayanti – bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā ādīnave ñāṇaṃ.**  
> 53. How is it that wisdom in the appearance as terror is knowledge of danger? ‘Arising is terror’—this wisdom in the appearance as terror is knowledge of danger.  
>

> The three kinds of becoming appear like charcoal pits full of glowing coals; the four great elements like fierce venomous snakes; the five aggregates like murderers with raised swords; the six internal bases like an empty village; the six external bases like village-raiding robbers; the seven stations of consciousness and the nine abodes of beings as though burning, blazing, and glowing with the eleven fires; and all formations appear as a huge mass of dangers, destitute of gratification or substance, being like a tumor, like a disease, like a dart, like a calamity, and like an affliction. They appear as a brushwood thicket infested with wild beasts even though of pleasant appearance; like a cave full of tigers; like water haunted by monsters and ogres; like enemies with raised swords; like poisoned food; like a road beset by robbers; like a burning house; and like a battlefield with armies ready for war, as they would appear to a man who is afraid and wants to live in peace. Just as that person, upon reaching such a thicket infested with wild beasts and so on, is fearful, agitated, and has his hair standing on end, seeing only danger all around; just so, when all formations appear as a terror by way of the contemplation of dissolution, this meditator sees only danger all around, without substance or gratification. As he sees in this way, knowledge of danger (ādīnavañāṇa) has arisen in him.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Āditta Sutta* / Fire Sermon (SN 35.28; heading "6. Ādittasuttaṃ", S-iv 84:1, discourse at S-iv 85:1) – the Buddha teaches the thousand bhikkhus at Gayāsīsa that all is burning. It does not name ādīnavañāṇa; it is cited as the background image.
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* (Ñāṇakathā; heading "8. Ādīnavañāṇaniddeso", Paṭis 309:1-330:1) – "Kathaṃ bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā ādīnave ñāṇaṃ?" (Paṭis 310:1-4), with "Anuppādo khemanti – santipade ñāṇaṃ" (Paṭis 311:1) as the counterpart.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter XXI, §752; Vism-ii 1719:3-1721:2) – the forest, cave, water, enemies, food, road, burning house and battlefield similes of the fearful man, and the arising of ādīnavañāṇa.
