### Grammatical Analysis
**Sakadāgāmī**: [m.; f.] Once-Returner. Formed by *sakiṃ* (once) + *āgāmī* (one who comes). The Aṭṭhakathā: "one who comes only once by way of rebirth-linking" (*paṭisandhivasena sakiṃ āgacchatīti sakadāgāmī*, Pp-a 256).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Sakadāgāmī* is the second of the four noble persons (*ariya-puggala*), after the Stream-enterer (*[Sotāpanna](/glossary/sotapanna/)*). The Mūla defines him: "with the destruction of the three fetters and the reduction of lust, hatred and delusion (*rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā*), he becomes a once-returner, who, having come back once to this world, makes an end of suffering" (DN 6; Pp 224). The Puggalapaññatti adds that the person practising for the realization of this fruit is one "working for the reduction of lust, hatred and delusion" (Pp 232).

He does not eliminate any further fetter, but at the path-moment (*sakadāgāmimaggakkhaṇe*) the path factors "emerge from" the gross fetters of sensual passion and of ill-will (*oḷārikā kāmarāgasaññojanā paṭighasaññojanā*) and the gross underlying tendencies to them (Paṭis 387).

The Aṭṭhakathā explains the phrase "having come back once to this world": it covers only one of five kinds of once-returner. The five are: one who attains the fruit here and attains final Nibbāna here; one who attains it here and attains final Nibbāna in the deva world; one who attains it in the deva world and attains final Nibbāna there; one who attains it in the deva world, is reborn here and attains final Nibbāna; and the one who attains it here, lives out his life-span in the deva world, is reborn here again and attains final Nibbāna. Only this last is the one meant in the Pāḷi (Pp-a 256).
### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Puna caparaṃ, mahāli, bhikkhu tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmī hoti, sakideva imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ karoti.**  
> “Again, Mahāli, a bhikkhu, with the destruction of the three bonds and the reduction of lust, hatred, and delusion, becomes a once-returner, who, having returned once more to this world, makes an end of suffering.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Puggalapaññatti)
> **Katamo ca puggalo sakadāgāmī? Idhekacco puggalo tiṇṇaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā rāgadosamohānaṃ tanuttā sakadāgāmī hoti, sakideva imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā dukkhassantaṃ karoti – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo “sakadāgāmī”.**  
> 34. What sort of person is a “once-returner”? Here a person, having completely destroyed the three fetters, having weakened lust, hatred, and delusion, becomes a once-returner; coming back once only to this world, he makes an end of suffering. Such a person is said to be a once-returner.  
>  
> — [Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/) (Niddeso, para. 34) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_04&page_number=119&name=puggalapa%C3%B1%C3%B1attip%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzQuIEthdGFtbyBjYSBwdWdnYWxvIHNha2FkxIFnxIFtxKs%2F)

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, the path moment)
> **Sakadāgāmimaggakkhaṇe dassanaṭṭhena sammādiṭṭhi … pe … avikkhepaṭṭhena sammāsamādhi oḷārikā kāmarāgasaññojanā paṭighasaññojanā oḷārikā kāmarāgānusayā paṭighānusayā vuṭṭhāti, tadanuvattakakilesehi ca khandhehi ca vuṭṭhāti, bahiddhā ca sabbanimittehi vuṭṭhāti.**  
> At the moment of the path of once-returning, right view ... right concentration in the sense of non-distraction emerges from gross fetters of sensual passion and of resistance, from gross underlying tendencies to sensual passion and to resistance, and it emerges from defilements and from aggregates that occur consequent upon them, and externally it emerges from all signs.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Paṭis 387) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=67&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=U2FrYWTEgWfEgW1pbWFnZ2Fra2hh4bmHZSBkYXNzYW5h4bmt4bmtaGVuYSBzYW1txIFkaeG5reG5rWhpIOKApiBwZSDigKY%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Sakadāgāminiddese – paṭisandhivasena sakiṃ āgacchatīti sakadāgāmī . Sakidevāti ekavāraṃyeva. Imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvāti iminā pañcasu sakadāgāmīsu cattāro vajjetvā ekova gahito. Ekacco hi idha sakadāgāmiphalaṃ patvā idheva parinibbāyati, ekacco idha patvā devaloke parinibbāyati, ekacco devaloke patvā tattheva parinibbāyati, ekacco devaloke patvā idhūpapajjitvā parinibbāyati – ime cattāropi idha na gahitā. Yo pana idha patvā devaloke yāvatāyukaṃ vasitvā puna idhūpapajjitvā parinibbāyati – ayaṃ ekova idha gahitoti veditabbo.**  
> 34. In the explanation of the once-returner (sakadāgāmī), it means ‘one who comes only once by way of rebirth-linking’. Only once (sakideva) means only one time. With the phrase coming back once only to this world (imaṃ lokaṃ āgantvā), only one out of the five types of once-returners is included, excluding the other four. Indeed, some (once-returners) attain the fruit of once-returning here and attain final Nibbāna right here; some attain here and attain final Nibbāna in the deva world; some attain in the deva world and attain final Nibbāna right there; some attain in the deva world, are reborn here, and attain final Nibbāna—these four types are not included here. However, the one who attains (the fruit of once-returning) here, stays for the full lifespan in the deva world, then is reborn here again and attains final Nibbāna—only this one type is included here. This should be understood.  
>  
> — Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā (Sakadāgāminiddesa, Pp-a 256) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=47&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MzQuIFNha2FkxIFnxIFtaW5pZGRlc2Ug4oCTIHBh4bmtaXNhbmRoaXZhc2VuYSBzYWtp4bmDIMSBZ2FjY2hhdMSrdGk%3D)
