### Grammatical Analysis
**Suññatā**: [f.] Emptiness; voidness. Derived from *suñña* (empty, void) + *tā* (abstract noun suffix, -ness). 

### Orthodox Definition
In orthodox Theravāda, *Suññatā* is not an absolute cosmic void. The Buddha's own definition is that the world is called empty "because it is empty of self and of what belongs to self" (*yasmā ca kho, ānanda, suññaṃ attena vā attaniyena vā, tasmā suñño lokoti vuccati*, S-iv 365:4), and he applies this to the eye, forms, eye-consciousness, contact and feeling, and so on through the six sense-bases (S-iv 365:6-7). This is the first application:

1. **Conditioned Emptiness**: the aggregates and sense-bases are empty (*suñña*) of a self (*attā*) or what belongs to a self (*attaniya*). The Aṭṭhakathā says the contemplation of voidness (*suññatānupassanā*) is simply the contemplation of non-self (*anattānupassanā*), by which clinging to "there is a self" is abandoned (Vism-ii 2031:1; and Paṭis I 227 cited at Vism-ii 1587:4).
2. **The Door to Liberation**: the Paṭisambhidāmagga defines the *suññato vimokkho*: a bhikkhu gone to the wilderness, a tree root or an empty place reflects "this is void of self or of what belongs to self" (Paṭis 1138:1-3). The Visuddhimagga says that "attending as not-self, one who is full of rapture acquires the liberation through emptiness" (Vism-ii 1787:5, citing Paṭis I 223), and that the Noble Path which occurs with Nibbāna as object in an emptiness mode (*suññatākārena*) is *suññato* (Vism-ii 1788:5).

Nibbāna itself is termed *suññata* because it is void of greed, hatred and delusion: the path obtains the name "voidness liberation" by quality because it is void of these, and by object because it takes Nibbāna, "named 'voidness' because it is void of greed and so on", as object (Paṭis-a 1990:4).

### Quote (Mūla: Suññatalokasutta)
> **‘“suñño loko, suñño loko ’ ti, bhante, vuccati. Kittāvatā nu kho, bhante, suñño lokoti vuccatī” ti? “Yasmā ca kho, ānanda, suññaṃ attena vā attaniyena vā tasmā suñño lokoti vuccati.**  
> “Venerable sir, it is said, ‘Empty is the world, empty is the world.’ In what way, venerable sir, is it said, ‘Empty is the world’?” “It is, Ānanda, because it is empty of self and of what belongs to self that it is said, ‘Empty is the world.’  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 35.85, Suññatalokasutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_04&page_number=280&name=sa%E1%B8%B7%C4%81yatanavaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCY4oCYIHN1w7HDsW8gbG9rbywgc3XDscOxbyBsb2tvIOKAmQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Katamo suññato vimokkho ? Idha bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā iti paṭisañcikkhati – “suññamidaṃ attena vā attaniyena vā” ti.**  
> 210. What is liberation by voidness? Here, a bhikkhu, who has gone to the wilderness or has gone to the root of a tree or has gone to an empty place, considers thus: “This is void of self or of what belongs to self.”  
>  
> — Paṭisambhidāmagga (§210, Paṭis 1138:1-3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=231&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjEwLiBLYXRhbW8gc3XDscOxYXRvIHZpbW9ra2hvID8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Anattato manasikaronto vedabahulo suññatavimokkhaṃ paṭilabhatī” ti (paṭi. ma.1.223). Suññatākārena nibbānaṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā pavatto suññatoti veditabbo.**  
> “Attending as not-self, one who is full of rapture acquires the liberation through emptiness.” That which has occurred having made Nibbana its object in an emptiness mode should be understood as emptiness (suññato).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 1787-1788, the three liberations) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=296&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=QW5hdHRhdG8gbWFuYXNpa2Fyb250byB2ZWRhYmFodWxvIHN1w7HDsWF0YXZpbW9ra2hh4bmDIHBh4bmtaWxhYmhhdMSrIOKAmeKAmQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Cūḷasuññata Sutta* (MN 121, M-iii 531:1, "1. Cūḷasuññatasuttaṃ") – the step-by-step attending to the oneness dependent on successive perceptions (earth, M-iii 533:1; then the signless concentration of mind, M-iii 539:1); the Buddha ends: whatever ascetics and brahmins of past, future and present enter upon the "pure, supreme and ultimate voidness" enter this very one (M-iii 540:1-3).
* **Sutta**: *Suññatalokasutta* (SN 35.85, S-iv 364:1, "2. Suññatalokasuttaṃ"; Ānanda's question at S-iv 365:1-8) – the world is empty of self and of what belongs to self.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 1587:4, 1787:1-5, 1788:5, 2031:1); *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 1990:4).
