### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭhavīdhātu**: [f.] earth element; element of extension; solidity. Formed by *paṭhavī* (earth) + *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element). The Atthasālinī explains *dhātu* in the sense of emptiness of a being or self: “nissattasuññatasabhāvaṭṭhena dhātu” (As 2135:4, on the *āpodhātu*, applied to the other elements as well).

### Orthodox Definition
The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it: “Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ pathavīdhātu? Yaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharagataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā upādiṇṇaṃ vā anupādiṇṇaṃ vā”: whatever is solid, harsh, the solidity, the nature of solidity, internal or external, grasped by kamma or not (Dhs 1710:1-2). The Mūla gives it in the Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta, spoken by Sāriputta: it is internal or external, and the internal earth element is whatever is internal, personal, solid, harsh and clung to (*upādinna*), namely hair, body hair, nails, teeth, skin, and so on to feces (MN 28, M-i 1037:1-4). The Visuddhimagga explains that “kakkhaḷa” is the characteristic and “kharigata” the mode: the earth element has the characteristic of hardness and so appears as harsh (Vism-i 2109:6).

The Atthasālinī and the Visuddhimagga give its marks: its characteristic is hardness (*kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā*), its function is to act as a foundation (*patiṭṭhānarasā*), its manifestation is receiving (*sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā*) (As 2135:7; Vism-i 2172:1). As a condition, the earth element is held together by the water element, maintained by the fire element and supported by the wind element, and is the foundation of the other three great elements (Vism-i 2209:1). The Visuddhimagga counts twenty aspects of the earth element in the body, from hair to feces, with twelve for the water element, four for fire and six for wind, forty-two in all (Vism-i 2113:1).

For practice, the sutta continues: “Taṃ netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā”, “this is not mine, I am not this, this is not my self”, seeing it as it really is with right wisdom; seeing the external earth element too, it is destroyed when the external water element is disturbed, so how could this body, clung to by craving, be “I” or “mine” (M-i 1037:6-8, 1038:1-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Katamā cāvuso, pathavīdhātu? Pathavīdhātu siyā ajjhattikā, siyā bāhirā. Katamā cāvuso, ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – kesā lomā nakhā dantā taco maṃsaṃ nhāru aṭṭhi aṭṭhimiñjaṃ vakkaṃ hadayaṃ yakanaṃ kilomakaṃ pihakaṃ papphāsaṃ antaṃ antaguṇaṃ udariyaṃ karīsaṃ, yaṃ vā panaññampi kiñci ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharigataṃ upādinnaṃ.**  
> “What, friends, is the earth element? The earth element might be either internal or external. What, friends, is the internal earth element? Whatever internally, belonging to oneself, is solid, harsh, and clung-to; namely—hairs of the head, body hairs, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone-marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, pleura, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, excrement, or whatever else internally, belonging to oneself, is solid, harsh, and clung-to.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **Ayaṃ vuccatāvuso, ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu. Yā ceva kho pana ajjhattikā pathavīdhātu, yā ca bāhirā pathavīdhātu, pathavīdhāturevesā. ‘ Taṃ netaṃ mama, nesohamasmi, na meso attā ’ ti – evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya daṭṭhabbaṃ. Evametaṃ yathābhūtaṃ sammappaññāya disvā pathavīdhātuyā nibbindati, pathavīdhātuyā cittaṃ virājeti.**  
> This, friends, is called the internal earth element. Now both the internal earth element and the external earth element are simply earth element. ‘This is not mine, this I am not, this is not my self’—thus should it be seen as it actually is with proper wisdom. When one sees it thus as it actually is with proper wisdom, one becomes disenchanted with the earth element and makes the mind dispassionate toward the earth element.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 28, Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=242&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QXlh4bmDIHZ1Y2NhdMSBdnVzbywgYWpqaGF0dGlrxIEgcGF0aGF2xKtkaMSBdHUu)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ pathavīdhātu? Yaṃ kakkhaḷaṃ kharagataṃ kakkhaḷattaṃ kakkhaḷabhāvo ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā upādiṇṇaṃ vā anupādiṇṇaṃ vā – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ pathavīdhātu.**  
> 967. Which form is the earth element? Whatever is solid (kakkhaḷaṃ), harsh (kharagataṃ), the solidity (kakkhaḷattaṃ), the nature of solidity (kakkhaḷabhāvo), whether internal or external, whether grasped by kamma or ungrasped by kamma – this is that form which is the earth element.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§967, Rūpakaṇḍa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=201&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTY3LiBLYXRhbWHhuYMgdGHhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgcGF0aGF2xKtkaMSBdHU%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Tattha kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā pathavīdhātu , patiṭṭhānarasā, sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā.**  
> Therein, the earth element (pathavīdhātu) has the characteristic of solidity, the function of providing a foundation, and the manifestation of receiving (or supporting associated phenomena).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on §647, phoṭṭhabbāyatana) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=368&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGtha2toYeG4t2F0dGFsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBwYXRoYXbEq2RoxIF0dSAsIHBhdGnhua3hua1oxIFuYXJhc8SBLCBzYW1wYeG5rWljY2hhbmFwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tattha paṭhamaṃ lakkhaṇavacanaṃ, dutiyaṃ ākāravacanaṃ, kakkhaḷalakkhaṇā hi pathavīdhātu, sā pharusākārā hoti, tasmā kharigatanti vuttā.**  
> Herein, the first is a characteristic word, and the second is a word for the mode; for the earth element has the characteristic of hardness, and its mode is rough, which is why it is called ‘harsh.’  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XI, on MN 28) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=344&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHBh4bmtaGFtYeG5gyBsYWtraGHhuYdhdmFjYW5h4bmDLCBkdXRpeWHhuYMgxIFrxIFyYXZhY2FuYeG5gywga2Fra2hh4bi3YWxha2toYeG5h8SB)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Lakkhaṇāditoti pathavīdhātu kiṃ lakkhaṇā, kiṃ rasā, kiṃ paccupaṭṭhānāti evaṃ catassopi dhātuyo āvajjetvā pathavīdhātu kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā, patiṭṭhānarasā, sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā.**  
> 350. Regarding by characteristic and so on (lakkhaṇādito): having reflected upon the four elements thus: 'What is the characteristic of the earth element? What is its function? What is its manifestation?'—the earth element (pathavīdhātu) has the characteristic of hardness, the function of acting as a foundation, and is manifested as receiving.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XI, 350, lakkhaṇādito) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=362&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MzUwLiBMYWtraGHhuYfEgWRpdG90aSBwYXRoYXbEq2RoxIF0dSBraeG5gyBsYWtraGHhuYfEgSwga2nhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Iti vīsatiyā ākārehi pathavīdhātu, dvādasahi āpodhātu, catūhi tejodhātu, chahi vāyodhātūti dvācattālīsāya ākārehi catasso dhātuyo vitthāritā hontīti ayaṃ tāvettha pāḷivaṇṇanā.**  
> Thus, the earth element in twenty ways, the water element in twelve, the fire element in four, and the air element in six — in forty-two ways the four elements have been detailed; this, first of all, is the word-commentary on the Pāli.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta* (MN 28, M-i 1032–1055; the earth element at M-i 1037–1038) – Sāriputta's exposition of the internal and external earth element, and that even the external earth element is subject to destruction.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§967, Dhs 1710) – the definition in the analysis of the form of the touch base.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 2135:7) and *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XI, Vism-i 2109:6, 2113:1, 2172:1, 2209:1) – the characteristic, function, manifestation, the twenty-aspect list and the conditionality of the elements.
