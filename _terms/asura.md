### Grammatical Analysis
**Asura**: [m.] The Ṭīkā to the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha gives two derivations. Of the *petāsurā* (ghost-asuras): "*na suranti issariyakīḷādīhi na dibbantīti asurā*", "they are asuras because they do not delight, that is, do not shine, with sovereignty, play and so on"; of the others: "*na surā surappaṭipakkhāti asurā*", "they are asuras because they are not *suras* (gods), being the opponents of the gods" (Abhidh-s-t 1304:8-9). The Aṭṭhakathā on the Suvīra Sutta gives a story-based explanation: the devas who had been thrown down to the foot of Sineru said "*na suraṃ pivimha*", "we did not drink liquor", and "from then on they came to be called asuras" (Spk-i 1402:11-12).

### Orthodox Definition
The *asuras* are a class of beings counted with the *[deva](/glossary/deva/)* in the Mūla's accounts of the war between the devas and the asuras: "once in the past, bhikkhus, the war between the devas and the asuras was arrayed; then Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, addressed the asuras", telling them to bind Sakka and bring him to the city of the asuras (S-i 3227:2-4). The Aṭṭhakathā places their realm at the foot of Sineru: "from their kamma and the elements an asura realm arose at the base of Sineru, ten thousand yojanas in extent" (Spk-i 1402:13).

The texts give two kinds. The Ṭīkā distinguishes the *petāsurā*, ghost-asuras, from the others who are the opponents of the gods (Abhidh-s-t 1304:8-9). The Kathāvatthu speaks of the Kālakañjaka asuras who are of the same appearance, enjoyments, food and lifespan as the petas (Kv 2029:5). The Aṭṭhakathā counts *asurakāya* with the states of deprivation, "*apāya* here is the hell realm, the animal realm, the ghost realm and the asura realm" (Paṭis-a 1451:1). In the Saṅgīti Sutta, being reborn in the realm of the asuras is one of the untimely intervals for living the spiritual life (D-iii 1623:1-2).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Bhūtapubbaṃ, bhikkhave, devāsurasaṅgāmo samupabyūḷho ahosi. Atha kho, bhikkhave, vepacitti asurindo asure āmantesi – ‘ sace, mārisā, devānaṃ asurasaṅgāme samupabyūḷhe asurā jineyyuṃ devā parājineyyuṃ, yena naṃ sakkaṃ devānamindaṃ kaṇṭhapañcamehi bandhanehi bandhitvā mama santike āneyyātha asurapuran ’ ti.**  
> “Once in the past, bhikkhus, the devas and the asuras were arrayed for battle. Then Vepacitti, lord of the asuras, addressed the asuras thus: ‘Dear sirs, in the impending battle between the devas and the asuras, if the asuras win and the devas are defeated, bind Sakka, lord of the devas, by his four limbs and neck and bring him to me in the city of the asuras.’  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sāratthappakāsinī)
> **Sakko “gaṇhatha puttahatāya putte” ti te pādesu gahetvā sinerupāde khipāpesi. Sakkassa puññatejena tadanuvattakāpi sabbe tattheva patiṃsu. Te sineruvemajjhakāle saññaṃ labhitvā,“tātā na suraṃ pivimha, na suraṃ pivimhā” ti āhaṃsu. Tato paṭṭhāya asurā nāma jātā. Atha nesaṃ kammapaccayautusamuṭṭhānaṃ sinerussa heṭṭhimatale dasayojanasahassaṃ asurabhavanaṃ nibbatti.**  
> Sakka said, “Take the sons of the woman whose son was killed,” and having seized them by the feet, he had them thrown to the foot of Mount Sineru. By the power of Sakka’s merit, all of those following him also fell right there. Recovering their senses at the middle point of Mount Sineru, they said, “Friends, we did not drink liquor, we did not drink liquor.” From then on, they came to be called Asuras. Then, their Asura realm arose at the base of Mount Sineru, ten thousand leagues in extent, arising from their kamma and the elements.  
>  
> — [Sāratthappakāsinī](/glossary/saratthappakasini/) (Sagāthāvagga-aṭṭhakathā, Suvīra Sutta, Spk-i 1402) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_sa_01&page_number=309&name=sag%C4%81th%C4%81vaggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2Fra28g4oCY4oCYIGdh4bmHaGF0aGEgcHV0dGFoYXTEgXlhIHB1dHRlIOKAmeKAmQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Na suranti issariyakīḷādīhi na dibbantīti asurā , petāsurā. Itare pana na surā surappaṭipakkhāti asurā, idha ca petāsurānameva gahaṇaṃ itaresaṃ tāvatiṃsesu gahaṇassa icchitattā.**  
> They are asuras (asurā) because they do not delight (na suranti), which means they do not shine (na dibbantīti) with sovereignty, play, and so on (issariyakīḷādīhi); the ghost-asuras (petāsurā). But for the others (itare pana), they are asuras (asurā) because they are not gods (na surā), meaning they are opponents of the gods (surappaṭipakkhāti); and here (idha ca), there is the taking (gahaṇaṃ) of only the ghost-asuras (petāsurānameva), because it is desired (icchitattā) to take (gahaṇassa) the others (itaresaṃ) among the Tāvatiṃsa devas (tāvatiṃsesu).  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)-ṭīkā (Abhidh-s-t 1304) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=162&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TmEgc3VyYW50aSBpc3Nhcml5YWvEq%2BG4t8SBZMSraGkgbmEgZGliYmFudMSrdGkgYXN1csSB)
