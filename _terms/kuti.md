### Grammatical Analysis
**Kuṭi**: [f.] hut; small dwelling. In the Vinaya word-analysis: "a hut means: plastered inside, or plastered outside, or plastered both inside and outside" (*kuṭi nāma ullittā vā hoti avalittā vā ullittāvalittā vā*, Vin-i 1510:1). The Samantapāsādikā explains *ullittā* as plastered on the inside, *avalittā* as plastered on the outside (Sp-i 2263:1-3).

### Orthodox Definition
A *kuṭi* is a hut that a monk builds for himself. The Kuṭikāra training rule (Saṅghādisesa 6) says that when a monk, by his own begging (*saññācikāya*), has a hut built without an owner (*assāmikaṃ*) and for himself (*attuddesaṃ*), it must be built to the proper measure: twelve spans in length by the Sugata span, and seven spans across (*dīghaso dvādasa vidatthiyo sugatavidatthiyā, tiriyaṃ sattantarā*). Monks must be brought to designate the site (*vatthudesanā*), and they must designate it as harmless (*anārambhaṃ*) and with space around it (*saparikkamanaṃ*). A monk who builds on a harmful site without space around, or does not bring monks for the site designation, or exceeds the measure, commits a Saṅghādisesa offence (Vin-i 1489:1-6).

The origin of the rule: the monks of Āḷavī were building huts by their own begging, without an owner, for themselves, of no measure; the huts were never finished, and they lived by constant begging for men, oxen, carts, tools, creepers, bamboo, grass and clay, so that the people were oppressed (Vin-i 1460:2-6).

The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī and the Samantapāsādikā comment on the word *saññācikā*, a request initiated by oneself (Kkh 1396:1; Sp-i 2262:1), and on the kinds of hut (Kkh 1396:6-8; Sp-i 2263). In the Suttanipāta, the herdsman Dhaniya also speaks of his hut: "My hut is thatched" (*channā kuṭi*, Sn 62:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, Saṅghādisesa 6)
> **“Saññācikāya pana bhikkhunā kuṭiṃ kārayamānena assāmikaṃ attuddesaṃ pamāṇikā kāretabbā . Tatridaṃ pamāṇaṃ – dīghaso dvādasa vidatthiyo , sugatavidatthiyā ; tiriyaṃ sattantarā . Bhikkhū abhinetabbā vatthudesanāya . Tehi bhikkhūhi vatthu desetabbaṃ – anārambhaṃ saparikkamanaṃ. Sārambhe ce bhikkhu vatthusmiṃ aparikkamane saññācikāya kuṭiṃ kāreyya, bhikkhū vā anabhineyya vatthudesanāya, pamāṇaṃ vā atikkāmeyya, saṅghādiseso” ti.**  
> 348. “When a monk, by means of begging, builds a hut without a sponsoring owner and intended for himself, it is to be no more than twelve standard handspans long, measured outside, by the Sugata span; and seven inside, measured inside. He must have monks approve a site where no harm will be done and which has space on all sides. If a monk builds a hut by means of begging, on a site where harm will be done and which has space on all sides, or he does not have monks approve the site, or he exceeds the right size, he commits an offense entailing suspension.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, the word-analysis)
> **Kuṭi nāma ullittā vā hoti vā avalittā vā ullittāvalittā vā.**  
> A hut means: plastered inside, or plastered outside, or plastered both inside and outside.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Ullittāti antolittā. Avalittāti bahilittā. Ullittāvalittāti antarabāhiralittāti vuttaṃ hoti.**  
> Plastered inside (ullittā) means plastered on the interior. Plastered outside (avalittā) means plastered on the exterior. Plastered inside and out (ullittāvalittā) means plastered on both the interior and the exterior.  
>

### Textual References
