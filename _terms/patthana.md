### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭṭhāna**: [nt.] The Book of Conditional Relations; The Great Treatise. Formed by *pa* + *ṭhāna*. The Aṭṭhakathā explains: "the syllable *pa* shows 'various kinds', the word *ṭhāna* shows 'condition'", so it is the treatise taught by way of the various kinds of conditions (*nānappakārapaccayaṭṭhena*, Paṭṭh-a 1911).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Paṭṭhāna* is the seventh, final, and by far the most massive book of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka. It is traditionally referred to as the *Mahāpakaraṇa* (The Great Treatise). 

While the *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* dissects the universe into ultimate realities, the *Paṭṭhāna* puts the universe back together by showing exactly how these realities condition one another. It sets out **24 Conditions** (*paccaya*), from root condition (*hetupaccaya*) to non-disappearance condition (*avigatapaccaya*), and then applies them in the *anuloma*, *paṭiloma*, *anulomapaccanīya* and *paccanīyānuloma* methods, each with its six sections (*tikapaṭṭhāna*, *dukapaṭṭhāna* and so on). The Atthasālinī counts "six Paṭṭhānas in the direct order, six in the reverse, six in the direct-negative and six in the negative-direct": this combination of twenty-four universal Paṭṭhānas is the Great Treatise (*mahāpakaraṇa*) (As 97).

The Aṭṭhakathā says the Buddha, in the fourth week, sat in the "jeweled house" (*ratanagharaṃ*), which means the place where he contemplated the seven treatises. Contemplating the Dhammasaṅgaṇī and the next five books, no rays came from his body; when he descended into the Great Treatise, his omniscient knowledge "found its opportunity" there, as the great fish Timirapiṅgala finds room only in the great ocean, and six-coloured rays came out (As 113-114).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Kenaṭṭhena paṭṭhānanti? Nānappakārapaccayaṭṭhena. ‘ Pa-kāro ’ hi nānappakāratthaṃ dīpeti, ṭhānasaddo paccayatthaṃ. Ṭhānāṭṭhānakusalatātiādīsu hi paccayo ṭhānanti vutto. Iti nānappakārānaṃ paccayānaṃ vasena desitattā imesu catuvīsatiyā paṭṭhānesu ekekaṃ paṭṭhānaṃ nāma. Imesaṃ pana paṭṭhānānaṃ samūhato sabbampetaṃ pakaraṇaṃ paṭṭhānanti veditabbaṃ.**  
> In what sense is it called the Paṭṭhāna? In the sense of various kinds of conditions. Indeed, the syllable 'Pa' declares the meaning of 'various kinds', and the word 'ṭhāna' declares the meaning of 'condition'. Indeed, in 'knowledge of possibility and impossibility' and so on, 'condition' is called 'ṭhāna'. Thus, because it was taught by way of various kinds of conditions, each one of these twenty-four Paṭṭhānas is called Paṭṭhāna. But by way of the collection of these Paṭṭhānas, this entire treatise should be understood as Paṭṭhāna.  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā (Paṭṭh-a 1911) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=343&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2VuYeG5reG5rWhlbmEgcGHhua3hua1oxIFuYW50aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Catutthe pana sattāhe pacchimuttarāya disāya ratanaghare nisīdi – ratanagharaṃ nāma neva sattaratanamayaṃ gehaṃ. Sattannaṃ pana pakaraṇānaṃ sammasitaṭṭhānaṃ ratanagharanti veditabbaṃ – tattha dhammasaṅgaṇiṃ sammasantassāpi sarīrato rasmiyo na nikkhantā. Vibhaṅgappakaraṇaṃ dhātukathaṃ puggalapaññattiṃ kathāvatthuppakaraṇaṃ yamakappakaraṇaṃ sammasantassāpi sarīrato rasmiyo na nikkhantā. Yadā pana mahāpakaraṇaṃ oruyha “hetupaccayo ārammaṇapaccayo … pe … avigatapaccayo” ti sammasanaṃ ārabhi, athassa catuvīsatisamantapaṭṭhānaṃ sammasantassa ekantato sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ mahāpakaraṇeyeva okāsaṃ labhi. Yathā hi timirapiṅgalamahāmaccho caturāsītiyojanasahassagambhīre mahāsamuddeyeva okāsaṃ labhati, evameva sabbaññutaññāṇaṃ ekantato mahāpakaraṇeyeva okāsaṃ labhi.**  
> In the fourth week, however, he sat in a jeweled house in the northwestern direction; the "jeweled house" was not actually a house made of the seven jewels. Rather, the place where he contemplated the seven treatises should be understood as the jeweled house. While he was contemplating the Dhammasaṅgaṇi there, rays still did not emit from his body. Even while he was contemplating the book of the Vibhaṅga, the Dhātukathā, the Designation of Individuals, the book of the Kathāvatthu, and the book of the Yamaka, rays still did not emit from his body. But when he descended into the Great Treatise and began his contemplation, "root condition, object condition... and so on... non-disappearance condition," his omniscient knowledge unfailingly found its opportunity in the Great Treatise itself as he contemplated the twenty-four universal Paṭṭhānas. Just as the great fish Timira-piṅgala finds his opportunity only in the great ocean that is eighty-four thousand leagues deep, so too did omniscient knowledge unfailingly find its opportunity only in the Great Treatise.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Nidānakathā, As 113) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=13&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dXR0aGUgcGFuYSBzYXR0xIFoZSBwYWNjaGltdXR0YXLEgXlhIGRpc8SBeWEgcmF0YW5hZ2hhcmU%3D)
