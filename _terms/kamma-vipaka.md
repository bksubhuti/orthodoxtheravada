### Grammatical Analysis
**Kammavipāka**: [m.] the result of action. A compound of *[kamma](/glossary/kamma/)* (volitional action, deed) + *vipāka* (ripening, result).

### Orthodox Definition
*[Kamma](/glossary/kamma/)* is the volitional cause; *vipāka* is its result. The Buddha lists *kammavipāka* among the four unconjecturables (*acinteyyāni*) that are not to be conjectured about, since one who conjectures about them would partake of madness and vexation (AN 4.77, A-iv 844:1-9).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines the resultant states (*vipākā*) as the resultants of wholesome and unwholesome states, in the sensuous, form, formless and supramundane spheres, namely the feeling, perception, formations and consciousness aggregates (Dhs 1759:2-3). Resultant states are classed as indeterminate (*abyākata*; Dhs 2186:2), that is, they are neither wholesome nor unwholesome.

The Atthasālinī explains by the simile of seed and fruit: only the formless (mental) phenomena, being with an object and similar to the kamma, are called *vipāka*, "like a fruit similar to a seed" (*bījasarikkhakaṃ phalaṃ viya*). Material phenomena may be kamma-born, but because they have no object they are not called *vipāka* (As 2199:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, Acinteyya Sutta)
> **Kammavipāko, bhikkhave, acinteyyo, na cintetabbo; yaṃ cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa.**  
> The result of kamma (kammavipāka), monks, is an unconjecturable that is not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about it would partake of madness and vexation.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā vipākā? Kusalākusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ vipākā kāmāvacarā, rūpāvacarā, arūpāvacarā, apariyāpannā; vedanākkhandho … pe … viññāṇakkhandho – ime dhammā vipākā.**  
> 991. What mental phenomena are resultant? Resultant states of wholesome and unwholesome mental phenomena – those of the sensuous plane, of the form sphere, of the formless sphere, and not included in the round of existences; the feeling aggregate, the perception aggregate, the formations aggregate, the consciousness aggregate – these mental phenomena are resultant.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Vipākattika, §991) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=206&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTkxLiBLYXRhbWUgZGhhbW3EgSB2aXDEgWvEgT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Tattha vipākattike tāva kiñcāpi arūpadhammā viya rūpadhammāpi kammasamuṭṭhānā atthi, anārammaṇattā pana te kammasarikkhakā na hontīti sārammaṇā arūpadhammāva kammasarikkhakattā vipākāti vuttā, bījasarikkhakaṃ phalaṃ viya. Sālibījasmiñhi vapite aṅkurapattādīsu nikkhantesupi sāliphalanti na vuccati. Yadā pana sālisīsaṃ pakkaṃ hoti pariṇataṃ, tadā bījasarikkhako sāli eva sāliphalanti vuccati.**  
> 991. In the triplet of karmic results (vipākattike), although material phenomena also have karma as their origin, just like formless phenomena, yet they are not similar to karma (kammasarikkhakā) because they are without an object (anārammaṇattā). Thus, only formless phenomena, being with an object and similar to karma, are called 'karmic results', like a fruit similar to a seed (bījasarikkhakaṃ phalaṃ). Indeed, even when shoots, leaves, and so on (aṅkurapattādīsu) emerge after rice seed (sālibījasmiṃ) is sown (vapite), they are not called 'paddy' (sāliphalaṃ). But when the ear of rice (sālisīsaṃ) is ripe (pakkaṃ) and mature, then only the rice that is similar to a seed (bījasarikkhako) is called 'paddy'.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, §991) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=382&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=OTkxLiBUYXR0aGEgdmlwxIFrYXR0aWtlIHTEgXZhIGtpw7FjxIFwaSBhcsWrcGFkaGFtbcSB)
