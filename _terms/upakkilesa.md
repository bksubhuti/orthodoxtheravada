### Grammatical Analysis
**Upakkilesa**: [m.] imperfection; impurity; that which defiles. Formed from the prefix *upa* + *[kilesa](/glossary/kilesa/)* (defilement). In the Vatthūpama Sutta the word is used in the phrase *cittassa upakkilesā*, "the imperfections that defile the mind".

### Orthodox Definition
In the Vatthūpama Sutta (MN 7) the Buddha compares a stained cloth with a defiled mind, then asks "Katame ca, bhikkhave, cittassa upakkilesā?" and gives **sixteen**: covetousness and unrighteous greed (*abhijjhāvisamalobha*), ill will (*[byāpāda](/glossary/byapada/)*), anger (*[kodha](/glossary/kodha/)*), resentment (*[upanāha](/glossary/upanaha/)*), contempt (*[makkha](/glossary/makkha/)*), insolence (*[paḷāsa](/glossary/palasa/)*), envy (*[issā](/glossary/issa/)*), avarice (*[macchariya](/glossary/macchariya/)*), deceit (*[māyā](/glossary/maya/)*), fraud (*[sāṭheyya](/glossary/satheyya/)*), obstinacy (*[thambha](/glossary/thambha/)*), rivalry (*sārambha*), conceit (*[māna](/glossary/mana/)*), over-conceit (*atimāna*), intoxication (*[mada](/glossary/mada/)*) and negligence (*[pamāda](/glossary/pamada/)*).

The Papañcasūdanī introduces the list as "the imperfections by which the mind becomes defiled" (*yehi upakkilesehi cittaṃ saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ hoti*), and then explains each item; for example it says covetousness is desire and lust for one's own possessions, and unrighteous greed is the same for another's possessions, or in a place where it is improper.

In the Upakkilesa Sutta (MN 128) the Buddha tells Anuruddha how, dwelling diligent, he perceived light (*obhāsa*) and visions of forms, and how he abandoned the imperfections of the mind (*cittassa upakkilesā*), starting with doubt (*vicikicchā*) and lack of attention (*amanasikāra*).

The same word is used of insight practice. The commentaries speak of the ten imperfections of insight (*dasa vipassanupakkilesā*): *obhāso ñāṇaṃ pīti passaddhi sukhaṃ adhimokkho paggaho upaṭṭhānaṃ upekkhā nikanti* (light, knowledge, rapture, tranquillity, happiness, resolution, exertion, assurance, equanimity, delight). The Visuddhimagga (Chapter XX) says they arise for a meditator who is practising correctly and has begun insight, not for one who has already penetrated the truths.

### Quote
> **“Katame ca, bhikkhave, cittassa upakkilesā? Abhijjhāvisamalobho cittassa upakkileso, byāpādo cittassa upakkileso, kodho cittassa upakkileso, upanāho cittassa upakkileso, makkho cittassa upakkileso, paḷāso cittassa upakkileso, issā cittassa upakkileso, macchariyaṃ cittassa upakkileso, māyā cittassa upakkileso, sāṭheyyaṃ cittassa upakkileso, thambho cittassa upakkileso, sārambho cittassa upakkileso, māno cittassa upakkileso, atimāno cittassa upakkileso, mado cittassa upakkileso, pamādo cittassa upakkileso.**  
> “What, monks, are the imperfections that defile the mind? Covetousness and unrighteous greed is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Ill will is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Anger is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Resentment is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Contempt is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Insolence is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Envy is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Avarice is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Deceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Fraud is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Obstinacy is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Rivalry is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Conceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Over-conceit is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Intoxication is an imperfection that defiles the mind. Negligence is an imperfection that defiles the mind.  
>

> **Evaṃ saṃkiliṭṭhe citte duggati pāṭikaṅkhā, asaṃkiliṭṭhe ca sugatīti vatvā idāni yehi upakkilesehi cittaṃ saṃkiliṭṭhaṃ hoti, te dassento katame ca , bhikkhave , cittassa upakkilesā? Abhijjhā visamalobhotiādimāha.**  
> 71. Having said, ‘When the mind is defiled, an unhappy destination is to be expected, and when it is undefiled, a happy destination is to be expected,’ now, to show by which imperfections the mind becomes defiled, he said, ‘And what, monks, are the imperfections that defile the mind?’ He said, ‘Covetousness and unrighteous greed,’ and so on.  
>

Sammāpaṭipannakassa pana yuttapayuttassa āraddhavipassakassa kulaputtassa uppajjantiyeva.**  
> 732. Then for him, for the one who has begun insight, the ten imperfections of insight (dasa vipassanupakkilesā) arise by way of this tender insight. For the imperfections of insight do not arise for a noble disciple who has attained penetration, nor for one who has gone astray, nor for a lazy person who has abandoned the meditation subject. But they certainly arise for whatever such clansman who is practicing correctly, who is dedicated and devoted, and who has begun insight.

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Vatthūpama Sutta* (MN 7) – the simile of the cloth and the sixteen imperfections of the mind (M-i 190–193).
* **Sutta**: *Upakkilesa Sutta* (MN 128, M-iii 675 ff.) – the Buddha's account to Anuruddha of the perception of light and visions and the imperfections of the mind he abandoned, beginning with doubt and lack of attention.
* **Commentary**: *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* on MN 7 (Ps-i 848–854) – explains the sixteen. *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* Ch. XX (Vism-ii 1609) – the ten imperfections of insight; the list is given in Nidd-a-ii 534.
