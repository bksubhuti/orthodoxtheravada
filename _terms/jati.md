### Grammatical Analysis
**Jāti**: [f.] birth; lineage; rebirth; production; species. From root *jan* (to be born, produced, generated). Doctrinally signifies the initial manifestation of an existential life cycle.

### Orthodox Definition
*Jāti* is the eleventh link in the chain of Dependent Origination (*[bhava](/glossary/bhava/)-paccayā jāti*). Within the First Noble Truth, it is isolated as a foundational, undeniable expression of *[dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)* (*jātipi dukkhā*).

The canonical definition (the Sutta formula of DN 22, repeated in the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) *Vibhaṅga*) clarifies that *jāti* is not merely the conventional event of exiting a womb or breaking an eggshell. Doctrinally, it is defined as the **absolute initial manifestation of the aggregates** (*khandhānaṃ pātubhāvo*) and the baseline acquisition of the sensory gateways (*āyatanānaṃ paṭilābho*) at the beginning of a new life-stream (which the commentaries place at the *paṭisandhi-citta* moment).

Whether taking place in a human womb, a heavenly palace, or a hellish plane of torture, jāti represents the critical structural initialization moment that guarantees the eventual arrival of decay, sickness, grief, and death (*[jarāmaraṇa](/glossary/jaramarana/)*).

### Quote
> **“Katamā ca, bhikkhave, jāti? Yā tesaṃ tesaṃ sattānaṃ tamhi tamhi sattanikāye jāti sañjāti okkanti abhinibbatti khandhānaṃ pātubhāvo āyatanānaṃ paṭilābho, ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, jāti.**  
> Now what, monks, is birth? For the various beings in the various classes of beings (there is) birth, being born, appearing, turning up; the manifestation of the constituents (of mind and body), the acquisition of the sense spheres: this, monks, is called birth.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, Dukkhasacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=243&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mzg4LuKAmOKAmCBLYXRhbcSBIGNhLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGrEgXRpPw%3D%3D)
