### Grammatical Analysis
**Garubhaṇḍa**: [nt.] heavy goods; the Saṅgha's heavy property. Formed by *garu* (heavy, weighty) + *bhaṇḍa* (goods, property). The Mūla calls them "the Saṅgha's heavy goods, heavy requisites" (*saṅghassa garubhaṇḍāni garuparikkhārāni*, Vin-i 540:1).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Vinaya the *garubhaṇḍa* are the Saṅgha's goods that belong to the five groups listed as "non-relinquishable" (*avissajjiya*) and "non-divisible" (*avebhaṅgiya*): "Bhikkhus, these five things are non-relinquishable; they are not to be relinquished by the Saṅgha, by a group, or by an individual. Even if they are relinquished, they remain unrelinquished. Whoever relinquishes them commits a *thullaccaya*" (Vin-iv 1669:1-4, Cv 6, Avissajjiyavatthu). The five are: a park and park site; a monastery and monastery site; a couch, bench, mattress, pillow; metal pot, jar, bucket, cauldron, razor, hatchet, axe, spade, chisel; and creeper, bamboo, reed, grass, clay, wooden goods and earthenware goods (Vin-iv 1669:5, 1670:1, 1672:1; Vin-i 540:1). The same five are "non-divisible" (*avebhaṅgiya*: "even if they are divided, they remain undivided", Vin-iv 1676:1-2). In the Catutthapārājika (on false claims of superhuman states) the "bad monk" who wins over householders with these goods of the Saṅgha is called "the fourth notorious great thief" (Vin-i 540:1-2).

The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī explains that "the five heavy goods are stated by way of groups": for these five groups belonging to the Saṅgha it is not proper for the Saṅgha, a group or individuals to relinquish or divide them, and "even if relinquished and divided they remain the Saṅgha's"; but it is proper to exchange an immovable for an immovable, or the other with a suitable item, after considering the benefit to the Saṅgha, and to relinquish the inferior to protect the superior dwellings and use them (Kkh 1941:2-4). In the fourth group the metal pot, jar and bowl are heavy goods even if they hold no more than a *pasata* measure of water; the metal bucket that holds more than a *pāda* (five Māgadha *nāḷi*) is heavy (Kkh 1941:5-6).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka (Cūḷavagga))
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, avissajjiyāni, na vissajjetabbāni, saṅghena vā gaṇena vā puggalena vā. Vissajjitānipi avissajjitāni honti. Yo vissajjeyya, āpatti thullaccayassa. Katamāni pañca? Ārāmo, ārāmavatthu – idaṃ paṭhamaṃ avissajjiyaṃ, na vissajjetabbaṃ, saṅghena vā gaṇena vā puggalena vā. “Lohakumbhī, lohabhāṇakaṃ, lohavārako, lohakaṭāhaṃ, vāsi, parasu, kuṭhārī, kudālo, nikhādanaṃ – idaṃ catutthaṃ avissajjiyaṃ, na vissajjetabbaṃ, saṅghena vā gaṇena vā puggalena vā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, these five things are non-relinquishable; they are not to be relinquished by the Sangha, by a group, or by an individual. Even if they are relinquished, they remain unrelinquished. Whoever should relinquish them, commits a serious offense. What are the five? A park, a park site—this is the first non-relinquishable item; it is not to be relinquished by the Sangha, by a group, or by an individual. A metal pot, a metal jar, a metal bucket, a metal cauldron, a razor, a hatchet, an axe, a spade, a chisel—this is the fourth non-relinquishable item; it is not to be relinquished by the Sangha, by a group, or by an individual.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Cūḷavagga, Senāsanakkhandhaka, Avissajjiyavatthu, Vin-iv 1667-1672) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_04&page_number=321&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFBhw7FjaW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGF2aXNzYWpqaXnEgW5pLCBuYSB2aXNzYWpqZXRhYmLEgW5p)


### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka (Pārājika 4))
> **“Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, idhekacco pāpabhikkhu yāni tāni saṅghassa garubhaṇḍāni garuparikkhārāni, seyyathidaṃ – ārāmo ārāmavatthu vihāro vihāravatthu mañco pīṭhaṃ bhisi bimbohanaṃ lohakumbhī lohabhāṇakaṃ lohavārako lohakaṭāhaṃ vāsī parasu kuṭhārī kudālo nikhādanaṃ valli veḷu muñjaṃ pabbajaṃ tiṇaṃ mattikā dārubhaṇḍaṃ mattikābhaṇḍaṃ, tehi gihīṃ saṅgaṇhāti upalāpeti.**  
> “Furthermore, monks, a certain bad monk, using the Sangha’s valuable goods and requisites—namely, a garden, the land of a garden, a dwelling, the site of a dwelling, a couch, a bench, a mattress, a pillow, a metal pot, a metal jar, a metal bucket, a metal bowl, a knife, an axe, a hatchet, a spade, a chisel, a creeper, bamboo, a reed, grass, clay, wooden goods, earthenware goods—wins over and deceives householders with them.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Pārājikapāḷi, Catutthapārājika, Vin-i 532-540) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_01&page_number=115&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFB1bmEgY2FwYXJh4bmDLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGlkaGVrYWNjbyBwxIFwYWJoaWtraHU%3D)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Rāsivasena hi pañca garubhaṇḍāni vuttāni, tattha ārāmo ārāmavatthūti ekaṃ, vihāro vihāravatthūti dutiyaṃ, mañco pīṭhaṃ bhisi bimbohananti tatiyaṃ, lohakumbhī lohabhāṇakaṃ lohavārako lohakaṭāhaṃ vāsi parasu kuṭhārī kudālo nikhādananti catutthaṃ, valli veḷu muñjaṃ pabbajaṃ tiṇaṃ mattikā dārubhaṇḍaṃ mattikābhaṇḍanti pañcamaṃ. Etāni hi pañca saṅghasantakāni neva saṅghassa, na gaṇapuggalānaṃ vissajjetuṃ vā vibhajituṃ vā vaṭṭanti, vissajjitavibhattānipi saṅghikāneva honti. Thāvarena pana thāvaraṃ, itarena ca akappiyena mahagghakappiyena vā itaraṃ saṅghassa upakāraṃ sallakkhetvā kappiyaparivattanena parivattetuṃ vaṭṭati, varasenāsanādīnaṃ saṃrakkhaṇatthaṃ lāmakāni vissajjetuṃ vissajjetvā paribhuñjituñca vaṭṭati. Ettha ca purimesu tīsu rāsīsu agarubhaṇḍaṃ nāma kiñci natthi, catutthe lohakumbhī arañjarasaṇṭhānaṃ lohabhāṇakaṃ lohakaṭāhanti imāni tīṇi antamaso pasatamattaudakaggaṇhanakānipi garubhaṇḍāni.**  
> Indeed, the five valuable items are mentioned by way of a group: therein, a garden and a garden-site is one; a monastery and a monastery-site is the second; a couch, a bench, a mattress, and a bolster is the third; a metal pot, a metal jar, a metal bucket, a metal bowl, an adze, an axe, a hatchet, a spade, and a chisel is the fourth; a creeper, bamboo, muñja-grass, reed, ordinary grass, clay, wooden goods, and clay goods is the fifth. For these five indeed, belonging to the Saṅgha, are not proper to be given or distributed to the Saṅgha or to groups of individuals, even if they are given and distributed, they remain belonging to the Saṅgha. However, it is proper to exchange a valuable item for a valuable item, or for another unsuitable item or a valuable suitable item, by way of a proper exchange, after noting the benefit to the Saṅgha; it is proper to discard inferior for the purpose of protecting high-quality dwellings and so on, and to use after discarding them. And here, among the previous three groups, there is nothing that is not a valuable item. In the fourth group, a metal pot, a metal jar, and a metal bowl—these three, even if they can hold no more than a pasata measure of water, are valuable items.  
>  
> — Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī (Kkh 1941, on the Dubbalasikkhāpada, Pācittiya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=260&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=UsSBc2l2YXNlbmEgaGkgcGHDsWNhIGdhcnViaGHhuYfhuI3EgW5pIHZ1dHTEgW5pLCB0YXR0aGE%3D)
