### Grammatical Analysis
**Vitakka**: [m.] applied thought; initial application; thought. The Aṭṭhakathā explains: *vitakkanaṃ vitakko, ūhananti vuttaṃ hoti*, "applied thinking is *vitakka*; hitting upon (*ūhana*) is what is meant" (Vism-i 936:2, IV 71). The Ṭīkā adds *ārammaṇaṃ vitakketi sampayuttadhamme abhiniropetīti vitakko*, "it thinks on the object, it directs the associated states onto it" (Abhidh-s-t 970:3).

### Orthodox Definition
The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it with a string of synonyms: *takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā sammāsaṅkappo* (Dhs §7, Dhs 397:2). In the Dvedhāvitakka Sutta (MN 19) the Bodhisatta sorts his thoughts (*vitakke*) into two classes: sensual, ill-will and harmful thought, and renunciation, non-ill-will and non-harmful thought (M-i 690:1-4).

The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha counts *vitakka* among the six *pakiṇṇaka* (occasional) mental factors (Abhidh-s 85:1), and says it arises in fifty-five cittas: the sense-sphere cittas except the two sets of five-fold sense-consciousness, and the eleven cittas of the first jhāna (Abhidh-s 103:1). It is the first factor of the first jhāna (Abhidh-s-t 970:1-3).

The Visuddhimagga (IV, 71) gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: *ārammaṇe cittassa abhiniropanalakkhaṇo, āhananapariyāhananaraso, ārammaṇe cittassa ānayanapaccupaṭṭhāno* (it has the characteristic of directing the mind onto the object, the function of striking and thrashing, and the manifestation of leading the mind to the object; Vism-i 936:3-5). Its similes: *vitakka* is the first striking of the mind on the object, like the striking of a bell (*ghaṇḍābhighāto viya*), gross and leading, while *vicāra* is the lingering, subtle after-sound (Vism-i 938:1-2). *Vitakka* is the firm-holding hand and *vicāra* the rubbing hand on a dirty bronze vessel (Vism-i 939:1). The Ṭīkā compares it to a villager who enters the royal house by relying on a relative or friend favoured by the king: so the mind ascends to the object relying on *vitakka* (Abhidh-s-t 970:3).

The Aṭṭhakathā cites the Peṭaka: *vitakko thinamiddhassa* paṭipakkho, "applied thought is the opponent of sloth and torpor" (As 1415:9). Among the jhāna factors, it is abandoned in the second jhāna: *vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā … avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ … dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ* (A-ii 41:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Dvedhāvitakka Sutta)
> **“Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi – ‘ yaṃnūnāhaṃ dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā vitakke vihareyyan ’ ti. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, yo cāyaṃ kāmavitakko yo ca byāpādavitakko yo ca vihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ ekaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ; yo cāyaṃ nekkhammavitakko yo ca abyāpādavitakko yo ca avihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ dutiyaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ.**  
> “Monks, before my enlightenment, while I was still only an unenlightened Bodhisatta, this thought occurred to me: ‘What if I were to divide my thoughts into two classes?’ So, monks, I made these—sensual thought, ill-will thought, and harmful thought—one division. And I made these—renunciation thought, non-ill-will thought, and non-harmful thought—the second division.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye vitakko hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye takko vitakko saṅkappo appanā byappanā cetaso abhiniropanā sammāsaṅkappo – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vitakko hoti.**  
> 7. What is reflection at that time? Whatever thought (takka), reflection, intention, effecting of an aim, strong effecting of an aim, directing the mind onto, or right intention exists at that time—this is reflection at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§7, vitakka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Ny4gS2F0YW1vIHRhc21p4bmDIHNhbWF5ZSB2aXRha2tvIGhvdGk%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the definition)
> **Tattha vitakkanaṃ vitakko , ūhananti vuttaṃ hoti. Svāyaṃ ārammaṇe cittassa abhiniropanalakkhaṇo, āhananapariyāhananaraso. Tathā hi tena yogāvacaro ārammaṇaṃ vitakkāhataṃ vitakkapariyāhataṃ karotīti vuccati. Ārammaṇe cittassa ānayanapaccupaṭṭhāno.**  
> Herein, applied thinking is applied thought (vitakko); "hitting upon" is what is meant. It has the characteristic of directing the mind onto an object, and the function of striking and thrashing. Because of this, it is said that by means of it, the meditator keeps the object struck and thrashed by applied thought. It is manifested as leading the mind onto an object.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IV, 71) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=138&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHZpdGFra2FuYeG5gyB2aXRha2tvICwgxatoYW5hbnRpIHZ1dHRh4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the similes)
> **Santepi ca nesaṃ katthaci avippayoge oḷārikaṭṭhena pubbaṅgamaṭṭhena ca ghaṇḍābhighāto viya cetaso paṭhamābhinipāto vitakko. Sukhumaṭṭhena anumajjanasabhāvena ca ghaṇḍānuravo viya anuppabandho vicāro. Vipphāravā cettha vitakko paṭhamuppattikāle paripphandanabhūto cittassa ākāse uppatitukāmassa pakkhino pakkhavikkhepo viya padumābhimukhapāto viya ca gandhānubandhacetaso bhamarassa. Santavutti vicāro nātiparipphandanabhāvo cittassa ākāse uppatitassa pakkhino pakkhappasāraṇaṃ viya, paribbhamanaṃ viya ca padumābhimukhapatitassa bhamarassa padumassa uparibhāge. Dukanipātaṭṭhakathāyaṃ pana “ākāse gacchato mahāsakuṇassa ubhohi pakkhehi vātaṃ gahetvā pakkhe sannisīdāpetvā gamanaṃ viya ārammaṇe cetaso abhiniropanabhāvena pavatto vitakko. Vātaggahaṇatthaṃ pakkhe phandāpayamānassa gamanaṃ viya anumajjanabhāvena pavatto vicāro” ti vuttaṃ, taṃ anuppabandhena pavattiyaṃ yujjati. So pana nesaṃ viseso paṭhamadutiyajjhānesu pākaṭo hoti.**  
> And although they are inseparable in some states, applied thought is the first impact of the mind, like the striking of a bell, because of its grossness and because of its being a forerunner. Sustained thought is a continuous succession, like the ringing of a bell, because of its subtleness and its nature of continued pressure. And here, applied thought is energetic, acting as a vibration of the mind at the time of its first arising, like a bird’s flapping of its wings when about to soar into the sky, and like a bee’s diving towards a lotus when its mind is drawn to the scent. Sustained thought is of calm conduct, without much vibration of the mind, like a bird gliding with outspread wings after soaring into the sky, and like a bee buzzing above the lotus after diving towards it. But in the commentary to the Book of Twos it is said: "Applied thought occurs as a state of directing the mind onto an object, like the flight of a large bird flying in the sky, taking the wind with both wings and then keeping them still. Sustained thought occurs with the nature of continued pressure, like the flight of a bird flapping its wings to catch the wind." This explanation fits its occurrence as a continuous succession. But this distinction between them becomes obvious in the first and second absorptions.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IV, 71) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=138&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FudGVwaSBjYSBuZXNh4bmDIGthdHRoYWNpIGF2aXBwYXlvZ2Ugb%2BG4t8SBcmlrYeG5reG5rWhlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Tattha ārammaṇaṃ vitakketi sampayuttadhamme abhiniropetīti vitakko , so sahajātānaṃ ārammaṇābhiniropanalakkhaṇo, yathā hi koci gāmavāsī puriso rājavallabhaṃ sambandhinaṃ mittaṃ vā nissāya rājagehaṃ anupavisati, evaṃ vitakkaṃ nissāya cittaṃ ārammaṇaṃ ārohati.**  
> Therein, applied thought (vitakko) is that which reflects on the object and applies the conjoined states (abhiniropeti) to it; it has the characteristic of applying the co-arisen states to the object. For just as some man who is a villager might enter a royal palace relying on a favorite of the king, a relative, or a friend, so the mind reaches the object relying on applied thought.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (on the first jhāna factors) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=91&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIMSBcmFtbWHhuYdh4bmDIHZpdGFra2V0aSBzYW1wYXl1dHRhZGhhbW1lIGFiaGluaXJvcGV0xKt0aSB2aXRha2tv)
