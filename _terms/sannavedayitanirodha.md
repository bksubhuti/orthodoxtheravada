### Grammatical Analysis
**Saññāvedayitanirodha**: [m.] the cessation of perception and feeling. Formed by *[saññā](/glossary/sanna/)* (perception) + *vedayita* (what is felt, feeling) + *[nirodha](/glossary/nirodha/)* (cessation). The attainment is called *saññāvedayitanirodhasamāpatti*, or simply *nirodhasamāpatti*, "the attainment of cessation" (Vism-ii 2092: "kā nirodhasamāpatti").

### Orthodox Definition
*Saññāvedayitanirodha* is the ninth and last of the nine progressive cessations (*anupubbanirodhā*, AN 9.31). The Mūla lists them: for one who has attained the first jhāna the perception of sensuality has ceased; the second, applied and sustained thought; the third, rapture; the fourth, in-and-out breathing; the base of infinite space, the perception of form; the base of infinite consciousness, the perception of infinite space; the base of nothingness, the perception of infinite consciousness; the base of neither perception nor non-perception, the perception of nothingness; and for one who has attained the cessation of perception and feeling, "perception and feeling have ceased" (*saññā ca vedanā ca niruddhā honti*, A-ix 237). It is the stage that follows the base of neither perception nor non-perception in the sequence of abodes (for example D-iii 1629, M-i 896), and the Mūla adds that for the one who enters it "seeing with wisdom, the taints are destroyed" (M-i 986, MN 26).

The Mahāvedalla Sutta (MN 43) distinguishes the one who has entered it from a dead person: in both the bodily, verbal and mental formations (*kāyasaṅkhārā, vacīsaṅkhārā, cittasaṅkhārā*) have ceased and subsided, but in the dead person the life span is exhausted, the warmth is stilled and the faculties are broken, while in the bhikkhu in cessation the life span is not exhausted, the warmth has not subsided and the faculties are clear. The Cūḷavedalla Sutta (MN 44) adds that on entering it verbal formation ceases first, then bodily, then mental formation (M-i 1594), and that on emerging his mind inclines to seclusion.

The *Visuddhimagga* (XXIII, Nirodhasamāpattikathā) defines it in the Abhidhamma way as "the non-occurrence (*appavatti*) of consciousness and mental factors by way of successive cessation" (*anupubbanirodhavasena cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ appavatti*), the knowledge of which is described in the Paṭisambhidāmagga as the wisdom of mastery through two powers, the calming of three formations, sixteen conducts of knowledge and nine conducts of concentration. The three formations calmed are the verbal (applied and sustained thought, in the second jhāna), the bodily (in-and-out breaths, in the fourth jhāna) and the mental (perception and feeling, in the cessation). Only non-returners and arahants who have obtained the eight attainments can attain it; ordinary persons, stream-enterers, once-returners and dry-insight non-returners and arahants cannot (Vism-ii 2093). Emergence occurs "for a non-returner by the arising of the fruit of non-return, for an arahant by the arising of the fruit of Arahantship" (Vism-ii 2122), and the mind of one who has emerged inclines to Nibbāna (Vism-ii 2123). The Paṭṭhāna's statement that the wholesome mind of neither perception nor non-perception is the immediate condition for the fruition attainment of one emerging from cessation refers to the non-returner (Vism-ii 2101).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the nine progressive cessations)
> **“Navayime, bhikkhave, anupubbanirodhā. Katame nava? Paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ samāpannassa kāmasaññā niruddhā hoti; dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ samāpannassa vitakkavicārā niruddhā honti; tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ samāpannassa pīti niruddhā hoti; catutthaṃ jhānaṃ samāpannassa assāsapassāsā niruddhā honti; ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samāpannassa rūpasaññā niruddhā hoti; viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samāpannassa ākāsānañcāyatanasaññā niruddhā hoti; ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samāpannassa viññāṇañcāyatanasaññā niruddhā hoti; nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ samāpannassa ākiñcaññāyatanasaññā niruddhā hoti; saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpannassa saññā ca vedanā ca niruddhā honti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, nava anupubbanirodhā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these nine progressive cessations (anupubbanirodhā). What nine? For one who has attained the first absorption, the perception of sensuality has been stopped. For one who has attained the second absorption, directed thoughts and evaluations have been stopped. For one who has attained the third absorption, rapture has been stopped. For one who has attained the fourth absorption, in-and-out breaths have been stopped. For one who has attained the dimension of infinite space, the perception of forms has been stopped. For one who has attained the dimension of infinite consciousness, the perception of the dimension of infinite space has been stopped. For one who has attained the dimension of nothingness, the perception of the dimension of infinite consciousness has been stopped. For one who has attained the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception, the perception of the dimension of nothingness has been stopped. For one who has attained the cessation of perception and feeling, perception and feelings have been stopped. Indeed, monks, these are the nine progressive cessations.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 9.31, Anupubbanirodha Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_09&page_number=208&name=navakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzEu4oCY4oCYIE5hdmF5aW1lLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGFudXB1YmJhbmlyb2RoxIEu)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya, the dead and the one in cessation)
> **“Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato, yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno – imesaṃ kiṃ nānākaraṇan” ti? “Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato tassa kāyasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, vacīsaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, cittasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, āyu parikkhīṇo, usmā vūpasantā, indriyāni paribhinnāni. Yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno tassapi kāyasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, vacīsaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, cittasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, āyu na parikkhīṇo, usmā avūpasantā, indriyāni vippasannāni. Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato, yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno – idaṃ nesaṃ nānākaraṇan” ti.**  
> “What is the difference, friend, between one who is dead and has passed away, and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling?” “Friend, in one who is dead and has passed away, the physical processes have ceased and subsided, the verbal processes have ceased and subsided, the mental processes have ceased and subsided, vitality is exhausted, warmth is stilled, and the faculties have disintegrated.” “But in a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling, the physical processes have ceased and subsided, the verbal processes have ceased and subsided, the mental processes have ceased and subsided, but vitality is not exhausted, warmth is not stilled, and the faculties are very clear.” “This, friend, is the difference between one who is dead and has passed away, and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling.”  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 43, Mahāvedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=370&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFl2xIF5YeG5gywgxIF2dXNvLCBtYXRvIGvEgWxh4bmFa2F0bywgeW8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya, the order of cessation)
> **“Saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpajjantassa panāyye, bhikkhuno katame dhammā paṭhamaṃ nirujjhanti – yadi vā kāyasaṅkhāro, yadi vā vacīsaṅkhāro, yadi vā cittasaṅkhāro” ti? “Saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpajjantassa kho, āvuso visākha, bhikkhuno paṭhamaṃ nirujjhati vacīsaṅkhāro, tato kāyasaṅkhāro, tato cittasaṅkhāro” ti.**  
> “But for a bhikkhu who is attaining the cessation of perception and feeling, Noble Lady, which qualities cease first: bodily volitional formation, verbal volitional formation, or mental volitional formation?” “For a bhikkhu who is attaining the cessation of perception and feeling, friend Visākha, first verbal volitional formation ceases, then bodily volitional formation, then mental volitional formation.”  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 44, Cūḷavedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=376&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNhw7HDscSBdmVkYXlpdGFuaXJvZGhh4bmDIHNhbcSBcGFqamFudGFzc2EgcGFuxIF5eWUsIGJoaWtraHVubyBrYXRhbWU%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, what it is and who attains)
> **Tattha kā nirodhasamāpattīti yā anupubbanirodhavasena cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ appavatti. Ke taṃ samāpajjanti , ke na samāpajjantīti sabbepi puthujjanā, sotāpannā, sakadāgāmino, sukkhavipassakā ca anāgāmino, arahanto na samāpajjanti. Aṭṭhasamāpattilābhino pana anāgāmino, khīṇāsavā ca samāpajjanti.**  
> 868. In that context, what is the attainment of cessation? It is the non-occurrence (appavatti) of mental states (consciousness and mental factors) by way of successive cessation. Who attain it, who do not attain it? All ordinary people, stream-enterers, once-returners, and non-returners and Arahants who are dry-insight practitioners do not attain it. But non-returners and Arahants who have obtained the eight attainments do attain it.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXIII, Nirodhasamāpattikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=344&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODY4LiBUYXR0aGEga8SBIG5pcm9kaGFzYW3EgXBhdHTEq3RpIHnEgSBhbnVwdWJiYW5pcm9kaGF2YXNlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the three formations)
> **“Tayo ca saṅkhārānaṃ paṭippassaddhiyāti katamesaṃ tiṇṇannaṃ saṅkhārānaṃ paṭippassaddhiyā? Dutiyajjhānaṃ samāpannassa vitakkavicārā vacīsaṅkhārā paṭippassaddhā honti. Catutthaṃ jhānaṃ samāpannassa assāsapassāsā kāyasaṅkhārā paṭippassaddhā honti. Saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpannassa saññā ca vedanā ca cittasaṅkhārā paṭippassaddhā honti.**  
> “And by the calming of the three formations: by the calming of which three formations? For one who has attained the second jhāna, the verbal formations (vacīsaṅkhārā)—the applied and sustained thought—are calmed. For one who has attained the fourth jhāna, the bodily formations (kāyasaṅkhārā)—the in-and-out breaths—are calmed. For one who has attained the cessation of perception and feeling, the mental formations (cittasaṅkhārā)—perception and feeling—are calmed.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXIII, Nirodhasamāpattikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=345&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFRheW8gY2Egc2HhuYVraMSBcsSBbmHhuYMgcGHhua1pcHBhc3NhZGRoaXnEgXRpIGthdGFtZXNh4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, emergence)
> **Kathaṃ vuṭṭhānanti anāgāmissa anāgāmiphaluppattiyā, arahato arahattaphaluppattiyāti evaṃ dvedhā vuṭṭhānaṃ hoti.**  
> 881. How is the emergence? (kathaṃ vuṭṭhānaṃ): Emergence occurs in two ways: for a non-returner by the arising of the fruit of non-return, and for an Arahant by the arising of the fruit of Arahantship.  
>
