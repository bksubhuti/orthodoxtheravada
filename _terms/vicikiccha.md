### Grammatical Analysis
**Vicikicchā**: [f.] doubt; uncertainty; perplexity. The Aṭṭhakathā give two derivations: "vigatā cikicchāti vicikicchā", doubt is so called because the remedy (*cikicchā*, cure) has departed; or "sabhāvaṃ vā vicinanto etāya kicchati kilamatīti vicikicchā", because while investigating (*vicinanto*) the true nature of a thing one is fatigued and weary (*kicchati kilamati*) by it (As 1822:5-6; Vism-ii 478:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Vicikicchā* is the fifth of the five hindrances (*[nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*): "kāmacchanda, byāpāda, thinamiddha, uddhaccakukkucca, vicikicchā" (AN 9.64, A-ix 444:3), and one of the three fetters (*saṃyojana*) sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā and sīlabbataparāmāsa (Dhs 1774:2, §1006), which the Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists among the states "to be abandoned by seeing" (*dassanena pahātabbā*; TPR translates "eradicated by the First Path"). The Abhidhamma lists it as an unwholesome mental factor that occurs only in the consciousness rooted in delusion, accompanied by equanimity and associated with doubt (Dhs 929:2, 934:2).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it by eight objects of doubt: "He doubts about the Teacher, about the Dhamma, about the Saṅgha, about the training (*sikkhā*), about the past (*pubbanta*), about the future (*aparanta*), about both past and future, and about specific conditionality and dependently arisen things (*idappaccayatā paṭiccasamuppannesu dhammesu*)", and then by its synonyms: "such doubt, perplexity, uncertainty, a fork in the road, wavering, vacillation, inability to fathom, stiffness of mind, mental friction (*manovilekho*)" (Dhs 1776:2-3, §1008). The Aṭṭhakathā give its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: "characteristic, uncertainty (*saṃsayalakkhaṇā*); function, wavering (*kampanarasā*); manifestation, indecision (*anicchayapaccupaṭṭhānā*) or grasping at many sides (*anekaṃsagāhapaccupaṭṭhānā*); proximate cause, unwise attention (*ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānā*); it should be seen as an obstacle to the practice (*paṭipattiantarāyakarā*)" (As 1822:7-8; Vism XIV §490).

Its opponent among the jhāna factors is sustained thought: "vicāro vicikicchāya", sustained thought is the opponent of doubt, as the Peṭaka says (Vism-i 934:3; As 1415:9).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, the five hindrances)
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, nīvaraṇāni. Katamāni pañca? Kāmacchandanīvaraṇaṃ, byāpādanīvaraṇaṃ, thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ, uddhaccakukkuccanīvaraṇaṃ, vicikicchānīvaraṇaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañca nīvaraṇāni.**  
> “Monks, there are these five hindrances. What are the five? Sensual desire as a hindrance, ill will as a hindrance, sloth and drowsiness as a hindrance, restlessness and anxiety as a hindrance, and uncertainty as a hindrance—these indeed, monks, are the five hindrances.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, the definition)
> **Tattha katamā vicikicchā? Satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati, dhamme kaṅkhati vicikicchati, saṅghe kaṅkhati vicikicchati, sikkhāya kaṅkhati vicikicchati, pubbante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, aparante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, pubbantāparante kaṅkhati vicikicchati, idappaccayatā paṭiccasamuppannesu dhammesu kaṅkhati vicikicchati. Yā evarūpā kaṅkhā kaṅkhāyanā kaṅkhāyitattaṃ vimati vicikicchā dveḷhakaṃ dvedhāpatho saṃsayo anekaṃsaggāho āsappanā parisappanā apariyogāhanā thambhitattaṃ cittassa manovilekho – ayaṃ vuccati vicikicchā.**  
> 1008. Which is uncertainty (vicikicchā) there? He is perplexed about the Teacher, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the Dhamma, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the Saṅgha, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the training, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the past, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the future, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the past and future, he is perplexed; he is perplexed about the conditionality of this and dependently arisen phenomena, he is perplexed. Whatever such doubt, perplexity, the state of being perplexed, uncertainty, uncertainty, uncertainty, a fork in the road, doubt, not taking a single stance, wavering, vacillation, inability to fathom, stiffness of mind, mental friction — this is called uncertainty (vicikicchā).  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§1008) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=208&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTAwOC4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtxIEgdmljaWtpY2NoxIE%2F)

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, to be abandoned by seeing)
> **Katame dhammā dassanena pahātabbā? Tīṇi saṃyojanāni – sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso.**  
> 1006. What mental phenomena are to be eradicated by the First Path? These are the three fetters: identity view, uncertainty, and adherence to rites and rituals.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Tattha vigatā cikicchāti vicikicchā. Sabhāvaṃ vā vicinanto etāya kicchati kilamatīti vicikicchā. Sā saṃsayalakkhaṇā, kampanarasā, anicchayapaccupaṭṭhānā anekaṃsagāhapaccupaṭṭhānā vā, ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānā. Paṭipattiantarāyakarāti daṭṭhabbā.**  
> Therein (tattha), doubt (vicikicchā) is so called because the remedy has departed (vigatā). Or (vā), doubt (vicikicchā) is so called because while investigating (vicinanto) the true nature (sabhāvaṃ), one is fatigued and weary (kicchati kilamati) by it (etāya). That (sā) has its characteristic as hesitation (saṃsayalakkhaṇā), its function as wavering (kampanarasā), its manifestation as indecision (anicchayapaccupaṭṭhānā) or its manifestation as not taking a single stance (anekaṃsagāhapaccupaṭṭhānā), and its proximate cause as unskillful attention (ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānā). It should be understood as causing hindrance to practice (paṭipattiantarāyakarā).  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (on vicikicchāsampayutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=300&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHZpZ2F0xIEgY2lraWNjaMSBdGkgdmljaWtpY2NoxIEu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Vigatā cikicchāti vicikicchā. Sā saṃsayalakkhaṇā, kampanarasā, anicchayapaccupaṭṭhānā, anekaṃsagāhapaccupaṭṭhānā vā, vicikicchāyaṃ ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānā, paṭipattiantarāyakarāti daṭṭhabbā.**  
> It is doubt (vicikicchā) because the cure has vanished. It has the characteristic of uncertainty; the function of trembling; is manifested as indecision or as taking many sides; has unwise attention as its proximate cause; and should be seen as a hindrance to the practice.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XIV, §490) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=101&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VmlnYXTEgSBjaWtpY2NoxIF0aSB2aWNpa2ljY2jEgS4%3D)
