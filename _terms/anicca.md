### Grammatical Analysis
**Anicca**: [adj.] impermanent; inconstant; unstable; dynamic. Formed by the negative prefix *a* (not) + *nicca* (constant, continuous, permanent).

### Orthodox Definition
*Anicca* is the first of the three universal characteristics of existence (*[tilakkhana](/glossary/tilakkhana/)*). It refers to the fact that all conditioned phenomena (*[saṅkhāra](/glossary/sankhara/)*), whether material (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*) or mental (*nāma*), are fundamentally impermanent.

The Mūla states it in the Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta: "Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ niccaṃ vā aniccaṃ vā? – Aniccaṃ, bhante. Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vā taṃ sukhaṃ vā? – Dukkhaṃ, bhante" (S-iii 328:1-4), and in Dhammapada verse 277, "Sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā" (Dhp 899:1). The commentaries explain that things are considered *anicca* primarily in the sense of existing and then ceasing to be (*hutvā abhāvaṭṭhena*, Vibh-a 192:2; As 2126:4), and also for four other reasons (Vibh-a 192:3; Vism-ii 1526:5-7):
1. Because they have origination and dissolution (*uppādavayavantato*; Vism-ii has *uppādavayavattito*).
2. Because they are subject to constant change (*vipariṇāmato*).
3. Because they are temporary and bounded by time (*tāvakālikato*).
4. Because they are the antithesis of permanence (*niccapaṭikkhepato*).

Anicca is not merely a philosophical concept but the object of direct insight meditation (*[vipassanā](/glossary/vipassana/)*). Witnessing the rapid dissolution (*bhaṅga*) of mental and physical states breaks the illusion of continuity and stability, weakening grasping.
### Quote
> **“Taṃ kiṃ maññatha, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ niccaṃ vā aniccaṃ vā” ti? “Aniccaṃ, bhante”. “Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vā taṃ sukhaṃ vā” ti? “Dukkhaṃ, bhante”. “Yaṃ panāniccaṃ dukkhaṃ vipariṇāmadhammaṃ, kallaṃ nu taṃ samanupassituṃ – ‘ etaṃ mama, esohamasmi, eso me attā”’ ti? “No hetaṃ, bhante”.**  
> “What do you think, bhikkhus, is form permanent or impermanent?” “Impermanent, venerable sir.” “Is what is impermanent suffering or happiness?” “Suffering, venerable sir.” “Is what is impermanent, suffering, and subject to change fit to be regarded thus: ‘This is mine, this I am, this is my self’?” “No, venerable sir.”  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 22.59, Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta; heading "7. ### Quote
> **“Sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā” ti, yadā paññāya passati; Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā.**  
> “All volitional formations are impermanent,” when one sees with wisdom, Then one wearies of suffering; this is the path to purity.  
>  
> — [Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/) (v. 277, Maggavagga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_02&page_number=53&name=dhammapadap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mjc3LiDigJjigJggU2FiYmUgc2HhuYVraMSBcsSBIGFuaWNjxIEg4oCZ4oCZ)

### Quote
> **Idāni tesaṃ vipassitabbākāraṃ dassetuṃ cakkhuṃ aniccantiādi āraddhaṃ. Tattha cakkhu tāva hutvā abhāvaṭṭhena aniccanti veditabbaṃ. Aparehipi catūhi kāraṇehi aniccaṃ – uppādavayavantato, vipariṇāmato, tāvakālikato, niccapaṭikkhepatoti.**  
> Now, to show the manner in which they should be contemplated with insight, he began with “the eye is impermanent” and so on. Therein, first of all, the eye should be understood as impermanent (aniccan) by reason of not existing (abhāvaṭṭhena) after having been. It is also impermanent for four other reasons: because of having an arising and a vanishing (uppādavayavantato), because of being subject to change (vipariṇāmato), because it is temporary (tāvakālikato), and because of the rejection of permanence.  
>  
> — [Sammohavinodanī](/glossary/sammohavinodani/) (Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_02&page_number=45&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SWTEgW5pIHRlc2HhuYMgdmlwYXNzaXRhYmLEgWvEgXJh4bmDIGRhc3NldHXhuYMgY2Fra2h14bmDIGFuaWNjYW50acSBZGk%3D)

### Quote
> **Etthantare sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā. Kasmā? Uppādavayavattito, vipariṇāmato, tāvakālikato, niccapaṭikkhepato ca.**  
> All formations in that interval are impermanent. Why? Because they occur by way of arising and passing away, because of change, because they are temporary, and because they deny permanence.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. XX) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=253&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=RXR0aGFudGFyZSBzYWJiZSBzYeG5hWtoxIFyxIEgYW5pY2PEgS4%3D)
