### Grammatical Analysis
**Nevasaññānāsaññāyatana**: [nt.] the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. Formed by *neva* (neither) + *saññā* (perception) + *na* (nor) + *asaññā* (non-perception) + *āyatana* (base).

### Orthodox Definition
*Nevasaññānāsaññāyatana* is the fourth immaterial absorption (*[arūpajhāna](/glossary/arupajhana/)*) and the absolute limit of worldly, conditioned mental development. It was the attainment that Udaka Rāmaputta declared as his teaching ("Udako rāmaputto nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ pavedesi", M-i 922:4), which the Bodhisatta also reached and then left behind (M-i 922:11; 927:9-10).

In this state, the mind takes the previous state (the Base of Nothingness) as its object, regarding that very attainment as peaceful and cultivating an attainment in which only a residue of formations remains (Vibh 1790:1: *taṃyeva ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ santato manasi karoti saṅkhārāvasesasamāpattiṃ bhāveti*; Vism-i 2053:1). The Visuddhimagga says that not only perception but feeling, consciousness and contact are here "neither-nor" (Vism-i 2026:1). 

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* explains "neither perception nor non-perception" with the story of a novice who smeared a bowl with oil: asked, he says "there is oil in the bowl"; asked for oil to fill the tube, he says "there is no oil" (Vism-i 2027:1-5; also As 1606:7-8). Perception here is too weak to do the work of perception, yet a residue remains. Because the mind is operating at the absolute minimum threshold of conscious activity, it is a state of near-total suspension. Rebirth in this plane carries a lifespan of 84,000 aeons (*Nevasaññānāsaññāyatanūpagānaṃ devānaṃ caturāsītikappasahassāni*, Abhidh-s 432:1).

### Quote
> **“Nevasaññīnāsaññī” ti taṃyeva ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ santato manasi karoti saṅkhārāvasesasamāpattiṃ bhāveti. 620.“Nevasaññānāsaññāyatanan” ti nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ samāpannassa vā upapannassa vā diṭṭhadhammasukhavihārissa vā cittacetasikā dhammā.**  
> The term “neither percipient nor nonpercipient” means one tranquilly attends to that very dimension of nothingness and develops the attainment of the residue of formations. 620. Regarding “the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception”: it is the mind and mental factors of one who has attained the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception, or one who has been reborn in it, or one dwelling happily in this very life.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Ariyapariyesanā Sutta* (MN 26; CST heading "6. Pāsarāsisuttaṃ" M-i 899:1) – Udaka Rāmaputta declares this attainment (M-i 922:4); the Buddha reaches it (M-i 922:11), then judges that this Dhamma leads only to rebirth in the nevasaññānāsaññāyatana and not to nibbāna, and departs ("nāyaṃ dhammo nibbidāya … yāvadeva nevasaññānāsaññāyatanūpapattiyā", M-i 927:9-10).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Jhānavibhaṅga, Vibh 1790:1-1791:1 "Nevasaññānāsaññāyatanan ti …"; the jhāna formula at Vibh 1630:18).
* **Commentary**: *Visuddhimagga* (Chapter X, Āruppaniddesa, Vism-i 1980:1; §285-287 at Vism-i 2018-2027) – the analysis that feeling, consciousness and contact are also "neither-nor" here, not just perception.
