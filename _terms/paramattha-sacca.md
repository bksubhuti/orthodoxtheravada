### Grammatical Analysis
**Paramatthasacca**: [nt.] Ultimate Truth. The Ṭīkā explains *paramattha*: “paramo uttamo aviparīto attho, paramassa vā uttamassa ñāṇassa attho gocaroti paramattho”, the supreme, the highest, the unperverted meaning, or the meaning that is the range of the supreme, the highest knowledge (Abhidh-s-t 898:3). It is formed with *sacca* (truth).

### Orthodox Definition
The Mūla uses the expression in the Kathāvatthu, in the first debate: “Puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthenāti?”, is a person found in the sense of a real and ultimate fact? (Kv 7:1). The Aṭṭhakathā explains that this question is asked of the whole of *paramatthasacca*: material form and the rest, down to the faculty of the one who has known the final knowledge (*aññātāvindriya*), are not a person (Kv-a 610:8).

The Buddha's teaching is of two kinds, conventional (*sammutidesanā*) and ultimate (*paramatthadesanā*). “Puggalo satto itthī puriso khattiyo brāhmaṇo devo māro” is conventional teaching; “aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattā khandhā dhātū āyatanāni satipaṭṭhānā” is ultimate teaching, and the Buddha teaches each to those who can understand and attain through it (Mp-i 369:3-7; the same in Ps-i 730:1, It-a 375:2, Kv-a 710:1). The Dhammapada commentary distinguishes the kinds of truth in the phrase “saccānaṃ caturo padā”: truth of speech (*vacīsacca*), conventional truth (*sammutisacca*, for example “sacco brāhmaṇo”), the truth of views (*diṭṭhisacca*) and ultimate truth (*paramatthasacca*), “dukkhaṃ ariyasaccan” and so on (Dhp-a 3688:2).

The Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha says that the topics of the Abhidhamma are fourfold by way of ultimate reality (*catudhā paramatthato*): *cittaṃ, cetasikaṃ, rūpaṃ, nibbānaṃ* (Abhidh-s 7:1, 8:1). The Ṭīkā glosses them, leaving conventions aside: *citta* is the consciousness aggregate, *cetasika* the three aggregates beginning with feeling, *rūpa* the form aggregate with its great elements and derived matter, and *nibbāna* the unconditioned state that is the object of the paths and fruits (Abhidh-s-t 898:2).
1. **[Citta](/glossary/citta/)**: Consciousness.
2. **[Cetasika](/glossary/cetasika/)**: Mental factors.
3. **[Rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)**: Materiality.
4. **[Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)**: The unconditioned element.

### Quote (Mūla: Kathāvatthu)
> **Puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthenāti? Āmantā.**  
> Theravādin: Is the person known in the sense of a real and ultimate fact? Puggalavādin: Yes.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha)
> **Tattha vuttābhidhammatthā, catudhā paramatthato. Cittaṃ cetasikaṃ rūpaṃ, nibbānamiti sabbathā.**  
> 2. The topics of the Abhidhamma mentioned therein are, fourfold from the standpoint of ultimate reality (paramatthato). Consciousness (cittaṃ), mental factors (cetasikaṃ), materiality (rūpaṃ), and Nibbāna—in every way.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (I, Catuparamatthadhammo) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=1&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=Mi4gVGF0dGhhIHZ1dHTEgWJoaWRoYW1tYXR0aMSBLCBjYXR1ZGjEgSBwYXJhbWF0dGhhdG8u)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Tattha tasmiṃ abhidhamme sabbathā kusalādivasena, khandhādivasena ca vuttā abhidhammatthā paramatthato sammutiṃ ṭhapetvā nibbattitaparamatthavasena cittaṃ viññāṇakkhandho, cetasikaṃ vedanādikkhandhattayaṃ, rūpaṃ bhūtupādāyabhedabhinno rūpakkhandho, nibbānaṃ maggaphalānamārammaṇabhūto asaṅkhatadhammoti evaṃ catudhā catūhākārehi ṭhitāti yojanā. Tattha paramo uttamo aviparīto attho, paramassa vā uttamassa ñāṇassa attho gocaroti paramattho.**  
> Therein (tattha), in that higher teaching (abhidhamme), the Abhidhamma meanings stated (vuttā) in every way (sabbathā)—by way of wholesome and so on, and by way of aggregates and so on—should be considered as being situated in four ways (catudhā) from the perspective of ultimate reality (paramatthato), setting aside conventional truth: consciousness (cittaṃ), which is the aggregate of consciousness; mental factors (cetasikaṃ), which is the triad of aggregates starting with feeling; materiality, which is the form (rūpaṃ) aggregate divided into the primary elements and derived materiality; and Nibbāna (nibbānaṃ), the unconditioned phenomenon that is the object of the paths and fruitions. This is the construction of the sentence. Therein, a ultimate reality (paramattho) is a meaning that is highest, supreme, and unerring, or it is the meaning that is the object of the highest and supreme knowledge.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (on “catudhā paramatthato”) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=74&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHRhc21p4bmDIGFiaGlkaGFtbWUgc2FiYmF0aMSBIGt1c2FsxIFkaXZhc2VuYSwga2hhbmRoxIFkaXZhc2VuYQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Manorathapūraṇī)
> **Puggaloti sammutikathā, na paramatthakathā. Buddhassa hi bhagavato duvidhā desanā – sammutidesanā, paramatthadesanā cāti. Tattha “puggalo satto itthī puriso khattiyo brāhmaṇo devo māro” ti evarūpā sammutidesanā ,“aniccaṃ dukkhaṃ anattā khandhā dhātū āyatanāni satipaṭṭhānā” ti evarūpā paramatthadesanā.**  
> “Person” is a conventional expression, not an ultimate expression. The teaching of the Blessed Buddha is of two kinds: conventional teaching and ultimate teaching. In this context, expressions such as “person,” “being,” “woman,” “man,” “aristocrat,” “brahmin,” “deva,” or “Māra” are conventional teachings. Expressions such as “impermanent,” “suffering,” “not-self,” “aggregates,” “elements,” “sense-bases,” or “applications of mindfulness” are ultimate teachings.  
>  
> — [Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/) (on AN 1.170, sammutidesanā and paramatthadesanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_an_01&page_number=73&name=ekakanip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UHVnZ2Fsb3RpIHNhbW11dGlrYXRoxIEsIG5hIHBhcmFtYXR0aGFrYXRoxIEu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā)
> **Saccānaṃ caturo padāti “saccaṃ bhaṇe na kujjheyyā” ti (dha. pa.224) āgataṃ vacīsaccaṃ vā hotu,“sacco brāhmaṇo sacco khattiyo” tiādibhedaṃ sammutisaccaṃ vā “idameva saccaṃ moghamaññan” ti (dha. sa.1144; ma. ni.2.187-188) diṭṭhisaccaṃ vā “dukkhaṃ ariyasaccan” tiādibhedaṃ paramatthasaccaṃ vā hotu, sabbesampi imesaṃ saccānaṃ parijānitabbaṭṭhena sacchikātabbaṭṭhena pahātabbaṭṭhena bhāvetabbaṭṭhena ekapaṭivedhaṭṭhena ca tathapaṭivedhaṭṭhena ca dukkhaṃ ariyasaccantiādayo caturo padā seṭṭhā nāma.**  
> “The four principles the best of truths”: whether it be the truth of speech that has come down as “speak the truth, do not get angry,” or conventional truth differentiated as “a true brahmin, a true aristocrat,” or the truth of view saying, “This alone is true, anything else is wrong,” or the ultimate truth differentiated as “suffering is the Noble Truth,” these four principles are called the best because they must be fully understood, abandoned, realized, or developed, and because they are penetrated by a single path-knowledge and penetrated as they truly are.  
>  
> — Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā (on Dhp 273, sammutisacca, diṭṭhisacca and paramatthasacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_02_02&page_number=254&name=dhammapadaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=U2FjY8SBbmHhuYMgY2F0dXJvIHBhZMSBdGkg4oCY4oCYIHNhY2Nh4bmDIGJoYeG5h2U%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Ayañca anuyogo rūpaṃ puggalo … pe … aññātāvindriyaṃ puggaloti sakalaṃ paramatthasaccaṃ sandhāya āraddho.**  
> And this pursuit—‘is materiality the person?’ ... ‘is the faculty of one who has finally known the person?’—was commenced referring to all ultimate truths.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Kathāvatthu](/glossary/kathavatthu/)* (Kv 7:1, the first debate) – the expression “saccikaṭṭhaparamattha”.
* **Historical**: *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (Abhidh-s 7:1, 8:1) and its *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā* (Abh-s-t 898) – the four ultimate realities.
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-i 369:3-7), *Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā* (Dhp-a 3688:2) and *Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā* (Kv-a 610:8, 710:1) – the two kinds of teaching and the use of *paramatthasacca*.
