### Grammatical Analysis
**Sekhiya**: [adj.] Connected with training; a rule of training. Derived from *sikkhati* (to train, learn). The Pātimokkha calls them *sekhiyā dhammā* and ends each rule with *sikkhā karaṇīyā*, "this is a training to be done" (Dvem-bhk 352:1, 354:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Sekhiya* rules are the last of the rule-groups recited in the Pātimokkha before the seven *adhikaraṇasamathā*, numbering **seventy-five** (*pañcasattati*): the Mūla numbers the rules in the Bhikkhupātimokkha from 1 (*Parimaṇḍalaṃ nivāsessāmīti sikkhā karaṇīyā*, Dvem-bhk 354:1) to 75 (*Na udake agilāno uccāraṃ vā passāvaṃ vā kheḷaṃ vā karissāmīti*, Dvem-bhk 496:1), and the Bhikkhunīpātimokkha also reaches 75 (Dvem-bhni 1188:1). The Aṭṭhakathā states the number: *sekhiyā pana uddiṭṭhā, ye dhammā pañcasattati* (Sp-ii-b 1456:1).

They concern decorum: wearing the robes evenly (rule 1), going and sitting in the village, receiving and eating almsfood respectfully (rule 27: *Sakkaccaṃ piṇḍapātaṃ paṭiggahessāmīti*, Dvem-bhk 396:1), not teaching Dhamma to one holding an umbrella who is not sick (rule 57, Dvem-bhk 459:1; the group of Dhamma-teaching rules runs to rule 72, Dvem-bhk 490:1), and not defecating, urinating or spitting while standing, on greenery or in water when not sick (rules 73-75, Dvem-bhk 492:1, 494:1, 496:1). In the Pātimokkha the 75 are arranged in seven vaggas: Parimaṇḍala, Ujjagghika, Khambhakata, Sakkacca, Kabaḷa, Surusuru and Pāduka (Dvem-bhk 368:1, 384:1, 403:1, 424:1, 445:1, 466:1, 497:1).

The offence for breaking a Sekhiya rule out of disrespect (*anādariyaṃ paṭicca*) is a *dukkaṭa* (Vin-ii 1729:2; the rule text itself, Vin-ii 1729:1). Many of the rules contain the exception for one who is not sick (*agilāna*), so a sick person is not bound by them.

### Quote (Mūla: Pātimokkha)
> **Ime kho panāyasmanto sekhiyā dhammā uddesaṃ āgacchanti. Parimaṇḍalasikkhāpadaṃ 1. Parimaṇḍalaṃ nivāsessāmīti sikkhā karaṇīyā.**  
> Venerables, these rules for training come up for recitation. The training rule on evenly all around 1. ‘I will wear my sarong evenly all around,’ this is a training to be done.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Suttavibhaṅga)
> **Parimaṇḍalaṃ nivāsetabbaṃ nābhimaṇḍalaṃ jāṇumaṇḍalaṃ paṭicchādentena. Yo anādariyaṃ paṭicca purato vā pacchato vā olambento nivāseti, āpatti dukkaṭassa.**  
> One should wear the sarong evenly all around, covering the navel and the knees. Whoever, out of disrespect, wears his sarong hanging down in front or behind, commits an offense of wrong-doing.  
>
