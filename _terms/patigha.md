### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭigha**: [m./nt.] Repulsion; aversion; resentment. From *paṭi* + *han* (to strike): the Aṭṭhasālinī says "*paṭihaññanavasena paṭighaṃ*", "resentment is by way of striking against" (As 2277), and the Ṭīkā "*ārammaṇe paṭihaññatīti paṭigho, doso*", "it strikes against the object, so it is paṭigha, which is hate (*dosa*)" (Abh-s-t 921). The Paramatthajotikā glosses it "*paṭighātakaṃ, cittavighātakaṃ*", "destructive, a disturbance of the mind", and "a specific term for *dosa*" (Pj-ii 1623).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paṭigha* is hatred (*[dosa](/glossary/dosa/)*) under another name. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines the *paṭighasaṃyojana* (fetter of aversion) as the vexation of the mind (*cittassa āghāto*) that arises, for example, from the thought "he did me harm", together with *paṭighāto, paṭigho, paṭivirodho, kopo, doso, byāpatti, kodho* and the other terms of the list (Dhs 1120). It is the second of the ten fetters of the Abhidhamma list: *kāmarāga, paṭigha, māna, diṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāsa, bhavarāga, issā, macchariya, avijjā* (Dhs 1118). The Aṭṭhasālinī explains the words in order of strength: the mind's vexation (*āghāto*), then the stronger hostility (*paṭighāto*), then *paṭigha* "by way of striking against", then *paṭivirodho* "because it opposes" (As 2277).

The Paṭisambhidāmagga assigns its abandonment to the paths: the gross fetter and underlying tendency of *paṭigha* are cut off by the once-return path, and what remains of them by the non-return path (Paṭis 416-417). The Suttanipāta lists "removing greed, hatred and resentment (*lobhaṃ dosaṃ vineyya paṭighaṃ*)" as part of the one who wanders rightly in the world (Sn 373). The Visuddhimagga prescribes mettā, and the Kakacūpama and related exhortations, for overcoming it (Vism IX).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, the ten fetters)
> **Katame dhammā saṃyojanā? Dasa saṃyojanāni – kāmarāgasaṃyojanaṃ, paṭighasaṃyojanaṃ, mānasaṃyojanaṃ, diṭṭhisaṃyojanaṃ, vicikicchāsaṃyojanaṃ, sīlabbataparāmāsasaṃyojanaṃ, bhavarāgasaṃyojanaṃ, issāsaṃyojanaṃ, macchariyasaṃyojanaṃ, avijjāsaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1118. What states are fetters? There are ten fetters: the fetter of sensual passion, the fetter of aversion, the fetter of conceit, the fetter of views, the fetter of doubt, the fetter of adherence to rites and rituals, the fetter of desire for continued existence, the fetter of jealousy, the fetter of stinginess, and the fetter of ignorance.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Saṃyojanagocchaka, para. 1118) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=224&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTExOC4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, the fetter of aversion)
> **Tattha katamaṃ paṭighasaṃyojanaṃ? Yo evarūpo cittassa āghāto paṭighāto paṭighaṃ paṭivirodho kopo pakopo sampakopo doso padoso sampadoso cittassa byāpatti manopadoso kodho kujjhanā kujjhitattaṃ doso dussanā dussitattaṃ byāpatti byāpajjanā byāpajjitattaṃ virodho paṭivirodho caṇḍikkaṃ asuropo anattamanatā cittassa – idaṃ vuccati paṭighasaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1120. Which among these is the fetter of aversion? Whatever such vexation of the mind, hostility, resentment, recurrent hostility, anger, strong anger, intense anger, hate, corruption, intense corruption, impairment of the mind, mental corruption, anger, the act of anger, the state of being angry, hate, the act of corrupting, the state of being corrupted, vexation, the act of vexing, the state of being vexed, hostility, recurrent hostility, fierceness, surliness, and disgruntlement of the mind there is — this is called the fetter of aversion.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Saṃyojanagocchaka, para. 1120, paṭighasaṃyojana) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=224&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEyMC4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyBwYeG5rWlnaGFzYeG5g3lvamFuYeG5gz8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Suttanipāta)

> “Endowed with faith, learned, a seer of the fixed course, a wise one who does not take sides among partisans; having removed greed, hatred, and resentment, such a one rightly would wander in the world.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, its abandoning)
> **Sakadāgāmimaggena oḷārikaṃ kāmarāgasaññojanaṃ, paṭighasaññojanaṃ, oḷāriko kāmarāgānusayo, paṭighānusayo – attano cittassa upakkilesā sammā samucchinnā honti. Anāgāmimaggena anusahagataṃ kāmarāgasaññojanaṃ, paṭighasaññojanaṃ, anusahagato kāmarāgānusayo, paṭighānusayo – attano cittassa upakkilesā sammā samucchinnā honti.**  
> By the once-return path the gross fetter of craving in regard to sense pleasures, the gross fetter of resistance, the gross underlying tendency to craving in regard to sense pleasures, and the gross underlying tendency to resistance—imperfections of his own mind—are completely cut off. By the non-return path the secondary fetter of craving in regard to sense pleasures, the secondary fetter of resistance, the secondary underlying tendency to craving in regard to sense pleasures, and the secondary underlying tendency to resistance—imperfections of his own mind—are completely cut off.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Vimuttiñāṇaniddesa, para. 64) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=70&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=U2FrYWTEgWfEgW1pbWFnZ2VuYSBv4bi3xIFyaWth4bmDIGvEgW1hcsSBZ2FzYcOxw7FvamFuYeG5gywgcGHhua1pZ2hhc2HDscOxb2phbmHhuYMsIG%2FhuLfEgXJpa28ga8SBbWFyxIFnxIFudXNheW8s)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī)
> **Cittaṃ āghātento uppannoti cittassa āghāto. Tato balavataro paṭighāto. Paṭihaññanavasena paṭighaṃ. Paṭivirujjhatīti paṭivirodho.**  
> The mind’s vexation (cittassa āghāto) is so called because it arises vexing the mind. More powerful than that is hostility (paṭighāto). Resentment (paṭighaṃ) is by the meaning of striking against. Recurrent hostility (paṭivirodho) is so called because it opposes.  
>  
> — [Aṭṭhasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on Dhs 1066, the Dosa exposition) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=399&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2l0dGHhuYMgxIFnaMSBdGVudG8gdXBwYW5ub3RpIGNpdHRhc3NhIMSBZ2jEgXRvLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Suttanipāta-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Paṭighanti paṭighātakaṃ, cittavighātakanti vuttaṃ hoti. Dosavisesanamevetaṃ.**  
> “Resentment” means destructive; it is said to be a disturbance of the mind. This is a specific term for hatred.  
>  
> — Suttanipāta-aṭṭhakathā (Paramatthajotikā II, on the twelfth verse, Sammāparibbājanīya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_05_02&page_number=97&name=suttanip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=UGHhua1pZ2hhbnRpIHBh4bmtaWdoxIF0YWth4bmDLCBjaXR0YXZpZ2jEgXRha2FudGkgdnV0dGHhuYMgaG90aS4%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Ārammaṇe paṭihaññatīti paṭigho , doso. Caṇḍikkasabhāvatāya hesa ārammaṇaṃ paṭihananto viya pavattati.**  
> Because it strikes against the object, it is aversion (paṭigho), which is hate (doso). Indeed, because of its fierce nature, it operates as if striking against the object.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinīṭīkā (on the dosamūla cittas) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=80&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=xIByYW1tYeG5h2UgcGHhua1paGHDscOxYXTEq3RpIHBh4bmtaWdobyAsIGRvc28u)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IX, overcoming paṭigha)
> **Sace panassa verimhi cittamupasaṃharato tena katāparādhānussaraṇena paṭighamuppajjati, athānena purimapuggalesu yattha katthaci punappunaṃ mettaṃ samāpajjitvā vuṭṭhahitvā punappunaṃ taṃ puggalaṃ mettāyantena paṭighaṃ vinodetabbaṃ.**  
> If, however, when transferring the mind to an enemy, vexation arises due to recollecting the wrongs committed by that enemy, then one should repeatedly enter and emerge from [mettā](/glossary/metta/) towards any of the previous persons, and then by developing mettā towards that person again and again, one should eliminate the vexation.  
> 
> **Sace evampi vāyamato na nibbāti, atha –**  
> If, even with such effort, it does not subside, then –  
> 
> **Kakacūpamaovāda-ādīnaṃ anusārato;**  
> Following the Kakacūpama-ovāda and so forth,  
> 
> **Paṭighassa pahānāya, ghaṭitabbaṃ punappunaṃ.**  
> One should strive again and again for the abandonment of vexation.  
> 
> **Tañca kho iminā ākārena attānaṃ ovadanteneva ‘‘are kujjhanapurisa, nanu vuttaṃ bhagavatā –**  
> And this striving should be done by admonishing oneself in this manner: "Hey, angry person! Has it not been said by the Blessed One –  
> 
> **‘‘‘Ubhatodaṇḍakena cepi, bhikkhave, kakacena corā ocarakā aṅgamaṅgāni okanteyyuṃ, tatrāpi yo mano padoseyya.**  
> ‘Even if, bhikkhus, bandits and rogues were to saw off your limbs joint by joint with a two-handled saw, whoever among you should defile his mind, he is not one who carries out my teaching.’  
> 
> **Na me so tena sāsanakaro’ti (ma. ni. 1.232) ca,**  
> And,  
> 
> **‘Tasseva tena pāpiyo, yo kuddhaṃ paṭikujjhati;**  
> ‘He who retaliates against an angry person is worse off for it;  
> 
> **Kuddhamappaṭikujjhanto, saṅgāmaṃ jeti dujjayaṃ.**  
> He who does not retaliate against an angry person wins a battle hard to win.  
> 
> **‘‘‘Ubhinnamatthaṃ carati, attano ca parassa ca;**  
> ‘‘‘He acts for the good of both, his own and the other’s;  
> 
> **Paraṃ saṅkupitaṃ ñatvā, yo sato upasammatī’ti ca.(saṃ. ni. 1.188); –**  
> He who, knowing another to be enraged, remains mindful and calms himself.’  
> 
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (pa)
