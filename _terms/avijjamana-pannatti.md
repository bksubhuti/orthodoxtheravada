### Grammatical Analysis
**Avijjamāna-paññatti**: [f.] Concept of something that does not ultimately exist. Formed by *avijjamāna* (not existing, not found) + *paññatti* (concept, designation).

### Orthodox Definition
In the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)'s strict linguistic analysis, an *Avijjamāna-paññatti* is a term or concept that refers to a subject that has no corresponding ultimate reality (*paramattha-dhamma*). 

The commentary gives the examples of women and men (*itthipurisādika*, Pp-a 183:3); the *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha* gives "land, mountains and so on" (*bhūmipabbatādi*, Abhidh-s 746:2). When you analyze a "mountain" or a "person", you find no such thing in the ultimate sense, only the ultimate realities (*paramattha-dhamma*) that the word is laid upon.

This category is also what the title *Puggalapaññatti* itself is: the commentary says that in the term "puggalapaññatti" there is *avijjamānapaññatti* (Pp-a 183:17), whereas the "designations of aggregates ... faculties" are *vijjamānapaññatti* (Pp-a 183:16). The commentary adds that the full set of six concepts is taught "by the commentarial method, independent of the Pāli text" (*pāḷimuttakena aṭṭhakathānayena*, Pp-a 183:1).

### Quote
> **Pāḷimuttakena pana aṭṭhakathānayena aparāpi cha paññattiyo – vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena avijjamānapaññattīti. Tattha kusalākusalasseva saccikaṭṭhaparamatthavasena vijjamānassa sato sambhūtassa dhammassa paññāpanā vijjamānapaññatti nāma. Tathā avijjamānassa lokaniruttimattasiddhassa itthipurisādikassa paññāpanā avijjamānapaññatti nāma.**  
> But according to the teacher's method, independent of the Pāli (text), there are six other designations: concept of what is existent, concept of what is non-existent, concept of what is non-existent by means of what is existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is non-existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is existent, and concept of what is non-existent by means of what is non-existent. Among these, the exposition of a quality that is actually existent and arisen, by way of ultimate reality and true meaning, such as the wholesome and unwholesome, is called a concept of what is existent. Likewise, the exposition of what is non-existent, such as women and men, and so on, which is established merely by worldly expression, is called a concept of what is non-existent.  
>
