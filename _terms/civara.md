### Grammatical Analysis
**Cīvara**: [nt.] robe; robe-cloth. In the Vinaya's word-analysis: "A robe (*cīvaraṃ*) is one of the six kinds of robe-cloth, not smaller than what can be assigned" (*cīvaraṃ nāma channaṃ cīvarānaṃ aññataraṃ cīvaraṃ, vikappanupagaṃ pacchimaṃ*, Vin-i 1926:1). The six are linen, cotton, silk, wool, coarse hemp and canvas (*khomaṃ, kappāsikaṃ, koseyyaṃ, kambalaṃ, sāṇaṃ, bhaṅgaṃ*, Vin-iii 409:23). The Samantapāsādikā adds that by *cīvara* "one should understand only that which can be worn or wrapped around" (*yaṃ nivāsetuṃ vā pārupituṃ vā sakkā hoti*, Sp-ii 592:12).

### Orthodox Definition
*Cīvara* is the monastic robe, the first of the four requisites (*cīvara-piṇḍapāta-senāsana-gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra*, for example A-iii 986:7). The Buddha allowed three robes: "I allow you, monks, three robes: a double outer cloak (*saṅghāṭi*), a single upper robe (*uttarāsaṅga*), a single inner robe (*antaravāsaka*)" (Vin-iii 2139:39, the Ticīvarānujānanā). He first considered it after a cold night in which he put on a fourth robe and was not cold, and decided that even those who were susceptible to cold could keep going with three robes (Vin-iii 2139:34-38).

When the Blessed One saw the fields of Magadha laid out in strips, lines, embankments and squares, he asked Ānanda whether he could arrange robes like this for the monks; Ānanda did so, and the Buddha praised him and allowed an outer cloak, an upper robe and an inner robe that were cut up (*chinnaka*, Vin-iii 2135-2137). The ordination formula names the "going forth on account of rag-robes" (*paṃsukūlacīvaraṃ nissāya pabbajjā*) with the extra gains of the six kinds of cloth (Vin-iii 409:22-23). Dyeing is dealt with in the Cīvararajanakathā (Vin-iii 2119, from the case of robes dyed with dung and yellow clay, which turned a bad colour, Vin-iii 2120:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka (Suttavibhaṅga, Nissaggiya 1))
> **Cīvaraṃ nāma channaṃ cīvarānaṃ aññataraṃ cīvaraṃ, vikappanupagaṃ pacchimaṃ.**  
> A robe: is one of the six kinds of robe-cloth, but not smaller than what can be assigned.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka (Mahāvagga))
> **Tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, etadahosi – “yepi kho te kulaputtā imasmiṃ dhammavinaye sītālukā sītabhīrukā tepi sakkonti ticīvarena yāpetuṃ. Yaṃnūnāhaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ cīvare sīmaṃ bandheyyaṃ, mariyādaṃ ṭhapeyyaṃ, ticīvaraṃ anujāneyyan ’ ti. Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, ticīvaraṃ – diguṇaṃ saṅghāṭiṃ, ekacciyaṃ uttarāsaṅgaṃ, ekacciyaṃ antaravāsakan” ti.**  
> Then, monks, this thought occurred to me: ‘Even those who in this Dhamma and Discipline are sons of respectable families, susceptible to cold, afraid of cold, even these are able to keep themselves going with three robes. What if I were to set a limit, were to establish bounds as to robes for the monks, and were to allow three robes?’ I allow you, monks, three robes: a double outer cloak, a single upper robe, a single inner robe.”  
>  
> — [Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/) (Cīvarakkhandhaka, Ticīvarānujānanā, Vin-iii 2138) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=402&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2EgbWF5aGHhuYMsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgZXRhZGFob3NpIOKAkw%3D%3D)


### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka (Mahāvagga))
> **Atha kho bhagavā rājagahe yathābhirantaṃ viharitvā yena dakkhiṇāgiri tena cārikaṃ pakkāmi. Addasā kho bhagavā magadhakhettaṃ acchibaddhaṃ pāḷibaddhaṃ mariyādabaddhaṃ siṅghāṭakabaddhaṃ, disvāna āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – “passasi no tvaṃ, ānanda, magadhakhettaṃ acchibaddhaṃ pāḷibaddhaṃ mariyādabaddhaṃ siṅghāṭakabaddhan” ti? “Evaṃ, bhante” ti. “Ussahasi tvaṃ, ānanda, bhikkhūnaṃ evarūpāni cīvarāni saṃvidahitun” ti? “Ussahāmi, bhagavā” ti. Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, chinnakaṃ saṅghāṭiṃ chinnakaṃ uttarāsaṅgaṃ chinnakaṃ antaravāsakan” ti.**

Addasā kho bhagavā magadhakhettaṃ acchibaddhaṃ pāḷibaddhaṃ mariyādabaddhaṃ siṅghāṭakabaddhaṃ, disvāna āyasmantaṃ ānandaṃ āmantesi – “Do you see, Ānanda, the field of Magadha laid out in strips, laid out in lines, laid out in embankments, laid out in squares?” “Yes, Lord.” “Are you able, Ānanda, to provide robes like this for the bhikkhus?” “I am able, Lord.” “I allow you, bhikkhus, an outer cloak that is cut up, an upper robe that is cut up, an inner robe that is cut up.”

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Padabhājane pana byañjanaṃ anādiyitvā yaṃ laddhaṃ taṃ dassetuṃ “cīvaraṃ nāma channaṃ cīvarānan” tiādi vuttaṃ. Cīvaranti ettha yaṃ nivāsetuṃ vā pārupituṃ vā sakkā hoti, tadeva veditabbaṃ.**  
> However, in the word-analysis, without being concerned with the formulation, the phrase 'A robe is called...' and so on was stated to show what was obtained. Regarding 'a robe' (cīvaraṃ), one should understand it as only that which can be worn or wrapped.  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Sp-ii 592, the word-analysis of cīvara) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_02&page_number=131&name=p%C4%81cittiyaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UGFkYWJoxIFqYW5lIHBhbmEgYnlhw7FqYW5h4bmDIGFuxIFkaXlpdHbEgSB5YeG5gyBsYWRkaGHhuYM%3D)
