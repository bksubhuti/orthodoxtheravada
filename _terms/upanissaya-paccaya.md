### Grammatical Analysis
**Upanissayapaccaya**: [m.] Powerful dependence condition; decisive support condition. Formed by *upa* + *nissaya* (support) + *paccaya* (condition). The Visuddhimagga explains: "just as extreme desponding (*āyāso*) is despair (*upāyāso*), so extreme support (*bhuso nissayo*) is *upanissayo*; this is a designation for a powerful cause (*balavakāraṇa*)" (Vism XVII §602).

### Orthodox Definition
*Upanissaya-paccaya* is the ninth of the twenty-four conditions: hetu, ārammaṇa, adhipati, anantara, samanantara, sahajāta, aññamañña, nissaya, upanissaya, ... (Paṭṭh-i 8:1; Vism XVII §594). In the Paṭṭhāna it is first stated as "purimā purimā kusalā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ upanissayapaccayena paccayo", preceding wholesome states are related to subsequent wholesome states by powerful dependence (Paṭṭh-i 37:1). The Visuddhimagga's definition: "a phenomenon that is helpful as a powerful cause (*balavakāraṇabhāvena upakārako dhammo*) should be known as a powerful dependence condition" (Vism XVII §602).

It is of three kinds (Paṭṭh-i 1460:1; Vism-ii 929:1):
1. **Object powerful dependence** (*ārammaṇūpanissaya*): in the Paṭṭhāna, having given a gift, undertaken virtue or kept the Uposatha, one reflects on it, "making it weighty" (*garuṃ katvā paccavekkhati*); likewise on past good deeds, jhāna, and the path (Paṭṭh-i 1461:1). The Visuddhimagga: an object that consciousness takes while esteeming it is necessarily a powerful object; it is object dominance in the sense of merely being something to be esteemed, object powerful dependence in the sense of being a powerful cause (Vism-ii 929:4-5).
2. **Proximity powerful dependence** (*anantarūpanissaya*): preceding wholesome aggregates are related to subsequent ones (Paṭṭh-i 1462:1). The Visuddhimagga: it is the power of the preceding consciousness in producing the subsequent consciousness; there is no arising of consciousness without a preceding consciousness, therefore it is a powerful condition (Vism-ii 930:4-7).
3. **Natural powerful dependence** (*pakatūpanissaya*): "natural" means what has been produced in one's own continuum, such as faith and virtue, or what has been habitually used, such as climate and food, and it is unmixed with object and proximity (Vism-ii 931:2-3). In the Paṭṭhāna: relying on faith, one gives gifts, undertakes virtue, keeps the Uposatha, arouses jhāna, insight, the path, direct knowledge and attainments; and the same with virtue, learning, generosity and wisdom (Paṭṭh-i 1463:1-2). Climate (*utu*) is a natural powerful dependence for bodily pleasure and pain and for the attainment of fruition (Paṭṭh-i 1483:3).

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Paṭṭhāna, the three kinds)
> **Kusalo dhammo kusalassa dhammassa upanissayapaccayena paccayo – ārammaṇūpanissayo, anantarūpanissayo, pakatūpanissayo. Ārammaṇūpanissayo – dānaṃ datvā sīlaṃ samādiyitvā uposathakammaṃ katvā, taṃ garuṃ katvā paccavekkhati, pubbe suciṇṇāni garuṃ katvā paccavekkhati, jhānā vuṭṭhahitvā jhānaṃ garuṃ katvā paccavekkhati, sekkhā gotrabhuṃ garuṃ katvā paccavekkhanti, vodānaṃ garuṃ katvā paccavekkhanti, sekkhā maggā vuṭṭhahitvā maggaṃ garuṃ katvā paccavekkhanti. Anantarūpanissayo – purimā purimā kusalā khandhā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ khandhānaṃ upanissayapaccayena paccayo. Pakatūpanissayo – saddhaṃ upanissāya dānaṃ deti, sīlaṃ samādiyati, uposathakammaṃ karoti, jhānaṃ uppādeti, vipassanaṃ uppādeti, maggaṃ uppādeti, abhiññaṃ uppādeti, samāpattiṃ uppādeti.**  
> A wholesome state is related to a wholesome state by powerful dependence condition — as object powerful dependence, as proximity powerful dependence, and as natural powerful dependence. As object powerful dependence: having given a gift, having undertaken virtue, having performed the Uposatha observance, one reflects on it with respect; one reflects on previous good deeds with respect; having emerged from jhāna, one reflects on the jhāna with respect. Learners reflect on change-of-lineage with respect; they reflect on cleansing with respect. Learners, having emerged from a path, reflect on that path with respect. As proximity powerful dependence: preceding wholesome aggregates are related to subsequent wholesome aggregates by powerful dependence condition. As natural powerful dependence: based on faith, one gives a gift, undertakes virtue, performs the Uposatha observance, arouses jhāna, arouses insight, arouses a path, arouses direct knowledge, and arouses an attainment.  
>  
> — *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (§423, Upanissayapaccayo) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_07_01&page_number=144&name=pa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADh%C4%81nap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=NDIzLiBLdXNhbG8gZGhhbW1vIGt1c2FsYXNzYSBkaGFtbWFzc2EgdXBhbmlzc2F5YXBhY2NheWVuYQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the meaning of the word)
> **Upanissayapaccayoti ettha pana ayaṃ tāva vacanattho, tadadhīnavuttitāya attano phalena nissito na paṭikkhittoti nissayo. Yathā pana bhuso āyāso upāyāso, evaṃ bhuso nissayo upanissayo, balavakāraṇassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tasmā balavakāraṇabhāvena upakārako dhammo upanissayapaccayoti veditabbo. So ārammaṇūpanissayo anantarūpanissayo pakatūpanissayoti tividho hoti.**  
> 602. Regarding the powerful dependence condition (upanissayapaccayo), this is the meaning of the word: it is a support (nissayo) because it is relied upon by its own fruit for its existence, and it is not rejected. Just as extreme desponding is called despair (upāyāso), so extreme support is powerful dependence (upanissayo); this is a designation for a powerful cause. Therefore, a phenomenon that is helpful as a powerful cause should be known as a powerful dependence condition. It is of three kinds: object powerful dependence, powerful dependence through contiguity, and natural powerful dependence.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVII, §602) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=167&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NjAyLiBVcGFuaXNzYXlhcGFjY2F5b3RpIGV0dGhhIHBhbmEgYXlh4bmDIHTEgXZh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, natural decisive support)
> **Pakatūpanissayo pana pakato upanissayo pakatūpanissayo. Pakato nāma attano santāne nipphādito vā saddhāsīlādi upasevito vā utubhojanādi. Pakatiyā eva vā upanissayo pakatūpanissayo, ārammaṇānantarehi asammissoti attho.**  
> As for natural powerful dependence (pakatūpanissayo), a powerful dependence that is natural is a natural powerful dependence. “Naturally” (pakato) means either what has been produced in one’s own continuum, such as faith and virtue, or what has been habitually used, such as climate and food. Alternatively, a powerful dependence by nature is a natural powerful dependence, meaning it is unmixed with object and proximity.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XVII, §602) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=168&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=UGFrYXTFq3Bhbmlzc2F5byBwYW5hIHBha2F0byB1cGFuaXNzYXlvIHBha2F0xatwYW5pc3NheW8u)
