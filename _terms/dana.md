### Grammatical Analysis
**Dāna**: [nt.] giving; a gift; generosity. From the root *dā* (to give). The commentary derives it from the passive: "the volition of relinquishing one's own gift to others, by which a gift is given, is *dāna*" (*pariccāgacetanā dīyati etāyāti dānaṃ*, It-a 983:3).

### Orthodox Definition
*Dāna* is the first of the three bases of meritorious action (*tīṇi puññakiriyavatthūni – dānamayaṃ, sīlamayaṃ, bhāvanāmayaṃ*, D-iii 1380:1). The Aṭṭhakathā says that *dānamayaṃ* is "giving itself" (*dānameva dānamayaṃ*), and that it is the volition (*cetanā*) of relinquishing one's own gift to others, whether out of support for those who still have the root of existence, or out of veneration (*anuggahavasena pūjāvasena vā*) (It-a 983:3; also As 1378:4). The same volition is explained as occurring at three times: before, at the time of giving, and afterwards when one recollects the gift with a joyful mind, concerning the four requisites, the ten objects of giving such as food, or the six sense objects (It-a 983:4; Mp-t-viii 152:3).

In the Itivuttaka the Buddha says that if beings knew, as he knows, the result of giving and sharing (*dānasaṃvibhāgassa vipāko*), they would not eat without having given, and the stain of avarice would not overpower their minds and persist; even their last morsel they would not eat without sharing it, if there were someone to receive it (It 210:1-3). The Dhammapada says "the gift of Dhamma overwhelms all gifts" (*sabbadānaṃ dhammadānaṃ jināti*, Dhp 354, para 1144:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā also lists giving (*dānapāramī*) first of the ten perfections (Sv-ii 978:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Tīṇi puññakiriyavatthūni – dānamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu, sīlamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu, bhāvanāmayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu.**  
> “Three bases of meritorious activity: basis of meritorious activity consisting in giving, basis of meritorious activity consisting in virtue, basis of meritorious activity consisting in meditative development.

### Quote (Mūla: Itivuttaka)
> **“Evañce, bhikkhave, sattā jāneyyuṃ dānasaṃvibhāgassa vipākaṃ yathāhaṃ jānāmi, na adatvā bhuñjeyyuṃ, na ca nesaṃ maccheramalaṃ cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭheyya. Yopi nesaṃ assa carimo ālopo carimaṃ kabaḷaṃ, tatopi na asaṃvibhajitvā bhuñjeyyuṃ, sace nesaṃ paṭiggāhakā assu. Yasmā ca kho, bhikkhave, sattā na evaṃ jānanti dānasaṃvibhāgassa vipākaṃ yathāhaṃ jānāmi, tasmā adatvā bhuñjanti, maccheramalañca nesaṃ cittaṃ pariyādāya tiṭṭhatī” ti.**  
> “Bhikkhus, if beings knew, as I know, the result of giving and sharing, they would not eat without having given, nor would the stain of avarice overpower and persist in their minds. Even if it were their last morsel, their last mouthful, they would not eat without having shared it, if there were someone to share it with. But, bhikkhus, as beings do not know, as I know, the result of giving and sharing, they eat without having given, and the stain of avarice overpowers and persists in their minds.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paramatthadīpanī (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā))
> **Dutiyavaggassa paṭhame puññakiriyavatthūnīti pujjabhavaphalaṃ nibbattenti, attano santānaṃ punantīti vā puññāni, puññāni ca tāni hetupaccayehi kattabbato kiriyā cāti puññakiriyā. Tā eva ca tesaṃ tesaṃ ānisaṃsānaṃ vatthubhāvato puññakiriyavatthūni. Dānamayanti anupacchinnabhavamūlassa anuggahavasena pūjāvasena vā attano deyyadhammassa paresaṃ pariccāgacetanā dīyati etāyāti dānaṃ, dānameva dānamayaṃ. Cīvarādīsu hi catūsu paccayesu annādīsu vā dasasu dānavatthūsu rūpādīsu vā chasu ārammaṇesu taṃ taṃ dentassa tesaṃ uppādanato paṭṭhāya pubbabhāge pariccāgakāle pacchā somanassacittena anussaraṇe cāti tīsu kālesu vuttanayena pavattacetanā dānamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu nāma.**  
> 60. In the first of the second Vagga, puññakiriyavatthūni: they produce the fruit of a meritorious state, and because they purify one's own continuity, they are 'merits'; and because these merits are to be done through causes and conditions, they are also 'actions', thus they are 'meritorious actions'. These very same things, because of being the bases for those respective benefits, are 'bases of meritorious activity'. Dānamayaṃ: because of the support of the unbroken root of existence, or by way of veneration, the intention of abandoning one's own property for others—giving is done by this, thus it is 'giving', and the giving itself is 'consisting in giving'. For in the four requisites, or in the ten objects of giving like food, or in the six objects like visual forms, for one who gives those things, starting from the production of them, in the preceding phase, at the time of abandonment, and at the time of subsequent recollection with a joyous mind—according to the method spoken of in these three times, the existing volition is called the 'basis of meritorious activity consisting in giving'.  
>  
> — [Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/) (Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā, It-a 983) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=193&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NjAuIER1dGl5YXZhZ2dhc3NhIHBh4bmtaGFtZSBwdcOxw7Fha2lyaXlhdmF0dGjFq27Eq3RpIHB1amphYmhhdmFwaGFsYeG5gyBuaWJiYXR0ZW50aSw%3D)


### Quote (Ṭīkā: Aṭṭhakanipāta-ṭīkā)
> **Anucchinnabhavamūlassa anuggahavasena, pūjāvasena vā attano deyyadhammassa parassa pariccāgacetanā dīyati etenāti dānaṃ, dānameva dānamayaṃ. Padapūraṇamattaṃ maya-saddo. Cīvarādīsu catūsu paccayesu (dī. ni. aṭṭha.3.305), annādīsu vā dasasu dānavatthūsu, rūpādīsu vā chasu ārammaṇesu taṃ taṃ dentassa tesaṃ uppādanato paṭṭhāya pubbabhāge pariccāgakāle pacchā somanassacittena anussaraṇe cāti tīsu kālesu pavattacetanā dānamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu nāma.**  
> Giving is called dāna because it is given through a volition of relinquishing objects to others for the sake of supporting those whose roots of existence are not yet severed, or out of veneration; giving itself is consisting in giving (dānamayaṃ). The word ‘maya’ (consisting in) is merely a suffix to complete the word. The intention that proceeds in the three times—starting from the time of generating the act, during the preliminary stage at the time of relinquishing, and later when recollecting with a joyful mind—regarding the four requisites, or the ten items of giving, or the six sense objects, while giving to someone or other, is called the basis of meritorious activity consisting in giving (dānamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu).  
>  
> — Aṭṭhakanipāta-ṭīkā (Mp-t-viii 152, on dānamayaṃ puññakiriyavatthu) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_an_04&page_number=237&name=a%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhak%C4%81dinip%C4%81ta%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QW51Y2NoaW5uYWJoYXZhbcWrbGFzc2EgYW51Z2dhaGF2YXNlbmEsIHDFq2rEgXZhc2VuYSB2xIEgYXR0YW5vIGRleXlhZGhhbW1hc3Nh)
