### Grammatical Analysis
**Sukha**: [nt.; adj.] happiness; ease; bliss; pleasure; pleasant feeling. The Atthasālinī gives two derivations: *sukhayatīti sukhaṃ*, "it makes happy; the meaning is that it makes happy the one in whom it arises"; or, "it thoroughly devours and digs up (*suṭṭhu khādati, khanati*) bodily and mental affliction" (As 1184). The Sāratthadīpanī adds that the agent-sense is only figurative, and that in the strict sense it is the state-sense: *sukhanaṃ sukhaṃ*, "making happy is happiness" (Sp-t-i-a 1366).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sukha* operates in two main fields in the Theravāda analysis: as pleasant feeling (*sukhā vedanā*) and as a factor of meditative absorption (*jhānaṅga*).

As feeling, the Mūla says: "whatever bodily or mental pleasant, agreeable feeling there is, this is pleasant feeling" (*yaṃ kāyikaṃ vā cetasikaṃ vā sukhaṃ sātaṃ vedayitaṃ – ayaṃ sukhā vedanā*, M-i 1603, MN 44). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī describes it in a given consciousness as "mental ease (*cetasikaṃ sātaṃ*), mental pleasure (*cetasikaṃ sukhaṃ*), the pleasant, pleasurable feeling born of mind-contact" (Dhs 393); born of body-contact, the same formula reads "bodily ease, bodily pleasure, the pleasant, pleasurable feeling born of body-contact" (*kāyikaṃ sātaṃ kāyikaṃ sukhaṃ kāyasamphassajaṃ sātaṃ sukhaṃ vedayitaṃ*, Dhs 958). The Atthasālinī says that in this context it is a name for the feeling of gladness (*somanassavedanā*, As 1184).

As a jhāna factor, the Mūla describes it in the third jhāna: "with the fading away of rapture, the bhikkhu dwells in equanimity, mindful and clearly comprehending, and experiences happiness with the body" (*sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti*, D-i 334). The *Atthasālinī* defines it by characteristic, function and manifestation: pleasantness (*sātalakkhaṇaṃ*) is its characteristic, increasing the co-arisen states (*sampayuttānaṃ upabrūhanarasaṃ*) its function, and favouring (*anuggahaṇapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ*) its manifestation (As 1185). The same text distinguishes it from rapture (*pīti*): rapture is the delight in obtaining a desirable object, happiness is experiencing the taste of what has been obtained; where there is rapture there is happiness, but where there is happiness there is not necessarily rapture; rapture is included in the aggregate of formations, happiness in the aggregate of feeling; rapture is like seeing or hearing of water for one exhausted in a wilderness, happiness like entering the shade and drinking the water (As 1185). The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī, in the same way, gives its characteristic as experiencing a desirable object (*iṭṭhānubhavanalakkhaṇaṃ*), like a king who enjoys the taste of fine food (Abhidh-s-t 972).

The Mūla links it with concentration: "in one who is glad, rapture is born; in one who is rapturous, the body becomes tranquil; one whose body is tranquil feels pleasure; in one who feels pleasure, the mind becomes concentrated (*sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati*)" (A-iii 966).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya, pleasant feeling)
> **“Yaṃ kho, āvuso visākha, kāyikaṃ vā cetasikaṃ vā sukhaṃ sātaṃ vedayitaṃ – ayaṃ sukhā vedanā.**  
> “Whatever bodily or mental pleasant and agreeable feeling there is, friend Visākha—this is pleasant feeling.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, happiness and concentration)
> **Pamuditassa pīti jāyati, pītimanassa kāyo passambhati, passaddhakāyo sukhaṃ vediyati, sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyati.**  
> When one is glad, rapture is born; in one who is rapturous, the body becomes tranquil; one whose body is tranquil feels pleasure; and in one who feels pleasure, the mind becomes concentrated.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti? Yaṃ tasmiṃ samaye tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajaṃ cetasikaṃ sātaṃ cetasikaṃ sukhaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ sātaṃ sukhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā sātā sukhā vedanā – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti.**  
> 3. What is feeling at that time? Whatever at that time is a mental ease arisen from the contact of the corresponding mind-consciousness element (tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajaṃ), a mental pleasure, a pleasant, pleasurable feeling arisen from mind-contact (cetosamphassajaṃ), a pleasant, blissful feeling arisen from mind-contact—this is feeling at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=My4gS2F0YW3EgSB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgdmVkYW7EgSBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the etymology)
> **Sukhayatīti sukhaṃ ; yassa uppajjati taṃ sukhitaṃ karotīti attho. Suṭṭhu vā khādati, khanati ca kāyacittābādhanti sukhaṃ. Somanassavedanāyetaṃ nāmaṃ. Tassa lakkhaṇādīni vedanāpade vuttanayeneva veditabbāni.**  
> Happiness (sukhaṃ) is that which makes happy. The meaning is that in whom it arises, it makes that person happy. Or, happiness is that which thoroughly devours and digs up bodily and mental affliction. This is a name for pleasant feeling. Its characteristics and so on should be understood in the very manner stated in the section on feeling.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=160&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U3VraGF5YXTEq3RpIHN1a2hh4bmDIDs%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, characteristic, function, manifestation)
> **Aparo nayo – sātalakkhaṇaṃ sukhaṃ, sampayuttānaṃ upabrūhanarasaṃ, anuggahaṇapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ. Satipi ca nesaṃ pītisukhānaṃ katthaci avippayoge, iṭṭhārammaṇapaṭilābhatuṭṭhi pīti; paṭiladdharasānubhavanaṃ sukhaṃ. Yattha pīti tattha sukhaṃ. Yattha sukhaṃ tattha na niyamato pīti. Saṅkhārakkhandhasaṅgahitā pīti, vedanākkhandhasaṅgahitaṃ sukhaṃ. Kantārakhinnassa vanantodakadassanasavanesu viya pīti. Vanacchāyāpavesanaudakaparibhogesu viya sukhaṃ.**  
> Another method: happiness has the characteristic of pleasantness; its function is the increase of co-arisen states; it manifests as favoring. And even when there is no separation between rapture and happiness in some instances, rapture is the delight in obtaining a desirable object; happiness is the experience of the acquired taste. Where there is rapture, there is happiness. Where there is happiness, there is not necessarily rapture. Rapture is included in the aggregate of mental formations; happiness is included in the aggregate of feeling. Rapture, for one exhausted in the wilderness, is like seeing and hearing water in a forest grove. Happiness is like entering the shade of the forest and consuming water.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=160&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBhcm8gbmF5byDigJMgc8SBdGFsYWtraGHhuYdh4bmDIHN1a2hh4bmDLCBzYW1wYXl1dHTEgW5h4bmD)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Pinayati kāyacittaṃ tappeti, vaḍḍhetīti vā pīti , sā sampiyāyanalakkhaṇā, ārammaṇaṃ kallato gahaṇalakkhaṇāti vuttaṃ hoti, sampayuttadhamme sukhayatīti sukhaṃ , taṃ iṭṭhānubhavanalakkhaṇaṃ subhojanarasassādako rājā viya.**  
> Rapture (pīti) is that which refreshes, satisfies, or increases the body and mind; it has the characteristic of being very fond, or it is said to have the characteristic of grasping the object as a suitable thing. Pleasure (sukhaṃ) is that which makes the conjoined states happy; it has the characteristic of experiencing a desirable object, like a king who enjoys the taste of excellent food.  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=92&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UGluYXlhdGkga8SBeWFjaXR0YeG5gyB0YXBwZXRpLCB2YeG4jeG4jWhldMSrdGkgdsSBIHDEq3Rp)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Sāratthadīpanī)
> **Sukhayatīti sukhaṃ , attanā sampayuttadhamme laddhassāde karotīti attho. Svāyaṃ kattuniddeso pariyāyaladdho dhammato aññassa kattunivattanattho, nippariyāyena pana bhāvasādhanameva labbhatīti “sukhanaṃ sukhan”ti vuttaṃ. Sātalakkhaṇanti iṭṭhasabhāvattā taṃsamaṅgīpuggalaṃ, sampayuttadhamme vā attani sādayatīti sātaṃ da-kārassa ta-kāraṃ katvā.**  
> ‘That which makes happy’ is ‘bliss’; the meaning is that it makes the associated phenomena enjoy the satisfaction they have obtained. This active voice term is obtained by way of a figurative expression and is for the purpose of excluding another by means of the Dhamma, but in the definitive sense, only the state-denotation is obtained, which is why ‘bliss is a state of happiness’ was said. ‘Having the characteristic of ease’ means, because of the desirable nature, it makes the person endowed with that—or the associated phenomena—enjoy it in oneself, which is why ‘sāta’ is used, having changed the ‘d’ to ‘t’.  
>  
> — *Sāratthadīpanī-ṭīkā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_vi_01&page_number=361&name=s%C4%81ratthad%C4%ABpan%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U3VraGF5YXTEq3RpIHN1a2hh4bmDICwgYXR0YW7EgSBzYW1wYXl1dHRhZGhhbW1lIGxhZGRoYXNzxIFkZQ%3D%3D)
