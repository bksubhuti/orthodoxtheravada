### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṃyuttanikāya**: [m.] The Collection of Connected Discourses. Formed by *saṃyutta* (joined together, connected, grouped) + *nikāya* (collection).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Saṃyutta-nikāya* is the third of the five Nikāyas of the [Sutta-piṭaka](/glossary/sutta-pitaka/) (As 204:2 lists them in order: Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara, Khuddaka). The canonical text itself is divided into five books named *Sagāthāvaggo*, *Nidānavaggo*, *Khandhavaggo*, *Saḷāyatanavaggo* and *Mahāvaggo* (S-i 3:1, S-ii 3:1, S-iii 3:1, S-iv 3:1, S-v 3:1), each made of *saṃyuttas* such as the *Devatāsaṃyutta*, *Nidānasaṃyutta*, *Khandhasaṃyutta*, *Saḷāyatanasaṃyutta* and *Maggasaṃyutta* (S-i 4:1, S-ii 4:1, S-iii 4:1, S-iv 4:1, S-v 4:1).

The Atthasālinī defines it: the discourses beginning with the Oghataraṇa Sutta, arranged by way of the Devatāsaṃyutta and so forth, are the Saṃyutta Nikāya, counted there as seven thousand seven hundred and sixty-two discourses (As 211:1-2; As 151:7; also Pj-i 24:3). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī records that at the First Council, after reciting the Saṃyuttanikāya of one hundred bhāṇavāras, the elders entrusted it to the Elder Mahākassapa (Sv-i 105:1). The reciters of this collection are the *saṃyuttabhāṇakā* (Vism-ii 258:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Katamo saṃyuttanikāyo? Devatāsaṃyuttādivasena ṭhitāni oghataraṇādīni satta suttasahassāni satta suttasatāni ca dvāsaṭṭhi ca suttāni.**  
> What is the Saṃyutta Nikāya (saṃyuttanikāyo)? It is the seven thousand, seven hundred, and sixty-two discourses, beginning with the Oghataraṇa Sutta, arranged by way of the Connected Discourses with Devas and so forth.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 211:1-2) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=26&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1vIHNh4bmDeXV0dGFuaWvEgXlvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Tato anantaraṃ satabhāṇavāraparimāṇaṃ saṃyuttanikāyaṃ saṅgāyitvā mahākassapattheraṃ paṭicchāpesuṃ – “bhante, imaṃ tumhākaṃ nissitake vācethā” ti.**  
> Immediately after that, having recited the Saṃyuttanikāya, consisting of one hundred bhāṇavāras, they entrusted it to the Elder Mahākassapa— “Venerable sir, teach this to those who rely on you.”  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Saṃyuttanikāya* (S-i 2:1 to S-v 2:1 title "Saṃyuttanikāyo"; five vaggas S-i 3:1, S-ii 3:1, S-iii 3:1, S-iv 3:1, S-v 3:1). *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* (SN 56.11, S-v 2474:1, "1. Dhammacakkappavattanasuttaṃ") – the Buddha's first discourse, in the *Saccasaṃyutta* (S-v 2430:1, "12. Saccasaṃyuttaṃ"), the last saṃyutta of the Mahāvagga.
* **Canonical**: *Anattalakkhaṇa Sutta* (SN 22.59, S-iii 322:1, "7. Anattalakkhaṇasuttaṃ") – the discourse on non-self.
* **Commentary**: *[Sāratthappakāsinī](/glossary/saratthappakasini/)* – Buddhaghosa's commentary on the Saṃyutta Nikāya (Spk-i to Spk-v); *Atthasālinī* (As 151:7, 211:1-2); *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (Sv-i 105:1).
