### Grammatical Analysis
**Maraṇassati**: [f.] mindfulness of death; recollection of death. Formed by *maraṇa* (death) + *[sati](/glossary/sati/)* (mindfulness).

### Orthodox Definition
*Maraṇassati* is one of the four "Protective Meditations" (*caturārakkha*): recollection of the Buddha, mettā, the foul (*asubha*) and mindfulness of death (Sp-t-iv 1920:2, explaining Sp-iv 345:2). It is the deliberate, systematic reflection on the absolute certainty of one's own biological death, combined with the extreme uncertainty of exactly when it will happen.

The orthodox practice is not a morbid dwelling on sorrow (which would be aversion/[dosa](/glossary/dosa/)), but a sharp, awakening reflection designed to generate spiritual urgency (*saṃvega*) and heedfulness (*[appamāda](/glossary/appamada/)*). 

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* gives eight ways to recollect death (Vism-i 1401:1): *vadhakapaccupaṭṭhānato* (as appearing like a murderer), *sampattivipattito* (through the ruin of success), *upasaṃharaṇato* (by comparison with others), *kāyabahusādhāraṇato* (because the body is shared with many), *āyudubbalato* (through the weakness of life), *animittato* (through the absence of signs), *addhānaparicchedato* (through the limitedness of the span) and *khaṇaparittato* (through the briefness of the moment).

### Quote
> **“Yo cāyaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu evaṃ maraṇassatiṃ bhāveti – ‘ aho vatāhaṃ tadantaraṃ jīveyyaṃ yadantaraṃ assasitvā vā passasāmi passasitvā vā assasāmi, bhagavato sāsanaṃ manasi kareyyaṃ, bahu vata me kataṃ assā ’ ti. Ime vuccanti, bhikkhave, bhikkhū appamattā viharanti tikkhaṃ maraṇassatiṃ bhāventi āsavānaṃ khayāya.**  
> “Monks, whoever is this bhikkhu who develops mindfulness of death thus: ‘Oh, if only I might live for the interval that it takes for me to breathe out after breathing in, or to breathe in after breathing out, if only I might attend to the Blessed One’s teaching, then indeed much would have been accomplished by me’— these bhikkhus, monks, are said to dwell diligently. They develop keen mindfulness of death for the destruction of the taints.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 6.19, Paṭhamamaraṇassati Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_06&page_number=270&name=chakkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFlvIGPEgXlh4bmDLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGJoaWtraHUgZXZh4bmD)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Paṭhamamaraṇassati Sutta* (AN 6.19, CST "9. Paṭhamamaraṇassatisuttaṃ" A-vi 110:1, "19." at A-vi 111:1) – The Buddha asks the monks whether they develop mindfulness of death; those who wish to live a day and night, a day, an alms-meal or four or five morsels are said to "live heedlessly and develop it slackly" (A-vi 118-121:3), while those who think of the span of one morsel or one breath "live heedfully and develop it keenly" (A-vi 122-123:3).
* **Canonical**: *[Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)* (v. 286) – "Here I shall live in the rains, here in winter and summer... thus the fool thinks, not realizing the danger of death."
* **Commentary**: *Visuddhimagga* (Chapter VIII, "8. Anussatikammaṭṭhānaniddeso" Vism-i 1395:1; the eight ways at Vism-i 1401:1) – The comprehensive manual on mindfulness of death.
