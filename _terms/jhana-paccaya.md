### Grammatical Analysis
**Jhānapaccaya**: [m.] jhāna condition. A compound of *[jhāna](/glossary/jhana/)* + *paccaya* (condition). The Ṭīkā explains the word *jhānaṅga*: the factors are *jhānas* because they approach the object and contemplate it (*upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena*) and burn up the opposing states in a fitting way (*paccanīkadhammajhāpanaṭṭhena*), and *aṅgas* because they are known as parts of the composite (Abhidh-s-t 1588:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Jhāna-paccaya* is the seventeenth of the 24 conditions in the Paṭṭhāna: "the jhāna factors are related to the states associated with jhāna and to the materiality produced by them, by jhāna condition" (Paṭṭh-i 51:1).

The Paṭṭhāna commentary says: the seven jhāna factors, which help in the sense of contemplation (*upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena*), excluding the pair of bodily pleasant and painful feeling in the two sets of fivefold sense-consciousness, in all their divisions as wholesome and so on, are the jhāna condition (Paṭṭh-a 1937:1). The seven are given in the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha: applied thought (*vitakka*), sustained thought (*vicāra*), zest (*pīti*), one-pointedness (*ekaggatā*), joy (*somanassa*), dejection (*domanassa*) and equanimity (*upekkhā*) (Abhidh-s 627:1). Here the feeling factors take the place of the *sukha* of the jhāna formula.

The Ṭīkā adds that although there is no whole apart from its parts, the factors are called separately "factors", like the parts of an army or of a chariot, and "jhāna" when taken together; dejection is an unwholesome jhāna factor, while the rest are wholesome, unwholesome or indeterminate (Abhidh-s-t 1588:2-3). Thus the condition works whenever these factors arise, whether the consciousness is wholesome, unwholesome or indeterminate.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Jhānapaccayoti – jhānaṅgāni jhānasampayuttakānaṃ dhammānaṃ taṃsamuṭṭhānānañca rūpānaṃ jhānapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 17. Jhāna condition: The jhāna factors are related to the states associated with jhāna and to the materiality produced by them, by jhāna condition.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā)

> The seven jhāna factors (satta jhānaṅgāni), which help in the sense of contemplation (upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena), excluding the pair of bodily pleasant and painful feelings in the two sets of fivefold consciousness (dvipañcaviññāṇesu kāyikasukhadukkhavedanādvayaṃ), in all their wholesome and other divisions, are jhāna condition (jhānapaccayo). Just as he said: ‘The jhāna factors (jhānaṅgāni) are a condition for phenomena associated with jhāna (jhānasampayuttakānaṃ dhammānaṃ) and materiality rooted in thought by way of jhāna condition.’  
>

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha: the seven jhāna factors)
> **Satta jhānaṅgāni – vitakko vicāro pīti ekaggatā somanassaṃ domanassaṃ upekkhā.**  
> 16. Seven jhāna factors: applied thought, sustained thought, zest, one-pointedness, happiness, dejection, and equanimity.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Missakasaṅgaha) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=48&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=MTYuIFNhdHRhIGpoxIFuYeG5hWfEgW5pIOKAkyB2aXRha2tvIHZpY8SBcm8%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Ārammaṇaṃ upagantvā cintanasaṅkhātena upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena yathārahaṃ paccanīkadhammajhāpanaṭṭhena ca jhānāni ca tāni aṅgāni ca samuditānaṃ avayavabhāvena aṅgīyanti ñāyantīti jhānaṅgāni. Avayavavinimuttassa ca samudāyassa abhāvepi senaṅgarathaṅgādayo viya visuṃ visuṃ aṅgabhāvena vuccanti ekato hutvā jhānabhāvena. Domanassañcettha akusalajhānaṅgaṃ, sesāni kusalākusalābyākatajhānaṅgāni.**  
> 16. Because they contemplate (upanijjhāyanaṭṭhena) the object—which is defined as thinking after having approached it—and because they burn up (jhāpanaṭṭhena) opposing states as appropriate, they are jhānas; and because they are recognized as factors (aṅgāni) by being parts of a composite whole, they are jhāna factors (jhānaṅgāni). Even though a whole does not exist apart from its parts, like the components of an army or a chariot, they are called factors when considered separately, and jhānas when taken together. In this context, displeasure is an unwholesome jhāna factor, while the rest are wholesome, unwholesome, and indeterminate jhāna factors.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Missakasaṅgahavaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=222&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MTYuIMSAcmFtbWHhuYdh4bmDIHVwYWdhbnR2xIEgY2ludGFuYXNh4bmFa2jEgXRlbmEgdXBhbmlqamjEgXlhbmHhua3hua1oZW5hIHlhdGjEgXJhaGHhuYM%3D)
