### Grammatical Analysis
**Cariyāpiṭaka**: [nt.] the title of the book, a compound of *cariyā* (conduct) + *piṭaka* (basket, collection). The Aṭṭhakathā's introduction names it in the verse "*lokanāthena cariyā-piṭakaṃ nāma desitaṃ*", "it was taught by the Lord of the World, called the Cariyāpiṭaka" (Cp-a 18:1). The Mūla's title line reads "*Khuddakanikāye Cariyāpiṭakapāḷi*" (Cp 2:1-3:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Cariyāpiṭaka* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya. The Aṭṭhakathā's introductory verses say that it shows "the power of the practices for awakening (*sambodhicariyā*) accumulated in this auspicious aeon, extremely difficult, having reached the highest perfection, beginning with the perfection of giving", that it was taught by the Great Seer while staying at the Nigrodha monastery among the Sakyans, for the Dhamma-general (Sāriputta), and that the great seers who compiled the Dhamma recited it in the Khuddaka Nikāya, "illustrating the Teacher's accomplishment of the causes" (Cp-a 13:1-20:1).

The Mūla has three chapters: "1. Akittivaggo" (Cp 4:1), "2. Hatthināgavaggo" (Cp 467:1) and "3. Yudhañjayavaggo" (Cp 771:1). They contain 10, 10 and 15 conducts (*cariyā*), thirty-five in all, from "1. Akitticariyā" (Cp 5:1) to "15. Mahālomahaṃsacariyā" (Cp 1161:1); the first chapter ends with "Dānapāraminiddeso niṭṭhito" (Cp 466:1), the second with "Sīlapāraminiddeso niṭṭhito" (Cp 770:1), and the closing verses end "*upekkhāpāramī āsi, iti vutthaṃ mahesinā*", "I was the perfection of equanimity, so spoke the Great Seer" (Cp 1181:1). The Aṭṭhakathā says that ten perfections are found in it (Cp-a 274:2).
