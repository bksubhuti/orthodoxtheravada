### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammāsamādhi**: [m.] Right Concentration. Formed by *sammā* (rightly) + *[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)* (concentration). The Visuddhimagga identifies it with the one-pointedness of mind (*cittekaggatā*) that destroys wrong concentration (Vism-ii 787).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammāsamādhi* is the eighth and last limb of the Noble Eightfold Path, the limb of concentration (*[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)-sikkhā*).

In the Suttas the Buddha defines it as the **four jhānas**, the first, second, third and fourth, with the standard formulas (DN 22; Vibh 749). In the Mahācattārīsaka Sutta he defines "noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites" as the unification of mind (*cittassa ekaggatā*) "equipped with" the other seven factors, right view to right mindfulness (MN 117).

The Abhidhamma defines it as stability of mind, non-scattering, non-distraction, tranquillity (*samatha*), the faculty and power of concentration (Dhs 414); in the supramundane state it is also a "path factor, included in the path" and the factor of awakening of concentration (*samādhisambojjhaṅga*) (Vibh 765). The supramundane jhāna is the one "that leads out, goes to diminution, for the abandoning of wrong views and the attainment of the first stage" (Dhs 759). The Visuddhimagga: for one whose mind is guarded by supreme mindfulness, the one-pointedness of mind (*cittekaggatā*) associated with that, which destroys wrong concentration, is right concentration; its characteristic is non-distraction (*avikkhepa*), its function concentrating (*samādhāna*), its manifestation the abandonment of wrong concentration (Vism-ii 787).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu vivicceva kāmehi vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Vitakkavicārānaṃ vūpasamā ajjhattaṃ sampasādanaṃ cetaso ekodibhāvaṃ avitakkaṃ avicāraṃ samādhijaṃ pītisukhaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Pītiyā ca virāgā upekkhako ca viharati, sato ca sampajāno, sukhañca kāyena paṭisaṃvedeti, yaṃ taṃ ariyā ācikkhanti ‘ upekkhako satimā sukhavihārī ’ ti tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Sukhassa ca pahānā dukkhassa ca pahānā pubbeva somanassadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamā adukkhamasukhaṃ upekkhāsatipārisuddhiṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsamādhi.**  
> “Now what, monks, is right concentration? Here, monks, a monk, quite secluded from sense desires, secluded from unwholesome states, with applied and sustained thought, and the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion, dwells having attained the first absorption. From the stilling of applied and sustained thought, with internal clarity, and unification of mind, being without applied and sustained thought, having the rapture and pleasure born of concentration, he dwells having attained the second absorption. With the fading away of rapture he dwells equanimous, mindful, clearly comprehending, experiencing happiness through the body, about which the noble ones announce: ‘The man indifferent and mindful is well at ease,’ he dwells having attained the third absorption. Having abandoned pleasure, abandoned pain, and by the previous dying away both of gladness and of sorrow, without ease, and without pain, and with complete purity of mindfulness owing to equanimity, he dwells having attained the fourth absorption. This, monks, is called right concentration.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, Magga-sacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=250&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYW1txIFzYW3EgWRoaT8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso saparikkhāro? Seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi, sammāsaṅkappo, sammāvācā, sammākammanto, sammāājīvo, sammāvāyāmo, sammāsati; yā kho, bhikkhave, imehi sattahaṅgehi cittassa ekaggatā parikkhatā – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, ariyo sammāsamādhi saupaniso itipi, saparikkhāro itipi.**  
> “And what, monks, is noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites? That is to say: right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, and right mindfulness. The unification of mind equipped with these seven factors is called, monks, noble right concentration with its supports and its requisites.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 117, Mahācattārīsaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=116&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBhcml5byBzYW1txIFzYW3EgWRoaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamo sammāsamādhi? Yā cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti … pe … sammāsamādhi samādhisambojjhaṅgo maggaṅgaṃ maggapariyāpannaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati “sammāsamādhi”.**  
> Therein, what (katamo) is right concentration (sammāsamādhi)? Whatever is stability of mind (cittassa ṭhiti), steadiedness (saṇṭhiti) ... (pe) right concentration (sammāsamādhi), the concentration enlightenment factor (samādhisambojjhaṅgo), a path factor (maggaṅgaṃ), included in the path (maggapariyāpannaṃ) — this (ayaṃ) is called (vuccati) ‘‘right concentration’’.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Saccavibhaṅga, Abhidhammabhājanīya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=113&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyBzYW1txIFzYW3EgWRoaT8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye sammāsamādhi hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti avaṭṭhiti avisāhāro avikkhepo avisāhaṭamānasatā samatho samādhindriyaṃ samādhibalaṃ sammāsamādhi – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye sammāsamādhi hoti.**  
> 24. What is right concentration at that time? Whatever stability of mind, firm stability, established stability, non-scattering, non-distraction, non-scatteredness of mind, tranquility, faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, or right concentration exists at that time—this is right concentration at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 24) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=21&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjQuIEthdGFtbyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgc2FtbcSBc2FtxIFkaGkgaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Evaṃ anuttarāya satiyā saṃrakkhiyamānacittassa taṃsampayuttāva micchāsamādhividdhaṃsikā cittekaggatā sammāsamādhi nāma. So avikkhepalakkhaṇo, samādhānaraso, micchāsamādhippahānapaccupaṭṭhānoti.**  
> For one whose mind is thus guarded by supreme mindfulness, the one-pointedness of mind (cittekaggatā) associated with that, which destroys wrong concentration, is called right concentration (sammāsamādhi). It has the characteristic of non-distraction, the function of concentrating, and it is manifested as the abandonment of wrong concentration.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVI, para. 562ff) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=142&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=RXZh4bmDIGFudXR0YXLEgXlhIHNhdGl5xIEgc2HhuYNyYWtraGl5YW3EgW5hY2l0dGFzc2EgdGHhuYNzYW1wYXl1dHTEgXZhIG1pY2NoxIFzYW3EgWRoaXZpZGRoYeG5g3Npa8SB)
