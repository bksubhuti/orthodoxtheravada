### Grammatical Analysis
**Yoga**: [m.] bond; yoke. The Atthasālinī derives it: *vaṭṭasmiṃ yojentīti yogā*, "they yoke (one) in the round of existence, therefore they are called yokes". The Dhammasaṅgaṇī-mūlaṭīkā distinguishes bond/fetter (*saṃyojana*, which does not let one escape even from afar), knot (*gantha*, which ties like chains) and *yoga*, which "makes a firm sticking" (*saṃsilisakaraṇaṃ yojanaṃ yogo*).

### Orthodox Definition
The four *yogas* are, in the Yoga Sutta (AN 4.10) and in the Yoga Sutta of the Oghavagga of the Maggasaṃyutta (S-v 384): *kāmayogo, bhavayogo, diṭṭhiyogo, avijjāyogo* (the bonds of sensuality, existence, views and ignorance). The Aṅguttara sutta explains each as not knowing, as they really are, the origin, disappearance, gratification, danger and escape: in the case of sensual pleasures, of states of existence, of views, and, for the bond of ignorance, of the six bases for contact. Then the bond lies deep within one (*sānuseti*). The sutta concludes: "Thus are the bond of sensuality ... one is bound by bad unwholesome states ... therefore one is said to be *ayogakkhemī*, 'not secure from bondage'". Its opposite is *yogakkhema* (security from bondage). On *anuttaro yogakkhemo* the Manorathapūraṇī says it is not only awakening (*bodhi*) but also the Nibbāna of the fruit of arahatship (Mp-ii 25:7), and the Papañcasūdanī says arahatship is the *anuttaro yogakkhemo* (Ps-ii 111:5).

In the Abhidhamma (Dhammasaṅgaṇī §1157) the *yogagocchaka* asks "Katame dhammā yogā?" and answers "Cattāro yogā": the four bonds. The Atthasālinī (As 2332:6-8) treats the clusters of taints, fetters, floods and yokes together: in the yokes it names the yoke of sensual passion and the yoke of becoming (*kāmayogo bhavayogo*).

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, yogā. Katame cattāro? Kāmayogo, bhavayogo, diṭṭhiyogo, avijjāyogo. Iti kāmayogo bhavayogo diṭṭhiyogo avijjāyogo, saṃyutto pāpakehi akusalehi dhammehi saṃkilesikehi ponobhavikehi sadarehi dukkhavipākehi āyatiṃ jātijarāmaraṇikehi. Tasmā ayogakkhemīti vuccati. Ime kho, bhikkhave, cattāro yogā.**  
> “Monks, there are these four bonds (yoga). What four? The bond of sensuality, the bond of existence, the bond of views, and the bond of ignorance. Thus are the bond of sensuality, the bond of existence, the bond of views, and the bond of ignorance; one is bound by bad unwholesome states that are defiling, conducive to renewed existence, troublesome, ripening in suffering, leading to future birth, old age, and death. Therefore one is said to be ‘not secure from bondage.’ These indeed, monks, are the four bonds.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 4.10, Yoga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=316&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTAu4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJvbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgeW9nxIEu)

> **38-43. Yogagocchake vaṭṭasmiṃ yojentīti yogā . Yoganiyā oghaniyā viya veditabbā.**  
> 38-43. In the cluster of yokes (yogagocchake): because they yoke (yojenti) (one) in the round of existence (vaṭṭasmiṃ), they are therefore called yokes (yogā). Conducive to yokes (yoganiyā) should be understood (veditabbā) like (viya) those to be overcome by floods (oghaniyā).  
>  
> — Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā ([Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/), Yogagocchaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=91&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MzgtNDMuIFlvZ2Fnb2NjaGFrZSB2YeG5reG5rWFzbWnhuYMgeW9qZW50xKt0aSB5b2fEgSAu)
