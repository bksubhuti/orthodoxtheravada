### Grammatical Analysis
**Aṭṭhakathā**: [f.] commentary, an explanation of the meaning, from *attha* (meaning) + *kathā* (talk, exposition). The word is used with that purpose in the introduction to the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī: "*atthappakāsanatthaṃ aṭṭhakathā ādito vasisatehi*", "the Aṭṭhakathā, for the sake of illuminating the meaning, [was recited] at the beginning by the five hundred masters" (Sv-i 16:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Aṭṭhakathā* is the commentary on the Tipiṭaka, and the commentaries are said to go back to the first council. The introductory verses of the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī say that the Aṭṭhakathā was recited at the start by the five hundred masters (*vasisatehi*), recited again afterwards, brought to the island of Sīhaḷa by the master Mahāmahinda and set down in the Sinhala language for the island's inhabitants; the author sets aside that language and will explain the meaning in the Māgadha language, in keeping with the Pāli text and without opposing the tradition of the elders of the Mahāvihāra (Sv-i 16:1-23:1). The Atthasālinī and the Ṭīkā say the same (As 22:1-37:1; Sp-t-i-b 135:11).

The Vinaya commentary classes it as the *ācariyavāda*, "the *aṭṭhakathātanti* (commentary tradition) established by the five hundred arahants who compiled the Dhamma, distinct from the Pāli and conveying decisions that have been handed down" (Sp-i 966:1). It also ranks it above one's own opinion: all the Theravāda found in the commentaries on the Suttanta, Abhidhamma and Vinaya is called *attanomati* when used for a private view, but the *ācariyavāda* is stronger than *attanomati*, and one's own opinion must be tested against it (Sp-i 968:1-8). The Ṭīkā explains that it is called *ācariyavāda* because the teachers explain the Pāli by it (Sp-t-i-b 135:8).

The great commentaries named in the texts include the Samantapāsādikā on the Vinaya (Sp-v 622:1), the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī on the Dīgha (Sv-iii 1130:1), and the Aṭṭhasālinī on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, composed by the elder Buddhaghosa of the Mahāvihāra (As 2483:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Atthappakāsanatthaṃ, aṭṭhakathā ādito vasisatehi; Pañcahi yā saṅgītā, anusaṅgītā ca pacchāpi. Sīhaḷadīpaṃ pana ābhatātha, vasinā mahāmahindena; Ṭhapitā sīhaḷabhāsāya, dīpavāsīnamatthāya. Apanetvāna tatohaṃ, sīhaḷabhāsaṃ manoramaṃ bhāsaṃ; Tantinayānucchavikaṃ, āropento vigatadosaṃ.**  
> To illuminate the meaning, the Commentary (aṭṭhakathā) was originally recited by the five hundred elders, And subsequently recited again. Brought to the island of Sihala by the master, the Great Mahinda, It was established in the Sinhala language for the benefit of the island's inhabitants. Discarding that Sinhala language, I shall express the meaning in the delightful Magadha language, Which conforms to the Pāli text and is free of errors.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Ganthārambhakathā, Sv-i 16-21) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=1&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXR0aGFwcGFrxIFzYW5hdHRoYeG5gywgYeG5reG5rWhha2F0aMSBIMSBZGl0byB2YXNpc2F0ZWhpOw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Ācariyavādo nāma dhammasaṅgāhakehi pañcahi arahantasatehi ṭhapitā pāḷivinimuttā okkantavinicchayappavattā aṭṭhakathātanti. Apica suttantābhidhammavinayaṭṭhakathāsu āgato sabbopi theravādo “attanomati” nāma.**  
> The Teachers' View (ācariyavādo) refers to the commentary tradition established by the five hundred Arahants who compiled the Dhamma; it is distinct from the Pāli text and consists of the decisions that have been passed down. Moreover, all the Elder-views found in the commentaries on the Suttanta, Abhidhamma, and Vinaya are called ‘one's own opinion.’  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Sp-i 966, 968) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=196&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=xIBjYXJpeWF2xIFkbyBuxIFtYSBkaGFtbWFzYeG5hWfEgWhha2VoaSBwYcOxY2FoaSBhcmFoYW50YXNhdGVoaSDhua1oYXBpdMSB)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Sāratthadīpanī)
> **Yadipi tattha tattha bhagavatā pavattitā pakiṇṇakadesanāva aṭṭhakathā, sā pana dhammasaṅgāhakehi paṭhamaṃ tīṇi piṭakāni saṅgāyitvā tassa atthavaṇṇanānurūpeneva vācanāmaggaṃ āropitattā “ācariyavādo” ti vuccati ācariyā vadanti saṃvaṇṇenti pāḷiṃ etenāti katvā. Tenāha – “ācariyavādo nāma … pe … aṭṭhakathātantī”ti. Tisso saṅgītiyo āruḷhoyeva ca buddhavacanassa atthasaṃvaṇṇanābhūto kathāmaggo mahāmahindattherena tambapaṇṇidīpaṃ ābhato, pacchā tambapaṇṇiyehi mahātherehi sīhaḷabhāsāya ṭhapito nikāyantaraladdhisaṅkarapariharaṇatthaṃ.**  
> Even if the scattered instructions taught by the Blessed One are the commentary, that commentary was placed by the Compilers of the Dhamma after they had recited the three Piṭakas, and because it was arranged in the way of recitation in accordance with the meaning-explanation, it is called the ‘Teachers’ View,’ because the teachers explain and comment on the Pāli with it. Therefore— ‘“Teachers’ View” refers to the commentary tradition established by the Compilers of the Dhamma.’ The path of discourse that constitutes the commentary on the meaning of the word of the Buddha, which was already ascended to the three recitations, was brought to the Island of Tambapaṇṇi by the Great Elder Mahinda. Later, it was established in the Sinhala language by the Great Elders residing on the Island of Tambapaṇṇi to ward off the corruptions of the views of other sects.  
>  
> — Sāratthadīpanī (Sp-t-i-b 135) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_vi_02&page_number=45&name=s%C4%81ratthad%C4%ABpan%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=WWFkaXBpIHRhdHRoYSB0YXR0aGEgYmhhZ2F2YXTEgSBwYXZhdHRpdMSBIHBha2nhuYfhuYdha2FkZXNhbsSBdmE%3D)
