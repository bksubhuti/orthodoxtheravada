### Grammatical Analysis
**Asubhabhāvanā**: [f.] meditation on the unbeautiful; development of foulness. Formed by *asubha* (unbeautiful, foul) + *bhāvanā* (development, cultivation). The Samantapāsādikā explains it as the development and increase of the mind that proceeds taking the aspect of the unbeautiful (*asubhākāraṃ gahetvā pavattassa cittassa bhāvanā vaḍḍhanā*) in objects internal and external, such as hair and so on or the bloated corpse and so on (Sp-i 1635:2).

### Orthodox Definition
The Mūla uses the term in the Vesālī Sutta, where the Buddha speaks in many ways on the unlovely (*asubhakathā*), in praise of the unlovely, and in praise of the development of foulness (*asubhabhāvanāya vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati*, S-v 1922:2). Among the four additional things to be developed, the Buddha says: *asubhā bhāvetabbā rāgassa pahānāya*, "the unlovely should be developed for the abandonment of lust" (A-ix 20:1; also A-ix 42:1, Meghiya Sutta). The Abhidhamma has the foulness jhāna (*asubhajjhānaṃ*), for example the first jhāna "accompanied by the perception of a bloated corpse" (*uddhumātakasaññāsahagataṃ*, Dhs 729:1-730:2).

The Visuddhimagga (Chapter VI, Asubhakammaṭṭhānaniddesa) gives ten *asubhas* (Vism-i 772:1): the bloated (*uddhumātakaṃ*), the livid (*vinīlakaṃ*), the festering (*vipubbakaṃ*), the cut-up (*vicchiddakaṃ*), the gnawed (*vikkhāyitakaṃ*), the scattered (*vikkhittakaṃ*), the hacked and scattered (*hatavikkhittakaṃ*), the bleeding (*lohitakaṃ*), the worm-infested (*puḷuvakaṃ*) and the skeleton (*aṭṭhikaṃ*). For one of lustful temperament (*rāgacarita*) the ten foulnesses and mindfulness of the body, eleven meditation subjects in all, are suitable (Vism-i 787:2), and these eleven lead to the first jhāna only (Vism-i 777:2). The Sp-i passage above also names the living body (hair and so on, *kesādīsu*) as an object, and MN 10 has the thirty-two parts (M-i 297:1) and the Nine Charnel Ground contemplations (M-i 305:1-308:1), where the monk draws the body to the corpse: *ayampi kho kāyo evaṃdhammo evaṃbhāvī evaṃanatīto*, "this body too is of such a nature, will become like that, and cannot escape that" (M-i 306:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **Evaṃ me sutaṃ – ekaṃ samayaṃ bhagavā vesāliyaṃ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṃ. Tena kho pana samayena bhagavā bhikkhūnaṃ anekapariyāyena asubhakathaṃ katheti, asubhāya vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati, asubhabhāvanāya vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati.**  
> Thus have I heard. On one occasion the Blessed One was dwelling at Vesālī in the Great Wood in the hall with the peaked roof. Now on that occasion the Blessed One spoke in many ways to the bhikkhus about the unlovely, spoke praise of the unlovely, and spoke praise of the contemplation of ugliness.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (Vesālī Sutta, S-v 1921 "9. Vesālīsuttaṃ") · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=278&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OTg1LiBFdmHhuYMgbWUgc3V0YeG5gyDigJMgZWth4bmD)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Asubhajhānaṃ soḷasakkhattukaṃ 263. Katame dhammā kusalā? Yasmiṃ samaye rūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti vivicceva kāmehi … pe … paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati uddhumātakasaññāsahagataṃ, tasmiṃ samaye phasso hoti … pe … avikkhepo hoti … pe … ime dhammā kusalā.**  
> The contemplation-of-foulness jhānas are sixteen-fold. 263. What mental phenomena are wholesome? At the time when one develops the path for rebirth in the fine-material realm, quite secluded from sensual pleasures… enters and dwells in the first jhāna accompanied by the perception of an inflated corpse. At that time contact exists… non-distraction exists… these mental phenomena are wholesome.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Asubhajjhāna, §263) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=67&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QXN1YmhhamjEgW5h4bmDIHNv4bi3YXNha2toYXR0dWth4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Asubhabhāvanāya vaṇṇaṃ bhāsatīti yā ayaṃ kesādīsu vā uddhumātakādīsu vā ajjhattabahiddhāvatthūsu asubhākāraṃ gahetvā pavattassa cittassa bhāvanā vaḍḍhanā phātikammaṃ, tassā asubhabhāvanāya ānisaṃsaṃ dassento vaṇṇaṃ bhāsati, guṇaṃ parikitteti.**  
> “Spoke in praise of developing the mind in unattractiveness”: he spoke in praise of and extolled the virtues of the cultivation of the mind that is fixed on the unattractiveness of both internal and external objects, whether hair or the swollen corpse, explaining the advantages of such development.  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Sp-i 1635, Vesālī asubha story) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_02&page_number=2&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=QXN1YmhhYmjEgXZhbsSBeWEgdmHhuYfhuYdh4bmDIGJoxIFzYXTEq3RpIHnEgSBheWHhuYMga2VzxIFkxKtzdQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Uddhumātakaṃ, vinīlakaṃ, vipubbakaṃ, vicchiddakaṃ, vikkhāyitakaṃ, vikkhittakaṃ, hatavikkhittakaṃ, lohitakaṃ, puḷuvakaṃ, aṭṭhikanti ime dasa asubhā.**  
> The bloated, the livid, the festering, the cut-up, the gnawed, the scattered, the hacked and scattered, the bleeding, the worm-infested, and the skeleton—these are the ten kinds of foulness (asubha).
