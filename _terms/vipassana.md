### Grammatical Analysis
**Vipassanā**: [f.] insight. The Atthasālinī and the Ṭīkā derive it: *aniccādivasena vividhehi ākārehi dhamme passatīti vipassanā*, "it sees phenomena in various ways, by way of impermanence and so on". The prefix *vi-* is thus taken in the sense "various ways"; the verb is *passati* ("sees").

### Orthodox Definition
In the Aṅguttara Nikāya (AN 2, ¶32) the Buddha names two things conducive to knowledge (*vijjābhāgiyā*): tranquillity (*samatha*) and insight (*vipassanā*). When tranquillity is developed, the mind is developed, and lust is abandoned; when insight is developed, wisdom (*paññā*) is developed, and ignorance is abandoned.

The Atthasālinī (As 1256) defines the pair: tranquillity "calms counteracting states such as sensual desire", and insight "sees phenomena in various ways, by way of impermanence and so on"; and adds: *paññāvesā atthato*, "by meaning this (insight) is indeed wisdom". The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Abhidh-s-t 1764) says the same, and calls it "the wisdom of development (*bhāvanāpaññā*) beginning with the contemplation of impermanence".

In the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha (§43) the meditation subject of insight (*vipassanākammaṭṭhāna*) is the sevenfold compendium of purifications (*sattavidhena visuddhisaṅgaho*): purification of virtue, of mind, of view, by overcoming doubt, of knowledge and vision of what is the path and what is not the path, of knowledge and vision of the practice, and of knowledge and vision.

On the pairing of the two, the Yuganaddha Sutta (AN 4.170) describes four ways: insight preceded by tranquillity, tranquillity preceded by insight, the two in tandem (*yuganaddha*), and the mind agitated by the restlessness for the Dhamma; in each the path is born, and as he cultivates it his fetters are abandoned and underlying tendencies are abolished.

### Quote
> **“Dve me, bhikkhave, dhammā vijjābhāgiyā. Katame dve? Samatho ca vipassanā ca. Samatho, bhikkhave, bhāvito kamattha manubhoti? Cittaṃ bhāvīyati. Cittaṃ bhāvitaṃ kamatthamanubhoti? Yo rāgo so pahīyati. Vipassanā, bhikkhave, bhāvitā kamatthamanubhoti? Paññā bhāvīyati. Paññā bhāvitā kamatthamanubhoti? Yā avijjā sā pahīyati. Rāgupakkiliṭṭhaṃ vā, bhikkhave, cittaṃ na vimuccati, avijjupakkiliṭṭhā vā paññā bhāvīyati. Iti kho, bhikkhave, rāgavirāgā cetovimutti, avijjāvirāgā paññāvimuttī” ti.**  
> “Monks, these two mental phenomena are conducive to knowledge (vijjābhāgiyā). Which two? Tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā). Monks, when tranquility is developed, what benefit does it experience? The mind is developed. When the mind is developed, what benefit does it experience? Any lust (rāga) is abandoned. Monks, when insight is developed, what benefit does it experience? Wisdom is developed. When wisdom is developed, what benefit does it experience? Any ignorance is abandoned. Monks, the mind defiled by lust is not liberated, and wisdom defiled by ignorance is not developed. Thus, monks, the liberation of mind (cetovimutti) is through the fading away of lust, and the liberation by wisdom (paññāvimutti) is through the fading away of ignorance.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 2, Dukanipāta ¶32, Vijjābhāgiya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=62&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzIu4oCY4oCYIER2ZSBtZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBkaGFtbcSBIHZpamrEgWJoxIFnaXnEgS4%3D)

> **Kāmacchandādayo paccanīkadhamme sametīti samatho. Aniccādivasena vividhehi ākārehi dhamme passatīti vipassanā. Paññāvesā atthato.**  
> ‘Calms counteracting states like sensual desire and so on’ is tranquility (samatho). ‘Sees qualities in various ways by way of impermanence and so on’ is insight (vipassanā). This insight, by meaning, is indeed wisdom.  
>

> **Vipassanākammaṭṭhāne pana sīlavisuddhi cittavisuddhi diṭṭhivisuddhi kaṅkhāvitaraṇavisuddhi maggāmaggañāṇadassanavisuddhi paṭipadāñāṇadassanavisuddhi ñāṇadassanavisuddhi ceti sattavidhena visuddhisaṅgaho.**

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Yuganaddha Sutta* (AN 4.170, A-iv 1316–1321) – tranquillity and insight in order, in tandem or otherwise. *Vijjābhāgiya* passage (AN 2, ¶32, A-ii 71) – what tranquillity and insight accomplish.
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)*, Vipassanākathā (Paṭis 1986–2014) – the chapter on insight; the same Yuganaddha passage is repeated in Paṭis 1367.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 1256:2-3); *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 1197:4); *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā* (Abhidh-s-t 1764). The seven purifications are in Abhidh-s 812:1 and in the *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)*.
