### Grammatical Analysis
**Ekaggatā**: [f.] one-pointedness; unification of mind. Formed by *eka* (one) + *agga* (point, peak) + *tā* (state). The Aṭṭhasālinī: "*cittassekaggatā* is the state of the mind being one-pointed (*cittassa ekaggabhāvo*); this is a name for concentration (*samādhissetaṃ nāmaṃ*)" (As 1188:1-2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Ekaggatā* is a name for *samādhi* (As 1188:2). In the Mūla, Dhammadinnā answers Visākha: "Whatever is one-pointedness of mind, friend Visākha, that is concentration (*yā kho, āvuso visākha, cittassa ekaggatā ayaṃ samādhi*)" (M-i 1585:1, MN 44). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it with a series of synonyms: stability of mind (*ṭhiti*), firm stability, established stability, non-scattering (*avisāhāro*), non-distraction (*avikkhepo*), non-scatteredness of mind, tranquillity (*samatho*), the faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, right concentration (Dhs 401:2).

The Aṭṭhasālinī gives the characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: it has the characteristic of non-scattering or of non-distraction (*avisāralakkhaṇo, avikkhepalakkhaṇo*), the function of gathering together the co-arisen states, like water poured on bathing powder (*sampiṇḍanaraso nhāniyacuṇṇānaṃ udakaṃ viya*), is manifested as peace (*upasamapaccupaṭṭhāno*) or as knowledge, and has happiness especially as its proximate cause (*visesato sukhapadaṭṭhāno*); it should be seen as the steadiness of the mind, like the steady flame of a lamp in a windless place (As 1191:1-3; the same in Vism-ii 435:2). It is chief (*pāmokkha*) among the states, as the roof peak of a peaked house binds the other materials and all the rafters lean toward it (As 1188:3-5, 1189:1).

The Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha lists *ekaggatā* among the seven universal mental factors (*sabbacittasādhāraṇā*) that arise in every consciousness (Abhidh-s 84:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Yā kho, āvuso visākha, cittassa ekaggatā ayaṃ samādhi; cattāro satipaṭṭhānā samādhinimittā; cattāro sammappadhānā samādhiparikkhārā. Yā tesaṃyeva dhammānaṃ āsevanā bhāvanā bahulīkammaṃ, ayaṃ ettha samādhibhāvanā” ti.**  
> “Whatever is one-pointedness of mind, friend Visākha, that is concentration. The four ways of attending to mindfulness are the causes of concentration. The four right efforts are the accessories to concentration. Whatever repetition, development, and cultivation there is of these same qualities—this is the development of concentration herein.”  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti avaṭṭhiti avisāhāro avikkhepo avisāhaṭamānasatā samatho samādhindriyaṃ samādhibalaṃ sammāsamādhi – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti.**  
> 11. What is unification of mind at that time? Whatever stability of mind (cittassa ṭhiti), firm stability, established stability, non-scattering, non-distraction, non-scatteredness of mind, tranquility, faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, or right concentration exists at that time—this is unification of mind at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs §11, the first wholesome consciousness of the sense sphere) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEuIEthdGFtxIEgdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGNpdHRhc3Nla2FnZ2F0xIEgaG90aT8%3D)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Aṭṭhasālinī)
> **Cittassekaggatāti cittassa ekaggabhāvo; samādhissetaṃ nāmaṃ. Lakkhaṇādīsu panassa aṭṭhakathāyaṃ tāva vuttaṃ – “pāmokkhalakkhaṇo ca samādhi avikkhepalakkhaṇo ca”. Yathā hi kūṭāgārakaṇṇikā sesadabbasambhārānaṃ ābandhanato pamukhā hoti evameva sabbakusaladhammānaṃ samādhicittena ijjhanato sabbesampi tesaṃ dhammānaṃ samādhi pāmokkho hoti. Aparo pana nayo – ayaṃ cittassekaggatāsaṅkhāto samādhi nāma avisāralakkhaṇo vā avikkhepalakkhaṇo vā, sahajātadhammānaṃ, sampiṇḍanaraso nhāniyacuṇṇānaṃ udakaṃ viya, upasamapaccupaṭṭhāno ñāṇapaccupaṭṭhāno vā. “Samāhito yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passatī” ti hi vuttaṃ. Visesato sukhapadaṭṭhāno, nivāte dīpaccīnaṃ ṭhiti viya cetaso ṭhitīti daṭṭhabbo.**  
> One-pointedness of mind (cittassekaggatā) is the state of the mind being one-pointed. This is a name for concentration. But regarding its characteristics and so on, it was stated first in the commentary – ‘‘Concentration has the characteristic of being a chief, and the characteristic of non-distraction.’’ Just as the roof peak of a peaked-roof house indeed becomes the chief due to binding all other materials, even so, because all wholesome states succeed by means of a mind in concentration, concentration is the chief of all those states. But another method: this concentration, called one-pointedness of mind, has the characteristic of non-scattering or the characteristic of non-distraction. Its function is gathering, like water for bathing powders for co-arisen states. It manifests as appeasement or it manifests as knowledge. ‘‘Indeed, one who is composed truly knows and sees,’’ thus it was said. Especially, its proximate cause is happiness. Concentration is the mind's steadfastness, like the steady flame of a lamp in a windless place, thus it should be understood.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As, Cittassekaggatā, As 1188-1191) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=161&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2l0dGFzc2VrYWdnYXTEgXRpIGNpdHRhc3NhIGVrYWdnYWJoxIF2bzs%3D)


### Quote (Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha: Cetasikaparicchedo)
> **Kathaṃ? Phasso vedanā saññā cetanā ekaggatā jīvitindriyaṃ manasikāro ceti sattime cetasikā sabbacittasādhāraṇā nāma.**  
> 2. How? Contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, and attention—these seven mental factors are universal to every consciousness.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Abhidh-s 84, Aññasamānacetasikaṃ) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=7&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=Mi4gS2F0aGHhuYM%2F)
