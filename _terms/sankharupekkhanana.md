### Grammatical Analysis
**Saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of equanimity towards formations. Formed by *saṅkhāra* (formations, conditioned phenomena) + *[upekkhā](/glossary/upekkha/)* (equanimity) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Mūla's own phrase is *saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ*, "knowledge in the equanimities about formations": "the wisdom of desire for deliverance, of reflecting and of composure (*muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā*)" (Paṭis 14, 332).

### Orthodox Definition
In the *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* the ninth knowledge is *saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇa*: it is the wisdom of desire for deliverance (*muñcitukamyatā*), of reflecting (*paṭisaṅkhā*) and of composure (*santiṭṭhanā*) about arising, occurrence, sign, accumulation, rebirth-linking, destination, production, reappearing, birth, old age, sickness, death, sorrow, lamentation and despair (Paṭis 332). It is called by three names, desire for deliverance, contemplation of reflecting (*paṭisaṅkhānupassanā*) and equanimity about formations, which "have one meaning, only the phrasing is different" (Paṭis 1247).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, Saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇakathā) describes how it arises. Having seen the formations as empty and applied the three characteristics, the meditator "abandons both fear and delight and becomes indifferent and neutral towards the formations, not grasping them as 'I' or 'mine', like a man who has given up his wife" (Vism-ii 1778): the man who first could not bear to be without his wife, then saw her fault, desired to be free of her, released her, and ever after was neither angry nor dejected but indifferent (Vism-ii 1779). Then the mind retreats, retracts and recoils from the three existences, like water drops on a lotus leaf, and "equanimity or revulsion becomes established; thus the knowledge of equanimity about formations has arisen in him" (Vism-ii 1780-1781).

The same commentary says that if this knowledge sees Nibbāna, the state of peace, as peaceful, it gives up all occurrence of formations and leaps into Nibbāna; if it does not, it occurs again and again with formations as its object, like the direction-finding crow of the seafaring merchants, which returns to the mast when it sees no shore (Vism-ii 1782). In the Visuddhimagga, insight reaches its culmination (*sikhāppattā*) and becomes conformable to emergence (*vuṭṭhānagāminī*) with the triad of knowledges that begins with this one (Vism-ii 1810); it also determines the special features of the factors of the noble path (Vism-ii 1842).

Uppādaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ, pavattaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ, nimittaṃ muñcitukamyatā … pe … āyūhanaṃ muñcitukamyatā … paṭisandhiṃ muñcitukamyatā … gatiṃ muñcitukamyatā … nibbattiṃ muñcitukamyatā … upapattiṃ muñcitukamyatā … jātiṃ muñcitukamyatā … jaraṃ muñcitukamyatā … byādhiṃ muñcitukamyatā … maraṇaṃ muñcitukamyatā … sokaṃ muñcitukamyatā … paridevaṃ muñcitukamyatā … pe … upāyāsaṃ muñcitukamyatāpaṭisaṅkhāsantiṭṭhanā paññā saṅkhārupekkhāsu ñāṇaṃ.**

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, the three names)
> **Yā ca muccitukamyatā yā ca paṭisaṅkhānupassanā yā ca saṅkhārupekkhā, ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca, udāhu ekatthā, byañjanameva nānanti? Yā ca muccitukamyatā yā ca paṭisaṅkhānupassanā yā ca saṅkhārupekkhā, ime dhammā ekatthā, byañjanameva nānaṃ.**  
> Desire for deliverance, contemplation of reflecting, and equanimity about formations—are these things different in meaning and different in phrasing, or are they one in meaning and only the phrasing is different? Desire for deliverance, contemplation of reflecting, and equanimity about formations—these things have one meaning, and only the phrasing is different.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the direction-finding crow)
> **Taṃ panetaṃ sace santipadaṃ nibbānaṃ santato passati, sabbaṃ saṅkhārappavattaṃ vissajjetvā nibbānameva pakkhandati. No ce nibbānaṃ santato passati, punappunaṃ saṅkhārārammaṇameva hutvā pavattati sāmuddikānaṃ disākāko viya. Sāmuddikā kira vāṇijakā nāvaṃ ārohantā disākākaṃ nāma gaṇhanti, te yadā nāvā vātakkhittā videsaṃ pakkhandati, tīraṃ na paññāyati, tadā disākākaṃ vissajjenti. So kūpakayaṭṭhito ākāsaṃ laṅghitvā sabbā disā ca vidisā ca anugantvā sace tīraṃ passati, tadabhimukhova gacchati. No ce passati, punappunaṃ āgantvā kūpakayaṭṭhiṃyeva allīyati. Evameva sace saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇaṃ santipadaṃ nibbānaṃ santato passati, sabbaṃ saṅkhārappavattaṃ vissajjetvā nibbānameva pakkhandati. No ce passati, punappunaṃ saṅkhārārammaṇameva hutvā pavattati.**  
> 767. Moreover, if it sees the peaceful state of Nibbāna as peaceful, it gives up all occurrence of formations and leaps into Nibbāna alone. If it does not see Nibbāna as peaceful, it occurs again and again having only formations as its object, like the direction-finding crow of seafaring merchants. Seafaring merchants, it is said, when they board a ship, take a crow called ‘direction-finding crow’. When the ship is blown by the wind into unfamiliar territory and the shore is not visible, then they release the direction-finding crow. It flies up from the mast into the sky, and having followed all the directions and intermediate directions, if it sees the shore, it goes directly towards it. If it does not see it, it returns again and again and clings only to the mast. Just so, if the knowledge of equanimity about formations sees the peaceful state of Nibbāna as peaceful, it gives up all occurrence of formations and leaps into Nibbāna alone. If it does not see it, it occurs again and again having only formations as its object.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, Saṅkhārupekkhāñāṇakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=294&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzY3LiBUYeG5gyBwYW5ldGHhuYMgc2FjZSBzYW50aXBhZGHhuYMgbmliYsSBbmHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the culmination of insight)

But for this clansman who has attained equanimity about formations, insight reaches its culmination (sikhāppattā) and becomes conformable to emergence (vuṭṭhānagāminī). ‘Insight that has reached its culmination’ or ‘conformable to emergence’ are names for this same triad of knowledges beginning with equanimity about formations.
