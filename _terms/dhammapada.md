### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhammapada**: [nt.] the Dhamma-verses; the word of Dhamma. Formed by *[dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/)* + *pada* (word, verse, line). The Mūla uses *dhammapada* in the singular for a "word of Dhamma", for example "who will select the well-taught *dhammapada*, as an expert selects a flower?" (Dhp 148:1) and "one *dhammapada* is better, hearing which one is at peace" (Dhp 336:1); and in the plural, as in the *cattāri dhammapadāni* (D-iii 1438:1). As the name of the book, it is the Dhammapadapāḷi (Dhp 3).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Dhammapada* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya; the Paramatthajotikā, listing the Khuddaka Nikāya, places it second after the Khuddakapāṭha (Pj-i 24:5). It has twenty-six chapters (*vagga*), from "1. Yamakavaggo" (Dhp 4) to "26. Brāhmaṇavaggo" (Dhp 1238), and ends: "In the Dhammapada division, taught by the Kinsman of the Sun. The Dhammapada Pāḷi is concluded" (Dhp 1393:1, 1394:1).

The commentary, the Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā, says that the Teacher, "skilled in Dhamma and non-Dhamma", taught the *dhammapada* "based on various reasons" (*taṃ taṃ kāraṇamāgamma*, Dhp-a 13:1, 14:1). It counts "four hundred and twenty-three verses" (*satevīsā catussatā*, Dhp-a 5214:1) with three hundred and five stories (Dhp-a 5211:1, 5215:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammapada)
> **Ko imaṃ pathaviṃ vicessati, yamalokañca imaṃ sadevakaṃ; Ko dhammapadaṃ sudesitaṃ, kusalo pupphamiva pacessati.**  
> Who will conquer this earth, and the realm of Yama, and this world with its gods? Who will select the well-taught path of Dhamma, as an expert selects a flower?  
>

> In the Dhammapada, taught by the Kinsman of the Sun. The Dhammapada Pāli is concluded.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā (introductory verses))
> **Taṃ taṃ kāraṇamāgamma, dhammādhammesu kovido; Sampattasaddhammapado, satthā dhammapadaṃ subhaṃ.**  
> 3. Based on various reasons, skilled in Dhamma and non-Dhamma, the Teacher, who had attained the true state of Dhamma, (taught) the auspicious Dhamma-verses.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā (Nigamanakathā))
> **Pattaṃ dhammapadaṃ yena, dhammarājenanuttaraṃ; Gāthā dhammapade tena, bhāsitā yā mahesinā. Satevīsā catussatā, catusaccavibhāvinā; Satattayañhi vatthūnaṃ, pañcādhikā samuṭṭhitā.**  
> The path of Dhamma which the King of Dhamma attained, that verse in the Dhammapada was spoken by the Great Sage. Four hundred and twenty-three verses were revealed by him who manifested the four truths; surpassing three hundred, the stories arose.  
>  
> — [Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā](/glossary/dhammapadatthakatha/) (Dhp-a, Nigamanakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_02_02&page_number=455&name=dhammapadaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=UGF0dGHhuYMgZGhhbW1hcGFkYeG5gyB5ZW5hLCBkaGFtbWFyxIFqZW5hbnV0dGFyYeG5gzs%3D)


### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Dhammapada* – Dhp 3 "Dhammapadapāḷi", Dhp 4 "1. Yamakavaggo" (verse 1, Dhp 6:1 "Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā"), Dhp 148:1 (verse 44), Dhp 336:1 (verse 102).
* **Canonical**: *Dīgha Nikāya* – D-iii 1438:1, "Cattāri dhammapadāni – anabhijjhā dhammapadaṃ, abyāpādo dhammapadaṃ, sammāsati dhammapadaṃ, sammāsamādhi dhammapadaṃ".
* **Commentary**: *[Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā](/glossary/dhammapadatthakatha/)* – Dhp-a 13-14 and Dhp-a 5211-5215 (305 stories, 423 verses).
