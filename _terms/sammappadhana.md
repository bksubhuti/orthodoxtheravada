### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammappadhāna**: [nt.] Right striving. The Visuddhimagga: "*padahanti etenāti padhānaṃ; sobhanaṃ padhānaṃ sammappadhānaṃ; sammā vā padahanti etenāti sammappadhānaṃ*", "they strive by means of this, so it is *padhāna*; beautiful striving is *sammappadhāna*; or they strive rightly by means of this", and it is "beautiful" because it lacks the deformity of defilements and "striving" because it brings about excellence by producing benefit and happiness (Vism-ii 1920, XXII 820). The Sammohavinodanī: "*kāraṇappadhānā upāyappadhānā yonisopadhānā*", strivings with proper cause, proper means, and wise attention (Vibh-a 1376).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Sammappadhānas* are the four right strivings (*cattāro sammappadhānā*): a bhikkhu generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind and strives (1) for the non-arising of unarisen bad unwholesome states, (2) for the abandoning of arisen bad unwholesome states, (3) for the arising of unarisen wholesome states, and (4) for the endurance, non-confusion, increase, extension, development and fulfilment of arisen wholesome states (SN 49; AN 4.13; Vibh 390).

The Visuddhimagga identifies the essence: "it is just the single energy (*ekameva vīriyaṃ*) that is called the four right strivings, because it accomplishes the functions beginning with non-arising of unarisen states" (Vism-ii 1925, XXII 825). In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī the same energy is listed under the names *vīriyaṃ, vīriyindriyaṃ, vīriyabalaṃ, sammāvāyāmo, vīriyasambojjhaṅgo* (Dhs 302). The Aṭṭhakathā also gives them as a designation for energy (*vīriyassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ*, Nidd-a-i 235), and the Visuddhimagga describes the first striving in the preliminary stage of mundane insight: seeing an unwholesome state arisen in another, one strives that it not arise in oneself, which is the first right striving; seeing one that has become habitual in oneself, one strives to abandon it, which is the second (Vism-ii 1924).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“cattārome, bhikkhave, sammappadhānā. Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Ime kho, bhikkhave, cattāro sammappadhānāti”.**  
> “Mendicants, there are these four right strivings. What four? Here, mendicants, a bhikkhu generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort for the non-arising of bad, unwholesome states that have not yet arisen. He generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort for the abandonment of bad, unwholesome states that have already arisen. He generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort for the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen. He generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort for the endurance, non-confusion, multiplication, extension, development, and fulfilment of wholesome states that have already arisen. These, mendicants, are the four right strivings.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (5. Sammappadhānasaṃyutta, S-v 1462, paras. 651-662) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=214&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIGNhdHTEgXJvbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtbWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, sammappadhānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati;**  
> “Monks, there are these four right strivings (sammappadhāna). What are the four? Here, monks, a bhikkhu generates desire for the non-arising of bad unwholesome states that have not yet arisen; he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 4.13, Padhāna Sutta, A-iv 162) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=322&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTMu4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJpbcSBbmksIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtbWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBbmku)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Cattāro sammappadhānā – idha bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati, uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati.**  
> 390. The four right strivings: Here, a bhikkhu, for the non-arising of bad, unwholesome states that have not yet arisen, generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. For the abandonment of bad, unwholesome states that have already arisen, he generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. For the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen, he generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. For the endurance, for their non-loss, for multiplication, for extension, for development, and for the fulfilment of wholesome states that have already arisen, he generates desire, endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Sammappadhānavibhaṅga, Suttantabhājanīya, para. 390) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=216&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzkwLiBDYXR0xIFybyBzYW1tYXBwYWRoxIFuxIEg4oCTIGlkaGEgYmhpa2todQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the word explained)
> **Padahanti etenāti padhānaṃ. Sobhanaṃ padhānaṃ sammappadhānaṃ. Sammā vā padahanti etenāti sammappadhānaṃ. Sobhanaṃ vā taṃ kilesavirūpattavirahato padhānañca hitasukhanipphādakattena seṭṭhabhāvāvahanato padhānabhāvakāraṇato cāti sammappadhānaṃ.**  
> 820. They strive by means of this, thus it is striving (padhānaṃ). Beautiful striving is right effort (sammappadhānaṃ). Or, they strive rightly (sammā) by means of this, thus it is right effort (sammappadhānaṃ). Or, it is right effort (sammappadhānaṃ) because it is beautiful (sobhanaṃ) due to the absence of the deformity of defilements, and it is striving (padhānaṃ) because it brings about a state of excellence by producing benefit and happiness, and because it is the cause of the state of excellence.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XXII, para. 820) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=319&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODIwLiBQYWRhaGFudGkgZXRlbsSBdGkgcGFkaMSBbmHhuYMu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, one energy in four functions)
> **Evaṃ ekacitte labbhamānesu cetesu ekāva nibbānārammaṇā sati kāyādīsu subhasaññādippahānakiccasādhanavasena cattāro satipaṭṭhānāti vuccati. Ekameva ca vīriyaṃ anuppannānaṃ anuppādādikiccasādhanavasena cattāro sammappadhānāti vuccati.**  
> 825. In this way, among those found in a single consciousness, it is just the single mindfulness having Nibbāna as its object that is called “the four foundations of mindfulness” because it accomplishes the function of abandoning the perceptions of beauty and so on in the body and so on. And it is just the single energy that is called “four right efforts” because it accomplishes the functions beginning with the non-arising of unarisen states.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XXII, para. 825) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=320&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODI1LiBFdmHhuYMgZWthY2l0dGUgbGFiYmhhbcSBbmVzdSBjZXRlc3UgZWvEgXZh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Catunnaṃ sammappadhānānanti padahanti etenāti padhānaṃ, sobhanaṃ padhānaṃ sammappadhānaṃ, sammā vā padahanti etenāti sammappadhānaṃ, sobhanaṃ vā taṃ kilesavirūpattavirahato, padhānañca hitasukhanipphādakaṭṭhena seṭṭhabhāvāvahanato, padhānabhāvakaraṇato cāti sammappadhānaṃ, vīriyassetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Uppannuppannānaṃ anuppannuppannānañca catunnaṃ akusalakusalānaṃ pahānānuppattiuppādaṭṭhitikiccasādhanavasena pavattito panassa catudhā pabhedo hoti, tesaṃ catunnaṃ sammappadhānānaṃ.**  
> Catunnaṃ sammappadhānānan: 'Padhāna' means they exert through this; noble exertion is 'sammappadhāna', or because they exert rightly through this; it is noble due to the lack of defiled forms, and 'padhāna' carries the highest meaning in terms of producing welfare and happiness, and leads to the state of exertion, hence it is 'sammappadhāna'; this is a synonym for energy. It has a fourfold distinction due to achieving the tasks of abandoning, non-arising, arising, and maintaining the four unwholesome and wholesome states, arisen and not arisen; of those four right efforts.  
>  
> — Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā (Nidd-a-i 235) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_16&page_number=56&name=mah%C4%81niddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dW5uYeG5gyBzYW1tYXBwYWRoxIFuxIFuYW50aSBwYWRhaGFudGkgZXRlbsSBdGkgcGFkaMSBbmHhuYMsIHNvYmhhbmHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sammohavinodanī)
> **Sammappadhānāti kāraṇappadhānā upāyappadhānā yonisopadhānā.**  
> Right strivings (sammappadhānā) means strivings with proper cause (kāraṇappadhānā), strivings with proper means (upāyappadhānā), and strivings with wise attention (yonisopadhānā).  
>  
> — Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā (Sammohavinodanī, Sammappadhānavibhaṅga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_02&page_number=276&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2FtbWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBdGkga8SBcmHhuYdhcHBhZGjEgW7EgSB1cMSBeWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBIHlvbmlzb3BhZGjEgW7EgS4%3D)
