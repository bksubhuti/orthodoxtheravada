### Grammatical Analysis
**Akusala**: [nt.; adj.] unwholesome; unskillful; blameworthy; unhealthy; kammically destructive. Formed by the negative prefix *a* (not) + *[kusala](/glossary/kusala/)* (wholesome/skillful).

### Orthodox Definition
*Akusala* designates any consciousness-moment or mental factor that is ethically compromised. The Atthasālinī analyses the word as *na kusalā akusalā*, "not wholesome, hence unwholesome", the opponents of the wholesome as enemies are of friends (As 846:11-12), and distinguishes the three classes by their mark: the wholesome have the characteristic of blameless, pleasant result, the unwholesome that of blameworthy (*sāvajja*) painful result (*dukkhavipāka*), the indeterminate that of no result (As 846:14). In the Sammādiṭṭhi Sutta, Sāriputta states what the unwholesome is: killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct, false, malicious and harsh speech, frivolous talk, covetousness, ill will and wrong view (M-i 241:3), and its roots are greed, hatred and delusion (M-i 241:5). It represents the exact psychological antithesis of *[kusala](/glossary/kusala/)*.

According to orthodox psychology, akusala consciousness is inherently diseased and unstable. It is driven directly by the three unwholesome roots (*[akusala-mūla](/glossary/akusala-mula/)*): greed (*[lobha](/glossary/lobha/)*), hatred (*[dosa](/glossary/dosa/)*), and delusion (*[moha](/glossary/moha/)*). 

The [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) lists **twelve unwholesome states** ("Dvādasa akusalāni", Dhs 866:1); the Visuddhimagga names them as the **twelve unwholesome cittas** (Vism-ii 1964:1): eight rooted in greed (*lobhamūla-citta*), two rooted in hatred (*dosamūla-citta*), and two rooted in sheer delusion (*mohamūla-citta*). These twelve states are responsible for all ten pathways of harmful action (*akusala-kammapatha*), and the Visuddhimagga also lists the ten unwholesome courses of action (*akusalakammapathā*, Vism-ii 1963:1).

### Quote
> **Na kusalā akusalā. Mittapaṭipakkhā amittā viya, lobhādipaṭipakkhā alobhādayo viya ca, kusalapaṭipakkhāti attho. Na byākatāti abyākatā , kusalākusalabhāvena akathitāti attho. Tesu pana anavajjasukhavipākalakkhaṇā kusalā, sāvajjadukkhavipākalakkhaṇā akusalā, avipākalakkhaṇā abyākatā.**  
> Not wholesome, they are akusala (unwholesome). The meaning is: they are opponents of wholesome, just as enemies are opponents of friends, and non-greed and so on are opponents of greed and so on. Not declared, they are abyākata (indeterminate); the meaning is that they are not declared as wholesome or unwholesome. But among these, those having the characteristic of blameless and pleasant result are wholesome; those having the characteristic of faulty (sāvajja) and painful result (dukkhavipāka) are unwholesome; and those having the characteristic of no result (avipāka) are indeterminate.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Nidānakathā to the Cittuppādakaṇḍa, on kusalā, akusalā, abyākatā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=81&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TmEga3VzYWzEgSBha3VzYWzEgS4%3D)
