### Grammatical Analysis
**Upekkhā**: [f.] equanimity; neutrality; looking on. From *upekkhati*, "looks on, is even-minded". The Visuddhimagga explains it for the fourth brahmavihāra: "by abandoning the effort of 'may they be free from enmity' and so on, and by attaining the state of neutrality, it looks on (*upekkhati*), thus it is *upekkhā*" (Vism-i 1939:7). The Mūla's synonyms: "upekkhā upekkhanā ajjhupekkhanā majjhattatā cittassa" (Vibh 1745:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Upekkhā* operates in several layers. The Atthasālinī says that upekkhā is of ten kinds: six-factored equanimity (*chaḷaṅgupekkhā*), equanimity as a divine abiding (*brahmavihārupekkhā*), as a factor of awakening (*bojjhaṅgupekkhā*), of energy (*vīriyupekkhā*), about formations (*saṅkhārupekkhā*), as feeling (*vedanupekkhā*), about insight (*vipassanupekkhā*), of specific neutrality (*tatramajjhattupekkhā*), of jhāna (*jhānupekkhā*) and purifying equanimity (*pārisuddhupekkhā*) (As 1440:1). Among them:

1. **As feeling (*vedanupekkhā*):** the neutral feeling, "neither ease nor disagreeableness, neither painful nor pleasant" (*neva sātaṃ nāsātaṃ ... adukkhamasukhaṃ vedayitaṃ*, Dhs 565:2, §152); the Atthasālinī: "the equanimity called neither painful nor pleasant" in a sense-sphere wholesome consciousness "conjoined with equanimity" (As 1446:1).
2. **As the mental factor *tatramajjhattatā*:** "equanimity, looking on with equanimity, closely looking on, neutrality of mind (*majjhattatā cittassa*)" (Vibh 1745:2; as a factor of awakening, Vibh 1550:2). The Vibhāvinī gives its characteristic: it looks on at the associated consciousness and mental factors that proceed evenly, like a charioteer looking on at horses running evenly (*ajjhupekkhanalakkhaṇā samappavattānaṃ assānaṃ ajjhupekkhako sārathi viya*, Abh-s-t 1053:8). The Atthasālinī places "equanimity of specific neutrality" among the factors that arise with desire and the like and are able to exert evenly on co-arisen phenomena (As 1448:1).
3. **As the fourth brahmavihāra:** in the Mūla, "abides pervading one quarter with a mind accompanied by equanimity (*upekkhāsahagatena cetasā*)" (AN 3.66; also DN and MN stock phrase). The Visuddhimagga gives its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: "characteristic, proceeding as neutrality towards beings; function, seeing equality in beings; manifestation, the quieting of resentment and approval; proximate cause, seeing beings as owners of their kamma ('Beings have each their own kamma: by whose preference will they be happy ...?')" (*kammassakā sattā*, Vism IX, Vism-i 1940:7). Its accomplishment is the quieting of aversion and attachment, its ruin the arising of the equanimity of unknowing based on household life (*gehasitā aññāṇupekkhā*, Vism-i 1940:8).

The near enemy of the brahmavihāra upekkhā is that equanimity of unknowing based on household life (*gehasitā aññāṇupekkhā*), because both share the nature of not considering faults and virtues; the far enemies are lust and aversion (*rāgapaṭighā*), because of complete dissimilarity (Vism-i 1946:2-3). Hence "it is impossible that one should be equanimous and yet be attached or resentful" (Vism-i 1946:5).

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga, the definition)
> **“Upekkhako” ti tattha katamā upekkhā? Yā upekkhā upekkhanā ajjhupekkhanā majjhattatā cittassa – ayaṃ vuccati “upekkhā”.**  
> 584. “Equanimous”—which equanimity is there? Whatever equanimity, looking on with equanimity, closely looking on with equanimity, or neutrality of mind—this is called “equanimity.”  
>  
> — *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Jhānavibhaṅga, §584) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=269&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NTg0LuKAmOKAmCBVcGVra2hha28g4oCZ4oCZIHRpIHRhdHRoYSBrYXRhbcSB)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, the brahmavihāra)
> **Karuṇāsahagatena cetasā … pe … muditāsahagatena cetasā … pe … upekkhāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati, tathā dutiyaṃ, tathā tatiyaṃ, tathā catutthaṃ, iti uddhamadho tiriyaṃ sabbadhi sabbattatāya sabbāvantaṃ lokaṃ upekkhāsahagatena cetasā vipulena mahaggatena appamāṇena averena abyāpajjhena pharitvā viharati.**  
> He abides pervading one quarter with a mind with thoughts of Pity … with a mind with thoughts of Sympathy … with a mind with thoughts of Equanimity, likewise the second, likewise the third, and likewise the fourth. Thus above, below, around, and everywhere, and to all as to himself, he abides pervading the all-encompassing world with a mind with thoughts of Equanimity, far-reaching, grown great, and beyond measure, without enmity, without ill will.  
>  
> — *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (AN 3.66, Kesamutti Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_03&page_number=192&name=tikanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=S2FydeG5h8SBc2FoYWdhdGVuYSBjZXRhc8SBIOKApiBwZSDigKYgbXVkaXTEgXNhaGFnYXRlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, neutral feeling)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti? Yaṃ tasmiṃ samaye tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajaṃ cetasikaṃ neva sātaṃ nāsātaṃ cetosamphassajaṃ adukkhamasukhaṃ vedayitaṃ cetosamphassajā adukkhamasukhā vedanā – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vedanā hoti … pe ….**  
> 152. What is feeling at that time? What at that time is the mental, neither ease nor disagreeableness, neither-pain-nor-pleasure feeling born of contact with the mind-consciousness element appropriate to it and born of mind-contact, the neither-painful-nor-pleasant feeling born of mind-contact—this is feeling at that time...  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§152) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=41&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTUyLiBLYXRhbcSBIHRhc21p4bmDIHNhbWF5ZSB2ZWRhbsSBIGhvdGk%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī, the ten kinds)
> **Upekkhā pana dasavidhā hoti – chaḷaṅgupekkhā brahmavihārupekkhā bojjhaṅgupekkhā vīriyupekkhā saṅkhārupekkhā vedanupekkhā vipassanupekkhā tatramajjhattupekkhā jhānupekkhā pārisuddhupekkhāti.**  
> But equanimity is of ten kinds: six-factored equanimity, equanimity as a divine abiding, equanimity as a factor of awakening, equanimity of energy, equanimity about formations, equanimity as feeling, equanimity about insight, equanimity of specific neutrality, equanimity of jhāna, and purifying equanimity.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Upekkhā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=216&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VXBla2toxIEgcGFuYSBkYXNhdmlkaMSBIGhvdGkg4oCTIGNoYeG4t2HhuYVndXBla2toxIE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the brahmavihāra)
> **Sattesu majjhattākārappavattilakkhaṇā upekkhā , sattesu samabhāvadassanarasā, paṭighānunayavūpasamapaccupaṭṭhānā,“kammassakā sattā, te kassa ruciyā sukhitā vā bhavissanti, dukkhato vā muccissanti, pattasampattito vā na parihāyissantī” ti evaṃ pavattakammassakatādassanapadaṭṭhānā. Paṭighānunayavūpasamo tassā sampatti, gehasitāya aññāṇupekkhāya sambhavo vipatti.**  
> Equanimity (upekkhā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of neutrality towards beings; its function is seeing equality in beings; it is manifested as the quieting of resentment and approval; and its proximate cause is seeing the ownership of deeds occurring thus: ‘Beings have each their own kamma; by whose preference will they become happy, or get free from suffering, or not fall away from the success they have reached?’ The quieting of resentment and approval is its success (sampatti), and the arising of the equanimity of unknowing based on the household life is its failure (vipatti).  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IX, Brahmavihāraniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=311&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2F0dGVzdSBtYWpqaGF0dMSBa8SBcmFwcGF2YXR0aWxha2toYeG5h8SBIHVwZWtraMSBICwgc2F0dGVzdSBzYW1hYmjEgXZhZGFzc2FuYXJhc8SBLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, near and far enemies)
> **Tasmā sā upekkhā gehasitāti vuccatī” tiādinā (ma. ni.3.308) nayena āgatā gehasitā aññāṇupekkhā dosaguṇāvicāraṇavasena sabhāgattā āsannapaccatthikā. Sabhāgavisabhāgatāya rāgapaṭighā dūrapaccatthikā.**  
> ‘Therefore, that equanimity is called based on the household life.’ This equanimity of unknowing based on the household life is the near enemy because both share a similar nature in not considering faults and virtues. Lust and aversion are its far enemies because of their complete dissimilarity.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IX, Brahmavihāraniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=313&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzbcSBIHPEgSB1cGVra2jEgSBnZWhhc2l0xIF0aSB2dWNjYXTEqyDigJnigJk%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī, tatramajjhattatā)
> **Tesu dhammesu majjhattatā tatramajjhattatā , sā cittacetasikānaṃ ajjhupekkhanalakkhaṇā samappavattānaṃ assānaṃ ajjhupekkhako sārathi viya.**  
> Neutrality among those phenomena is called neutrality of mind (tatramajjhattatā). It is characterized by looking on (ajjhupekkhanalakkhaṇā) with equanimity at consciousness and mental factors, like a charioteer looking on at horses progressing evenly.  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* (on Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha II §6, the beautiful factors) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=110&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGVzdSBkaGFtbWVzdSBtYWpqaGF0dGF0xIEgdGF0cmFtYWpqaGF0dGF0xIEgLCBzxIE%3D)
