### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭipatti**: [f.] Practice; conduct. The Aṭṭhakathā derives it: “lokuttaradhammato pubbabhāgo tadatthaṃ paṭipajjīyatīti paṭipatti”, the part that comes before the supramundane Dhamma, which is practised for the sake of that Dhamma (Nidd-a-i 1017:1). Related Mūla terms: *sammāpaṭipadā, anulomapaṭipadā, dhammānudhammapaṭipadā* (Nidd-i 759:2), *dhammānudhammappaṭipanno* (A-viii 300:9).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paṭipatti* is the second of the three kinds of the good Dhamma (*saddhamma*), after *pariyatti* and before *adhigama*. The Samantapāsādikā defines it: “terasa dhutaguṇā, cuddasa khandhakavattāni, dveasīti mahāvattāni, sīlasamādhivipassanāti ayaṃ paṭipattisaddhammo nāma”, the thirteen *dhutaguṇas*, the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, and virtue, concentration and insight (Sp-i 932:3). The Mahāniddesa gives the *paṭipattisāsana* as: right practice, practice in conformity, without opposition, in accordance with the meaning, practice in accordance with the Dhamma, fullness in virtues, guarding the sense doors, moderation in eating, devotion to wakefulness, mindfulness and clear comprehension, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of success, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of awakening, and the Noble Eightfold Path (Nidd-i 759:2).

The Mūla contrasts learning and practice in the Dhammapada: one who recites much scripture but is heedless is like a cowherd counting others' cows (Dhp 19, Dhp 64:1-65:1), while one who recites little but lives in conformity with the Dhamma, abandoning lust, hatred and delusion, shares in the fruits of the monastic life (Dhp 20, Dhp 67:1-69:1). The commentary on *dhammānudhammappaṭipanno* says it means one who has entered the preliminary practice (*pubbabhāgapaṭipadā*) together with virtue, in line with the nine supramundane dhammas (Mp-iii 452:5).

On how the three relate and last, see *[pariyatti](/glossary/pariyatti/)*: the Papañcasūdanī says that *paṭivedha* and *paṭipatti* sometimes exist and sometimes do not, and that *pariyatti* is the measure for the lasting of the dispensation (Ps-iii 313:9).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāniddesa)
> **Katamaṃ paṭipattisāsanaṃ? Sammāpaṭipadā anulomapaṭipadā apaccanīkapaṭipadā anvatthapaṭipadā dhammānudhammapaṭipadā sīlesu paripūrakāritā indriyesu guttadvāratā bhojane mattaññutā jāgariyānuyogo satisampajaññaṃ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā cattāro sammappadhānā cattāro iddhipādā pañcindriyāni pañca balāni satta bojjhaṅgā ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo – idaṃ paṭipattisāsanaṃ.**  
> What is the teaching of practice? Right practice, the way of practice, practice not in conflict, practice in accordance with the goal, practice in accordance with the Dhamma, fulfilling the precepts, guardedness of sense-doors, moderation in food, devotion to wakefulness, mindfulness and clear comprehension, the four ways of attending to mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases for psychic potency, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, and the noble eight-factored path—this is the teaching of practice.  
>  
> — [Khuddaka Nikāya](/glossary/khuddaka-nikaya/), Mahāniddesa (7. Tissametteyyasuttaniddeso, on Sn 821 “mussate vāpi sāsanaṃ”) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_16&page_number=111&name=mah%C4%81niddesap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1h4bmDIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc8SBc2FuYeG5gz8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammapada)
> **Appampi ce saṃhita bhāsamāno, dhammassa hoti anudhammacārī; Rāgañca dosañca pahāya mohaṃ, sammappajāno suvimuttacitto; Anupādiyāno idha vā huraṃ vā, sa bhāgavā sāmaññassa hoti.**  
> Even if one recites little scripture, but lives in conformity with the Teaching, having abandoned lust, ill will, and delusion, with right knowledge and a well-liberated mind, not clinging here or hereafter, he shares in the fruits of monastic life.  
>  
> — [Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/) (Dhp 20, Dhammapada Yamakavagga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_02&page_number=16&name=dhammapadap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjAuIEFwcGFtcGkgY2Ugc2HhuYNoaXRhIGJoxIFzYW3EgW5vLCBkaGFtbWFzc2E%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Katamaṃ paṭipattisāsananti lokuttaradhammato pubbabhāgo tadatthaṃ paṭipajjīyatīti paṭipatti. Sāsīyanti ettha veneyyāti sāsanaṃ.**  
> “What is the teaching of practice?” It is the preliminary stage to the supramundane path; it is called “practice” because one proceeds for that goal. It is “teaching” because trainees are instructed in it.  
>  
> — [Saddhammapajjotikā](/glossary/saddhammapajjotika/) (Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā, on the passage above) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_16&page_number=234&name=mah%C4%81niddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1h4bmDIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc8SBc2FuYW50aSBsb2t1dHRhcmFkaGFtbWF0byBwdWJiYWJoxIFnbyB0YWRhdHRoYeG5gyBwYeG5rWlwYWpqxKt5YXTEq3Rp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Manorathapūraṇī)
> **Dhammānudhammappaṭipannoti navalokuttaradhammassa anudhammaṃ saha sīlena pubbabhāgapaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno.**  
> “Practising the Teaching in accordance with the Teaching” means practising the preliminary path together with virtue, in accordance with the nine supramundane states.  
>  
> — [Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/) (on dhammānudhammappaṭipanno) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_an_02&page_number=98&name=duk%C4%81dinip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RGhhbW3EgW51ZGhhbW1hcHBh4bmtaXBhbm5vdGkgbmF2YWxva3V0dGFyYWRoYW1tYXNzYSBhbnVkaGFtbWHhuYMgc2FoYSBzxKtsZW5hIHB1YmJhYmjEgWdhcGHhua1pcGFkYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Saddhammaṭṭhitiyāti tividho saddhammo – pariyattisaddhammo, paṭipattisaddhammo, adhigamasaddhammoti. Terasa dhutaguṇā, cuddasa khandhakavattāni, dveasīti mahāvattāni, sīlasamādhivipassanāti ayaṃ “paṭipattisaddhammo” nāma.**  
> For the continuation of the true Dhamma (saddhammaṭṭhitiyā) means: the true Dhamma is threefold—the true Dhamma of scripture, the true Dhamma of practice, and the true Dhamma of attainment. The thirteen ascetic practices, the fourteen duties of the Khandhakas, the eighty-two great duties, virtue, concentration, and insight are called the “true Dhamma of practice.”  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Pārājikakaṇḍa-aṭṭhakathā, on “saddhammaṭṭhitiyā”) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=190&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FkZGhhbW1h4bmt4bmtaGl0aXnEgXRpIHRpdmlkaG8gc2FkZGhhbW1vIOKAkyBwYXJpeWF0dGlzYWRkaGFtbW8sIHBh4bmtaXBhdHRpc2FkZGhhbW1vLA%3D%3D)
