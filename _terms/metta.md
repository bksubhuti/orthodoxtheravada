### Grammatical Analysis
**Mettā**: [f.] loving-kindness; friendliness. The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IX, 262-263) gives the derivation: *mejjatīti mettā, siniyhatīti attho* ("it cherishes, thus it is mettā; it is affectionate, is the meaning"), and also *mitte vā bhavā, mittassa vā esā pavattītipi mettā* ("because it arises towards a friend, or because it is the behaviour of a friend, it is mettā").

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla, *mettā* is defined in the Vibhaṅga as "whatever loving-kindness in beings, the manner of loving-kindness, the state of being endowed with loving-kindness, the liberation of the mind by loving-kindness". The Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists *mettāyanā* and *mettāyitattaṃ* among the terms that define non-hatred (*adoso*), "a root of the wholesome" (*kusalamūlaṃ*); so *mettā* is the wholesome root non-hatred acting towards beings. The Mettā Sutta (Sn 1.8) has the monk wish "may all beings be happy and safe" and then cultivate "a boundless heart of loving-kindness towards the entire world".

*Mettā* is the first of the four *[brahmavihāras](/glossary/brahmavihara/)* (Vibh, Appamaññāvibhaṅga: *catasso appamaññāyo*). The *Visuddhimagga* gives its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause:
* **Lakkhaṇa** (Characteristic): promoting the aspect of welfare (*hitākārappavattilakkhaṇā*).
* **Rasa** (Function): presenting welfare (*hitūpasaṃhārarasā*).
* **Paccupaṭṭhāna** (Manifestation): the removal of resentment (*āghātavinayapaccupaṭṭhānā*).
* **Padaṭṭhāna** (Proximate Cause): seeing the lovable aspect of beings (*sattānaṃ manāpabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā*).

The *Visuddhimagga* also says that *mettā* has the purpose of warding off ill will (*byāpādapaṭighātappayojanā*), and that it has two enemies: the near enemy is lust (*rāgo*), which shares with it the seeing of virtues, and the far enemy is ill will (*byāpādo*), which is its complete opposite. Therefore *mettā* should be well protected from lust, and one can practise it without fear of ill will, since it is impossible to practise *mettā* and yet harbour anger.

### Quote (Mūla: Suttanipāta)
> **Mātā yathā niyaṃ puttamāyusā ekaputtamanurakkhe; Evampi sabbabhūtesu, mānasaṃ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṃ. 150. Mettañca sabbalokasmi, mānasaṃ bhāvaye aparimāṇaṃ; Uddhaṃ adho ca tiriyañca, asambādhaṃ averamasapattaṃ.**  
> 149. Just as a mother would protect her only child with her life, Even so, let one cultivate a boundless heart towards all beings. 150. Let one cultivate a boundless heart of loving-kindness towards the entire world— Above, below, and across—unhindered, without ill will, without enmity.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Kathañca bhikkhu mettāsahagatena cetasā ekaṃ disaṃ pharitvā viharati? Seyyathāpi nāma ekaṃ puggalaṃ piyaṃ manāpaṃ disvā mettāyeyya, evameva sabbe satte mettāya pharati. Tattha katamā mettā? Yā sattesu metti mettāyanā mettāyitattaṃ mettācetovimutti – ayaṃ vuccati “mettā”.**  
> 643. And how does a bhikkhu abide pervading one quarter with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness? Just as one would extend loving-kindness on seeing a dear, pleasant person, just so does he pervade all beings with loving-kindness. Therein, what is loving-kindness? Whatever loving-kindness in beings, the manner of loving-kindness, the state of being endowed with loving-kindness, the liberation of the mind by loving-kindness – this is called loving-kindness (mettā).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Appamaññāvibhaṅga, Mettā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=284&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjQzLiBLYXRoYcOxY2EgYmhpa2todSBtZXR0xIFzYWhhZ2F0ZW5hIGNldGFzxIEgZWth4bmD)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamo adoso? Yo adoso adussanā adussitattaṃ metti mettāyanā mettāyitattaṃ anuddā anuddāyanā anudāyitattaṃ hitesitā anukampā abyāpādo abyāpajjo adoso kusalamūlaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati adoso.**  
> 1062. Among these, what is non-hatred? Whatever is non-hatred, non-hatefulness, the state of not being hateful, friendship, the manner of loving-kindness (mettāyanā), the state of being endowed with loving-kindness (mettāyitattaṃ), compassion (anuddā), the manner of compassion (anuddāyanā), the state of being compassionate (anudāyitattaṃ), wishing for welfare (hitesitā), sympathy, non-ill will, unafflicted, non-hatred, a root of the wholesome — this is called non-hatred.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1062, adosa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=215&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTA2Mi4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyBhZG9zbz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, meaning and characteristics)
> **Etāsu hi mettākaruṇāmuditāupekkhāsu atthato tāva mejjatīti mettā , siniyhatīti attho. Mitte vā bhavā, mittassa vā esā pavattītipi mettā. 263. Lakkhaṇādito panettha hitākārappavattilakkhaṇā mettā , hitūpasaṃhārarasā, āghātavinayapaccupaṭṭhānā, sattānaṃ manāpabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā.**  
> Among these, namely loving-kindness, compassion, altruistic joy, and equanimity, first as to the meaning (atthato): it cherishes (mejjati), thus it is loving-kindness (mettā); it is affectionate (siniyhati) is the meaning. Or because it arises with respect to a friend, or because it is the behaviour towards a friend, it is loving-kindness (mettā). As to the characteristics and so forth (lakkhaṇādito) here, loving-kindness (mettā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of welfare; its function is presenting welfare; it is manifested as the removal of resentment; and its proximate cause is seeing the lovable aspect of beings.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IX, 262-263) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=311&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RXTEgXN1IGhpIG1ldHTEgWthcnXhuYfEgW11ZGl0xIF1cGVra2jEgXN1IGF0dGhhdG8gdMSBdmEgbWVqamF0xKt0aQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, near and far enemy)
> **Ekekassa cettha āsannadūravasena dve dve paccatthikā . Mettābrahmavihārassa hi samīpacāro viya purisassa sapatto guṇadassanasabhāgatāya rāgo āsannapaccatthiko, so lahuṃ otāraṃ labhati, tasmā tato suṭṭhu mettā rakkhitabbā. Pabbatādigahananissito viya purisassa sapatto sabhāgavisabhāgatāya byāpādo dūrapaccatthiko, tasmā tato nibbhayena mettāyitabbaṃ. Mettāyissati ca nāma, kopañca karissatīti aṭṭhānametaṃ.**  
> And here, each one of these has two enemies, by way of the near and the far. For the near enemy of the divine abiding of loving-kindness is lust, since both share in seeing virtues, like an enemy who keeps close to a person; it easily finds an opportunity. Therefore, loving-kindness should be well protected from it. Ill will is its far enemy, like an enemy relying on a mountain thicket, because of their complete dissimilarity. Therefore, one should practice loving-kindness with no fear of it. Indeed, it is impossible that one should practice loving-kindness and yet harbor anger.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IX, 265) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=312&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MjY1LiBFa2VrYXNzYSBjZXR0aGEgxIFzYW5uYWTFq3JhdmFzZW5hIGR2ZSBkdmU%3D)
