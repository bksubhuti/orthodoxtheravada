### Grammatical Analysis
**Vevaṇṇiya**: [nt.] the state of being changed in colour or caste, as of one gone forth. The Manorathapūraṇī glosses it *vivaṇṇabhāvaṃ* ("the state of being without colour / caste"); TPR's translation renders it "castelessness".

### Orthodox Definition
*Vevaṇṇiya* is the first of the ten things on which one gone forth (*pabbajita*) should reflect often, in the Pabbajitaabhiṇha Sutta (AN 10.48): "*Vevaṇṇiyamhi ajjhupagato*" ("I have entered the state of vevaṇṇiya"). The other nine begin with "my life is dependent on others".

The Manorathapūraṇī explains that this state is twofold: *sarīravevaṇṇiya*, of the body, understood through shaving the hair and beard; and *parikkhāravevaṇṇiya*, of the requisites. For the second it contrasts the life before going forth (fine clothes, choice food in gold and silver vessels, superior beds, ghee and butter as medicine) with the life after: robes cut and sewn together and dyed, mixed rice in an iron or clay bowl, a bed of grass at the root of a tree, and fermented urine as medicine. For one who reflects thus, it says, anger (*kopo*) and conceit (*māno*) are abandoned.

### Quote
> **“Dasayime, bhikkhave, dhammā pabbajitena abhiṇhaṃ paccavekkhitabbā. Katame dasa? ‘ Vevaṇṇiyamhi ajjhupagato ’ ti pabbajitena abhiṇhaṃ paccavekkhitabbaṃ;**  
> “Monks, there are these ten qualities that a person gone forth should reflect on often. What ten? ‘I have become casteless’—a person gone forth should reflect on this often.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.48, Pabbajitaabhiṇha Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_10&page_number=325&name=dasakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDgu4oCY4oCYIERhc2F5aW1lLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGRoYW1txIEgcGFiYmFqaXRlbmEgYWJoaeG5h2hh4bmD)

> **Aṭṭhame pabbajitenāti gharāvāsaṃ pahāya sāsane pabbajjaṃ upagatena. Abhiṇhanti abhikkhaṇaṃ punappunaṃ, paccavekkhitabbā oloketabbā sallakkhetabbā. Vevaṇṇiyanti vivaṇṇabhāvaṃ. Taṃ panetaṃ vevaṇṇiyaṃ duvidhaṃ hoti sarīravevaṇṇiyaṃ parikkhāravevaṇṇiyañca. Tattha kesamassuoropanena sarīravevaṇṇiyaṃ veditabbaṃ. Evamettha parikkhāravevaṇṇiyaṃ veditabbaṃ. Evaṃ paccavekkhato kopo ca māno ca pahīyati.**  
> 48. In the eighth, 'a person gone forth' (pabbajitena) means one who has abandoned the household life and entered the going forth in the Dispensation. 'Often' (abhiṇhaṃ) means frequently, again and again; 'should reflect on' (paccavekkhitabbā) means should observe and consider. 'Castelessness' (vevaṇṇiyaṃ) means the state of being without a caste. This state of castelessness is twofold: castelessness of the body and castelessness of the requisites. Of these, castelessness of the body should be understood through the shaving of hair and beard. In this way, castelessness of the requisites should be understood here. For one who reflects in this way, hatred and conceit are abandoned.  
>  
> — [Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/) (Aṅguttara-aṭṭhakathā, Dasakanipāta, on AN 10.48) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_an_04&page_number=313&name=a%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhak%C4%81dinip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NDguIEHhua3hua1oYW1lIHBhYmJhaml0ZW7EgXRpIGdoYXLEgXbEgXNh4bmDIHBhaMSBeWEgc8SBc2FuZQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Pabbajitaabhiṇha Sutta* (AN 10.48, A-x 362–363) – the ten subjects of frequent reflection for one gone forth, the first being *vevaṇṇiyamhi ajjhupagato*. The same line recurs in the Nettippakaraṇa (Nett 1567:3) and in the AN 10 sutta on the three perceptions of a samaṇa (A-x 968:3).
* **Commentary**: *[Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/)* (Mp-x 540) – the twofold *vevaṇṇiya*, of body and of requisites. *Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā* (Th-a-i 3580:1) uses *vevaṇṇiyaṃ ajjhupagato* of one with shaven head and patched robes.
