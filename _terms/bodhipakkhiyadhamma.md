### Grammatical Analysis
**Bodhipakkhiyadhamma**: [m.] factors partaking of awakening, a compound of *bodhi* (awakening) + *pakkhiya* (belonging to the side of) + *dhamma*. The Visuddhimagga explains: these thirty-seven "are called *bodhipakkhiyā* because they exist on the side (*pakkhe bhavattā*) of the noble path, which has received the name *bodha* in the sense of awakening (*bujjhanaṭṭhena*)"; "*pakkhe bhavattā*" means "because they stand in a state of assistance (*upakārabhāve ṭhitattā*)" (Vism-ii 1918:2-3).

### Orthodox Definition
The *bodhipakkhiyadhammā* are thirty-seven in number. In the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, at Vesālī, the Buddha had the monks assembled in the Attendance Hall and said: "whatever teachings I have taught with direct knowledge, you should learn well and practise, develop and make much of, so that the spiritual life may last long, for the benefit of many", and named them: the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of awakening and the noble eightfold path (D-ii 466:1-467:7). The same list is given in the Kinti Sutta, with the instruction to train in them in concord (M-iii 69:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā counts these seven sets as the thirty-seven *bodhipakkhiyadhammā* (Paṭis-a 1999:4) and says they are taught as both mundane and supramundane (Ps-iii 69:1; Nidd-a-i 1282:6). The Vinaya commentary says that, by the Suttanta method, the thirty-seven are *dhamma*, while the contrary sets (three foundations of mindfulness, three strivings, ... a nine-factored path, the four graspings, five hindrances, seven underlying tendencies, eight wrongnesses) are *adhamma* (Sp-iv 522:2-3).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Nisajja kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi – “tasmātiha, bhikkhave, ye te mayā dhammā abhiññā desitā, te vo sādhukaṃ uggahetvā āsevitabbā bhāvetabbā bahulīkātabbā, yathayidaṃ brahmacariyaṃ addhaniyaṃ assa ciraṭṭhitikaṃ, tadassa bahujanahitāya bahujanasukhāya lokānukampāya atthāya hitāya sukhāya devamanussānaṃ. Katame ca te, bhikkhave, dhammā mayā abhiññā desitā, ye vo sādhukaṃ uggahetvā āsevitabbā bhāvetabbā bahulīkātabbā, yathayidaṃ brahmacariyaṃ addhaniyaṃ assa ciraṭṭhitikaṃ, tadassa bahujanahitāya bahujanasukhāya lokānukampāya atthāya hitāya sukhāya devamanussānaṃ. Seyyathidaṃ – cattāro satipaṭṭhānā cattāro sammappadhānā cattāro iddhipādā pañcindriyāni pañca balāni satta bojjhaṅgā ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo. Ime kho te, bhikkhave, dhammā mayā abhiññā desitā, ye vo sādhukaṃ uggahetvā āsevitabbā bhāvetabbā bahulīkātabbā, yathayidaṃ brahmacariyaṃ addhaniyaṃ assa ciraṭṭhitikaṃ, tadassa bahujanahitāya bahujanasukhāya lokānukampāya atthāya hitāya sukhāya devamanussānan” ti.**  
> While sitting the Gracious One addressed the monks, saying: “Therefore, monks, whatever Teachings have, with deep knowledge, been taught by me, after grasping them well, you should practise, develop, and make a lot of them, so that the Spiritual Life may last long, and may endure for a long time, and that will be for the benefit of many people, for the happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, for the welfare, benefit, and happiness of Divinities and men. And what are those Teachings that have, with deep knowledge, been taught by me, which after grasping them well, you should practise, develop, and make a lot of them, so that the Spiritual Life may last long, and may endure for a long time, that will be for the benefit of many people, for the happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, for the welfare, benefit, and happiness of Divinities and men? They are as follows: the four ways of attending to mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four paths to power, the five faculties, the five strengths, the seven factors of awakening, and the noble eightfold path. These, monks, are those Teachings that have, with deep knowledge, been taught by me, after grasping them well, you should practise, develop, and make a lot of them, so that the Spiritual Life may last long, and may endure for a long time, and that will be for the benefit of many people, for the happiness of many people, out of compassion for the world, for the welfare, benefit, and happiness of Divinities and men.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Tasmātiha, bhikkhave, ye vo mayā dhammā abhiññā desitā, seyyathidaṃ – cattāro satipaṭṭhānā cattāro sammappadhānā cattāro iddhipādā pañcindriyāni pañca balāni satta bojjhaṅgā ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, tattha sabbeheva samaggehi sammodamānehi avivadamānehi sikkhitabbaṃ.**  
> “Therefore, bhikkhus, these things that I have taught you after directly knowing them—namely, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right kinds of striving, the four bases for spiritual power, the five spiritual faculties, the five powers, the seven enlightenment factors, the Noble Eightfold Path—in these things you should all train in concord, with mutual appreciation, without disputing.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 103, Kinti Sutta, M-iii 66 "3. Kintisuttaṃ", para 35) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=27&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzUu4oCY4oCYIFRhc23EgXRpaGEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgeWUgdm8gW3ll)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā, cattāro sammappadhānā, cattāro iddhipādā, pañcindriyāni, pañca balāni, satta bojjhaṅgā, ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggoti hi ime sattatiṃsa dhammā bujjhanaṭṭhena bodhoti laddhanāmassa ariyamaggassa pakkhe bhavattā bodhipakkhiyā nāma. Pakkhe bhavattāti upakārabhāve ṭhitattā.**  
> For the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases for psychic potency, the five spiritual faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, and the Noble Eightfold Path—these thirty-seven things are called factors partaking of enlightenment (bodhipakkhiyā) because they exist on the side (pakkhe bhavattā) of the Noble Path, which has received the name enlightenment (bodho) in the sense of awakening (bujjhanaṭṭhena). Because they exist on the side (pakkhe bhavattā) means because they are established in a state of assistance (upakārabhāve ṭhitattā).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 1918) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=318&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dMSBcm8gc2F0aXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLCBjYXR0xIFybyBzYW1tYXBwYWRoxIFuxIEsIGNhdHTEgXJvIGlkZGhpcMSBZMSBLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cūḷavagga-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tathā cattāro satipaṭṭhānā, cattāro sammappadhānā, cattāro iddhipādā, pañcindriyāni, pañca balāni, satta bojjhaṅgā, ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggoti sattatiṃsa bodhipakkhiyadhammā dhammo nāma; tayo satipaṭṭhānā, tayo sammappadhānā, tayo iddhipādā, cha indriyāni, cha balāni, aṭṭha bojjhaṅgā, navaṅgiko maggoti ca cattāro upādānā, pañca nīvaraṇā, satta anusayā, aṭṭha micchattāti ca ayaṃ adhammo.**  
> Likewise, the thirty-seven factors of awakening—the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases for psychic potency, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, and the noble eightfold path—are called Dhamma; whereas the three foundations of mindfulness, three right strivings, three bases for psychic potency, six faculties, six powers, eight factors of enlightenment, the nine-factored path, the four attachments, the five hindrances, the seven underlying tendencies, and the eight wrong factors are non-Dhamma.  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Cūḷavagga-aṭṭhakathā, Sp-iv 522) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_04&page_number=113&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0aMSBIGNhdHTEgXJvIHNhdGlwYeG5reG5rWjEgW7EgSwgY2F0dMSBcm8gc2FtbWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBLCBjYXR0xIFybw%3D%3D)
