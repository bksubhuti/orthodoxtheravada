### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammāājīva**: [m.] Right Livelihood; Correct Way of Living. Formed by *sammā* (correctly) + *ājīva* (livelihood, means of support, subsistence).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammāājīva* is the fifth limb of the Noble Eightfold Path, one of the three limbs (right speech, right action, right livelihood) gathered in the aggregate of virtue (*[sīla](/glossary/sila/)-kkhandha*, Vism-ii 813, quoting the Cūḷavedalla). The Mūla defines it: "Here a noble disciple, having abandoned wrong livelihood, makes his living by right livelihood" (SN 45.8; Vibh 746).

The Mahācattārīsaka Sutta says what wrong livelihood (*micchāājīva*) is: *kuhanā* (scheming), *lapanā* (talking), *nemittikatā* (hinting), *nippesikatā* (belittling), *lābhena lābhaṃ nijigīsanatā* (pursuing gain with gain). It says right livelihood is twofold: that with taints, partaking of merit, ripening in the acquisitions (*sāsavo puññabhāgiyo*), and the noble, taintless, supramundane one that is a path factor (MN 117). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it at the moment of the supramundane path as the refraining, abstaining, non-transgression "from wrong livelihood" (*micchāājīvā ārati virati…*), a path factor (Dhs 783). The Visuddhimagga gives the four-fold definition: it is the abstinence from wrong livelihood (*micchājīvavirati*) that cuts off scheming and the rest; its characteristic is cleansing (*vodāna*), its function the occurrence of proper livelihood (*ñāyājīvapavattiraso*), its manifestation the abandonment of wrong livelihood (Vism-ii 786); and at the path moment it "emerges from wrong livelihood in the sense of cleansing" (Vism-ii 1939).

For lay disciples the Buddha named **five trades** that should not be engaged in (AN 5.177):
1. Trade in weapons (*satthavaṇijjā*).
2. Trade in living beings (*sattavaṇijjā*).
3. Trade in meat (*maṃsavaṇijjā*).
4. Trade in intoxicants (*majjavaṇijjā*).
5. Trade in poison (*visavaṇijjā*).

For bhikkhus, the Visuddhimagga explains the virtue of livelihood-purification (*ājīvapārisuddhisīla*) as abstinence from wrong livelihood, by way of transgressing the six rules laid down on account of livelihood and the evil states beginning with scheming and talking (Vism-i 139, 189). The Dīghanikāya lists as wrong livelihood the "low arts" (*tiracchānavijjā*) by which some ascetics earn their living, such as palmistry and signs (D-i 54).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako micchāājīvaṃ pahāya sammāājīvena jīvitaṃ kappeti – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo.**  
> “And what, bhikkhus, is right livelihood? Here, bhikkhus, a noble disciple, having abandoned a wrong way of livelihood, earns his living by a right livelihood—this is called right livelihood, bhikkhus.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 45.8, Vibhaṅga Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=8&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtbyBjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBzYW1txIHEgWrEq3ZvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **Katamo ca, bhikkhave, micchāājīvo? Kuhanā, lapanā, nemittikatā, nippesikatā, lābhena lābhaṃ nijigīsanatā– ayaṃ, bhikkhave, micchāājīvo. Katamo ca, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo? Sammāājīvaṃpahaṃ, bhikkhave, dvāyaṃ vadāmi – atthi, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo sāsavo puññabhāgiyo upadhivepakko; atthi, bhikkhave, sammāājīvo ariyo anāsavo lokuttaro maggaṅgo.**  
> “And what, monks, is wrong livelihood? Scheming, talking, hinting, belittling, pursuing gain with gain: this, monks, is wrong livelihood. “And what, monks, is right livelihood? Right livelihood, I say, is twofold: there is right livelihood that is affected by taints, partaking of merit, ripening in the acquisitions; and there is right livelihood that is noble, taintless, supramundane, a factor of the path.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 117, Mahācattārīsaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=119&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1vIGNhLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIG1pY2NoxIHEgWrEq3ZvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Pañcimā, bhikkhave, vaṇijjā upāsakena akaraṇīyā. Katamā pañca? Satthavaṇijjā, sattavaṇijjā, maṃsavaṇijjā, majjavaṇijjā, visavaṇijjā – imā kho, bhikkhave, pañca vaṇijjā upāsakena akaraṇīyā” ti.**  
> “Monks, these five types of trade are beyond the reach of a lay follower. Which five? Trade in weapons, trade in living beings, trade in meat, trade in intoxicants, and trade in poison—these indeed, monks, are the five types of trade that are beyond the reach of a lay follower.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 5.177, Vaṇijjā Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=183&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTc3LuKAmOKAmCBQYcOxY2ltxIEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgdmHhuYdpamrEgSB1cMSBc2FrZW5hIGFrYXJh4bmHxKt5xIEu)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye sammāājīvo hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye micchāājīvā ārati virati paṭivirati veramaṇī akiriyā akaraṇaṃ anajjhāpatti velāanatikkamo setughāto sammāājīvo maggaṅgaṃ maggapariyāpannaṃ – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye sammāājīvo hoti.**  
> 301. What is right livelihood at that time? Whatever refraining, refraining, refraining, abstention, inoperative one, No doing, non-transgression, non-transgression of limits, cutting off of the bridge from wrong livelihood, being right livelihood, being a path factor, included in the path exists at that time—this is right livelihood at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 301) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=76&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzAxLiBLYXRhbW8gdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIHNhbW3EgcSBasSrdm8gaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Yā panassa tesaṃ sammāvācākammantānaṃ visuddhibhūtā taṃsampayuttāva kuhanādiupacchedikā micchājīvavirati, so sammāājīvo nāma. So vodānalakkhaṇo, ñāyājīvapavattiraso, micchājīvappahānapaccupaṭṭhāno.**  
> But for one whose right speech and right action are purified, the abstinence from wrong livelihood associated with that, which cuts off scheming and so forth, is called right livelihood (sammāājīvo). It has the characteristic of cleansing, the function of the occurrence of proper livelihood, and it is manifested as the abandonment of wrong livelihood.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVI, Dukkhanirodhagāminipaṭipadāniddesa, para. 562ff) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=141&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=WcSBIHBhbmFzc2EgdGVzYeG5gyBzYW1txIF2xIFjxIFrYW1tYW50xIFuYeG5gyB2aXN1ZGRoaWJoxat0xIEgdGHhuYNzYW1wYXl1dHTEgXZh)
