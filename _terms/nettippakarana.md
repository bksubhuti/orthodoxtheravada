### Grammatical Analysis
**Nettippakaraṇa**: [nt.] The Book of Guidance; The Treatise on the Guide. Formed by *netti* (guide, conductor, that which leads) + *pakaraṇa* (treatise, literary work).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Nettippakaraṇa* (often just called *The Netti*) is an exegetical manual, placed in the Khuddaka Nikāya (“Khuddakanikāye”, Nett 2:1). It is attributed to the Elder Mahākaccāyana: the Mūla itself says “Aṭṭhārasamūlapadā, mahakaccānena niddiṭṭhā” (Nett 10:1), and the Aṭṭhakathā says “Adesayi mahāthero, mahākaccāyano vasī” (Nett-a 1079:1). The Aṭṭhakathā also recalls that the Buddha ranked him foremost among those who explain concisely (*saṃkhittaṃ vibhajantānaṃ*, Nett-a 17:1).

The opening verses state its method. The sutta consists of twelve terms (*dvādasa padāni suttaṃ*), which are both expression (*byañjana*) and meaning (*attha*). The Netti has sixteen *hāras* and five *nayas* for the investigation (*pariyeṭṭhi*) of the Teaching, and eighteen root-terms (*mūlapada*). The *hāras* investigate the expression of the sutta, and the three *nayas* its meaning; the sutta is then explained “according to the sutta” (*yathāsuttaṃ*), and the whole is called the ninefold investigation of the Suttanta (*navavidhasuttantapariyeṭṭhi*).

### Quote (Mūla: Nettippakaraṇa)
> **Dvādasa padāni suttaṃ, taṃ sabbaṃ byañjanañca attho ca; Taṃ viññeyyaṃ ubhayaṃ, ko attho byañjanaṃ katamaṃ. Soḷasahārā netti, pañcanayā sāsanassa pariyeṭṭhi; Aṭṭhārasamūlapadā, mahakaccānena niddiṭṭhā. Hārā byañjanavicayo, suttassa nayā tayo ca suttattho; Ubhayaṃ pariggahītaṃ, vuccati suttaṃ yathāsuttaṃ. Yā ceva desanā yañca, desitaṃ ubhayameva viññeyyaṃ; Tatrāyamānupubbī, navavidhasuttantapariyeṭṭhīti.**  
> Twelve terms represent the discourse, the entire expression and meaning; Both should be known: What is the meaning? Which is the expression? Sixteen conveyings (hāra), five guidelines (naya) for the dispensation's search (pariyeṭṭhi); Eighteen root-terms were demonstrated by Mahākaccāna. The conveyings investigate the discourse's expression, and the three guidelines investigate the discourse's meaning; Comprised in both these ways, a discourse is called 'according to the discourse'. Since the teaching and that which is taught should both be known; Here follows the order for testing the ninefold discourse-of-argument (suttanta-pariyeṭṭhi).  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Nettippakaraṇa* (Nett 1–14 for the opening saṅgahavāra) – the manual on interpreting the Teaching by sixteen *hāras*, five *nayas* and eighteen root-terms.
* **Commentary**: *Nettippakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā* (Nett-a) – its closing verses say it was written at the request of the Elder Dhammarakkhita (Nett-a 14:1), written while the author was living in the Dhammāsoka Great King's monastery at the port called Nāga (Nett-a 1080:1–1081:1), and finished in twenty-seven recital sections (Nett-a 1085:1). The colophon names the author: “Iti badaratitthavihāravāsinā ācariyadhammapālena katā”, made by the Teacher Dhammapāla, resident of the Badaratittha monastery (Nett-a 1094:1).
