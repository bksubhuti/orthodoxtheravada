### Grammatical Analysis
**Indriya**: [nt.] faculty; controlling faculty. The Visuddhimagga explains the word through *inda* (ruler, lord): the meaning of a faculty is that it is the sign of a ruler (*indaliṅgaṭṭho*), taught by a ruler (*indadesitaṭṭho*), seen by a ruler (*indadiṭṭhaṭṭho*), created by a ruler (*indasiṭṭhaṭṭho*) and fostered by a ruler (*indajuṭṭhaṭṭho*), where the ruler is the Buddha or kamma; and it is also a faculty in the sense of sovereignty known as predominance (*ādhipaccasaṅkhātena issariyaṭṭhena*) (Vism-ii 597:1-6, 598:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Indriya* is a term for states that are predominant in their own function. The Vibhaṅga lists twenty-two faculties (*bāvīsatindriyāni*): eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind, feminine, masculine, life, pleasure, pain, mental pleasure, mental displeasure, equanimity, faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, wisdom, the faculty of "I shall know the unknown" (*anaññātaññassāmītindriyaṃ*), the faculty of final knowledge (*aññindriyaṃ*) and the faculty of one who has final knowledge (*aññātāvindriyaṃ*) (Vibh 831:1).

Among these are the five spiritual faculties (*pañcindriyāni*): *[saddhā](/glossary/saddha/)* (faith), *[viriya](/glossary/viriya/)* (energy), *[sati](/glossary/sati/)* (mindfulness), *[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)* (concentration) and *[paññā](/glossary/panna/)* (wisdom) (SN 48.1, S-v 1154:2-4).

The Visuddhimagga (Ch. IV) teaches the balancing of the faculties (*indriyasamattapaṭipādanaṃ*). If faith is strong and the others weak, the others cannot perform their functions (energy exerting, mindfulness establishing, concentration non-distraction, wisdom seeing). Specifically, the balance of faith with wisdom and of concentration with energy is praised: one strong in faith and weak in wisdom is blindly confident, confiding in what is groundless; one strong in wisdom and weak in faith sides with the cunning; one strong in concentration and weak in energy is overcome by laziness; one strong in energy and weak in concentration is overcome by restlessness (Vism-i 870:1-14). Mindfulness, however, is suitable when strong in all cases, "like a seasoning of salt in all sauces, or a prime minister in all the king's affairs" (Vism-i 870:23-24).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, Indriyasaṃyutta)
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, indriyāni. Katamāni pañca? Saddhindriyaṃ, vīriyindriyaṃ, satindriyaṃ, samādhindriyaṃ, paññindriyaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañcindriyānī” ti.**  
> “Mendicants, there are these five faculties. What five? The faculty of faith, the faculty of energy, the faculty of mindfulness, the faculty of concentration, and the faculty of wisdom—these, indeed, mendicants, are the five faculties.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the meaning of indriya)
> **Ko pana nesaṃ indriyaṭṭho nāmāti? Indaliṅgaṭṭho indriyaṭṭho. Indadesitaṭṭho indriyaṭṭho. Indadiṭṭhaṭṭho indriyaṭṭho. Indasiṭṭhaṭṭho indriyaṭṭho. Indajuṭṭhaṭṭho indriyaṭṭho.**  
> But what is this meaning of a faculty (indriyaṭṭho)? The meaning of being the sign of a ruler is the meaning of a faculty. The meaning of being taught by a ruler is the meaning of a faculty. The meaning of being seen by a ruler is the meaning of a faculty. The meaning of being created by a ruler is the meaning of a faculty. The meaning of being fostered by a ruler is the meaning of a faculty.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. XVI, Indriyasaccaniddesa, Indriyavitthārakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=122&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=S28gcGFuYSBuZXNh4bmDIGluZHJpeWHhua3hua1obyBuxIFtxIF0aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, balancing the faculties)
> **Indriyasamattapaṭipādanaṃ nāma saddhādīnaṃ indriyānaṃ samabhāvakaraṇaṃ. Sace hissa saddhindriyaṃ balavaṃ hoti itarāni mandāni, tato vīriyindriyaṃ paggahakiccaṃ, satindriyaṃ upaṭṭhānakiccaṃ, samādhindriyaṃ avikkhepakiccaṃ, paññindriyaṃ dassanakiccaṃ kātuṃ na sakkoti, tasmā taṃ dhammasabhāvapaccavekkhaṇena vā yathā vā manasikaroto balavaṃ jātaṃ, tathā amanasikārena hāpetabbaṃ.**  
> Establishing the balance of the faculties (indriyasamattapaṭipādanaṃ) means equalizing the faculties of faith and the rest. For if his faculty of faith is strong and the others are weak, then the faculty of energy is not able to perform its function of exerting, the faculty of mindfulness its function of establishing, the faculty of concentration its function of non-distraction, and the faculty of wisdom its function of seeing. Therefore, that faculty of faith should be reduced either by reflecting on the nature of things, or by not giving attention to it in the way that made it strong.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. IV, §62) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=125&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=NjIuIEluZHJpeWFzYW1hdHRhcGHhua1pcMSBZGFuYeG5gyBuxIFtYSBzYWRkaMSBZMSrbmHhuYMgaW5kcml5xIFuYeG5gyBzYW1hYmjEgXZha2FyYeG5h2HhuYMu)
