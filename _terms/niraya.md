### Grammatical Analysis
**Niraya**: [m.] hell. The Itivuttaka commentary gives two derivations: “natthi ettha assādasaññito ayoti nirassādaṭṭhena nirayo”, there is no *aya* here (*aya* being what is called happiness), so it is *niraya* in the sense of being without enjoyment (It-a 337:7); and “niratiatthena nirassādaṭṭhena vā nirayanti laddhanāmaṃ”, it has got the name by the sense of being without delight or without enjoyment (It-a 454:6). The Ṭīkā has “ayato sukhato niggatoti nirayo”, gone out from *aya*, from happiness (Abhidh-s-t 1304:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Niraya* is one of the four planes of misery (*apāyabhūmi*): *niraya, tiracchānayoni, pettivisaya, asurakāya* (Abh-s 383:1). The Mūla says that those who have done bodily, verbal and mental misconduct, at the breaking up of the body after death, are reborn in the plane of misery, the bad destination, the lower realm, in hell (*apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjati*, AN 3.36, A-iii 256:4). The Itivuttaka commentary treats *apāya, duggati* and *vinipāta* as synonyms of *niraya* (“apāyantiādi sabbaṃ nirayasseva vevacanaṃ”) and explains each (It-a 337:1-7), and it counts as *nirayagati* the eight great hells beginning with Sañjīva, the sixteen *ussada* hells and the *lokantarika* hell, all entirely painful (*ekantadukkhatāya*) (It-a 621:1-2).

The Devadūta Sutta describes the torments and the great hell (*mahāniraya*): four-cornered, four-doored, divided into quarters (MN 130, M-iii 801:7-802:1); there the hell-wardens (*nirayapālā*) carry out the punishments, and the being does not die until the evil kamma is exhausted (“na ca tāva kālaṅkaroti yāva na taṃ pāpakammaṃ byantīhoti”, M-iii 801:3). In MN 130 it is King Yama who questions the one brought before him about the five divine messengers (M-iii 801:1). The sutta thus shows hell as the result of the being's own bad kamma, lasting until that kamma is exhausted. The text does not describe it as the work of a creator god.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **So kāyena duccaritaṃ caritvā, vācāya duccaritaṃ caritvā, manasā duccaritaṃ caritvā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjati.**  
> In consequence, after the breaking up of the body, upon death, he reappears in a state of deprivation, a bad destination, a downfall, hell.

### Quote (Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha)
> **Tāsu nirayo tiracchānayoni pettivisayo asurakāyo ceti apāyabhūmi catubbidhā hoti.**  
> 4. Among those, the plane of misery is of four kinds (apāyabhūmi catubbidhā): hell, the animal realm, the ghost realm (pettivisayo), and the Asura group (asurakāyo).  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Ayato sukhato niggatoti nirayo.**  
> 4. It is hell (nirayo) because it has gone out (niggato) from merit or pleasure (ayato sukhato).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Apāyantiādi sabbaṃ nirayasseva vevacanaṃ. Nirayo hi ayasaṅkhātā sukhā apetoti apāyo ; saggamokkhahetubhūtā vā puññasammatā ayā apetotipi apāyo. Dukkhassa gati paṭisaraṇanti duggati ; dosabahulattā vā duṭṭhena kammunā nibbattā gatītipi duggati. Vivasā nipatanti ettha dukkaṭakammakārino, vinassantā vā ettha nipatanti sambhijjamānaṅgapaccaṅgāti vinipāto. Natthi ettha assādasaññito ayoti nirassādaṭṭhena nirayo.**  
> “State of deprivation” (apāyaṃ) and so on, all are synonyms for hell. For hell is called “state of deprivation” (apāyo) because it is deprived of happiness, known as ‘prosperity’ (aya). Or, it is called “state of deprivation” because it is deprived of prosperity, which is esteemed as merit, the cause of heaven and Nibbāna. “Bad destination” (duggati) means the destination of suffering, the support of suffering. Alternatively, it is called “bad destination” because of being abundant in hatred, or because it is a destination produced by bad kamma. “Perdition” (vinipāto) means that those who perform bad kamma fall helplessly into it, or perish there, falling into it with fragmented limbs and appendages. “Hell” (nirayo) means, because there is no happiness there known as ‘gratification’, it is called “hell” in the sense of being devoid of gratification.  
>  
> — [Itivuttaka](/glossary/itivuttaka/)-aṭṭhakathā (Paramatthadīpanī, on apāya, duggati, vinipāta, niraya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=69&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXDEgXlhbnRpxIFkaSBzYWJiYeG5gyBuaXJheWFzc2V2YSB2ZXZhY2FuYeG5gy4%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tathā nirayagati, tiracchānagati, petagati, manussagati, devagatīti pañca gatiyo. Tāsu sañjīvādayo aṭṭha mahānirayā, kukkuḷādayo soḷasa ussadanirayā, lokantarikanirayo cāti sabbepime ekantadukkhatāya nirassādaṭṭhena nirayā ca, sakakammunā gantabbato gati cāti nirayagati.**  
> Likewise, there are five destinations: the hell destination, the animal destination, the ghost destination, the human destination, and the god destination. Among these, the eight great hells and the sixteen minor hells, along with the hell between the worlds, are called 'hell' because they are purely suffering and devoid of pleasure, and 'destination' because one must go there according to one's own kamma; thus it is called the hell destination.  
>  
> — [Itivuttaka](/glossary/itivuttaka/)-aṭṭhakathā (Paramatthadīpanī, the nirayagati) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_04&page_number=121&name=itivuttakaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0aMSBIG5pcmF5YWdhdGksIHRpcmFjY2jEgW5hZ2F0aSwgcGV0YWdhdGksIG1hbnVzc2FnYXRpLCBkZXZhZ2F0xKt0aQ%3D%3D)
