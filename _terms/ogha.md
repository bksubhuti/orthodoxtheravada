### Grammatical Analysis
**Ogha**: [m.] flood; torrent. The Aṭṭhakathā derives it: “yassa saṃvijjanti taṃ vaṭṭasmiṃyeva ohananti osīdāpentīti oghā” – those states that, found in a person, cast him down and make him sink in the round of existence itself (As 881:1); similarly “vaṭṭasmiṃ satte ohananti osīdāpentīti oghā” (Sv-iii 858:1). The Saṃyutta commentary gives another sense: *duruttaraṇaṭṭho oghaṭṭho*, the meaning of “flood” is that which is hard to cross (Spk-iv 365:1-3).

### Orthodox Definition
The four floods (*cattāro oghā*) are *kāmogho* (flood of sensuality), *bhavogho* (flood of existence), *diṭṭhogho* (flood of views) and *avijjogho* (flood of ignorance) (SN 45.171, S-v 382:2-4). In the Abhidhamma they form the Oghagocchaka, the cluster of floods (Dhs 1939:1-2).

The floods are not a separate set of defilements. The Peṭakopadesa and the Nettippakaraṇa say that the four *āsavas*, when they reach abundance (*vepulla*), become floods: *kāmāsavo kāmogho, bhavāsavo bhavogho, avijjāsavo avijjogho, diṭṭhāsavo diṭṭhogho* (Pet 1152:1-2; Nett 779:1-3). The Atthasālinī adds that the other states that are objects of the floods are “oghaniyā”, to be overcome by floods because they are made their object (As 881:2). The Abhidhamma gives the same four terms for the yokes (*yoga*), in the next cluster (Dhs 1941:1-2; As 882:1-2).

The Oghataraṇa Sutta shows the floods as what one crosses: the Buddha crossed the flood “by not halting and by not straining” (*appatiṭṭhaṃ anāyūhaṃ*): when he stood still he sank, when he struggled he was swept away (SN 1.1, S-i 7:5-10). The Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā lists *ogha* among the defilements explained by their sense: “ohanantīti oghā” (Vism-mht-ii 1675:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, oghā. Katame cattāro? Kāmogho, bhavogho, diṭṭhogho, avijjogho – ime kho, bhikkhave, cattāro oghā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, there are these four floods. What four? The flood of sensuality, the flood of existence, the flood of views, the flood of ignorance—these indeed, bhikkhus, are the four floods.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **‘“kathaṃ nu tvaṃ, mārisa, oghamatarī ’ ti? ‘ Appatiṭṭhaṃ khvāhaṃ, āvuso, anāyūhaṃ oghamatarin ’ ti. ‘ Yathā kathaṃ pana tvaṃ, mārisa, appatiṭṭhaṃ anāyūhaṃ oghamatarī ’ ti? ‘ Yadākhvāhaṃ, āvuso, santiṭṭhāmi tadāssu saṃsīdāmi; yadākhvāhaṃ, āvuso, āyūhāmi tadāssu nibbuyhāmi. Evaṃ khvāhaṃ, āvuso, appatiṭṭhaṃ anāyūhaṃ oghamatarin”’ ti.**  
> “How, friend, did you cross the flood?” “By not halting, friend, and by not straining, I crossed the flood.” “But how is it, friend, that by not halting and by not straining you crossed the flood?” “When I came to a standstill, friend, then I sank; but when I struggled, then I was swept away. It is in this way, friend, that by not halting and by not straining I crossed the flood.”  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 1.1, Oghataraṇa Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_01&page_number=1&name=sag%C4%81th%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCY4oCYIGthdGhh4bmDIG51IHR2YeG5gywgbcSBcmlzYSwgb2doYW1hdGFyxKs%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Peṭakopadesa)
> **Te cattāro āsavā vepullabhāvaṃ gatā oghā honti, tena vuccanti “oghā” ti. Tattha kāmāsavo kāmogho, bhavāsavo bhavogho, avijjāsavo avijjogho, diṭṭhāsavo diṭṭhogho.**  
> These four taints, when they come to abundance, are floods; that is why they are called 'floods'. Herein, the taint of sensual desire is the flood of sensual desire, the taint of being is the flood of being, the taint of ignorance is the flood of ignorance, and the taint of views is the flood of views.  
>  
> — [Peṭakopadesa](/glossary/petakopadesa/) (the oghas as the āsavas grown to abundance) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_21&page_number=331&name=pe%E1%B9%ADakopadesap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGUgY2F0dMSBcm8gxIFzYXbEgSB2ZXB1bGxhYmjEgXZh4bmDIGdhdMSBIG9naMSB)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā oghā? Cattāro oghā … pe … ime dhammā oghavippayuttā oghaniyā.**  
> 1156. What mental phenomena are floods? The four floods......these mental phenomena are disassociated from floods (but) akin to floods.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1156, Oghagocchaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=232&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTE1Ni4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgb2doxIE%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **32-37. Oghagocchake yassa saṃvijjanti taṃ vaṭṭasmiṃyeva ohananti osīdāpentīti oghā. Ārammaṇaṃ katvā atikkamanīyato oghehi atikkamitabbāti oghaniyā.**  
> 32-37. In the cluster of floods (oghagocchake): those (qualities) that, when found (saṃvijjanti) in a person (yassa taṃ puggalaṃ), cast that (person) down (ohananti) and cause them to sink (osīdāpentīti) in the round of existence itself (vaṭṭasmiṃyeva) are therefore called floods (oghā). Those (qualities) that are to be overcome (atikkamitabbāti) by floods (oghehi) by reason of being to be transcended (atikkamanīyato) after making them an object (ārammaṇaṃ katvā) are therefore called to be overcome by floods (oghaniyā).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on the Oghagocchaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=91&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MzItMzcuIE9naGFnb2NjaGFrZSB5YXNzYSBzYeG5g3ZpamphbnRpIHRh4bmDIHZh4bmt4bmtYXNtaeG5g3lldmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Oghāti vaṭṭasmiṃ satte ohananti osīdāpentīti oghā.**  
> “Floods” (oghā) are so called because they cast down beings in the round of existence, making them sink.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (on DN 33, oghā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_03&page_number=206&name=p%C4%81thikavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=T2doxIF0aSB2YeG5reG5rWFzbWnhuYMgc2F0dGUgb2hhbmFudGkgb3PEq2TEgXBlbnTEq3RpIG9naMSBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā)
> **Ye dhammāti ye saṃkilesadhammā. Saṃyojanādīsu saṃyojanaṭṭhena bandhanaṭṭhena saṃyojanāni , vibādhanaṭṭhena, upatāpanaṭṭhena vā kilesā , micchāsabhāvāti micchattā , loke dhammā lokapariyāpannā dhammāti lokadhammā , maccherassa bhāvo, kammaṃ vāti macchariyaṃ , viparītaṭṭhena vipallāsā , ganthanaṭṭhena ganthā , ayuttā gatīti agati , āsavantīti āsavā , ohanantīti oghā , yojanaṭṭhena yogā , nīvarantīti nīvaraṇāni , dhammasabhāvaṃ atikkamma parato āmasantīti parāmāsā , bhusaṃ ādiyantīti upādānāni , appahīnabhāvena santāne anu anu sentīti anusayā , malīnabhāvakaraṇaṭṭhena malā , akusalakammāni ca tāni sugatiduggatīnaṃ, tattha nibbattanakasukhadukkhānañca pathā cāti akusalakammapathā , cittaṃ uppajjati etthāti cittuppādo , cittacetasikarāsi, akusalo ca so cittuppādo cāti akusalacittuppādoti akusalasaddaṃ ānetvā sambandhitabbaṃ.**  
> 829. ‘The things’ (ye dhammā) refers to those states that are defiling. Among the fetters and so on: fetters (saṃyojanāni) are so called in the sense of fettering and binding; defilements (kilesā) in the sense of tormenting and burning; wrongnesses (micchattā) are so called due to their false nature; worldly conditions (lokadhammā) are things within the world; stinginess (macchariyaṃ) is a stingy act/disposition; perversions (vipallāsā) are so called in the sense of being perverted; ties (ganthā) in the sense of tying; bad ways (agati) are inappropriate paths; taints (āsavā) are so called because they flow; floods (oghā) because they submerge; bonds (yogā) in the sense of binding; hindrances (nīvaraṇāni) because they obstruct; misapprehension (parāmāsā) because they grasp at things incorrectly, having exceeded the true nature of the Dhamma; clingings (upādānāni) because they grasp strongly; underlying tendencies (anusayā) because they lie dormant in the continuum again and again due to being unabandoned; stains (malā) in the sense of causing impurity; unwholesome courses of action (akusalakammapathā) are unwholesome actions and also paths to happy or unhappy destinations; thought-arising (cittuppādo) because consciousness arises here; consciousness and its mental factors are unwholesome and are a 'thought-arising,' so by adding the word 'unwholesome' (akusala), it should be connected.  
>  
> — Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā (Pahātabbadhammapahānakathāvaṇṇanā, on ‘ye dhammā’) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_04&page_number=495&name=visuddhimaggamah%C4%81%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODI5LiBZZSBkaGFtbcSBdGkgeWUgc2HhuYNraWxlc2FkaGFtbcSBLg%3D%3D)
