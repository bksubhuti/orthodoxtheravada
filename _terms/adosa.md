### Grammatical Analysis
**Adosa**: [m.] Non-hatred; non-aversion; goodwill. Formed by negative prefix *a* (not) + *dosa* (hatred, anger, aversion).

### Orthodox Definition
*Adosa* is the second Wholesome Root (*[kusala-mūla](/glossary/kusala-mula/)*). Like non-greed, it is a negative term masking a profoundly positive mental factor. 

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it with a string of synonyms that includes loving-kindness: "adoso adussanā adussitattaṃ metti mettāyanā mettāyitattaṃ anuddā anuddāyanā anudāyitattaṃ hitesitā anukampā abyāpādo abyāpajjo adoso kusalamūlaṃ" (Dhs 1833:2); in the first wholesome citta the list is the shorter "adoso adussanā adussitattaṃ abyāpādo abyāpajjo adoso kusalamūlaṃ" (Dhs 423:2). The Aṭṭhakathā gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: *adoso* has non-ferocity (*acaṇḍikka*), or non-opposition, as its characteristic, like an agreeable friend; the removing of resentment (*āghātavinaya*), or the removing of burning, as its function, like sandalwood; and gentleness (*sommabhāva*) as its manifestation, like the full moon (As 1233:6-8; Vism-ii 440:1).

### Quote
> **“Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, kusalamūlāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Alobho kusalamūlaṃ, adoso kusalamūlaṃ, amoho kusalamūlaṃ. “Yadapi, bhikkhave, adoso tadapi kusalamūlaṃ; yadapi aduṭṭho abhisaṅkharoti kāyena vācāya manasā tadapi kusalaṃ; yadapi aduṭṭho dosena anabhibhūto apariyādinnacitto na parassa asatā dukkhaṃ uppādayati vadhena vā bandhanena vā jāniyā vā garahāya vā pabbājanāya vā balavamhi balattho itipi tadapi kusalaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these three roots of the wholesome. What are these three? Non-greed is a root of the wholesome, non-hatred is a root of the wholesome, non-delusion is a root of the wholesome. “Monks, non-hatred itself is a root of the wholesome; whatever a person not full of hate generates by means of body, speech, or mind, that too is wholesome; whatever suffering a person not full of hate—his mind not overcome by hatred, his mind not obsessed by it—does not wrongly inflict on another person through beating, imprisonment, confiscation, placing blame, or banishment, thinking, ‘I have power. I want power,’ that too is wholesome.  
>

### Quote
> **Katamo tasmiṃ samaye adoso hoti? Yo tasmiṃ samaye adoso adussanā adussitattaṃ abyāpādo abyāpajjo adoso kusalamūlaṃ – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye adoso hoti?**  
> 33. What is non-hatred at that time? Whatever non-hatred, non-hatefulness (adussanā), the state of not being hateful (adussitattaṃ), non-ill will, unafflicted, non-hatred, a root of the wholesome, exists at that time—this is non-hatred at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§33, first wholesome citta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=22&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzMuIEthdGFtbyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgYWRvc28gaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote
> **Adoso acaṇḍikkalakkhaṇo, avirodhalakkhaṇo vā anukūlamitto viya. Āghātavinayaraso pariḷāhavinayaraso vā candanaṃ viya. Sommabhāvapaccupaṭṭhāno puṇṇacando viya.**  
> Non-hatred (adoso) is characterized by non-ferocity (acaṇḍikkalakkhaṇo), or by non-opposition, like an agreeable friend. Its function is the dispelling of resentment, or the dispelling of mental distress, like sandalwood (candanaṃ). Its manifestation is gentleness, like the full moon.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Akusalamūla Sutta* (AN 3; heading "9. Akusalamūlasuttaṃ", A-iii 720:1) – the three roots of the unwholesome (A-iii 721-728), then the three roots of the wholesome (A-iii 729:1-3); "Yadapi, bhikkhave, adoso tadapi kusalamūlaṃ" (A-iii 731:1). Whatever the unhateful one does by body, speech or mind is wholesome (A-iii 731:2).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Dhs 423:2; the fuller definition including *metti* and *hitesitā* at Dhs 1833:2); *Vibhaṅga* (Vibh 1858:2) defines *mettā* as "yā sattesu metti mettāyanā mettāyitattaṃ mettācetovimutti".
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 1233:6-8) and *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 440:1, §468) – the characteristic, function and manifestation of adosa, with the similes of the agreeable friend, sandalwood and the full moon.
