### Grammatical Analysis
**Thīnamiddha** (also spelt *thinamiddha*): [nt.] sloth and torpor. A dvanda compound, *thīna* + *middha* ("thinañca middhañca thinamiddhaṃ", Vism XIV 483). *Thīna* is the mind's state of being sluggish (*thinanatā thinaṃ*); *middha* is the state of being torpid or heavy (*middhanatā middhaṃ*). The Atthasālinī derives *middha* from *medhati*, "it oppresses": "akammaññabhāvena vihiṃsatīti attho", it harms by making one unfit for action (As 2311:8-9).

### Orthodox Definition
*Thīna-middha* is the third of the five hindrances (*[nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*). The [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) lists it as one hindrance made of two factors, "Atthi thinaṃ, atthi middhaṃ" (Dhs 1161): *thīna* is the unhealthiness and unwieldiness of the mind (*cittassa akallatā akammaññatā*, Dhs 1162), *middha* the unhealthiness and unwieldiness of the body (*kāyassa akallatā akammaññatā*, Dhs 1163). The Atthasālinī explains that here "body" means the mental body (*nāmakāya*), "which is called the three aggregates" (As 2311:1), and that *thīna* "remains in a solid state, like a lump of ghee, because it does not spread out" (As 2310:12). They are taken together as one hindrance because the two do not abandon each other (As 2324:1) and, joined, they obstruct (*āvaraṇaṭṭhena*, As 2311:14).

The Visuddhimagga gives the usual four-fold definition (Vism XIV 483):
1. *Thīna*: characteristic, lack of energy (*anussāhalakkhaṇa*); function, removing energy (*vīriyavinodanarasa*); manifestation, shrinking (*saṃsīdanapaccupaṭṭhāna*).
2. *Middha*: characteristic, unwieldiness (*akammaññatālakkhaṇa*); function, enveloping (*onahanarasa*); manifestation, sluggishness (*līnatā*), or nodding and sleep (*pacalāyikāniddā*).
3. Both have unwise attention (*ayonisomanasikāra*) toward boredom, stretching and the like as their proximate cause (*padaṭṭhāna*).

In the Mūla the Buddha gives the cure to Mahāmoggallāna, who was nodding (AN 7.61): in turn, recall and ponder the Dhamma, recite it aloud, pull the earlobes and rub the limbs, rise and wash the eyes with water and look at the stars, attend to the perception of light, walk meditation, and finally lie down mindfully with the thought of rising. The Peṭaka teaching quoted in the Visuddhimagga and Atthasālinī names the direct opponent: "vitakko thinamiddhassa", applied thought (*vitakka*), a jhāna factor, is the opponent of sloth and torpor (Vism-i 934:3, As 1415:9). According to the Atthasālinī, the sloth and torpor that arise in trainees and ordinary people before and after sleep are cut off by the path of Arahatship (As 2311:15); an Arahant may still sleep from bodily weakness, but this is not torpor, which is unwholesome (As 2311:16, 23).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara)
> **“No ce te evaṃ viharato taṃ middhaṃ pahīyetha, tato tvaṃ, moggallāna, yathāsutaṃ yathāpariyattaṃ dhammaṃ cetasā anuvitakkeyyāsi anuvicāreyyāsi, manasā anupekkheyyāsi. “No ce te evaṃ viharato taṃ middhaṃ pahīyetha, tato tvaṃ, moggallāna, yathāsutaṃ yathāpariyattaṃ dhammaṃ vitthārena sajjhāyaṃ kareyyāsi. “No ce te evaṃ viharato taṃ middhaṃ pahīyetha, tato tvaṃ, moggallāna, ubho kaṇṇasotāni āviñcheyyāsi, pāṇinā gattāni anumajjeyyāsi. “No ce te evaṃ viharato taṃ middhaṃ pahīyetha, tato tvaṃ, moggallāna, uṭṭhāyāsanā udakena akkhīni anumajjitvā disā anuvilokeyyāsi, nakkhattāni tārakarūpāni ullokeyyāsi. “No ce te evaṃ viharato taṃ middhaṃ pahīyetha, tato tvaṃ, moggallāna, ālokasaññaṃ manasi kareyyāsi, divāsaññaṃ adhiṭṭhaheyyāsi – yathā divā tathā rattiṃ yathā rattiṃ tathā divā.**  
> “If, while you are dwelling thus, that drowsiness is not shaken off, then, Moggallāna, recall to your mind the Dhamma as you have heard it and as you have mastered it, re-examine it and ponder it over with your mind. “If, while you are dwelling thus, that drowsiness is not shaken off, then, Moggallāna, repeat aloud in detail the Dhamma as you have heard it and as you have mastered it. “If, while you are dwelling thus, that drowsiness is not shaken off, then, Moggallāna, pull both your earlobes and rub your limbs with your hand. “If, while you are dwelling thus, that drowsiness is not shaken off, then, Moggallāna, rise from your seat, and after washing your eyes with water, look around in all directions and upward at the constellations and stars. “If, while you are dwelling thus, that drowsiness is not shaken off, then, Moggallāna, attend to the perception of light, resolve on the perception of daytime—by night as by day, and by day as by night.  
>  
> — *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (AN 7.61, Pacalāyamāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_07&page_number=462&name=sattakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIE5vIGNlIHRlIGV2YeG5gyB2aWhhcmF0bw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamaṃ thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ? Atthi thinaṃ, atthi middhaṃ. 1162. Tattha katamaṃ thinaṃ? Yā cittassa akallatā akammaññatā olīyanā sallīyanā līnaṃ līyanā līyitattaṃ thinaṃ thiyanā thiyitattaṃ cittassa – idaṃ vuccati thinaṃ. 1163. Tattha katamaṃ middhaṃ? Yā kāyassa akallatā akammaññatā onāho pariyonāho antosamorodho middhaṃ soppaṃ pacalāyikā soppaṃ supanā supitattaṃ – idaṃ vuccati middhaṃ. Iti idañca thinaṃ, idañca middhaṃ – idaṃ vuccati thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ.**  
> 1161. Which is sloth and drowsiness as a hindrance? There is mental inertia, there is drowsiness. 1162. Which is mental inertia? Whatever is the mind's unhealthiness, unwieldiness, backsliding, languor, slackness, the act of slackening, the state of being slack, mental inertia, stupor, the state of being in stupor of the mind — this is called mental inertia (thinaṃ). 1163. Which is drowsiness? Whatever is the body's unhealthiness, unwieldiness, oppression, excessive oppression, internal obstruction, drowsiness, sleepiness, twitching of eyelids, the act of sleeping, the state of being asleep — this is called drowsiness (middhaṃ). Thus, this mental inertia and this drowsiness — this is called sloth and drowsiness as a hindrance.  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§1161-1163, Nīvaraṇagocchaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=233&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTE2MS4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyB0aGluYW1pZGRoYW7Eq3ZhcmHhuYdh4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tattha thinanatā thinaṃ. Middhanatā middhaṃ. Anussāhasaṃhananatā asattivighāto cāti attho. Thinañca middhañca thinamiddhaṃ. Tattha thinaṃ anussāhalakkhaṇaṃ, vīriyavinodanarasaṃ, saṃsīdanapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ. Middhaṃ akammaññatālakkhaṇaṃ, onahanarasaṃ, līnatāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, pacalāyikāniddāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ vā. Ubhayampi arativijambhikādīsu ayonisomanasikārapadaṭṭhānaṃ.**  
> Therein, a sluggish state is sloth (thinaṃ). A heavy state is torpor (middhaṃ). The meaning is a lack of striving and shriveling, and an impediment to ability. Sloth and torpor together are 'sloth and torpor'. Therein, sloth (thinaṃ) has the characteristic of a lack of striving, the function of removing energy, and manifests as shrinking. Torpor (middhaṃ) has the characteristic of unwieldiness, the function of enveloping, and manifests as sluggishness, or manifests as nodding and sleep. Both are rooted in unwise attention to boredom, stretching, and so on.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XIV, 483) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=99&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHRoaW5hbmF0xIEgdGhpbmHhuYMu)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Thinanti sappipiṇḍo viya avipphārikatāya ghanabhāvena ṭhitaṃ. 1163. Kāyassāti khandhattayasaṅkhātassa nāmakāyassa. Medhatīti middhaṃ ; akammaññabhāvena vihiṃsatīti attho. Idaṃ vuccati thinamiddhanīvaraṇanti idaṃ thinañca middhañca ekato katvā āvaraṇaṭṭhena thinamiddhanīvaraṇanti vuccati. Yaṃ yebhuyyena sekkhaputhujjanānaṃ niddāya pubbabhāgaaparabhāgesu uppajjati taṃ arahattamaggena samucchijjati.**  
> Mental inertia (thinaṃ) is that which remains in a solid state due to not spreading out, like a lump of ghee. 1163. Of the body (kāyassa) means of the mental body, which is called the three aggregates. It oppresses, therefore it is drowsiness (middhaṃ); the meaning is: it harms by making unsuitable for actions. 'This is called sloth and drowsiness as a hindrance' (idaṃ vuccati thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ) means that, having combined this mental inertia (thinaṃ) and this drowsiness (middhaṃ) together, it is called the hindrance of sloth and drowsiness in the sense of obstructing. That which mostly arises for initiates and ordinary people in the stages before and after sleep is eradicated by the path of Arahantship.  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (§1162-1163) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=409&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGhpbmFudGkgc2FwcGlwaeG5h%2BG4jW8gdml5YSBhdmlwcGjEgXJpa2F0xIF5YSBnaGFuYWJoxIF2ZW5hIOG5rWhpdGHhuYMu)
