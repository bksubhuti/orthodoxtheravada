### Grammatical Analysis
**Thīnamiddha**: [nt.] sloth and torpor; mental stiffness and sluggishness. Formed by *thīna* (sloth/stiffness of mind) + *middha* (torpor/sluggishness of the accompanying mental factors).

### Orthodox Definition
*Thīna-middha* is the third of the five mental hindrances (*[nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*). Although treated as a single compound hindrance because they produce the identical result of heavy, sleepy mental paralysis, the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) distinguishes them as two separate unwholesome mental factors (*[cetasika](/glossary/cetasika/)*):

1. *Thīna*: Sloth. The unwieldiness, lack of driving energy, and stiffness of the consciousness (*[citta](/glossary/citta-base/)*).
2. *Middha*: Torpor. The heavy, somnolent dragging down of the accompanying mental factors (*cetasikas*).

The commentaries compare thīna-middha to mental imprisonment or being wrapped in a thick blanket of fog. It is counteracted practically by generating bodily and mental energy (*[viriya](/glossary/viriya/)*), changing postures, splashing water on the face, reflecting on the light-percept (*āloka-saññā*), or reciting suttas aloud. 

It is temporarily countered by the first [jhāna](/glossary/jhana/) factor of applied thought (*[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)*), which directs the mind with crisp force onto the object, and is fully uprooted at the stage of Arahatship.
