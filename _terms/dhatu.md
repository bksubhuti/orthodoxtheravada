### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhātu**: [f.] element. The Visuddhimagga and the Sammohavinodanī derive it from the verb "to bear": "these are called elements (*dhātuyo*) because they bear their own nature (*attano sabhāvaṃ dhārentīti dhātuyo*)", unlike the self of the sectarians, which does not exist by nature (Vism-ii 560:1; Vibh-a 288:1-2). They give two comparisons: just as the mineral constituents of rock, yellow orpiment, red arsenic and the like, are called "elements" in the world, so these are like elements, being various constituents of knowledge and the knowable; or as the name "element" is applied to the constituents of the body such as chyle and blood, so it is applied to the constituents of the five aggregates (Vism-ii 560:2-5).

### Orthodox Definition
*Dhātu* is the Buddha's teaching on elements for dissolving the perception of a soul: "the word 'element' is a designation for what is merely soulless (*nijjīvamattassevetaṃ adhivacanaṃ*); for this reason the Blessed One gave the teaching on elements in passages such as 'this person consists of six elements, bhikkhu'" (Vism-ii 560:6-7; Vibh-a 288:7-9).

The Mūla gives the eighteen elements: the eye element, the form element, the eye-consciousness element; and likewise for ear, nose, tongue, body, and, for the mind, the mind element, the element of the Dhamma and the mind-consciousness element; one who knows and sees these eighteen is "skilled in the elements" (*dhātukusalo bhikkhu*, M-iii 344:2-8, MN 115 Bahudhātukasutta). The Vism also lists them at Vism-ii 556:1. The Mūla also gives six elements: earth, water, fire, air, space and consciousness (*pathavīdhātu, āpodhātu, tejodhātu, vāyodhātu, ākāsadhātu, viññāṇadhātu*; M-iii 1140:1 "Chadhāturo ayaṃ, bhikkhu, puriso", MN 140; Vibh 603:1).

The Visuddhimagga treats the meditation on the four elements as "discernment of the four elements" (*catudhātuvavatthāna*), calling reflection on the elements (*dhātumanasikāro*), the element meditation subject (*dhātukammaṭṭhānaṃ*) and the discernment of the four elements one in meaning; it is shown in brief in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna and in detail in the Mahāhatthipadopama, the Rāhulovāda and the Dhātuvibhaṅga (Vism-i 2098-2099).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Kittāvatā pana, bhante,‘ dhātukusalo bhikkhū ’ ti alaṃ vacanāyā” ti? “Aṭṭhārasa kho imā, ānanda, dhātuyo – cakkhudhātu, rūpadhātu, cakkhuviññāṇadhātu; sotadhātu, saddadhātu, sotaviññāṇadhātu; ghānadhātu, gandhadhātu, ghānaviññāṇadhātu; jivhādhātu, rasadhātu, jivhāviññāṇadhātu; kāyadhātu, phoṭṭhabbadhātu, kāyaviññāṇadhātu; manodhātu, dhammadhātu, manoviññāṇadhātu. Imā kho, ānanda, aṭṭhārasa dhātuyo yato jānāti passati – ettāvatāpi kho, ānanda,‘ dhātukusalo bhikkhū ’ ti alaṃ vacanāyā” ti.**  
> “But venerable sir, in what way is it possible to call him a ‘bhikkhu skilled in the elements’?” “There are, Ānanda, these eighteen elements: the eye element, the form element, the eye-consciousness element; the ear element, the sound realm, the ear-consciousness element; the nose element, the odour element, the nose-consciousness element; the tongue element, the flavour element, the tongue-consciousness element; the body element, the touch realm, the body-consciousness element; the mind-element, the element of the Dhamma, the mind-consciousness-element. When, Ānanda, he knows and sees these eighteen elements, at that point, indeed, it is possible to call him a ‘bhikkhu skilled in the elements’.”  
>

> 172. There are six elements: earth element (pathavīdhātu), water element (āpodhātu), fire element (tejodhātu), air element (vāyodhātu), space element (ākāsadhātu), and consciousness element (viññāṇadhātu).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (the meaning of the term))
> **Apica yathā titthiyānaṃ attā nāma sabhāvato natthi, na evametā, etā pana attano sabhāvaṃ dhārentīti dhātuyo. Yathā loke vicittā haritālamanosilādayo selāvayavā dhātuyoti vuccanti, evametāpi dhātuyo viya dhātuyo. Vicittā hete ñāṇañeyyāvayavāti. Yathā vā sarīrasaṅkhātassa samudāyassa avayavabhūtesu rasasoṇitādīsu aññamaññavisabhāgalakkhaṇaparicchinnesu dhātusamaññā, evametesupi pañcakkhandhasaṅkhātassa attabhāvassa avayavesu dhātusamaññā veditabbā. Aññamaññavisabhāgalakkhaṇaparicchinnā hete cakkhādayoti. Apica dhātūti nijjīvamattassevetaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tathā hi bhagavā “cha dhāturo ayaṃ bhikkhu puriso” tiādīsu (ma. ni.3.344) jīvasaññāsamūhananatthaṃ dhātudesanaṃ akāsīti.**  
> 518. Furthermore, unlike the so-called 'self' of the heretics which does not exist by nature, these are not like that; rather, they are elements because they bear their own nature. Just as in the world various mineral constituents of rock such as yellow orpiment and red arsenic are called elements, so too these are like elements. For they are various constituents of knowledge and the knowable. Or just as the name 'element' is applied to the constituents of the collection called a 'body', such as chyle and blood, which are defined by their mutually distinct characteristics, so too the name 'element' should be understood as applied to these constituents of the body (attabhāva) called the 'five aggregates'. For these eye and other elements are defined by their mutually distinct characteristics. Furthermore, the word 'element' is a designation for what is merely soulless. For this indeed is why the Blessed One gave a teaching on elements in such passages as: 'Bhikkhu, this person consists of six elements,' for the purpose of uprooting the perception of a soul.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Chapter XV, Āyatanadhātuniddesa, Vism-ii 560) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=116&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NTE4LiBBcGljYSB5YXRoxIEgdGl0dGhpecSBbmHhuYMgYXR0xIEgbsSBbWE%3D)


### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga (the four-element meditation))
> **Catudhātuvavatthānabhāvanā 306. Idāni āhāre paṭikkūlasaññānantaraṃ ekaṃ vavatthānanti evaṃ uddiṭṭhassa catudhātuvavatthānassa bhāvanāniddeso anuppatto. Tattha vavatthānanti sabhāvūpalakkhaṇavasena sanniṭṭhānaṃ, catunnaṃ dhātūnaṃ vavatthānaṃ catudhātuvavatthānaṃ. Dhātumanasikāro, dhātukammaṭṭhānaṃ, catudhātuvavatthānanti atthato ekaṃ. Tayidaṃ dvidhā āgataṃ saṅkhepato ca vitthārato ca. Saṅkhepato mahāsatipaṭṭhāne āgataṃ. Vitthārato mahāhatthipadūpame rāhulovāde dhātuvibhaṅge ca.**  
> Development of the Defining of the Four Elements Now, after the perception of repulsiveness in nutriment, the exposition of the development of the defining of the four elements, which was thus listed as “one defining,” has been reached. Herein, “defining” (vavatthāna) is a resolution by way of characterizing individual essences; the defining of the four elements is “defining of the four elements” (catudhātuvavatthāna). “Attention given to elements,” “the element meditation subject,” and “defining of the four elements” are one and the same in meaning. This indeed is presented in two ways: in brief and in detail. In brief, it is presented in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta. In detail, it is presented in the Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta, the Rāhulovāda Sutta, and the Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Chapter XI, Samādhiniddesa, Catudhātuvavatthānabhāvanā, Vism-i 2098) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=342&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dWRoxIF0dXZhdmF0dGjEgW5hYmjEgXZhbsSB)
