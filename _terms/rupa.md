### Grammatical Analysis
**Rūpa**: [nt.] Materiality; form; matter. The Mūla itself derives it from *ruppati* (to be deformed): "*Ruppatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā rūpanti vuccati*", "it is deformed, bhikkhus, therefore it is called form", deformed by cold, heat, hunger, thirst, and contact with flies, mosquitoes, wind, sun and snakes (SN 22.79). The Aṭṭhakathā glosses *ruppati* as "it is troubled, struck, pressed, broken" (Spk-iii 216), and the Aṭṭhasālinī defines *rūpa* as "*ruppanalakkhaṇaṃ vā rūpaṃ*", that which has the characteristic of being molested (As 877).

### Orthodox Definition
*Rūpa* is the first of the five aggregates (*pañcakkhandhā*: *rūpakkhandho, vedanākkhandho, saññākkhandho, saṅkhārakkhandho, viññāṇakkhandho*, Vibh 6). The *rūpakkhandha* is "whatever form is past, future or present, internal or external, gross or subtle, inferior or superior, far or near", gathered together (Vibh 8). In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, all form is "the four great elements (*cattāro mahābhūtā*) and the form derived from the four great elements (*upādāya rūpaṃ*)" (Dhs 584). In the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha it is one of the four ultimate things (*cittaṃ, cetasikaṃ, rūpaṃ, nibbānaṃ*), and the twofold form, the great elements and derived form, is included in eleven categories and becomes twenty-eight by way of specific properties: eighteen produced (*nipphanna*) and ten non-produced (*anipphanna*) (Abhidh-s 3, 11, 16, 17).

The Visuddhimagga gives the detailed definition: "*ruppanalakkhaṇaṃ rūpaṃ, vikiraṇarasaṃ, abyākatapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, viññāṇapadaṭṭhānaṃ*", materiality has the characteristic of being molested, its function is to be dispersed, it is manifested as indeterminate, and its proximate cause is consciousness (Vism-ii 899). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists form together with Nibbāna among the states that have no object (*anārammaṇā*: "*rūpañca, nibbānañca*", Dhs 1526), so form itself does not know an object.

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Kiñca, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ vadetha? Ruppatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ rūpan ’ ti vuccati. Kena ruppati? Sītenapi ruppati, uṇhenapi ruppati, jighacchāyapi ruppati, pipāsāyapi ruppati, ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīsapasamphassenapi ruppati. Ruppatīti kho, bhikkhave, tasmā ‘ rūpan ’ ti vuccati.**  
> “And why, bhikkhus, do you call it form? ‘It is deformed,’ bhikkhus, therefore it is called form. Deformed by what? It is deformed by cold, it is deformed by heat, it is deformed by hunger, it is deformed by thirst, it is deformed by contact with flies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, and serpents. ‘It is deformed,’ bhikkhus, therefore it is called form.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 22.79, Khajjanīya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_03&page_number=71&name=khandhavaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEtpw7FjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCByxatwYeG5gyB2YWRldGhhPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga, the form aggregate)
> **Tattha katamo rūpakkhandho? Yaṃ kiñci rūpaṃ atītānāgatapaccuppannaṃ ajjhattaṃ vā bahiddhā vā oḷārikaṃ vā sukhumaṃ vā hīnaṃ vā paṇītaṃ vā yaṃ dūre santike vā, tadekajjhaṃ abhisaññūhitvā abhisaṅkhipitvā – ayaṃ vuccati rūpakkhandho.**  
> 2. Among these, what is the form aggregate? Whatever form is past, future, or present; internal or external; gross or subtle; inferior or superior; far or near—gathering these together, having summarized them—this is called the form aggregate.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Khandhavibhaṅga, Suttantabhājanīya, para. 2) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=1&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Mi4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyByxatwYWtraGFuZGhvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, all form)
> **Tattha katamaṃ sabbaṃ rūpaṃ? Cattāro ca mahābhūtā, catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāya rūpaṃ – idaṃ vuccati sabbaṃ rūpaṃ.**  
> 584. Therein, what is all form? The four primary elements, and form derived from the four primary elements—this is called all form.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Rūpakaṇḍa, para. 584) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=145&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NTg0LiBUYXR0aGEga2F0YW1h4bmDIHNhYmJh4bmDIHLFq3Bh4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, form has no object)
> **Katame dhammā anārammaṇā? Rūpañca, nibbānañca – ime dhammā anārammaṇā.**  
> 1526. What mental phenomena are states which have no objects? Materiality and Nibbāna – these mental phenomena are states which have no objects.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Aṭṭhakathākaṇḍa, para. 1526) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=287&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTUyNi4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgYW7EgXJhbW1h4bmHxIE%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sāratthappakāsinī)
> **Ruppatīti khoti ettha itīti kāraṇuddeso, yasmā ruppati, tasmā rūpanti vuccatīti attho. Ruppatīti kuppati ghaṭṭīyati pīḷīyati, bhijjatīti attho.**  
> ‘It is deformed, bhikkhus’: herein, ‘that’ is a particle indicating the reason; the meaning is that because it is deformed, it is called form. ‘It is deformed’ means it is troubled, it is struck, it is pressed, it is broken.  
>  
> — [Sāratthappakāsinī](/glossary/saratthappakasini/) (Saṃyuttanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, on SN 22.79) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_sa_03&page_number=267&name=khandhavaggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UnVwcGF0xKt0aSBraG90aSBldHRoYSBpdMSrdGkga8SBcmHhuYd1ZGRlc28sIHlhc23EgQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, characteristic, function, manifestation, proximate cause)
> **Ruppanalakkhaṇaṃ rūpaṃ , vikiraṇarasaṃ, abyākatapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, viññāṇapadaṭṭhānaṃ.**  
> Materiality (rūpaṃ) has the characteristic of being molested (ruppanalakkhaṇaṃ); its function is to be dispersed (vikiraṇarasaṃ); it is manifested as indeterminate (abyākatapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ); and its proximate cause is consciousness (viññāṇapadaṭṭhānaṃ).  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVII, para. 588) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=159&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=UnVwcGFuYWxha2toYeG5h2HhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgLCB2aWtpcmHhuYdhcmFzYeG5gywgYWJ5xIFrYXRhcGFjY3VwYeG5reG5rWjEgW5h4bmDLCB2acOxw7HEgeG5h2FwYWRh4bmt4bmtaMSBbmHhuYMu)

### Quote (Handbook: Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, the eleven categories)
> **Cattāri mahābhūtāni, catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāyarūpanti duvidhampetaṃ rūpaṃ ekādasavidhena saṅgahaṃ gacchati.**  
> 3. The four great elements (mahābhūtāni) and the materiality derived (upādāyarūpaṃ) from the four great elements—this twofold materiality (duvidhampetaṃ rūpaṃ) is included in (saṅgahaṃ gacchati) eleven categories.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Rūpasamuddesa, para. 3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=39&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=My4gQ2F0dMSBcmkgbWFoxIFiaMWrdMSBbmksIGNhdHVubmHDsWNhIG1haMSBYmjFq3TEgW5h4bmDIHVwxIFkxIF5YXLFq3BhbnRp)

### Quote (Handbook: Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, twenty-eight by specific property)
> **Jātirūpameva panettha upacayasantatināmena pavuccatīti ekādasavidhampetaṃ rūpaṃ aṭṭhavīsatividhaṃ hoti sarūpavasena.**  
> 16. Here, the materiality of birth (jātirūpameva) is indeed spoken of (pavuccatīti) by the names of growth and continuity; thus these eleven kinds of materiality become twenty-eight by way of their specific properties (sarūpavasena).  
>

### Quote (Handbook: Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, the list of twenty-eight)
> **Bhūtappasādavisayā, bhāvo hadayamiccapi; Jīvitāhārarūpehi, aṭṭhārasavidhaṃ tathā. Paricchedo ca viññatti, vikāro lakkhaṇanti ca; Anipphannā dasa ceti, aṭṭhavīsavidhaṃ bhave.**  
> Elements (bhūta), sensitive organs (pasāda), objects (visaya), sex (bhāvo), and the heart (hadaya); together with the materiality of life (jīvita) and nutriment (āhāra)—thus concrete matter is eighteenfold. Limitation (paricchedo), intimation (viññatti), mutability (vikāro), and characteristics (lakkhaṇa); these ten are non-produced; thus there would be twenty-eight in all.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Rūpasamuddesa, para. 17) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=40&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=QmjFq3RhcHBhc8SBZGF2aXNhecSBLCBiaMSBdm8gaGFkYXlhbWljY2FwaTs%3D)
