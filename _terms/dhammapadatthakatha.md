### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā**: [f.] The Commentary on the [Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/). Formed by *[dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)* (Verses of Truth) + *[aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/atthakatha/)* (commentary).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Dhammapadaṭṭhakathā* is the commentary on the verses of the *[Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/)*. Its colophon (Nigamanakathā) names the author: it "was composed by the Elder Buddhaghosa", of the Mahāvihāra tradition, and states its own size as seventy-two *bhāṇavāras*.

The commentary explains why the Buddha spoke the verses: in the opening verses it says that "based on various reasons" (*taṃ taṃ kāraṇamāgamma*) the Teacher taught the Dhammapada verses. Its colophon counts 423 verses and 305 stories (*vatthu*), set out by chapter: for example fourteen stories in the Yamaka Vagga and forty in the Brāhmaṇa Vagga, the twenty-sixth and last. So the stories are fewer than the verses, since some stories explain several verses. Each story begins by naming the place, the person and the occasion of the teaching, then gives the verse and its word-by-word explanation, and usually closes with the result of the discourse (for example "at the end of the discourse many attained the fruit of stream-entry").

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā, stories and verses)
> **Ettāvatā sabbapaṭhame yamakavagge cuddasa vatthūni, appamādavagge nava, cittavagge nava, pupphavagge dvādasa, bālavagge pannarasa, paṇḍitavagge ekādasa, arahantavagge dasa, sahassavagge cuddasa, pāpavagge dvādasa, daṇḍavagge ekādasa, jarāvagge nava, attavagge dasa, lokavagge ekādasa, buddhavagge nava, sukhavagge aṭṭha, piyavagge nava, kodhavagge aṭṭha, malavagge dvādasa, dhammaṭṭhavagge dasa, maggavagge dvādasa, pakiṇṇakavagge nava, nirayavagge nava, nāgavagge aṭṭha, taṇhāvagge dvādasa, bhikkhuvagge dvādasa, brāhmaṇavagge cattālīsāti pañcādhikāni tīṇi vatthusatāni pakāsetvā nātisaṅkhepanātivitthāravasena uparacitā dvāsattatibhāṇavārapamāṇā dhammapadassa atthavaṇṇanā niṭṭhitāti. Pattaṃ dhammapadaṃ yena, dhammarājenanuttaraṃ; Gāthā dhammapade tena, bhāsitā yā mahesinā. Satevīsā catussatā, catusaccavibhāvinā; Satattayañhi vatthūnaṃ, pañcādhikā samuṭṭhitā.**  
> In this way, having set forth three hundred and five stories—fourteen in the Yamaka Vagga, nine in the Appamāda Vagga, nine in the Citta Vagga, twelve in the Puppha Vagga, fifteen in the Bāla Vagga, eleven in the Paṇḍita Vagga, ten in the Arahanta Vagga, fourteen in the Sahassa Vagga, twelve in the Pāpa Vagga, eleven in the Daṇḍa Vagga, nine in the Jarā Vagga, ten in the Atta Vagga, eleven in the Loka Vagga, nine in the Buddha Vagga, eight in the Sukha Vagga, nine in the Piya Vagga, eight in the Kodha Vagga, twelve in the Mala Vagga, ten in the Dhammaṭṭha Vagga, twelve in the Magga Vagga, nine in the Pakiṇṇaka Vagga, nine in the Niraya Vagga, eight in the Nāga Vagga, twelve in the Taṇhā Vagga, twelve in the Bhikkhu Vagga, and forty in the Brāhmaṇa Vagga—the commentary on the Dhammapada, consisting of seventy-two bhāṇavāras and composed neither too concisely nor too elaborately, is finished. The path of Dhamma which the King of Dhamma attained, that verse in the Dhammapada was spoken by the Great Sage. Four hundred and twenty-three verses were revealed by him who manifested the four truths; surpassing three hundred, the stories arose.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā, the author)
> **Paramavisuddhasaddhābuddhivīriyapaṭimaṇḍitena sīlācārajjavamaddavādiguṇasamudayasamuditena sakasamayasamayantaragahanajjhogāhaṇasamatthena paññāveyyattiyasamannāgatena tipiṭakapariyattippabhede sāṭṭhakathe satthusāsane appaṭihatañāṇappabhāvena mahāveyyākaraṇena karaṇasampattijanitasukhaviniggatamadhurodāravacanalāvaṇṇayuttena yuttamuttavādinā vādīvarena mahākavinā pabhinnapaṭisambhidāparivāre chaḷabhiññāpaṭisambhidādippabhedaguṇapaṭimaṇḍite uttarimanussadhamme suppatiṭṭhitabuddhīnaṃ theravaṃsappadīpānaṃ therānaṃ mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena vipulavisuddhabuddhinā buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena katāyaṃ dhammapadaṭṭhakathā –**  
> This Dhammapada Commentary was composed by the Elder Buddhaghosa—who is adorned with supremely pure faith, wisdom, and energy; who is established in the aggregate of virtues such as morality, conduct, integrity, and gentleness; who is capable of probing the depths of his own and others’ doctrines; who is endowed with readiness of wisdom; who is a master of the three Piṭakas, the Pariyatti, and the commentaries; who has the brilliance of unimpeded knowledge in the Teacher’s Dispensation; who is a great grammarian; who is endowed with the beauty of sweet, noble words born of correct execution; who is a speaker of truth; who is a great poet; who is surrounded by the analytical knowledges; who is adorned with virtues such as the six direct knowledges and the analytical knowledges; whose wisdom is firmly established in the higher human states; who is a lamp of the Elder lineage; and who is an ornament to the lineage of the Great Monastery Elders—  
>

### Textual References
* **Subject Matter**: Commentary on the [Dhammapada](/glossary/dhammapada/); see the Ganthārambhakathā (Dhp-a 7-17) and the Nigamanakathā (Dhp-a 5210-5224).
* **Key Features**: The story of Visākhā (Dhp-a, Puppha Vagga, "8. Visākhāvatthu", Dhp-a 1104-1121), told on the occasion of the verse "Yathāpi puppharāsimhā". The Aṭṭhakathā counts forty stories in the Brāhmaṇa Vagga and 305 in all (Dhp-a 5211).
