### Grammatical Analysis
**Santati**: [f.] Continuity; unbroken succession; continuum. Derived from *saṃ* (together) + root *tan* (to stretch, extend). The commentary glosses it as *pavatti*, continuous occurring (As 2119:5-6).

### Orthodox Definition
*Santati* has two usages in the texts:
1. **As a Material Phenomenon**: In the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/), *rūpassa santati* ("continuity of form") is the second of the four characteristics of matter. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī says: *yo rūpassa upacayo, sā rūpassa santati*, "that which is the growth of form is the continuity of form" (Dhs 1372:2; see *[upacaya](/glossary/upacaya/)*). The Atthasālinī: after the growth of accumulated forms, in the later period of occurring, it is called *santati*, meaning *pavatti*, continuous occurring (As 2119:5-6). The Visuddhimagga: *pavattilakkhaṇā rūpassa santati, anuppabandhanarasā, anupacchedapaccupaṭṭhānā, anuppabandhakarūpapadaṭṭhānā*: its characteristic is occurrence, its function is linking, its manifestation is non-interruption, its proximate cause is the materiality to be linked (Vism-ii 352:2). By meaning it is the same as upacaya, both being designations for birth-form (Vism-ii 352:3-5).
2. **As what conceals impermanence**: The Visuddhimagga says the characteristic of impermanence does not appear because, through not attending to rise and fall, it is concealed by continuity (*santatiyā paṭicchannattā*); but when continuity is disrupted by grasping rise and fall, the characteristic of impermanence appears truly as it is (Vism-ii 1655:2, 1655:5; also Vibh-a 198:2).

The Saṃyutta reciters speak of two kinds of continuity, material and immaterial (*rūpasantati arūpasantati*), the material one illustrated by the water-line that remains where one has stepped into water (Vism-ii 258:1).

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa santati? Yo rūpassa upacayo, sā rūpassa santati – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa santati.**  
> 642. Which form is the continuity of form? That which is the growth of form – that is the continuity of form – this is that continuity of form.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs 642, Rūpakaṇḍa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=169&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NjQyLiBLYXRhbWHhuYMgdGHhuYMgcsWrcGHhuYMgcsWrcGFzc2Egc2FudGF0aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Lakkhaṇāni pana kissa amanasikārā kena paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhahanti? Aniccalakkhaṇaṃ tāva udayabbayānaṃ amanasikārā santatiyā paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhāti. Udayabbayampana pariggahetvā santatiyā vikopitāya aniccalakkhaṇaṃ yāthāvasarasato upaṭṭhāti.**  
> 739. But by not attending to what do the characteristics not appear, and by what are they concealed? Firstly, the characteristic of impermanence does not appear because, by not attending to rise and fall, it is concealed by continuity. But when continuity is disrupted by grasping rise and fall, the characteristic of impermanence appears truly as it is.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Vism-ii 1655, §739) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=276&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzM5LiBMYWtraGHhuYfEgW5pIHBhbmEga2lzc2EgYW1hbmFzaWvEgXLEgSBrZW5h)
