### Grammatical Analysis
**Vimānavatthu**: [nt.] the Stories of the Mansions (TPR: "Vimānavatthu Pāli"). Formed by *vimāna* (celestial mansion) + *vatthu* (story). The chapter and story names show it: for example *Paṭhamapīṭhavimānavatthu*, "the First Chair Mansion Story".

### Orthodox Definition
The *Vimānavatthu* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya. In the list of the Khuddaka books given in the Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā it is sixth: *Khuddakapāṭho, dhammapadaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, suttanipāto, vimānavatthu, petavatthu, ...* (Pj-i 24:5). The Pāḷi has seven chapters (*vagga*): Pīṭha, Cittalatā, Pāricchattaka, Mañjiṭṭhaka, Mahāratha, Pāyāsi and Sunikkhitta.

The commentary, the Paramatthadīpanī, explains how it was spoken: it proceeds by way of questions and answers. The answer-verses were spoken by the various devas; the question-verses were spoken in part by the Buddha, in part by Sakka and others, and in part by disciples. Most of them were spoken by the Venerable Mahāmoggallāna, "foremost among those with psychic power" (Vv-a 26:2-4).

In the first story the Venerable Mahāmoggallāna asks a deva who shines with a golden chair what merit she did as a human; she answers that she gave a seat to those who arrived, paid homage with joined palms, and gave a gift according to her ability.

### Quote
> **“Pucchāmi taṃ devi mahānubhāve, manussabhūtā kimakāsi puññaṃ; Kenāsi evaṃ jalitānubhāvā, vaṇṇo ca te sabbadisā pabhāsatī” ti. 4. Sā devatā attamanā, moggallānena pucchitā; Pañhaṃ puṭṭhā viyākāsi, yassa kammassidaṃ phalaṃ. 5. “Ahaṃ manussesu manussabhūtā, abbhāgatānāsanakaṃ adāsiṃ; Abhivādayiṃ añjalikaṃ akāsiṃ, yathānubhāvañca adāsi dānaṃ.**  
> 3. “I ask you, mighty devas, what merit did you do when you were a human? Because of what do you have such blazing power, and your color shines in all directions?” 4. That devatā, elated, having been questioned by Moggallāna, being questioned about that question, explained the fruit of the kamma. 5. “I, when I was a human among human beings, gave a chair to those who had arrived. I paid homage, I made an añjali (palm-fold), and I gave a gift according to my ability.

> It is said: this Stories of the Mansions proceeded in two ways—by way of questions and by way of answers. There, the answer-verses were spoken by the various devas; but as for the question-verses, some were spoken by the Blessed One, some by Sakka and others, and some by senior monk disciples. Among them, most were spoken by the venerable Mahāmoggallāna, who accumulated the equipment of merit and knowledge for the status of a chief disciple of the Buddha, the Blessed One, for one incalculable eon and a hundred thousand eons more; who gradually fulfilled the perfections of a disciple and attained the culmination of the entire knowledge of a disciple's perfections, accompanied by the special qualities of the six supernormal knowledges and the four analytical knowledges; who was established in the position of second chief disciple; and who was designated by the Blessed One as foremost among those with psychic power.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Vimānavatthu* (Vv 3–), Pīṭhavagga, *Paṭhamapīṭhavimānavatthu* (Vv 6–) – a deva questioned by Mahāmoggallāna, who gave a seat to arriving guests. The seven chapters end at Vv 513 (Pīṭha), 874, 1521, 1986, 2550, 2718 and 3273.
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* (Vv-a 26, how and by whom it was spoken; Vv-a 3987 names the commentary "Paramatthadīpanī"); *Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā* (Pj-i 24:5) for the place of the book in the Khuddaka Nikāya.
