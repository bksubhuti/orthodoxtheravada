### Grammatical Analysis
**Vimānavatthu**: [nt.] Stories of the Heavenly Mansions. Formed by *vimāna* (heavenly palace, mansion, flying chariot) + *vatthu* (story, basis, subject).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Vimānavatthu* is the sixth book of the Khuddaka Nikāya. It is a collection of 85 poetic stories specifically demonstrating the direct, immediate, and magnificent karmic fruits of wholesome deeds (*[puñña](/glossary/punna/)*).

The structural premise of the book involves Venerable Mahāmoggallāna (the foremost disciple in psychic powers) traveling to the heavenly realms (*devaloka*). Upon seeing various devas living in spectacularly radiant mansions (*vimānas*), he asks them what specific merit they performed in their previous human life to earn such glory. The devas reply, revealing that even simple acts of giving a spoonful of rice, sweeping a monastery, or keeping the precepts lead to massive celestial rewards. 

The text serves as a primary pedagogical tool to inspire faith and generosity among the laity.

### Quote
> **Pīṭhaṁ te sovaṇṇamayaṁ uḷāraṁ,**  
> Your splendid seat is made of gold,  
> 
> **Manojavaṁ gacchati yenakāmaṁ;**  
> Swift as thought, it goes wherever you wish;  
> 
> **Alaṅkate malyadhare suvatthe,**  
> Adorned with garlands and finely dressed,  
> 
> **Obhāsasi vijjurivabbhakūṭaṁ.**  
> You shine like lightning in a mass of clouds.  
> 
> **Pucchāmi taṁ devi mahānubhāve,**  
> I ask you, goddess of great power,  
> 
> **Manussabhūtā kimakāsi puññaṁ;**  
> When you were human, what merit did you do?  
> 
> **Ahaṁ manussesu manussabhūtā,**  
> When I was a human among human beings,  
> 
> **Abbhāgatānāsanakaṁ adāsiṁ;**  
> I gave a seat to a visitor.  
> 
> **Abhivādayiṁ añjalikaṁ akāsiṁ,**  
> I paid homage and raised my hands in reverence,  
> 
> **Yathānubhāvañca adāsi dānaṁ.**  
> And gave a gift according to my means.  
> 
> **Tena metādiso vaṇṇo, tena me idha mijjhati;**  
> Because of that, my appearance is such, and here this succeeds for me;  
> 
> **Uppajjanti ca me bhogā, ye keci manaso piyā.**  
> And whatever enjoyments are dear to the mind arise for me.  
> 
> — *Paṭhamapīṭhavimānavatthu* (Vimānavatthu 1)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Pīṭhavimāna* – A [deva](/glossary/deva/) explains she received a golden mansion simply because, as a poor woman, she offered a small stool for a monk to sit on.
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* – Dhammapāla's commentary providing the earthly background story of the devas before they died.
