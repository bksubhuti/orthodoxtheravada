### Grammatical Analysis
**Āsava**: [m.] influx; taint; canker. The Atthasālinī and the Papañcasūdanī explain *āsavantīti āsavā*, "they flow, therefore they are āsavas": they flow from the eye and so on up to the mind (*cakkhutopi … manatopi sandanti pavattantīti*); or they flow from the Dhamma-sphere up to the change-of-lineage (*gotrabhu*) and in space up to the peak of existence (*bhavagga*), the prefix *ā* having the sense of including (*antokaraṇattho*); and liquors and the like that have fermented for a long time are called *āsavā* in the sense of long fermentation (*cirapārivāsiyaṭṭhena*) (As 878:1-8; Ps-i 367:4-8).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla, the noble disciple of right view understands the taint, its origin, its cessation and the path to its cessation (M-i 276:2-3). Here there are three taints, *kāmāsavo, bhavāsavo, avijjāsavo*, and "from the origin of ignorance comes the origin of the taints" (M-i 276:4-5, MN 9). The Abhidhamma gives four: *kāmāsavo, bhavāsavo, diṭṭhāsavo, avijjāsavo* (Dhs 1878:2, §1102). The Atthasālinī defines them (As 2283:1-4):
1. *Kāmāsavo*: lust connected with the five strands of sensual pleasure.
2. *Bhavāsavo*: desire and lust for the form and formless existences, delight in jhāna, and lust accompanied by the eternalist view.
3. *Diṭṭhāsavo*: the sixty-two views.
4. *Avijjāsavo*: ignorance regarding the eight grounds.

The Atthasālinī explains why the number varies: by meaning, the taints are one-fold, in the sense of long fermentation; in the Vinaya they come in two kinds (Pārājika 39), and in the Saḷāyatana Saṃyutta in three (As 2283:5-8). Freedom from them is the mark of the arahant: "a bhikkhu is an arahant, one whose taints are destroyed" (*arahaṃ hoti khīṇāsavo*, A-iii 59:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **Katamo panāvuso, āsavo, katamo āsavasamudayo, katamo āsavanirodho, katamā āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadāti? Tayome, āvuso, āsavā – kāmāsavo, bhavāsavo, avijjāsavo. Avijjāsamudayā āsavasamudayo, avijjānirodhā āsavanirodho, ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo āsavanirodhagāminī paṭipadā, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi … pe … sammāsamādhi.**  
> And what, friends, are the taints, what is the origin of the taints, what is the cessation of the taints, what is the path leading to the cessation of the taints? There are these three taints: the taint of sensual desire, the taint of being, and the taint of ignorance. From the origin of ignorance comes the origin of the taints. From the cessation of ignorance comes the cessation of the taints. Just this noble eight-factored path is the path leading to the cessation of the taints; that is to say, right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right concentration.”  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katame dhammā āsavā? Cattāro āsavā – kāmāsavo, bhavāsavo, diṭṭhāsavo, avijjāsavo.**  
> 1102. What states are taints? There are four taints: the taint of sensual desire, the taint of being, the taint of views, and the taint of ignorance.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Āsavagocchaka, §1102) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=221&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEwMi4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgxIFzYXbEgT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Āsavaniddese pañcakāmaguṇiko rāgo kāmāsavo nāma. Rūpārūpabhavesu chandarāgo jhānanikanti sassatadiṭṭhisahajāto rāgo bhavavasena patthanā bhavāsavo nāma. Dvāsaṭṭhi diṭṭhiyo diṭṭhāsavo nāma. Aṭṭhasu ṭhānesu aññāṇaṃ avijjāsavo nāma.**  
> 1102. In the enumeration of taints, lust connected with the five strands of sensual pleasure is called the taint of sensual desire (kāmāsavo). Desire and lust for form and formless existences, desire for jhāna, lust accompanied by the eternalist view, and aspiration due to existence are called the taint of being (bhavāsavo). The sixty-two views are called the taint of views (diṭṭhāsavo). Ignorance regarding the eight grounds is called the taint of ignorance (avijjāsavo).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 2283, Āsavaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=401&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MTEwMi4gxIBzYXZhbmlkZGVzZSBwYcOxY2FrxIFtYWd14bmHaWtvIHLEgWdvIGvEgW3EgXNhdm8gbsSBbWEu)
