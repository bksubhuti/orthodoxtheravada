### Grammatical Analysis
**Petavatthu**: [nt.] Stories of the Hungry Ghosts. Formed by *[peta](/glossary/peta/)* (departed spirit, hungry ghost) + *vatthu* (story).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Petavatthu* is the seventh book of the Khuddaka Nikāya, serving as the dark, karmic mirror to the *[Vimānavatthu](/glossary/vimanavatthu/)*. It contains 51 stories demonstrating the horrific karmic consequences of unwholesome deeds, specifically stinginess, malice, and disrespect for the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/).

Venerable Mahāmoggallāna (and other monks) encounter wretched beings (*petas*) suffering from extreme hunger, thirst, or grotesque physical deformities. When asked about their past karma, the ghosts recount their earthly deeds of greed, stealing from the monastic order, or abusing their parents. 

Crucially, the *Petavatthu* establishes the orthodox Theravāda mechanics for the "sharing of merit" (*pattidāna*). It demonstrates that ghosts cannot grow their own food or find water; they can only be relieved of their suffering when living relatives make offerings to the Saṅgha and dedicate the merit specifically to them.

### Quote
> **Tirokuṭṭesu tiṭṭhanti, sandhisiṅghāṭakesu ca;**  
> They stand outside the walls, and at crossroads and junctions;  
> 
> **Dvārabāhāsu tiṭṭhanti, āgantvāna sakaṁ gharaṁ.**  
> They stand by the doorposts, having come back to their own homes.  
> 
> **Evaṁ dadanti ñātīnaṁ, ye honti anukampakā;**  
> Thus those who are compassionate give for their relatives;  
> 
> **Suciṁ paṇītaṁ kālena, kappiyaṁ pānabhojanaṁ;**  
> Pure, excellent, allowable food and drink at the proper time;  
> 
> **"Idaṁ vo ñātīnaṁ hotu, sukhitā hontu ñātayo".**  
> "May this be for our relatives; may our relatives be happy."  
> 
> **Na hi tattha kasī atthi, gorakkhettha na vijjati;**  
> For there is no farming there, nor is cattle-keeping found there;  
> 
> **Vaṇijjā tādisī natthi, hiraññena kayākayaṁ;**  
> There is no such trade, no buying and selling with money;  
> 
> **Ito dinnena yāpenti, petā kālagatā tahiṁ.**  
> The petas who have passed away live there on what is given from here.  
> 
> **Evamevaṁ ito dinnaṁ, petānaṁ upakappati.**  
> In just this way, what is given from here serves the petas.  
> 
> — *Tirokuṭṭapetavatthu* (Petavatthu 1.5)
>
> **Kāyo te sabbasovaṇṇo, sabbā obhāsate disā;**  
> Your whole body is golden, shining in every direction;  
> 
> **Mukhaṁ te sūkarasseva, kiṁ kammamakarī pure.**  
> But your mouth is like a pig's. What deed did you do in the past?  
> 
> **Kāyena saññato āsiṁ, vācāyāsimasaññato;**  
> I was restrained in body, but I was unrestrained in speech;  
> 
> **Tena metādiso vaṇṇo, yathā passasi nārada.**  
> That is why my appearance is like this, as you see, Nārada.  
> 
> **Mākāsi mukhasā pāpaṁ, mā kho sūkaramukho ahū.**  
> Do not do evil with the mouth; do not become pig-mouthed.  
> 
> — *Sūkaramukhapetavatthu* (Petavatthu 1.2)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Tirokuṭṭa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Pv 1.5) – The foundational discourse on merit-sharing, describing ghosts standing outside the walls of their former homes waiting for their relatives to make offerings on their behalf.
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* – Detailing the exact malicious acts that lead to rebirth in the [Peta](/glossary/peta/) realm.
