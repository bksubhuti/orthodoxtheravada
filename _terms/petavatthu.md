### Grammatical Analysis
**Petavatthu**: [nt.] Stories of the Hungry Ghosts. Formed by *[peta](/glossary/peta/)* (departed spirit, hungry ghost) + *vatthu* (story).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Petavatthu* is the seventh book of the Khuddaka Nikāya in the Khuddakanikāya list (Pj-i 24:5: "Khuddakapāṭho, dhammapadaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, suttanipāto, vimānavatthu, petavatthu, ..."), following the *[Vimānavatthu](/glossary/vimanavatthu/)* in that list. It contains 51 stories in four vaggas: "Fifty-one stories in all, divided into four recitation portions" (Pv 2654:1); the Aṭṭhakathā counts 12, 13, 10 and 16 stories (Pv-a 30:2). The stories show the bitter fruit of evil kamma in the ghost realm (Pv-a 3214:1-3215:1).

The Petavatthu commentary says the work proceeds in two ways: by way of an occasion (spoken by the Buddha) and by way of question and answer, where the questions were asked by elders such as Nārada and answered by the petas themselves (Pv-a 26:2-3). In the story of Sāriputta's former mother (Pv 393:1, "2. Sāriputtattheramātupetivatthu") the peta says "I was your mother in former lives, reborn in the ghost realm, afflicted by hunger and thirst" (Pv 398:1-399:1); Sāriputta then calls Moggallāna, Anuruddha and Kappina (Pv 414:1). Other petas recount their past deeds, for instance a being who "formerly killed his mother" (Pv 1459:1).

The *Petavatthu* also sets out the "sharing of merit" (*pattidāna*): petas do not plough or trade (Khp 171:1-172:1, the Tirokuṭṭa verses, repeated in Pv), and what is given here is of benefit to the departed (Pv 79:1); the commentary says that people give for their dead kin, saying "may this be for our relatives", and they receive it (Pv-a 126:2).

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Tirokuṭṭa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Pv 1.5, "5. Tirokuṭṭapetavatthu", Pv 56:1; the same verses are Khp 7, "7. Tirokuṭṭasuttaṃ", Khp 153:1) – ghosts stand outside walls, at crossroads, by doorposts (Pv 58:1-59:1; Khp 155:1-156:1).
* **Commentary**: *[Paramatthadīpanī](/glossary/paramatthadipani/)* (Pv-a; named "paramatthadīpanī" in its closing verse, Pv-a 3223:1) – the occasions and details of each story (Pv-a 26:1-4, 30:2, 126:2).
