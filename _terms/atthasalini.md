### Grammatical Analysis
**Atthasālinī**: [f.] the name of the commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaṇī. The Pāḷi text itself gives the name as *Aṭṭhasālinī*: its title page reads "Abhidhammapiṭake … Aṭṭhasālinī nāma … Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā" (As 2:1-4:1), and its colophon says *ayaṃ aṭṭhasālinī nāma dhammasaṅgahaṭṭhakathā*, "this commentary on the Dhammasaṅgaha, named Aṭṭhasālinī" (As 2483:1). *Atthasālinī* is the usual romanization of the same title. The texts give no explanation of the name.

### Orthodox Definition
The *Atthasālinī* is the Aṭṭhakathā on the *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)*, the first book of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka. The colophon says it was composed by the elder given the name Buddhaghosa by his teachers, a resident of the Mahāvihāra (As 2483:1). In its introductory verses the author says that the Abhidhamma which the Buddha taught to the devas and then in outline to the Elder Sāriputta was brought down by Sāriputta to the bhikkhus and was recited at the Saṅgīti; that the ancient commentary recited by Mahākassapa and others was brought to the island by the Elder Mahinda and composed in the language of the islanders; and that he now sets it in a faultless language, unmixed with the views of other schools, "illustrating the conclusions of the dwellers of the Mahāvihāra" (As 22:1-37:1, quoted in two blocks above).

The book opens with an introductory discourse, the Nidānakathā (As 46:1 to As 829:5, "Ayaṃ tāva nidānakathā"). It explains the Abhidhamma in the order of the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, and it often defines a dhamma by its characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause (*lakkhaṇa, rasa, paccupaṭṭhāna, padaṭṭhāna*), for example mindfulness (As 1207:1) and loving-kindness (As 1537:1).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī (colophon))
> **Paramavisuddhasaddhābuddhivīriyapaṭimaṇḍitena sīlācārajjavamaddavādiguṇasamudayasamuditena sakasamayasamayantaragahanajjhogāhaṇasamatthena paññāveyyattiyasamannāgatena tipiṭakapariyattippabhede sāṭṭhakathe satthusāsane appaṭihatañāṇappabhāvena mahāveyyākaraṇena karaṇasampattijanitasukhaviniggatamadhurodāravacanalāvaṇṇayuttena yuttamuttavādinā vādīvarena mahākavinā pabhinnapaṭisambhidāparivāre chaḷabhiññādippabhedaguṇapaṭimaṇḍite uttarimanussadhamme suppatiṭṭhitabuddhīnaṃ theravaṃsappadīpānaṃ therānaṃ mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena vipulavisuddhabuddhinā buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena katā ayaṃ aṭṭhasālinī nāma dhammasaṅgahaṭṭhakathā.**  
> This Aṭṭhasālinī, the Commentary to the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, was composed by the Elder, given the name Buddhaghosa by his teachers, who was adorned with supremely pure faith, wisdom, and energy; endowed with an abundance of virtues such as morality, good conduct, rectitude, and gentleness; capable of immersing deeply into the profundity of his own doctrine and other doctrines; endowed with sharpness of wisdom; possessing unhindered power of knowledge in the Teacher’s dispensation, which has divisions of the Tipiṭaka Pariyatti and its commentaries; a great grammarian; endowed with the charm of sweet and sublime words, uttered with ease, born of perfect articulation; one who speaks appropriately and delightfully; a supreme debater; a great poet; surrounded by the analytical knowledges that have been differentiated; adorned with qualities distinguished by the six supernormal powers, etc.; whose wisdom was well-established in the superhuman Dhammas; like a lamp of the lineage of Elders; residing in the Great Monastery; being an ornament of the lineage of Elders; and having vast and pure wisdom.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī (introductory verses, the Abhidhamma))
> **Yaṃ devadevo devānaṃ, desetvā nayato puna; Therassa sāriputtassa, samācikkhi vināyako. Anotattadahe katvā, upaṭṭhānaṃ mahesino; Yañca sutvāna so thero, āharitvā mahītalaṃ. Bhikkhūnaṃ payirudāhāsi, iti bhikkhūhi dhārito; Saṅgītikāle saṅgīto, vedehamuninā puna.**  
> That which the god of gods taught to the devas, and then explained again by way of method to the Elder Sāriputta, as the Guide declared it. Having performed service for the Great Seer at Lake Anotatta; and the Elder, having heard it and brought it to the earth; he recited it to the bhikkhus, and thus it was retained by them; and at the time of the Council, it was again chanted by the Vedeha Sage.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 22-27, Ganthārambhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=2&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=WWHhuYMgZGV2YWRldm8gZGV2xIFuYeG5gywgZGVzZXR2xIEgbmF5YXRvIHB1bmE7)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī (introductory verses, the ancient commentary))
> **Yā mahākassapādīhi, vasīhiṭṭhakathā purā; Saṅgītā anusaṅgītā, pacchāpi ca isīhi yā. Ābhatā pana therena, mahindenetamuttamaṃ; Yā dīpaṃ dīpavāsīnaṃ, bhāsāya abhisaṅkhatā. Apanetvā tato bhāsaṃ, tambapaṇṇinivāsinaṃ; Āropayitvā niddosaṃ, bhāsaṃ tantinayānugaṃ. Nikāyantaraladdhīhi, asammissaṃ anākulaṃ; Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ, dīpayanto vinicchayaṃ.**  
> The ancient commentary which was chanted in the past by the Elders with mastery, beginning with Mahākassapa; and which was later chanted again by the sages. It was brought by the Elder Mahinda to this supreme island; and it was composed in the language of the island's inhabitants. Having removed from it the language of the inhabitants of Tambapaṇṇi; and having translated it into the faultless language that follows the method of the canonical texts. Unmixed with the views of other schools, and without confusion; illustrating the conclusions of the dwellers of the Mahāvihāra.  
>
