### Grammatical Analysis
**Udayabbayañāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of arising and passing away. Formed by *udaya* (arising) + *vaya* (passing, decay; the compound has *abbaya*) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). In the Paṭisambhidāmagga the full name is *udayabbayānupassane ñāṇaṃ*, "knowledge in the contemplation of rise and fall", the Visuddhimagga's *udayabbayānupassanāñāṇa*.

### Orthodox Definition
The Mūla defines it in the Paṭisambhidāmagga: "paccuppannānaṃ dhammānaṃ vipariṇāmānupassane paññā udayabbayānupassane ñāṇaṃ", the wisdom in contemplating the change of presently arisen dhammas is knowledge of contemplation of rise and fall (Paṭis 6, 49). Of what is present (*paccuppanna*, "jāta", born), "its characteristic of generation (*nibbattilakkhaṇa*) is rise (*udaya*), its characteristic of change (*vipariṇāmalakkhaṇa*) is fall (*vaya*), and the contemplation is knowledge" (Paṭis 283:2); this is said of materiality, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness, and so on. One who sees the rise of the five aggregates sees twenty-five characteristics, one who sees the fall twenty-five, and one who sees rise and fall fifty (Paṭis 284:2-3); the Paṭisambhidāmagga then lists, for each aggregate, the five characteristics of its rise (for form, for example, the origin of ignorance, of craving, of kamma and of nutriment, and the characteristic of generation, Paṭis 286).

The Visuddhimagga places it right after comprehension knowledge (*sammasanañāṇa*), in the Purification by Knowledge and Vision of What Is and Is Not the Path (XX): having gone beyond comprehension, the meditator "begins the effort for the attainment" of this knowledge (Vism XX §723). At this stage, called "tender insight" (*taruṇavipassanā*), the ten imperfections of insight (*dasa vipassanupakkilesā*) arise for the one who practises correctly: illumination (*obhāso*), knowledge, rapture, tranquillity, pleasure, decision (*adhimokkho*), exertion, establishment (*upaṭṭhānaṃ*), equanimity, and attachment (*nikanti*) (Vism XX §732). They do not arise for a noble disciple who has attained penetration, nor for one who has gone astray, nor for a lazy person who has abandoned the meditation subject (Vism-ii 1609:2). The meditator who takes the illumination to be the path or the fruit has gone off the track of insight (Vism-ii 1613:2-3).

When he is free of the imperfections, he applies effort to this knowledge again, "for the purpose of discerning the characteristics" (*lakkhaṇasallakkhaṇattho*): the knowledge of rise and fall earlier "was defiled by the ten imperfections and unable to discern the three characteristics as they truly are, but the knowledge free of imperfections is able" (Vism XXI §738). The Visuddhimagga then explains how the three characteristics, hidden by continuity, postures and compactness, appear when rise and fall are grasped (Vism XXI §739; see [tilakkhaṇa](/glossary/tilakkhana/)).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, definition)
> **Kathaṃ paccuppannānaṃ dhammānaṃ vipariṇāmānupassane paññā udayabbayānupassane ñāṇaṃ? Jātaṃ rūpaṃ paccuppannaṃ, tassa nibbattilakkhaṇaṃ udayo, vipariṇāmalakkhaṇaṃ vayo, anupassanā ñāṇaṃ. Jātā vedanā … pe … jātā saññā … jātā saṅkhārā … jātaṃ viññāṇaṃ … jātaṃ cakkhu … pe … jāto bhavo paccuppanno, tassa nibbattilakkhaṇaṃ udayo, vipariṇāmalakkhaṇaṃ vayo, anupassanā ñāṇaṃ.**  
> 49. How is it that understanding of contemplating presently arisen ideas' change is knowledge of contemplation of rise and fall? Presently arisen materiality is born; the characteristic of its generation is rise, the characteristic of its change is decay, and the contemplation is knowledge. Presently arisen feeling... perception... volitional formations... consciousness... eye... existence is born; the characteristic of its generation is rise, the characteristic of its change is decay, and the contemplation is knowledge.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, fifty characteristics)
> **Pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ udayaṃ passanto kati lakkhaṇāni passati, vayaṃ passanto kati lakkhaṇāni passati, udayabbayaṃ passanto kati lakkhaṇāni passati? Pañcannaṃ khandhānaṃ udayaṃ passanto pañcavīsati lakkhaṇāni passati, vayaṃ passanto pañcavīsati lakkhaṇāni passati; udayabbayaṃ passanto paññāsa lakkhaṇāni passati.**  
> 50. How many characteristics does he see who sees the rise of the five aggregates? How many characteristics does he see who sees the decay? How many characteristics does he see who sees the rise and fall? He who sees the rise of the five aggregates sees twenty-five characteristics; he who sees the decay sees twenty-five characteristics; he who sees the rise and fall sees fifty characteristics.  
>  
> — *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* (Udayabbayañāṇaniddesa, §50) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=52&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NTAuIFBhw7FjYW5uYeG5gyBraGFuZGjEgW5h4bmDIHVkYXlh4bmDIHBhc3NhbnRvIGthdGk%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XX, beginning the effort)
> **So evaṃ aniccānupassanādipaṭipakkhānaṃ niccasaññādīnaṃ pahānena visuddhañāṇo sammasanañāṇassa pāraṃ gantvā, yaṃ taṃ sammasanañāṇānantaraṃ “paccuppannānaṃ dhammānaṃ vipariṇāmānupassane paññā udayabbayānupassane ñāṇan” ti (paṭi. ma. mātikā 1.6) udayabbayānupassanaṃ vuttaṃ, tassa adhigamāya yogaṃ ārabhati.**  
> 723. Thus, with his knowledge purified by the abandonment of the perceptions of permanence and so on, which are the opposites of the contemplation of impermanence and the rest, having gone beyond comprehension knowledge, he begins the effort for the attainment of that which was stated immediately after comprehension knowledge as: “Wisdom in the contemplation of the change of present states is the knowledge of contemplation of arising and vanishing” (Paṭis I 6).  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XX, §723, Udayabbayañāṇakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=265&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzIzLiBTbyBldmHhuYMgYW5pY2PEgW51cGFzc2FuxIFkaXBh4bmtaXBha2toxIFuYeG5gyBuaWNjYXNhw7HDscSBZMSrbmHhuYMgcGFoxIFuZW5h)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XX, the ten imperfections)
> **Athassa imāya taruṇavipassanāya āraddhavipassakassa dasa vipassanupakkilesā uppajjanti. Vipassanupakkilesā hi paṭivedhappattassa ariyasāvakassa ceva vippaṭipannakassa ca nikkhittakammaṭṭhānassa kusītapuggalassa nuppajjanti. Sammāpaṭipannakassa pana yuttapayuttassa āraddhavipassakassa kulaputtassa uppajjantiyeva. Katame pana te dasa upakkilesāti? Obhāso, ñāṇaṃ, pīti, passaddhi, sukhaṃ, adhimokkho, paggaho, upaṭṭhānaṃ, upekkhā, nikantīti.**  
> 732. Then for him, for the one who has begun insight, the ten imperfections of insight (dasa vipassanupakkilesā) arise by way of this tender insight. For the imperfections of insight do not arise for a noble disciple who has attained penetration, nor for one who has gone astray, nor for a lazy person who has abandoned the meditation subject. But they certainly arise for whatever such clansman who is practicing correctly, who is dedicated and devoted, and who has begun insight. What then are those ten imperfections? Illumination, knowledge, rapture, tranquility, pleasure, decision, exertion, establishment, equanimity, and attachment.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XX, §732, Vipassanupakkilesakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=269&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzMyLiBBdGhhc3NhIGltxIF5YSB0YXJ14bmHYXZpcGFzc2FuxIF5YSDEgXJhZGRoYXZpcGFzc2FrYXNzYSBkYXNh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XXI, applying effort again)
> **Puna udayabbayañāṇe yogo kimatthiyoti ce? Lakkhaṇasallakkhaṇattho. Udayabbayañāṇaṃ hi heṭṭhā dasahi upakkilesehi upakkiliṭṭhaṃ hutvā yāthāvasarasato tilakkhaṇaṃ sallakkhetuṃ nāsakkhi. Upakkilesavimuttaṃ pana sakkoti. Tasmā lakkhaṇasallakkhaṇatthamettha puna yogo karaṇīyo.**  
> 738. But why does one again apply effort to the knowledge of rise and fall? For the purpose of discerning the characteristics. For the knowledge of rise and fall mentioned earlier, being defiled by the ten imperfections, was unable to discern the three characteristics truly as they are. But when free from imperfections, it is able to do so. Therefore, one should apply further effort here again for the purpose of discerning the characteristics.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, §738) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=276&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzM4LiBQdW5hIHVkYXlhYmJheWHDscSB4bmHZSB5b2dvIGtpbWF0dGhpeW90aSBjZT8%3D)
