### Grammatical Analysis
**Saddhā**: [f.] faith; confidence; trust. The Aṭṭhakathā (Vism XIV, As) derives it: *saddahanti etāya, sayaṃ vā saddahati, saddahanamattameva vā esā*, "by it they have faith, or it itself has faith, or it is merely the act of having faith". The Dhammasaṅgaṇī gives the synonyms *saddhā saddahanā okappanā abhippasādo*, "faith, trusting, placing confidence, clear confidence" (Dhs 402).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla *saddhā* is the first of the five faculties (*[indriya](/glossary/indriya/)*): "Here a noble disciple has faith (*saddho hoti*), he has faith in the Tathāgata's enlightenment: 'Thus indeed is that Blessed One, an arahant, perfectly self-awakened…'" (SN, Paṭhamavibhaṅgasutta). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists it, in the wholesome consciousness, as the faculty and the power of faith (*saddhindriyaṃ, saddhābalaṃ*; Dhs 402, 415) and it is one of the beautiful factors (*sobhana-cetasika*).

The Aṭṭhakathā: it is a faculty "from overcoming faithlessness, in the sense of dominance" (As 1193), and it "has the characteristic of faith or of trusting (*saddahana, okappana*); its function is to clarify (*pasādana*) like a water-clearing gem, or to plunge in (*pakkhandana*) like crossing a flood; it is manifested as non-turbidity (*akālussiya*) or as decision (*adhimutti*); its proximate cause is an object worthy of faith, or the factors of stream-entry such as hearing the True Dhamma; it should be seen like a hand, wealth and a seed" (Vism-ii 436). The Atthasālinī and the Milindapañha (Mil 170-172) give the two characteristics: *sampasādanalakkhaṇā* (clarifying) and *sampakkhandanalakkhaṇā* (aspiring, leaping forward). The water-clearing gem simile: when the gem is thrown into water, the weeds, shells and mud disappear and the water becomes clear and unagitated; so faith, when it arises, suppresses the hindrances and the mind becomes free of hindrances (Mil 172).

The Visuddhimagga (IV) says the balance of faith and wisdom (and of concentration and energy) is praised: "one who is strong in faith but of dull wisdom is blindly confident and has confidence in what is groundless" (Vism-i 870).

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **Katamañca, bhikkhave, saddhindriyaṃ? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako saddho hoti, saddahati tathāgatassa bodhiṃ – ‘ itipi so bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho vijjācaraṇasampanno sugato lokavidū anuttaro purisadammasārathi satthā devamanussānaṃ buddho bhagavā ’ ti – idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, saddhindriyaṃ.**  
> And what, mendicants, is the faculty of faith? Here, mendicants, a noble disciple is endowed with faith; he has faith in the Tathāgata’s enlightenment: ‘That Blessed One is indeed an Arahant, a Perfectly Self-Awakened One, perfect in true knowledge and conduct, sublime, knower of worlds, unsurpassed leader of persons to be tamed, teacher of gods and humans, enlightened, the Blessed One.’ This is called, mendicants, the faculty of faith.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ tasmiṃ samaye saddhindriyaṃ hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye saddhā saddahanā okappanā abhippasādo saddhā saddhindriyaṃ saddhābalaṃ – idaṃ tasmiṃ samaye saddhindriyaṃ hoti.**  
> 12. What is the faculty of faith at that time? Whatever faith, believing, establishing faith, serene faith, faith, faculty of faith, or the power of faith exists at that time—this is the faculty of faith at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 12) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTIuIEthdGFtYeG5gyB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgc2FkZGhpbmRyaXlh4bmDIGhvdGk%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Saddahanti etāya, sayaṃ vā saddahati, saddahanamattameva vā esāti saddhā. Sāva assaddhiyassa abhibhavanato adhipatiyaṭṭhena indriyaṃ. Adhimokkhalakkhaṇe vā indaṭṭhaṃ kāretīti indriyaṃ. Saddhāva indriyaṃ saddhindriyaṃ. Sā panesā sampasādanalakkhaṇā ca saddhā sampakkhandanalakkhaṇā ca.**  
> It is that by which (others) have faith, or it believes itself, or it is merely the act of having faith: this is called faith (saddhā). That very faith, from overcoming faithlessness, is a faculty in the sense of dominance. Or, because it performs the function of dominance in terms of the characteristic of decision, it is a faculty. Faith itself, as a faculty, is the faith-faculty (saddhindriya). Moreover, this faith has the characteristic of clarifying and the characteristic of aspiration.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Saddhindriya, As 1193) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=162&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2FkZGFoYW50aSBldMSBeWEsIHNheWHhuYMgdsSBIHNhZGRhaGF0aSwgc2FkZGFoYW5hbWF0dGFtZXZh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XIV)
> **Saddahanti etāya, sayaṃ vā saddahati, saddahanamattameva vā esāti saddhā. Sā saddahanalakkhaṇā, okappanalakkhaṇā vā, pasādanarasā udakappasādakamaṇi viya, pakkhandanarasā vā oghuttaraṇo viya. Akālussiyapaccupaṭṭhānā, adhimuttipaccupaṭṭhānā vā, saddheyyavatthupadaṭṭhānā, saddhammassavanādisotāpattiyaṅga (dī. ni.3.311; saṃ. ni.5.1001) padaṭṭhānā vā, hatthavittabījāni viya daṭṭhabbā.**  
> 464. They have faith by means of it, or it itself has faith, or it is just the mere act of having faith—thus it is faith (saddhā). It has the characteristic of having faith or trusting. Its function is to clarify, like a water-clearing gem, or its function is to enter into, like crossing a flood. It is manifested as non-turbidity or as decision. Its proximate cause is an object worthy of faith, or its proximate cause is the factors of stream-entry such as hearing the True Dhamma. It should be seen as being like a hand, wealth, and seed.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIV, para. 464) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=94&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDY0LiBTYWRkYWhhbnRpIGV0xIF5YSwgc2F5YeG5gyB2xIEgc2FkZGFoYXRpLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IV)
> **Visesato panettha saddhāpaññānaṃ samādhivīriyānañca samataṃ pasaṃsanti. Balavasaddho hi mandapañño muddhappasanno hoti, avatthusmiṃ pasīdati.**  
> Specifically, however, they praise the balance of faith and wisdom, and of concentration and energy. For one who is strong in faith but of dull wisdom is blindly confident, acquiring confidence in what is groundless.  
>
