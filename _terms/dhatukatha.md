### Grammatical Analysis
**Dhātukathā**: [f.] the Discourse on Elements. Formed by *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element) + *kathā* (discourse). The commentary's opening verse calls the Buddha "the one who illuminates the distinctions of the elements" (*dhātubhedappakāsano*) who "taught the Dhātukathā" (*adesayi dhātukathaṃ*) (Dhatuk-a 7:1). The Mūla names itself "Dhātukathāpāḷi" (Dhatuk 3:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Dhātukathā* is the third of the seven books of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, after the Dhammasaṅgaha and the Vibhaṅga (Sp-i 133:1). The commentary says that the Buddha taught it immediately after the Vibhaṅga, which has eighteen divisions (Dhatuk-a 5:1-7:1), and that the treatise is divided in fourteen ways, by way of "inclusion and uninclusion" and so on, in two parts, the brief statement (*uddesa*) and the detailed exposition (*niddesa*) (Dhatuk-a 11:1-2).

In the Mūla the brief statement has a Method Matrix (*nayamātikā*) beginning "inclusion and uninclusion, the unincluded by the included, the included by the unincluded … association and dissociation …" (Dhatuk 6:1), an Internal Matrix (*abbhantaramātikā*) of sixteen items beginning with the five aggregates, twelve bases and eighteen elements and including the four truths, twenty-two faculties, dependent origination, the thirty-seven constituents of awakening and "contact, feeling, perception, volition, mind, decision and attention" (Dhatuk 8:1), and an External Matrix (*bāhiramātikā*): "all of the Dhammasaṅgaṇī is the matrix of the Dhātukathā" (*sabbāpi dhammasaṅgaṇī dhātukathāya mātikā*, Dhatuk 14:1). The exposition proceeds in questions and answers, for example: "Under how many aggregates, bases and elements is the matter aggregate included? The matter aggregate is included under one aggregate, eleven bases and eleven elements" (Dhatuk 18:1-2).

> **Sabbāpi dhammasaṅgaṇī dhātukathāya mātikāti.**

### Quote (Mūla: Dhātukathā, the first question)
> **Rūpakkhandho katihi khandhehi katihāyatanehi katihi dhātūhi saṅgahito? Rūpakkhandho ekena khandhena ekādasahāyatanehi ekādasahi dhātūhi saṅgahito.**  
> Under how many aggregates, bases and elements is the matter aggregate included? The matter aggregate is included under one aggregate, eleven bases and eleven elements.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhātukathā-aṭṭhakathā (Pañcappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā))
> **Aṭṭhārasahi bhedehi, vibhaṅgaṃ mārabhañjano; Desayitvā mahāvīro, yaṃ tasseva anantaraṃ. Adesayi dhātukathaṃ, dhātubhedappakāsano; Tassatthaṃ dīpayissāmi, taṃ suṇātha samāhitāti.**  
> The Destroyer of Māra, with eighteen divisions, (taught) the Vibhaṅga; the great hero having taught that immediately after. The Illuminator of the Element-Distinctions taught the Dhātukathā; I will illustrate its meaning; listen to it with concentrated minds.  
>  
> — [Dhātukathā-aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/dhatukatha-atthakatha/) (Pañcappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā, Dhatuk-a 5-8) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=1&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QeG5reG5rWjEgXJhc2FoaSBiaGVkZWhpLCB2aWJoYeG5hWdh4bmDIG3EgXJhYmhhw7FqYW5vOw%3D%3D)
