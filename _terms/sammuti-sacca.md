### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammutisacca**: [nt.] Conventional Truth. Formed by *sammuti* (convention) + *sacca* (truth). The Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā glosses *sammutisaccamhi* as "in the truth that has entered into conventional designation" (*sammutiṃ anupaviṭṭhe saccamhi*, Kv-a 996). The Dīgha uses the terms *lokasamaññā lokaniruttiyo lokavohārā lokapaññattiyo*, "the world's designations, expressions, ways of speaking and descriptions" (DN 9).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammuti-sacca* is conventional truth, one of the "two truths" (*sammutisaccaṃ paramatthasaccanti dve saccāni*, Kv-a 996). In the Poṭṭhapāda Sutta the Buddha says of the "gross, mind-made and formless acquisition of a self": these are "the world's designations, expressions, ways of speaking and descriptions, with which the Tathāgata expresses himself without grasping to them" (DN 9). The Kathāvatthu has the Sammutiñāṇakathā, the discussion whether "conventional knowledge has only truth as its object" (Kv 434): the Andhakas, who do not distinguish the two truths and say so "by the generality of the word 'truth'", are corrected (Kv-a 996).

The Aṭṭhakathā: "the Buddhas have two kinds of teaching, conventional (*sammutikathā*) and ultimate (*paramatthakathā*). Therein the teaching beginning with 'a being, a person, a god, a Brahmā' is *sammutikathā*; the teaching beginning with 'impermanent, suffering, non-self, the aggregates, the elements, the sense-bases, the foundations of mindfulness, the right efforts' is *paramatthakathā*." To one who can understand and realise by conventional teaching he speaks conventionally from the start, and he does not speak ultimate teaching first to one who will awaken by conventional teaching, but instructs by conventional teaching and afterwards teaches ultimate teaching (Sv-i 1407; Kv-a 703). The Ṭīkā explains why: the Tathāgata has fully penetrated ultimate truth, and yet speaks with the world's conventions because the real meaning is hard for the world to understand without them; he speaks "without grasping", unlike ordinary people who speak thinking "this is mine" (Sv-nt-ii 1196).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Imā kho citta, lokasamaññā lokaniruttiyo lokavohārā lokapaññattiyo, yāhi tathāgato voharati aparāmasan” ti.**  
> These, Citta, are the world’s designations, the world’s expressions, the world’s ways of speaking, the world’s descriptions, with which the Tathagata expresses himself but without grasping to them.”  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 9, Poṭṭhapāda Sutta, para. 440) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_01&page_number=186&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SW3EgSBraG8gY2l0dGEsIGxva2FzYW1hw7HDscSBIGxva2FuaXJ1dHRpeW8gbG9rYXZvaMSBcsSB)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Kathāvatthu)
> **Na vattabbaṃ – “sammutiñāṇaṃ saccārammaṇaññeva na aññārammaṇan” ti? Āmantā. Nanu pathavīkasiṇaṃ samāpattiṃ samāpannassa atthi ñāṇaṃ, pathavīkasiṇañca sammutisaccamhīti?**  
> It should not be said: “Conventional knowledge has only truth as its object, not other objects”? Yes. Wouldn’t you admit that one who has attained the earth kasiṇa meditation has knowledge, and that the earth kasiṇa is a conventional truth?  
>  
> — [Kathāvatthu](/glossary/kathavatthu/) (Sammutiñāṇakathā, para. 434) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_05&page_number=233&name=kath%C4%81vatthup%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDM0LiBOYSB2YXR0YWJiYeG5gyDigJM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā)
> **434-435. Idāni sammutiñāṇakathā nāma hoti. Tattha sammutisaccaṃ paramatthasaccanti dve saccāni. Ye pana evaṃ vibhāgaṃ akatvā saccanti vacanasāmaññena sammutiñāṇampi “saccārammaṇamevā” ti vadanti, seyyathāpi andhakā; te ayuttavādinoti tesaṃ vādavisodhanatthaṃ ayaṃ kathā āraddhā. Tattha na vattabbanti pucchā paravādissa, paramatthasaccaṃ sandhāya paṭiññā sakavādissa. Sammutisaccamhīti sammutiṃ anupaviṭṭhe saccamhi.**  
> Now begins the discussion called the Discussion on Conventional Knowledge. There, there are two truths: conventional truth and ultimate truth. But some, like the Andhakas, without making such a distinction, say, by the generality of the word 'truth', that even conventional knowledge “has only truth as its object.” Because they are wrong speakers, this discussion was begun for the purpose of purifying their doctrine. There, the question 'It should not be said' is for the opponent, and the claim is for the proponent of the orthodox view, referring to ultimate truth. The 'in conventional truth' refers to the truth that has entered into conventional designation.  
>  
> — Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā (Sammutiñāṇakathāvaṇṇanā, Kv-a 996) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=187&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NDM0LTQzNS4gSWTEgW5pIHNhbW11dGnDscSB4bmHYWthdGjEgSBuxIFtYSBob3RpLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Buddhānaṃ pana dve kathā sammutikathā ca paramatthakathā ca. Tattha “satto poso devo brahmā” tiādikā “sammutikathā” nāma. “Aniccaṃ dukkhamanattā khandhā dhātuyo āyatanāni satipaṭṭhānā sammappadhānā” tiādikā paramatthakathā nāma.**  
> But the Buddhas have two kinds of teachings: conventional teaching and ultimate teaching. Therein, the teaching beginning with ‘being, person, god, brahma’ is called ‘conventional teaching.’ The teaching beginning with ‘impermanent, suffering, non-self, the aggregates, the elements, the sense-bases, the foundations of mindfulness, the right strivings’ is called ‘ultimate teaching.’  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Sv-i 1407) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=315&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QnVkZGjEgW5h4bmDIHBhbmEgZHZlIGthdGjEgSBzYW1tdXRpa2F0aMSBIGNh)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, the order of teaching)
> **Tathā sammutikathāya bujjhanakasattassāpi na paṭhamaṃ paramatthakathaṃ katheti. Sammutikathāya pana bodhetvā pacchā paramatthakathaṃ katheti.**  
> Likewise, for a being who will understand through conventional teaching, he does not speak ultimate teaching first. Having instructed through conventional teaching, he speaks ultimate teaching afterwards.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Sv-i 1407) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=316&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0aMSBIHNhbW11dGlrYXRoxIF5YSBidWpqaGFuYWthc2F0dGFzc8SBcGkgbmEgcGHhua1oYW1h4bmDIHBhcmFtYXR0aGFrYXRoYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Sīlakkhandhavagga-abhinavaṭīkā)
> **Yadi tathāgato paramatthasaccaṃ sammadeva abhisambujjhitvā ṭhitopi lokasamaññābhūtaṃ sammutisaccaṃ gahetvāva vadati, evañcettha ko lokiyamahājanehi visesoti vuttaṃ “yāhī”tiādi, ayaṃ pāḷiyaṃ sambandho. Idaṃ vuttaṃ hoti – lokiyamahājano appahīnaparāmāsattā “etaṃ mamā” tiādinā parāmasanto voharati. Tathāgato pana sabbaso pahīnaparāmāsattā aparāmasantova yasmā lokasamaññāhi vinā lokiyo attho lokena dubbiññeyyo, tasmā tāhi taṃ voharati.**  
> If the Tathāgata, even while established having fully realized ultimate truth, speaks taking conventional truth that has become worldly agreement, what is the difference between him and ordinary worldly people? To this inquiry he said 'yāhī', etc.; this is the connection in the Pāli. This is what is said: the ordinary worldly person speaks, grasping 'this is mine', due to having not abandoned clinging. But the Tathāgata, because he has completely abandoned clinging, speaks without grasping. Since worldly ultimate reality is difficult for the world to understand without worldly conventionality, he speaks using those.  
>  
> — Sīlakkhandhavaggaabhinavaṭīkā (Sv-nt-ii 1196) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_di_01_03&page_number=401&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavaggaabhinava%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=WWFkaSB0YXRoxIFnYXRvIHBhcmFtYXR0aGFzYWNjYeG5gyBzYW1tYWRldmEgYWJoaXNhbWJ1ampoaXR2xIEg4bmtaGl0b3Bp)
