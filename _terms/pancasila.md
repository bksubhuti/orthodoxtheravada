### Grammatical Analysis
**Pañcasīla**: [nt.] The five precepts; fivefold virtue. Formed by *pañca* (five) + *[sīla](/glossary/sila/)* (moral conduct). The Mūla gives the set as *pañca sikkhāpadāni* (five training rules), each phrased *veramaṇī* (abstention, from *virati*); the compound *pañcasīla* is used in the Aṭṭhakathā, e.g. Mp-iii 818:6 "*pañcasīlaṃ sīlaṃ nāma*", the five precepts are called "virtue" (*sīla*).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Pañcasīla* are the five training rules: abstaining from killing living beings (*pāṇātipātā veramaṇī*), from taking what is not given (*adinnādānā veramaṇī*), from sexual misconduct (*kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī*), from false speech (*musāvādā veramaṇī*), and from intoxicating drinks and drugs that cause heedlessness (*surāmerayamajjappamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇī*) (DN 33; Vibh 703).

The Visuddhimagga assigns them to lay disciples: "for lay disciples, male and female, the five training rules by way of permanent virtue (*niccasīla*), or ten if there is enthusiasm, and eight as Uposatha factors, is the virtue of the laity" (Vism-i 137). Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā (Pj-i 110) adds that, of the ten training rules, the first five are "naturally blameworthy" (*pakativajja*), since killing and the rest arise only from unwholesome minds, while the others are blameworthy by designation (*paṇṇattivajja*). The commentary also gives the factors (*aṅga*) of each breach: five for killing and for stealing, four for each of the other three (Pj-i 139).

The Aṭṭhakathā calls these the basic "virtue" (*sīla*), with the ten precepts and the fourfold purification of virtue built on them as "higher virtue" (*adhisīla*) (Mp-iii 818). The Ṭīkā uses the phrase *dhammatāsiddha pañcasīla* ("five precepts established by nature") of the Uttarakuru people, who are said to be reborn in the deva world by the power of that naturally established observance, their thousand-year lifespan being natural in the same way (Sv-pt-iii 615; Mp-t-ix 386; Sp-t-i-a 1106).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)

> “The five precepts: refraining from killing living beings, refraining from taking what has not been given, refraining from sexual misconduct, refraining from false speech, refraining from intoxicating drinks and drugs that cause heedlessness.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **()“Yo ca sataṃ sakadāgāmīnaṃ bhojeyya, yo cekaṃ anāgāmiṃ bhojeyya … pe … yo ca sataṃ anāgāmīnaṃ bhojeyya, yo cekaṃ arahantaṃ bhojeyya … yo ca sataṃ arahantānaṃ bhojeyya, yo cekaṃ paccekabuddhaṃ bhojeyya … yo ca sataṃ paccekabuddhānaṃ bhojeyya, yo ca tathāgataṃ arahantaṃ sammāsambuddhaṃ bhojeyya … yo ca buddhappamukhaṃ bhikkhusaṅghaṃ bhojeyya … yo ca cātuddisaṃ saṅghaṃ uddissa vihāraṃ kārāpeyya … yo ca pasannacitto buddhañca dhammañca saṅghañca saraṇaṃ gaccheyya … yo ca pasannacitto sikkhāpadāni samādiyeyya – pāṇātipātā veramaṇiṃ, adinnādānā veramaṇiṃ, kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇiṃ, musāvādā veramaṇiṃ, surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇiṃ, yo ca antamaso gandhohanamattampi mettacittaṃ bhāveyya,() idaṃ tato mahapphalataraṃ.**  
> Greater in fruit than the great gift that the brahmin Velāma gave… if one were to feed one hundred once-returners, is if one were to feed one non-returner… if one were to feed one hundred non-returners, is if one were to feed one arahant… if one were to feed one hundred arahants, is if one were to feed one Private Buddha… if one were to feed one hundred Private Buddhas, is if one were to feed a Tathāgata, a worthy one, rightly self-awakened… is if one were to feed the community of bhikkhus headed by the Buddha… is if one were to have a dwelling built for the Saṅgha of the four directions… is if one with a confident mind were to go to the Buddha, to the Dhamma, and to the Saṅgha for refuge… is if one with a confident mind were to undertake the training rules—abstaining from taking life, abstaining from taking what is not given, abstaining from sexual misconduct, abstaining from false speech, abstaining from distilled and fermented drinks that cause heedlessness—is if one were to develop a heart of good will even for the duration of a whiff of fragrance.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 9.20, Velāma Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_09&page_number=196&name=navakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=KClbKHlhw7FjYSBnYWhhcGF0aSB2ZWzEgW1vIGJyxIFobWHhuYdvIGTEgW5h4bmDIGFkxIFzaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Pañca sikkhāpadāni – pāṇātipātā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ, adinnādānā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ, kāmesumicchācārā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ, musāvādā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ, surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇī sikkhāpadaṃ.**  
> 703. The five Training Rules are: the training rule of Refraining from killing living creatures; the training rule of refraining from taking what is not given; the training rule of refraining from sexual misconduct; the training rule of Refraining from false speech; the training rule of abstaining from drinking distilled and fermented drinks that cause heedlessness.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Sikkhāpadavibhaṅga, Abhidhammabhājanīya, para. 703) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=299&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NzAzLiBQYcOxY2Egc2lra2jEgXBhZMSBbmkg4oCTIHDEgeG5h8SBdGlwxIF0xIEgdmVyYW1h4bmHxKs%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Upāsakaupāsikānaṃ niccasīlavasena pañcasikkhāpadāni, sati vā ussāhe dasa, uposathaṅgavasena aṭṭhāti idaṃ gahaṭṭhasīlanti evaṃ bhikkhusīlādivasena catubbidhaṃ.**  
> For lay disciples, male and female, the five training precepts as a permanent virtue, or ten if there is effort, and eight as the Uposatha factors, is the virtue of the laity; thus it is fourfold by way of the virtue of bhikkhus and so on.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (I, Sīlaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=15&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VXDEgXNha2F1cMSBc2lrxIFuYeG5gyBuaWNjYXPEq2xhdmFzZW5hIHBhw7FjYXNpa2toxIFwYWTEgW5pLCBzYXRpIHbEgSB1c3PEgWhl)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Aṅgatoti ettha ca pāṇātipātassa pañca aṅgāni bhavanti – pāṇo ca hoti, pāṇasaññī ca, vadhakacittañca paccupaṭṭhitaṃ hoti, vāyamati, tena ca maratīti. Adinnādānassāpi pañceva – parapariggahitañca hoti, parapariggahitasaññī ca, theyyacittañca paccupaṭṭhitaṃ hoti, vāyamati, tena ca ādātabbaṃ ādānaṃ gacchatīti.**  
> Regarding the factors: there are five factors of killing living creatures—there is a living being, there is the perception of a living being, the volition to kill is established, one puts forth effort, and by that effort it dies. There are also five for stealing—it is owned by another, one perceives it as owned by another, the intention to steal is established, one puts forth effort, and by that effort the taking that is meant to be taken occurs.  
>  
> — Khuddakapāṭha-aṭṭhakathā (Paramatthajotikā, on the Dasasikkhāpada) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_01&page_number=21&name=khuddakap%C4%81%E1%B9%ADhaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QeG5hWdhdG90aSBldHRoYSBjYSBwxIHhuYfEgXRpcMSBdGFzc2EgcGHDsWNhIGHhuYVnxIFuaQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Pāthikavaggaṭīkā)
> **“Dhammatāsiddhassa pañcasīlassa ānubhāvena te devaloke nibbattantī” ti vadanti. Vassasahassameva ca nesaṃ sabbakālaṃ āyuppamāṇaṃ. Sabbametaṃ tesaṃ pañcasīlaṃ viya dhammatāsiddhaṃ evāti veditabbaṃ.**  
> 'By the power of the five precepts established by natural law, they are reborn in the heavenly world,' they say. And for them, the lifespan is always a full thousand years. All of this should be understood as being established by natural law, just like their five precepts.  
>  
> — Pāthikavaggaṭīkā (on the Uttarakuru people) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_di_03&page_number=157&name=p%C4%81thikavagga%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIERoYW1tYXTEgXNpZGRoYXNzYSBwYcOxY2FzxKtsYXNzYSDEgW51YmjEgXZlbmEgdGUgZGV2YWxva2U%3D)
