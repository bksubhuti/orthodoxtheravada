### Grammatical Analysis
**Vihāra**: [m.] dwelling; monastery; abiding. The Suttanipāta-aṭṭhakathā glosses it *vihāroti vasanokāso*, "a place of dwelling" (Pj-ii 994:5). It belongs with the verb *viharati* ("dwells, abides").

### Orthodox Definition
*Vihāra* has two main uses in the texts.

1. **A dwelling.** In the Senāsanakkhandhaka of the Cūḷavagga (the Allowance of Dwellings, *Vihārānujānanaṃ*) the Buddha says: "I allow five kinds of dwellings (*leṇāni*): a *vihāra*, an *aḍḍhayoga* (half-roofed house), a *pāsāda* (mansion), a *hammiya* (turreted building) and a *guhā* (cave)", after the merchant of Rājagaha wished to have *vihāras* built. In thanks the Buddha speaks verses that say such dwellings ward off cold, heat, wild beasts and rain, "for the sake of shelter and comfort, to meditate and practice insight", and that "the gift of a dwelling to the Sangha is praised by the Buddha as foremost". The Samantapāsādikā adds that *vihāra* in the Vinaya rules is to be understood as an inner room or any other well-protected, completely enclosed lodging (Sp-ii 164:6).
2. **A way of abiding.** The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha names loving-kindness, compassion, altruistic joy and equanimity the four illimitables (*appamaññā*), also called *brahmavihāra* (divine abodes). The Peṭakopadesa lists "four *vihāras*" as *dibbo, brahmā, ariyo, āneñjo* (the Nettippakaraṇa also lists *cattāro vihārā*); the commentary Mp-t-i 178:2 names *iriyāpathavihāra, dibbavihāra, brahmavihāra, ariyavihāra*.
