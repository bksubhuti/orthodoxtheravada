### Grammatical Analysis
**Suttavibhaṅga**: [m.] The Analysis of the Rules. Formed by *sutta* (rule, thread, clause) + *vibhaṅga* (analysis, breakdown, explanation). In the Vinaya context, "sutta" refers specifically to the rules of the *[Pātimokkha](/glossary/patimokkha/)*.

### Orthodox Definition
The *Suttavibhaṅga* is the first major division of the *[Vinaya-piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya/)*. The Aṭṭhakathā says that the two *vibhaṅgas* (*ubhatovibhaṅgā*) are the "sutta", and the *Khandhakas* and the *Parivāra* are the "vinaya"; in this way the Vinaya Piṭaka is completely covered (Sv-ii 614:9). The two are the *Bhikkhuvibhaṅga*, the analysis of the monks' rules, and the *Bhikkhunīvibhaṅga*, the analysis of the nuns' rules.

In the TPR texts the *Bhikkhuvibhaṅga* is in the *Pārājika-pāḷi* (Vin-i) and the *Pācittiya-pāḷi* (Vin-ii, which begins with "5. Pācittiyakaṇḍaṃ"), and the *Bhikkhunīvibhaṅga* is a book of its own (Vin-ii-b). (The next books, the *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* and *[Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/)*, belong to the *[Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/)*.) The Mūla itself uses the name when the elders at the Second Council ask where a practice was forbidden and are told: "At Sāvatthī, in the Suttavibhaṅga" (Vin-iv 2787:5-6).

The word *sutta* here refers to the rule schedule: the Samantapāsādikā glosses "the sutta has not come" as "the *mātikā* (Pātimokkha) has not come", and "no *suttavibhaṅga*" as "the Vinaya is also not well mastered" (Sp-iv 215:2-3).

The *Suttavibhaṅga* is structured meticulously. For every rule, as in the *Vikappanasikkhāpada* (Vin-ii 1156-1174), the text provides:
1. **The Origin Story**: where the Buddha was dwelling and the event that led to the rule (Vin-ii 1157:1).
2. **The Rule Itself**: the exact wording of the precept (Vin-ii 1158:1).
3. **The Word Analysis**: a word-by-word definition of the rule, beginning "*Yo panāti yo yādiso*…" (Vin-ii 1159:1).
4. **Cases and Exceptions**: the offences in each case (Vin-ii 1171:1) and the cases without offence, for example "*ummattakassa, ādikammikassa*" (for one who is insane, for the first offender; Vin-ii 1173:1), or the answer "*Anāpatti, bhikkhu, ajānantassā*" (no offence for one who did not know; Vin-i 465:5).

The *Samantapāsādikā* provides the detailed precedents and definitions needed to apply these rules to new situations.

### Textual References
* **Vinaya**: The *Suttavibhaṅga* is divided into the *Bhikkhuvibhaṅga* (the *Pārājika-pāḷi*, Vin-i, and the *Pācittiya-pāḷi*, Vin-ii) and the *Bhikkhunīvibhaṅga* (Vin-ii-b, the nuns' rules). The name occurs in the Mūla at Vin-iv 2787:6 and in the procedure of the *adhikaraṇa* (Vin-iv 1131:1).
* **Commentary**: *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* – The essential Vinaya commentary by Ācariya Buddhaghosa. It explains how to correctly apply the rules (e.g. Sp-iv 215 on the name; Sp-ii-b 1347 on the nuns' rules).
