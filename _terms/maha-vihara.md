### Grammatical Analysis
**Mahāvihāra**: [m.] great monastery. A compound of *mahā* (great) + *[vihāra](/glossary/vihara/)* (monastery). In the Cūḷavagga the word is used generally, for a great monastery of the Saṅgha that was falling to pieces (Vin-iv 1702:1). As a proper name it refers to the Great Monastery of Laṅkā, in the Mahāvaṃsa and the commentaries.

### Orthodox Definition
The Mahāvaṃsa speaks of "this first Great Monastery in Laṅkā" built for the Great Elder Mahinda (Mhv 3802:1-3804:1; chapter 15 is named "The Reception of the Great Monastery", Mhv 3806:1). Earlier in the same chapter the king gives the Mahāmeghavana park to the Saṅgha, pouring the water of offering into the hands of Mahinda (Mhv 3166:1; 3168:1). In the list of monasteries built by the king, the Great Monastery comes first (Mhv 4596:1; the king named at Mhv 4624:1 is Devānaṃpiyatissa).

The Mahāvihāra is the source that the commentators name. The author of the Visuddhimagga says he will expound the path of purification "relying on the teaching method of the dwellers in the Mahāvihāra" (*Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ desanānayanissitaṃ*, Vism-i 23:1-25:1). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says that the commentary, first recited by the five hundred elders and recited again later, was brought to the island of Sīhaḷa by Mahinda and set down in the Sinhala language for the inhabitants of the island; the author discards the Sinhala language and gives the meaning in the Magadha language, "not opposing the tradition of the Elders", and "relying on the Mahāvihāra residents who have profound understanding" (Sv-i 16:1-24:1). The Samantapāsādikā records that in the time of King Bhātiya, the elders of the Mahāvihāra and those of the Abhayagiri differed on a point (Sp-i 2333:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka, Cūḷavagga)
> **Tena kho pana samayena saṅghassa mahāvihāro undriyati. Bhikkhū kukkuccāyantā senāsanaṃ nātiharanti. Bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ.**  
> Now on that occasion, the great monastery of the Sangha was falling to pieces. The monks, being scrupulous, did not take away the lodging. They reported this matter to the Blessed One.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Cūḷavagga, Senāsanakkhandhaka) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_04&page_number=328&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGVuYSBraG8gcGFuYSBzYW1heWVuYSBzYeG5hWdoYXNzYSBtYWjEgXZpaMSBcm8%3D)

### Quote (Añña: Mahāvaṃsa, ch. 15)
> **Laṃkāyaṃ paṭhamamimaṃ mahāvihāraṃ; Rājā so sumatimahāmahindatheraṃ, Āgammā malamatimetthakārayitthāti.**  
> this first Great Monastery in Laṅkā; that king, of great wisdom, for the Great Elder Mahinda, having arrived, he had made for the purpose of removing impurities.  
>  
> — Mahāvaṃsa (Mahāvihārapaṭiggahaka, ch. 15)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, Nidānakathā)
> **Tesaṃ pāmojjakaraṇaṃ, suvisuddhavinicchayaṃ; Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ, desanānayanissitaṃ. Visuddhimaggaṃ bhāsissaṃ, taṃ me sakkacca bhāsato;**  
> To bring them joy, with highly purified decisions, Relying on the teaching method of the dwellers in the Great Monastery, I shall expound the Path of Purification. As I carefully expound it,  
>

### Textual References
* **Vinaya**: *[Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/)* (Vin-iv 1702) – *mahāvihāra* as a great monastery of the Saṅgha.
* **Historical**: *Mahāvaṃsa* (Mhv 3166-3168, 3802-3806, 4596, 4624) – the Great Monastery in Laṅkā, built for Mahinda.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-i 23-25) and *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (Sv-i 16-24) – the commentators rely on the Mahāvihāra teaching method; *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (Sp-i 2333:1).
