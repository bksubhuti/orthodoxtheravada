### Grammatical Analysis
**Mahāvihāra**: [m.] The Great Monastery. Formed by *mahā* (great, large) + *[vihāra](/glossary/vihara/)* (monastery).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Mahāvihāra* is the absolute epicenter of orthodox Theravāda Buddhism. Founded in the 3rd century BCE by King Devānampiya Tissa in Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka, it was the headquarters of Venerable Mahinda, the son of Emperor Ashoka who brought Buddhism to the island.

The monks of the Mahāvihāra—the *Mahāvihāravāsins*—are responsible for preserving the Pali [Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/) in its purest form. They fiercely defended the orthodox Vibhajjavāda doctrine against emerging Mahāyāna and esoteric influences. In the 5th century CE, the great commentator Buddhaghosa traveled explicitly to the Mahāvihāra to translate their ancient Sinhalese commentaries into the Pali *[Aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/aṭṭhakathā/)*, securing the doctrinal framework that defines global Theravāda today. 

When modern practitioners refer to "orthodox Theravāda," they are explicitly referring to the unadulterated doctrinal positions preserved by the Mahāvihāra lineage.

### Textual References
* **Historical**: *Mahāvaṃsa* – The Great Chronicle of Sri Lanka, detailing the founding of the Mahāvihāra and the planting of the Sri Maha [Bodhi](/glossary/bodhi/) tree within its grounds.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Nidāna) – Buddhaghosa's opening verses state that he is composing the text relying entirely on the pristine tradition of the residents of the Mahāvihāra.
