### Grammatical Analysis
**Vinayapiṭaka**: [nt.] the Basket of Discipline. Compound of *vinaya* (discipline) + *piṭaka* (basket). The Atthasālinī defines it by its contents, not by an etymology (below).

### Orthodox Definition
The Atthasālinī classifies the whole Buddha-word by baskets into three: the Vinaya Piṭaka, the Suttanta Piṭaka and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, and defines the first: "both Pātimokkhas, the two Vibhaṅgas, the twenty-two Khandhakas and the sixteen Parivāras: this is called the Vinaya Piṭaka" (*ubhayāni pātimokkhāni dve vibhaṅgā dvāvīsati khandhakā soḷasa parivārā*).

The same commentary (and the Samantapāsādikā and Sumaṅgalavilāsinī) classifies the three baskets by the manner of teaching: the Vinaya Piṭaka is **āṇādesanā** (teaching by command), because the Buddha, who is worthy of command (*āṇāraha*), taught it mostly by way of command (*āṇābāhullato desitattā*); the Suttanta is *vohāradesanā* (teaching by conventional expression).

So the Vinaya Piṭaka consists of the Bhikkhu- and Bhikkhunī-vibhaṅga (the Suttavibhaṅga, with the two Pātimokkhas), the twenty-two Khandhakas (the Mahāvagga and Cūḷavagga) and the Parivāra.

The first council recited it: the Cūḷavagga's Pañcasatikakkhandhaka records Mahākassapa informing the Saṅgha, "I would question the venerable Upāli about the Vinaya"; and the Buddha himself placed Upāli as foremost among those who hold the Vinaya (AN 1, Etadagga).

### Quote
> **Atha kho āyasmā mahākassapo saṅghaṃ ñāpesi – “Suṇātu me, āvuso, saṅgho. Yadi saṅghassa pattakallaṃ, ahaṃ upāliṃ vinayaṃ puccheyyan” ti.**  
> Then the venerable Mahākassapa informed the Saṅgha: “Let the Saṅgha hear me, friends. If the Saṅgha is ready, I will question the venerable Upāli about the Vinaya.”  
>  
> — [Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/) (Pañcasatikakkhandhaka, the First Council) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_04&page_number=482&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QXRoYSBraG8gxIF5YXNtxIEgbWFoxIFrYXNzYXBvIHNh4bmFZ2hh4bmDIMOxxIFwZXNp)

> **Sabbampi hetaṃ piṭakato vinayapiṭakaṃ suttantapiṭakaṃ abhidhammapiṭakanti tippabhedameva hoti. Tattha ubhayāni pātimokkhāni dve vibhaṅgā dvāvīsati khandhakā soḷasa parivārāti idaṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ nāma.**  
> Indeed, all of that, when classified by basket, is of just three divisions: the Vinaya Piṭaka, the Suttanta Piṭaka, and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka. Therein, both monastic codes (pātimokkhāni), the two Expositions (vibhaṅgā), the twenty-two Chapters (khandhakā), and the sixteen Parivāra sections comprise the Vinaya Piṭaka.  
>  
> — Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā ([Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/), on the three baskets) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2FiYmFtcGkgaGV0YeG5gyBwaeG5rWFrYXRvIHZpbmF5YXBp4bmtYWth4bmDIHN1dHRhbnRhcGnhua1ha2HhuYMgYWJoaWRoYW1tYXBp4bmtYWthbnRp)

> **Ettha hi vinayapiṭakaṃ āṇārahena bhagavatā āṇābāhullato desitattā āṇādesanā ;**  
> Herein, the Vinaya Piṭaka is called the teaching of authority (āṇādesanā) because it was taught primarily as a command by the Blessed One, who is worthy of authority.
