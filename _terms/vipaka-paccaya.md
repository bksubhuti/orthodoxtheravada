### Grammatical Analysis
**Vipākapaccaya**: [m.] Kamma-result condition. Formed by *vipāka* (result, ripening) + *paccaya* (condition). The Paṭṭhāna names the related states *vipākābyākata* (resultant indeterminate) and the condition is the fourteenth of the 24.

### Orthodox Definition
*Vipāka-paccaya* is the fourteenth of the 24 conditions (Paṭṭh-i 8:1, 47:1).

The Mūla gives it in one line: *vipākā cattāro khandhā arūpino aññamaññaṃ vipākapaccayena paccayo*, the four formless resultant aggregates are a condition for one another by the kamma-result condition (Paṭṭh-i 47:1). The Aṭṭhakathā defines it: *nirussāhasantabhāvena nirussāhasantabhāvāya upakārako vipākadhammo vipākapaccayo*, a resultant phenomenon that helps by way of effortless quiet, for the sake of effortless quiet (Paṭṭh-a 1934:1; Vism-ii 936:1, XVII 607). It is a condition for mind-originated matter in the course of existence, for kamma-born matter at rebirth-linking, and for associated phenomena everywhere (Paṭṭh-a 1934:2; Vism-ii 936:2). At rebirth-linking one resultant aggregate is a condition for three aggregates and for kamma-born matter, and the aggregates are a condition for the base (*vatthu*; Paṭṭh-a 1934:3-4).

The Ṭīkā explains that the resultant mind and mental factors are of "effortless quiet" (*nirussāhasantabhāva*) because they are not accomplished by present effort but are merely the result of kamma already done (*kammassa kaṭattā nipphajjamānamattato*). This is not the quiet of the calming of defilements (*na kilesavūpasamasantabhāvā*). Because of this quiet nature, the *bhavaṅga* and similar states are difficult to discern (Abhidh-s-t 1668:2-3).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Vipākapaccayoti – vipākā cattāro khandhā arūpino aññamaññaṃ vipākapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 14. Kamma-result condition: The four immaterial resultant aggregates are mutually related to one another by kamma-result condition.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Nirussāhasantabhāvena nirussāhasantabhāvāya upakārako vipākadhammo vipākapaccayo. So pavatte cittasamuṭṭhānānaṃ, paṭisandhiyaṃ kaṭattā ca rūpānaṃ, sabbattha ca sampayuttadhammānaṃ vipākapaccayo hoti. Yathāha – vipākābyākato eko khandho tiṇṇaṃ khandhānaṃ, cittasamuṭṭhānānañca rūpānaṃ, vipākapaccayena paccayo … pe … paṭisandhikkhaṇe vipākābyākato eko khandho … pe … dve khandhā dvinnaṃ khandhānaṃ kaṭattā ca rūpānaṃ vipākapaccayena paccayo. Khandhā vatthussa vipākapaccayena paccayoti.**  
> A resultant phenomenon that helps by way of effortless quiet (nirussāhasantabhāvena) for the purpose of effortless quiet is kamma-result condition (vipākapaccayo). It is a kamma-result condition (vipākapaccayo) for mind-produced matter (cittasamuṭṭhānānaṃ) in the course of existence, for kamma-produced matter (kaṭattā ca rūpānaṃ) at rebirth, and for associated phenomena (sampayuttadhammānaṃ) in all cases (sabbattha). Just as he said: ‘One resultant indeterminate (vipākābyākato) aggregate is a condition for three aggregates and for mind-produced matter by way of kamma-result condition... and so on... at the moment of rebirth (paṭisandhikkhaṇe) one resultant indeterminate aggregate... and so on... two aggregates are a condition for two aggregates and for kamma-produced matter by way of kamma-result condition.’ ‘The aggregates are a condition for the heart-base (vatthussa) by way of kamma-result condition.’  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Attano nirussāhasantabhāvena sahajātanāmarūpānaṃ nirussāhasantabhāvāya upakārakā vipākacittacetasikā vipākapaccayo. Te hi payogena asādhetabbatāya kammassa kaṭattā nipphajjamānamattato nirussāhasantabhāvā honti, na kilesavūpasamasantabhāvā. Tathā santabhāvatoyeva hi bhavaṅgādayo dubbiññeyyā. Abhinipātasampaṭicchanasantīraṇamattā pana vipākā dubbiññeyyāva.**  
> Resultant mind and mental factors are the result condition (vipākapaccayo), being helpful for co-nascent mind-and-matter for the sake of their effortless quiet (nirussāhasantabhāvāya) by way of their own effortless quiet. For they are of an effortless quiet nature because they do not have to be accomplished by effort, because of kamma being already performed, and because they are merely a result being produced; they do not have the quiet nature of the subsiding of defilements. Indeed, because of such a quiet nature, the life-continuum and other such states are difficult to discern. But resultants that are mere moments of adverting, receiving, and investigating are certainly difficult to discern.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (on the vipākapaccaya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=244&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXR0YW5vIG5pcnVzc8SBaGFzYW50YWJoxIF2ZW5hIHNhaGFqxIF0YW7EgW1hcsWrcMSBbmHhuYMgbmlydXNzxIFoYXNhbnRhYmjEgXbEgXlhIHVwYWvEgXJha8SBIHZpcMSBa2FjaXR0YWNldGFzaWvEgQ%3D%3D)
