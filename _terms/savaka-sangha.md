### Grammatical Analysis
**[Sāvaka](/glossary/savaka/)-[saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)**: [m.] The Community of Disciples. Formed by *[sāvaka](/glossary/savaka/)* (hearer, disciple) + *[saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)* (community, assembly). The Visuddhimagga explains: "They hear the Blessed One's advice and instruction attentively (*sakkaccaṃ suṇanti*), thus they are disciples (*sāvakā*). The group of disciples is the *sāvakasaṅgho*; the meaning is that they are an assembly of disciples who have achieved unity through having virtue and view in common (*sīladiṭṭhisāmaññatāya saṅghātabhāvamāpanno sāvakasamūho*)" (Vism-i 1335, VII).

### Orthodox Definition
The *[Sāvaka](/glossary/savaka/)-[saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/)* is the community of the Buddha's noble disciples: in the formula of recollection it is the "four pairs of persons, the eight types of individuals" (*cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā*). The Visuddhimagga explains that taken in pairs these are four pairs (the one on the path and the one in the fruition of each stage), and taken singly they are eight individuals (Vism-i 1341). It is the noble community (*ariyasaṅgho*) of the eight noble individuals (*aṭṭha ariyapuggalasamūho*), as the Udāna commentary says (Ud-a 440).

The Mūla gives the formula in the Dhajagga Sutta (SN 11.3): the practitioner who recollects the Saṅgha "the Saṅgha of the Blessed One's disciples practises well (*suppaṭipanno*), practises straight (*ujuppaṭipanno*), practises the true way (*ñāyappaṭipanno*), practises properly (*sāmīcippaṭipanno*)", that is the four pairs and eight individuals, "worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world", will have fear, terror and horripilation abandoned. The same formula is the standard statement of unwavering confidence in the Saṅgha (A-ix 223). The Visuddhimagga (VII, Saṅghānussatikathā) takes it as the subject of the recollection of the Saṅgha (*saṅghānussati*), and it is the third of the Three Jewels (*saṅghe ratanaṃ paṇītaṃ*, Khp 109).

The conventional Order (*sammutisaṅgho*) is "in general a group of bhikkhus" (Kkh 1235), so the *sāvakasaṅgho* of this formula is not the same as the conventional Order, though a bhikkhu may belong to both.

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“No ce dhammaṃ anussareyyātha, atha saṅghaṃ anussareyyātha – ‘ suppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho ujuppaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho ñāyappaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho sāmīcippaṭipanno bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, yadidaṃ cattāri purisayugāni aṭṭha purisapuggalā esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, āhuneyyo pāhuneyyo dakkhiṇeyyo añjalikaraṇīyo anuttaraṃ puññakkhettaṃ lokassā ’ ti. Saṅghañhi vo, bhikkhave, anussarataṃ yaṃ bhavissati bhayaṃ vā chambhitattaṃ vā lomahaṃso vā, so pahīyissati.**  
> “If you cannot recollect the Dhamma, then you should recollect the Saṅgha: ‘The Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is practising the good way, practising the straight way, practising the true way, practising the proper way; that is, the four pairs of persons, the eight types of individuals—this Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples is worthy of gifts, worthy of hospitality, worthy of offerings, worthy of reverential salutation, the unsurpassed field of merit for the world.’ For when you recollect the Saṅgha, bhikkhus, whatever fear or trembling or terror you may have will be abandoned.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, sāvakasaṅgha)
> **Bhagavato ovādānusāsaniṃ sakkaccaṃ suṇantīti sāvakā. Sāvakānaṃ saṅgho sāvakasaṅgho , sīladiṭṭhisāmaññatāya saṅghātabhāvamāpanno sāvakasamūhoti attho.**  
> They hear the Blessed One’s advice and instruction attentively, thus they are disciples (sāvakā). The group of disciples is the Saṅgha of the disciples (sāvakasaṅgho); the meaning is that they are an assembly of disciples who have achieved unity through having virtue and view in common.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the four pairs and eight individuals)
> **Cattāri purisayugānīti yugaḷavasena paṭhamamaggaṭṭho phalaṭṭhoti idamekaṃ yugaḷanti evaṃ cattāri purisayugaḷāni honti. Aṭṭha purisapuggalāti purisapuggalavasena eko paṭhamamaggaṭṭho eko phalaṭṭhoti iminā nayena aṭṭheva purisapuggalā honti. Ettha ca purisoti vā puggaloti vā ekatthāni etāni padāni. Veneyyavasena panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅghoti yānimāni yugavasena cattāri purisayugāni, pāṭiekkato aṭṭha purisapuggalā, esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho, āhuneyyotiādīsu ānetvā hunitabbanti āhunaṃ, dūratopi ānetvā sīlavantesu dātabbanti attho.**  
> Four pairs of men (cattāri purisayugāni) means that, taking them in pairs, the one who stands on the first path and the one who stands in the first fruition make one pair; thus, there are four pairs of men. Eight types of individuals (aṭṭha purisapuggalā) means that, taking them as individuals, the one who stands on the first path is one, and the one who stands in the first fruition is one; in this way, there are exactly eight individuals. And here, 'man' (purisa) and 'individual' (puggala) are terms with the same meaning. But it is expressed in this way for the sake of those to be trained. This Saṅgha of the Blessed One’s disciples (esa bhagavato sāvakasaṅgho) refers to these four pairs of men when taken in pairs, and the eight individuals when taken individually. In the passage beginning with 'worthy of offerings' (āhuneyyo), what should be brought and offered is an offering (āhuna); the meaning is that which is to be brought even from far away and given to the virtuous.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VII, Saṅghānussatikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=212&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dMSBcmkgcHVyaXNheXVnxIFuxKt0aSB5dWdh4bi3YXZhc2VuYSBwYeG5rWhhbWFtYWdnYeG5reG5rWhvIHBoYWxh4bmt4bmtaG90aSBpZGFtZWth4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Udāna-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tassa yathāvuttaguṇassa bhagavato dhammassavanante jātattā sīladiṭṭhisāmaññena saṃhatattā ca sāvakasaṅghoti laddhanāmo aṭṭhaariyapuggalasamūho suppaṭipanno vata aho vata sammā paṭipanno, yo ariyasaṅgho evarūpassa īdisassa vaṭṭadukkhassa pahānāya anuppādanirodhāya anivattipaṭipadaṃ paṭipanno.**  
> The community of eight noble individuals, who have received the name 'the Sangha of disciples' because they were born near the Blessed One—who possesses the qualities just described—at the end of hearing the Dhamma, and because they are united by a commonality of moral conduct and views, is indeed practicing the good way, is indeed practicing rightly. This noble Sangha is practicing the non-declining course of practice for the abandonment and non-arising cessation of the suffering of cyclic existence such as this.  
>  
> — *Udāna-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_03&page_number=110&name=ud%C4%81naa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2EgeWF0aMSBdnV0dGFndeG5h2Fzc2EgYmhhZ2F2YXRvIGRoYW1tYXNzYXZhbmFudGUgasSBdGF0dMSBIHPEq2xhZGnhua3hua1oaXPEgW1hw7HDsWVuYQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Sutta**: *Dhajagga Sutta* (SN 11.3, S-i 3205–3216) – the recollection of the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha to dispel fear and terror.
* **Canonical**: *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (Navaka Nipāta, AN 9.27, A-ix 216–223) – the same formula as the unwavering confidence in the Saṅgha of a stream-enterer.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VII, Saṅghānussatikathā, Vism-i 1333–1341) – the explanation of each term of the formula; *Udāna-aṭṭhakathā* (Ud-a 440).
