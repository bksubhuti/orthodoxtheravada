### Grammatical Analysis
**Puggalapaññatti**: [f.] Designation of Individuals. Formed by *puggala* (person, individual) + *paññatti* (designation, concept, making known).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Puggalapaññatti* is the fourth book of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka (after the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, Vibhaṅga and Dhātukathā, as listed in Ps-i 2135). It begins with six designations (*paññatti*): of the aggregates, sense bases, elements, truths, faculties and persons (Pp 6). The Aṭṭhakathā explains that the first five were already treated at length in the Vibhaṅga, so "the sixth, the designation of persons, had not been spoken of earlier" (Pp-a 194).

The Aṭṭhakathā also gives six other designations by the teachers' method, independent of the Pāḷi: the concepts of what exists and of what does not exist, and their combinations. A quality such as the wholesome and unwholesome, "existent by way of ultimate reality", is *vijjamānapaññatti*; "women, men and so on", established "merely by worldly expression" (*lokaniruttimattasiddha*), is *avijjamānapaññatti* (Pp-a 183). So persons are conventional designations, not ultimate realities.

The book sets out the types of person in numbered sections, from "Ekakauddesa" (singles) to "Dasakaniddesa" (tens) (Pp 5, 560). It defines, for example, the worldling (*puthujjana*): "the person whose three fetters have not been abandoned, and who is not practising to abandon them" (Pp 199); the stream-enterer (*sotāpanno*, Pp 221); the angry person (*kodhano*, Pp 237); and the one "practising for his own welfare, not for another's" (Pp 441).

### Quote (Mūla: Puggalapaññatti)
> **Cha paññattiyo – khandhapaññatti, āyatanapaññatti, dhātupaññatti, saccapaññatti, indriyapaññatti, puggalapaññattīti.**  
> 1. There are six designations: the designation of groups, the designation of sense bases, the designation of elements, the designation of truths, the designation of faculties, and the designation of human types.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Puggalapaññatti, the worldling)
> **Katamo ca puggalo puthujjano? Yassa puggalassa tīṇi saṃyojanāni appahīnāni; na ca tesaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya paṭipanno – ayaṃ vuccati puggalo “puthujjano”.**  
> 9. What sort of person is said to be an average person? The person whose three fetters have not been put away; and who is not proceeding to put these away—such a person is said to be an average person.  
>  
> — Puggalapaññattipāḷi (Niddeso, para. 9) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_04&page_number=115&name=puggalapa%C3%B1%C3%B1attip%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OS4gS2F0YW1vIGNhIHB1Z2dhbG8gcHV0aHVqamFubz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Pāḷimuttakena pana aṭṭhakathānayena aparāpi cha paññattiyo – vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena avijjamānapaññattīti. Tattha kusalākusalasseva saccikaṭṭhaparamatthavasena vijjamānassa sato sambhūtassa dhammassa paññāpanā vijjamānapaññatti nāma. Tathā avijjamānassa lokaniruttimattasiddhassa itthipurisādikassa paññāpanā avijjamānapaññatti nāma.**  
> But according to the teacher's method, independent of the Pāli (text), there are six other designations: concept of what is existent, concept of what is non-existent, concept of what is non-existent by means of what is existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is non-existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is existent, and concept of what is non-existent by means of what is non-existent. Among these, the exposition of a quality that is actually existent and arisen, by way of ultimate reality and true meaning, such as the wholesome and unwholesome, is called a concept of what is existent. Likewise, the exposition of what is non-existent, such as women and men, and so on, which is established merely by worldly expression, is called a concept of what is non-existent.  
>  
> — Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā (Pp-a 183) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=26&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UMSB4bi3aW11dHRha2VuYSBwYW5hIGHhua3hua1oYWthdGjEgW5heWVuYSBhcGFyxIFwaSBjaGEgcGHDscOxYXR0aXlv)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā, the sixth designation)
> **Sammāsambuddhena hi tile visārayamānena viya, vāke hīrayamānena viya ca, heṭṭhā vibhaṅgappakaraṇe imāsaṃ pañcannaṃ paññattīnaṃ vatthubhūtā khandhādayo nippadesena kathitāti tena te idha ekadeseneva kathesi. Chaṭṭhā puggalapaññatti heṭṭhā akathitāva.**  
> Indeed, just as if scattering sesame seeds or separating fibers, the Perfectly Self-Awakened One, in the Vibhaṅga treatise earlier, had spoken completely about the aggregates and so on, which are the bases of these five designations. Therefore, he spoke about them here only in one aspect. The sixth designation of persons was indeed unspoken earlier.  
>  
> — Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā (Pp-a 194) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=29&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U2FtbcSBc2FtYnVkZGhlbmEgaGkgdGlsZSB2aXPEgXJheWFtxIFuZW5hIHZpeWEsIHbEgWtl)
