### Grammatical Analysis
**Māna**: [m.] conceit. The Atthasālinī explains: "it is called *māna* because it thinks 'I am' (*maññatīti māno*)" (As 1803:3); the Ṭīkā adds that it conceives by "I am superior" and so on (*seyyohamasmītyādinā maññatīti māno*, Abhidh-s-t 1034:6). So from *maññati* (to think, to conceive).

### Orthodox Definition
*Māna* is conceit. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines the fetter of conceit (*mānasaṃyojanaṃ*) as three conceits: "I am superior", "I am equal", "I am inferior" (*seyyohamasmīti māno, sadisohamasmīti māno, hīnohamasmīti māno*), "whatever such conceit, conceiving, the state of conceiving, elevation, elevated state, standard, self-exaltation, the mind's desire for the standard" (Dhs 1900:2-3).

In the suttas it is the third of the five higher fetters: lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness and ignorance (AN 9.70, A-ix 462:3), which after the five lower fetters makes it the eighth of the ten. The Khemaka Sutta (SN 22.89) says that even a noble disciple who has abandoned the five lower fetters still has, in the five aggregates, a residual conceit "I am", a desire "I am" and an underlying tendency "I am" that has not been uprooted; it is uprooted by contemplating the rise and fall of the aggregates (S-iii 604:1-5). The sutta compares this to a cloth washed with cleaning salt, lye and cowdung, which keeps a residual smell until it is put in a scented casket (S-iii 605).

The Atthasālinī gives its marks: characteristic, haughtiness (*unnatilakkhaṇo*); function, exalting (*sampaggaharaso*); manifestation, desire to be like a banner (*ketukamyatāpaccupaṭṭhāno*); proximate cause, greed disconnected from wrong view (*diṭṭhivippayuttalobhapadaṭṭhāno*); and it should be seen as like madness (*ummādo viya daṭṭhabbo*) (As 1803:4; Vism-ii 469:3). In the Abhidhamma it therefore arises in the greed-rooted consciousnesses that are dissociated from views.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, Uddhambhāgiya Sutta)
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, uddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañca? Rūparāgo, arūparāgo, māno, uddhaccaṃ, avijjā – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni.**  
> “Mendicants, there are five higher fetters. What five? Lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance—these indeed are the five higher fetters.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, Khemaka Sutta)
> **“Kiñcāpi, āvuso, ariyasāvakassa pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni pahīnāni bhavanti, atha khvassa hoti – ‘ yo ca pañcasu upādānakkhandhesu anusahagato asmīti māno, asmīti chando, asmīti anusayo asamūhato.**  
> “Friends, even though a noble disciple has abandoned the five lower fetters, still, in relation to the five aggregates subject to clinging, there lingers in him a residual conceit ‘I am,’ a desire ‘I am,’ an underlying tendency ‘I am’ that has not yet been uprooted. Sometime later he dwells contemplating rise and fall in the five aggregates subject to clinging: ‘Such is form, such its origin, such its passing away; such is feeling … such is perception … such are volitional formations … such is consciousness, such its origin, such its passing away.’  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 22.89, Khemaka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_03&page_number=106&name=khandhavaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEtpw7FjxIFwaSwgxIF2dXNvLCBhcml5YXPEgXZha2Fzc2EgcGHDsWNvcmFtYmjEgWdpecSBbmkgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBbmk%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamaṃ mānasaṃyojanaṃ? Seyyohamasmīti māno, sadisohamasmīti māno, hīnohamasmīti māno. Yo evarūpo māno maññanā maññitattaṃ unnati unnamo dhajo sampaggāho ketukamyatā cittassa – idaṃ vuccati mānasaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1121. Which among these is the fetter of conceit? The conceit ‘I am superior’, the conceit ‘I am equal’, the conceit ‘I am inferior’. Whatever such conceit, conceiving, the state of conceiving, elevation, elevated state, standard, self-exaltation, and the mind’s desire for the standard there is — this is called the fetter of conceit.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Saṃyojanagocchaka, §1121) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=225&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEyMS4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyBtxIFuYXNh4bmDeW9qYW5h4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Tattha maññatīti ‘ māno ’. So unnatilakkhaṇo, sampaggaharaso, ketukamyatāpaccupaṭṭhāno, diṭṭhivippayuttalobhapadaṭṭhāno, ummādo viya daṭṭhabboti.**  
> Among these, it is called ‘conceit’ (māno) because it thinks ‘I am.’ That (so) (conceit) has the characteristic of haughtiness (unnatilakkhaṇo), its function is exalting (sampaggaharaso), its manifestation is desiring to be like a banner (ketukamyatāpaccupaṭṭhāno), its proximate cause is greed disconnected from wrong view (diṭṭhivippayuttalobhapadaṭṭhāno), and it should be understood (daṭṭhabbo) like (viya) madness.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā, §400) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=297&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIG1hw7HDsWF0xKt0aSDigJggbcSBbm8g4oCZLg%3D%3D)
