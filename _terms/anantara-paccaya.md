### Grammatical Analysis
**Anantarapaccaya**: [m.] Proximity condition; contiguity condition. Formed by *an* (without) + *antara* (gap, interval) + *paccaya* (condition). Literally, the condition of having "no gap."

### Orthodox Definition
*Anantara-paccaya* is the fourth of the 24 Conditions. It governs the unbroken sequence of the mind-stream (*[citta](/glossary/citta/)-santāna*). 

The Paṭṭhāna states it in its first form: "Cakkhuviññāṇadhātu taṃsampayuttakā ca dhammā manodhātuyā taṃsampayuttakānañca dhammānaṃ anantarapaccayena paccayo" – eye-consciousness and its associated states are related to the mind-element and its associated states by the contiguity condition, and likewise mind-element to mind-consciousness (Paṭṭh-i 15:1-2). The Visuddhimagga defines it: "a phenomenon that is helpful by way of immediacy is the contiguity condition" (*anantarabhāvena upakārako dhammo anantarapaccayo*), and explains that the fixed order of cittas (eye-consciousness, then mind-element, then mind-consciousness) is accomplished by way of each preceding citta, so that each phenomenon able to bring about the arising of the fitting next citta is the contiguity condition (Vism-ii 923:1, 923:4, 923:6). The Paṭṭhānuddesadīpanī glosses the word: it is *anantara* because it causes to arise, in its own immediate sequence, another phenomenon like itself (*attano anantare attasadisassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhena*), and *paccaya* in the sense of helping; even when the preceding citta has ceased, its momentum of cogitation (*cintanakiriyāvego*) does not subside until it has produced the succeeding citta (Paṭṭhuddes 52:2-5).

### Quote
> **Anantarapaccayoti – cakkhuviññāṇadhātu taṃsampayuttakā ca dhammā manodhātuyā taṃsampayuttakānañca dhammānaṃ anantarapaccayena paccayo. Manodhātu taṃsampayuttakā ca dhammā manoviññāṇadhātuyā taṃsampayuttakānañca dhammānaṃ anantarapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 4. Contiguity condition: Eye-consciousness-element and its associated states are related to mind-element and its associated states by contiguity condition. Mind-element and its associated states are related to mind-consciousness-element and its associated states by contiguity condition.

### Quote
> **Anantarabhāvena upakārako dhammo anantarapaccayo. Ayaṃ panettha sāro, yo hi esa cakkhuviññāṇānantarā manodhātu, manodhātuanantarā manoviññāṇadhātūtiādi cittaniyamo, so yasmā purimapurimacittavaseneva ijjhati, na aññathā, tasmā attano attano anantaraṃ anurūpassa cittuppādassa uppādanasamattho dhammo anantarapaccayo. “anantarapaccayoti cakkhuviññāṇadhātu taṃsampayuttakā ca dhammā manodhātuyā taṃsampayuttakānañca dhammānaṃ anantarapaccayena paccayo” tiādi (paṭṭhā.1.1.4).**  
> 598. A phenomenon that is helpful by way of immediate contiguity is an immediate contiguity condition (anantarapaccayo). The essence here is this: the fixed order of consciousness such as “the mind element follows immediately after eye-consciousness, the mind-consciousness element follows immediately after the mind element,” and so on, is accomplished only by the power of each preceding consciousness and not otherwise; therefore, a phenomenon that is capable of producing a suitable arising of consciousness immediately after itself is an immediate contiguity condition. “Immediate contiguity condition: the eye-consciousness element and its associated phenomena are conditions for the mind element and its associated phenomena by way of immediate contiguity condition,” and so on.  
>

### Quote
> **Kenaṭṭhena anantaro, kenaṭṭhena paccayoti. Attano anantare attasadisassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhena anantaro, upakārakaṭṭhena paccayo. Tattha attasadisassāti sārammaṇa bhāvena attanā sadisassa. Sārammaṇabhāvenāti ca yo dhammo ārammaṇena vinā na pavattati, so sārammaṇo nāma, evaṃ sārammaṇabhāvena. Dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhenāti purimasmiṃ citte niruddhepi tassa cintanakiriyāvego na vūpasammati, pacchimaṃ cittaṃ uppādetvā eva vūpasammati, evaṃ pacchimassa dhammantarassa uppādanaṭṭhena.**  
> In what sense is it contiguity, and in what sense is it a condition? It is contiguity in the sense of causing the arising of a different phenomenon that is similar to itself in its own immediate succession; it is a condition in the sense of being a support. Therein, 'similar to itself' means similar to itself in being a state that has a mental object. And 'in being a state that has a mental object' means: any phenomenon that does not proceed without an object is called 'having a mental object'; thus, it is in the sense of having a mental object. In the sense of causing the arising of a different phenomenon, even when the former consciousness has ceased, its impetus of the act of thinking does not subside; it subsides only after having caused the subsequent consciousness to arise; thus, it is in the sense of causing the arising of the subsequent different phenomenon.  
>  
> — Paṭṭhānuddesadīpanīpāṭha (Kenaṭṭhena anantaro)

### Textual References
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (Paṭṭh-i 15:1-2; the list of the 24 conditions at Paṭṭh-i 8:1 and Abhidh-s 709:1 puts it fourth; the statement for wholesome states at Paṭṭh-i 20:1-2: "Purimā purimā kusalā dhammā pacchimānaṃ pacchimānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anantarapaccayena paccayo").
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 923:1-9) – the definition, the fixed order of cittas, and that anantara and samanantara differ only in wording, not in meaning (Vism-ii 923:7-9); the *Paṭṭhāna-aṭṭhakathā* quotes the same Paṭṭhāna text (Paṭṭh-a 1922:7).
* **Exposition**: *Paṭṭhānuddesa-dīpanī* (Paṭṭhuddes 52:1-5) – the sense of *anantara*, and the momentum of cogitation (*cintanakiriyāvego*).
