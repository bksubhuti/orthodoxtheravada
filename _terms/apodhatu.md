### Grammatical Analysis
**Āpodhātu**: [f.] water element; element of cohesion; fluidity. Formed by *āpo* (water, liquid) + *[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)* (element). 

### Orthodox Definition
*Āpodhātu* is the second of the four great physical elements (*mahābhūta*). Rather than literal water, it represents the abstract material principle of **cohesion, binding, and fluidity**. 

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as "that which is water, belonging to water, wetness, belonging to wetness, the binding quality of matter" (*yaṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ bandhanattaṃ rūpassa*, Dhs 1384:2, §651). The Atthasālinī explains the word (*āpo ca taṃ nissattasuññatasabhāvaṭṭhena dhātu cāti āpodhātu*, As 2135:4) and gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: trickling or flowing (*paggharaṇalakkhaṇā*), increasing or swelling (*brūhanarasā*) and cohesion (*saṅgahapaccupaṭṭhānā*); each of the four elements has the other three as its proximate cause (As 2135:10-11; Vism-i 2172:2). Without the water element, the earth element (solidity) would not hold together; *āpo* provides the cohesion that holds physical shapes intact.

Of the four great elements the Atthasālinī says that only three are known by touching: earth, fire and wind; the water element is not (As 2135:5-9, where the three touchable ones are listed and āpodhātu is given separately). Internally, the Rāhulovāda Sutta lists it as bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, saliva, snot, oil of the joints and urine (*pittaṃ semhaṃ pubbo lohitaṃ sedo medo assu vasā kheḷo siṅghāṇikā lasikā muttaṃ*, M-ii 328:4).

### Quote
> **“Katamā ca, rāhula, āpodhātu? Āpodhātu siyā ajjhattikā, siyā bāhirā. Katamā ca, rāhula, ajjhattikā āpodhātu? Yaṃ ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ āpo āpogataṃ upādinnaṃ, seyyathidaṃ – pittaṃ semhaṃ pubbo lohitaṃ sedo medo assu vasā kheḷo siṅghāṇikā lasikā muttaṃ, yaṃ vā panaññampi kiñci ajjhattaṃ paccattaṃ āpo āpogataṃ upādinnaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati, rāhula, ajjhattikā āpodhātu. Yā ceva kho pana ajjhattikā āpodhātu yā ca bāhirā āpodhātu āpodhāturevesā.**  
> “And what, Rāhula, is the water element? The water element might be internal, or it might be external. And what, Rāhula, is the internal water element? Whatever is internal, belonging to oneself, water, watery, and clung-to, namely: bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, spittle, snot, oil-of-the-joints, urine, or whatever else is internal, belonging to oneself, water, watery, and clung-to — this is called the internal water element. The internal water element and the external water element are just the water element.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 62, Mahārāhulovāda Sutta; heading "2. ### Quote
> **Katamaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ āpodhātu? Yaṃ āpo āpogataṃ sineho sinehagataṃ bandhanattaṃ rūpassa – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ āpodhātu.**  
> 651. Which form is the water element? Which is water, watery; which is cohesion, having the quality of cohesion; which is the binding nature of form – this is that water element.  
>

### Quote
> **Tattha kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā pathavīdhātu , patiṭṭhānarasā, sampaṭicchanapaccupaṭṭhānā. Tejodhātu uṇhattalakkhaṇā, paripācanarasā, maddavānuppadānapaccupaṭṭhānā. Vāyodhātu vitthambhanalakkhaṇā, samudīraṇarasā, abhinīhārapaccupaṭṭhānā. Purimā pana ‘ āpodhātu ’ paggharaṇalakkhaṇā, brūhanarasā, saṅgahapaccupaṭṭhānā. Ekekā cettha sesattayapadaṭṭhānāti veditabbā.**  
> Therein, the earth element (pathavīdhātu) has the characteristic of solidity, the function of providing a foundation, and the manifestation of receiving (or supporting associated phenomena). The fire element (tejodhātu) has the characteristic of heat, the function of maturation, and the manifestation of producing softness. The air element (vāyodhātu) has the characteristic of distension, the function of propulsion, and the manifestation of movement. But the previously mentioned water element (āpodhātu) has the characteristic of flowing, the function of growth, and the manifestation of cohesion. And here, each one (of these elements) should be known as the proximate cause for the other three.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on the four elements) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=368&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGtha2toYeG4t2F0dGFsYWtraGHhuYfEgSBwYXRoYXbEq2RoxIF0dSAsIHBhdGnhua3hua1oxIFuYXJhc8SBLCBzYW1wYeG5rWljY2hhbmFwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBLg%3D%3D)
