### Grammatical Analysis
**Satipaṭṭhāna**: [nt.] foundation of mindfulness; establishment of awareness. The Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā says that the word is used in three senses: the domain of mindfulness (*satigocara*, the body and so on), the Teacher's freedom from aversion and fondness towards disciples who practise in three ways, and mindfulness itself (*sati*) (Ps-i 1175). It explains the compound in two ways:
1. *Satiyā paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ*: the foundation (*paṭṭhāna*) of mindfulness, as the domain of mindfulness; as in the Paṭisambhidāmagga, "the body is the *paṭṭhāna*, not mindfulness; mindfulness is both the *paṭṭhāna* and mindfulness". It is "to be established by mindfulness" (*paṭṭhapetabbato*), like *hatthiṭṭhāna*, "elephant-stable".
2. *Satiyeva paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ*: mindfulness itself is the *paṭṭhāna*, because it "stands" (*patiṭṭhāti*): it enters and plunges into the object (*upaṭṭhāti okkantitvā pakkhanditvā pavattati*), as in "the four satipaṭṭhānas, developed and made much of, fulfil the seven factors of awakening". The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī says the same: *satiyeva paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ* (Abhidh-s-t 1606).

### Orthodox Definition
*Satipaṭṭhāna* is the practice taught as the *ekāyano maggo* in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (DN 22) and the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10): "this is the one-way path (*ekāyano maggo*) for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of sorrow and lamentation, for the passing away of pain and displeasure, for the attainment of the right way, for the realization of Nibbāna, that is to say the four satipaṭṭhānas" (D-ii 1344, M-i 283). The four are: contemplating the body in the body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind and dhammas in dhammas, "ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure in the world".

The sutta expands them (M-i 283–351):
1. *Kāyānupassanā*: the sections on breathing (*ānāpāna*), the postures (*iriyāpatha*), clear comprehension (*sampajāna*), repulsiveness (*paṭikūlamanasikāra*), the elements (*dhātumanasikāra*) and the nine charnel-ground contemplations (*navasivathika*): fourteen in all (M-i 286–311).
2. *Vedanānupassanā*: understanding a pleasant feeling as pleasant, a painful one as painful and a neither-painful-nor-pleasant one as such, and further whether worldly or unworldly (M-i 313).
3. *Cittānupassanā*: understanding a mind with lust as with lust and one free of lust as free of lust, likewise with hatred and delusion, and so on (M-i 316).
4. *Dhammānupassanā*: contemplating dhammas under the hindrances (*nīvaraṇa*), the aggregates (*khandha*), the sense bases (*āyatana*), the factors of awakening (*bojjhaṅga*) and the Noble Truths (*sacca*) (M-i 318–351).

The Vibhaṅga (7, Satipaṭṭhānavibhaṅga) gives the same four, adding that each is practised internally, externally and both (Vibh 1318), and defines "mindful" (*satimā*) by *sati*: "whatever is mindfulness, recollection ... right mindfulness, the mindfulness factor of awakening" (Vibh 1353).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Ekāyano ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṃ visuddhiyā, sokaparidevānaṃ samatikkamāya dukkhadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamāya ñāyassa adhigamāya nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṃ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā. “Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā, vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ.**  
> “This is a one-way path, monks, for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of grief and lamentation, for the extinction of pain and sorrow, for attaining the right way, for the direct realisation of Nibbāna, that is to say, the four ways of attending to mindfulness. Which four? Here, monks, a bhikkhu dwells contemplating the nature of the body in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of feelings in feelings, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of the mind in the mind, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating the nature of things in things, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Ekāyano ayaṃ, bhikkhave, maggo sattānaṃ visuddhiyā, sokaparidevānaṃ samatikkamāya, dukkhadomanassānaṃ atthaṅgamāya, ñāyassa adhigamāya, nibbānassa sacchikiriyāya, yadidaṃ cattāro satipaṭṭhānā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, this is the one-way path for the purification of beings, for the overcoming of grief and lamentation, for the extinction of pain and sorrow, for attaining the right way, for the direct realisation of Nibbāna—that is to say, the four ways of attending to mindfulness.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā – idha bhikkhu ajjhattaṃ kāye kāyānupassī viharati bahiddhā kāye kāyānupassī viharati ajjhattabahiddhā kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, ajjhattaṃ vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati bahiddhā vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ajjhattabahiddhā vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, ajjhattaṃ citte cittānupassī viharati bahiddhā citte cittānupassī viharati ajjhattabahiddhā citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ, ajjhattaṃ dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati bahiddhā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ajjhattabahiddhā dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ.**  
> 355. The four ways of attending to mindfulness: Here, a bhikkhu dwells contemplating the nature of the body in the body, dwells contemplating the nature of the body outwardly in the body, dwells contemplating the nature of the body internally and externally in the body, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world; he dwells contemplating the nature of feelings in feelings, dwells contemplating the nature of feelings outwardly in feelings, dwells contemplating the nature of feelings internally and externally in feelings, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world; he dwells contemplating the nature of the mind in the mind, dwells contemplating the nature of the mind outwardly in the mind, dwells contemplating the nature of the mind internally and externally in the mind, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world; he dwells contemplating the nature of things in things, dwells contemplating the nature of things outwardly in things, dwells contemplating the nature of things internally and externally in things, ardent, clearly comprehending, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Satipaṭṭhānavibhaṅga, §355) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=200&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzU1LiBDYXR0xIFybyBzYXRpcGHhua3hua1oxIFuxIEg4oCTIGlkaGEgYmhpa2todQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tassattho – patiṭṭhāti asminti paṭṭhānaṃ. Kā patiṭṭhāti? Sati. Satiyā paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ. Padhānaṭṭhānanti vā paṭṭhānaṃ. Satiyā paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ, hatthiṭṭhānaassaṭṭhānādīni viya. “Tayo satipaṭṭhānā, yadariyo sevati, yadariyo sevamāno satthā gaṇamanusāsitumarahatī” ti (ma. ni.3.311) etthāpi tidhā paṭipannesu sāvakesu satthuno paṭighānunayavītivattatā “satipaṭṭhānan” ti vuttā. Tassattho – paṭṭhapetabbato paṭṭhānaṃ, pavattayitabbatoti attho. Kena paṭṭhapetabbatoti? Satiyā. Satiyā paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānanti. “Cattāro satipaṭṭhānā bhāvitā bahulīkatā satta bojjhaṅge paripūrentī” tiādīsu (saṃ. ni.5.989) pana satiyeva “satipaṭṭhānan” ti vuccati. Tassattho – patiṭṭhātīti paṭṭhānaṃ, upaṭṭhāti okkantitvā pakkhanditvā pavattatīti attho.**  
> The meaning there is: because it is established in this, it is called ‘paṭṭhāna’ (foundation/establishment). What is established? Mindfulness. The establishment of mindfulness is ‘satipaṭṭhāna.’ Alternatively, it is called ‘paṭṭhāna’ in the sense of a primary foundation. The establishment of mindfulness is ‘satipaṭṭhāna,’ like the terms for ‘elephant-base’ or ‘horse-base.’ In the passage: “Three foundations of mindfulness that the noble one practices; practicing them, the teacher is worthy to instruct the group” (MN 117), the Teacher’s freedom from aversion and attachment toward disciples practicing in three ways is called ‘satipaṭṭhāna.’ Its meaning is: because it should be established, it is called ‘paṭṭhāna’ (foundation), meaning it should be brought about. By what should it be established? By mindfulness. The establishment of mindfulness is ‘satipaṭṭhāna.’ In the passage: “Four foundations of mindfulness, cultivated and practiced frequently, fulfill the seven factors of awakening” (SN 47.9), mindfulness itself is called ‘satipaṭṭhāna.’ Its meaning is: because it establishes itself, it is called ‘paṭṭhāna’ (foundation); it occurs by entering, leaping forth, and spreading out.  
>  
> — *Mūlapaṇṇāsa-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=243&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2F0dGhvIOKAkyBwYXRp4bmt4bmtaMSBdGkgYXNtaW50aSBwYeG5reG5rWjEgW5h4bmDLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Paṭṭhātīti paṭṭhānaṃ, asubhaggahaṇādivasena anupavisitvā kāyādiārammaṇe pavattatītyattho, satiyeva paṭṭhānaṃ satipaṭṭhānaṃ.**  
> 30. It is a foundation (paṭṭhānaṃ) because it “stands” (paṭṭhāti); the meaning is that, having entered (anupavisitvā) by way of the grasping of the sign of foulness and so forth, it occurs in objects such as the body. Mindfulness itself is the foundation: “foundation of mindfulness” (satipaṭṭhānaṃ).  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=227&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MzAuIFBh4bmt4bmtaMSBdMSrdGkgcGHhua3hua1oxIFuYeG5gywgYXN1YmhhZ2dhaGHhuYfEgWRpdmFzZW5hIGFudXBhdmlzaXR2xIEga8SBecSBZGnEgXJhbW1h4bmHZQ%3D%3D)
