### Grammatical Analysis
**Animitta**: [adj./nt.] Signless; markless; devoid of perceptual signs. Formed by negative prefix *a* (not, without) + *nimitta* (sign, mark, characteristic). 

### Orthodox Definition
*Animitta* is the name of one of the three liberations (*vimokkha*: suññata, animitta, appaṇihita; Paṭis 1135:4), intimately connected to the contemplation of impermanence (*[anicca](/glossary/anicca/)*).

The Mūla gives two uses. In the Paṭisambhidāmagga, "Katamo animitto vimokkho? Idha bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā iti paṭisañcikkhati – 'suññamidaṃ attena vā attaniyena vā' ti. So tattha nimittaṃ na karotīti – animitto vimokkho" (Paṭis 1139:1-5), and the three liberations are listed as *suññato, animitto, appaṇihito* (Paṭis 1135:4). In the Animittapañhā Sutta the Elder Moggallāna says (the Buddha addresses him as "moggallāna", S-iv 1608:1-2): "sabbanimittānaṃ amanasikārā animittaṃ cetosamādhiṃ upasampajja viharāmi" (S-iv 1607:6). The Paṭisambhidāmagga also says that for one who attends to impermanence, with much resolution (*adhimokkha*), the signless liberation predominates (Paṭis 1231:1).

The commentaries explain: when the path emerges from the contemplation of impermanence, that insight, occurring as the opponent of the sign of permanence (*niccanimittapaṭipakkhavasena*), is called signless (Paṭis-a 978:4); Nibbāna is "signless" as the opposite of the signs of formations (*saṅkhāranimittapaṭipakkhe nibbāne*, Paṭis-a 1182:2; Paṭis-a 1994:2); and the knowledge of change-of-lineage takes the signless Nibbāna as its object and gives the path the name, so "the path is called signless" (*animittaṃ nibbānaṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā … maggo animittoti vutto*, Vism-ii 1852:5-6; Paṭis-a 1952:15-16). The path that arrives by the contemplation of impermanence is therefore the "signless liberation" (*animittavimokkha*), and it also receives the names "desireless" and "emptiness" liberation from its qualities and its object (Paṭis-a 1990:1-4).

### Quote
> **Katamo animitto vimokkho ? Idha bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā iti paṭisañcikkhati – “suññamidaṃ attena vā attaniyena vā” ti. So tattha nimittaṃ na karotīti – animitto vimokkho. Ayaṃ animitto vimokkho.**  
> What is liberation by signlessness? Here, a bhikkhu, who has gone to the wilderness or has gone to the root of a tree or has gone to an empty place, considers thus: “This is void of self or of what belongs to self.” He contrives no sign thereon; thus it is liberation by signlessness. This is liberation by signlessness.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Vimokkhakathā, §211) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=232&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=S2F0YW1vIGFuaW1pdHRvIHZpbW9ra2hvID8%3D)

### Quote

### Quote
> **Tatra hi gotrabhuñāṇaṃ animittaṃ nibbānaṃ ārammaṇaṃ katvā animittanāmakaṃ hutvā sayaṃ āgamanīyaṭṭhāne ṭhatvā maggassa nāmaṃ detīti vadanti. Tena maggo animittoti vutto.**  
> For there they say that change-of-lineage knowledge (gotrabhuñāṇaṃ), having made the signless Nibbāna its object and having become named ‘signless’, standing (ṭhatvā) at the place of arrival, gives its name to the path. Because of that, the path is stated to be 'signless'.  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Animittapañhā Sutta* (SN 40.9; heading "9. Animittapañhāsuttaṃ", S-iv 1606:1) – the signless concentration of mind (*animitto cetosamādhi*), S-iv 1607:1-6.
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* (Vimokkhakathā: Paṭis 1135:4, 1139:1-5, 1231:1) – the three liberations and the definition of the signless liberation.
* **Commentary**: *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 978:4, 1182:2, 1990:1-4) and *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 1852:5-6) – the signless liberation as the path reached through contemplation of impermanence, and Nibbāna as the opposite of the signs of formations.
