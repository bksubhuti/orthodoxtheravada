### Grammatical Analysis
**Cetanā**: [f.] volition, intention. The Aṭṭhakathā gives the derivation: "*cetayatīti cetanā*", it is volition because it intends, that is, "it unifies its associated states on the object" (As 1168:1). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it with the synonyms *cetanā sañcetanā cetayitattaṃ*, "volition, intention, the state of having intended" (Dhs 395:2).

### Orthodox Definition
The Buddha says: "Monks, I say that intention (*cetanā*) is kamma. Having intended, one does kamma by body, speech and mind" (A-vi 650:2-3). In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, volition at the time of the first wholesome consciousness is "the volition arisen from the contact of the corresponding mind-consciousness element, intention, the state of having intended" (Dhs 395:2).

The Atthasālinī gives its characteristic, function and the simile: it is characterized by intending (*cetayitalakkhaṇā*), its function is accumulating (*āyūhanarasā*); the accumulating function exists only in wholesome and unwholesome states, where volition acts with redoubled zeal and effort (As 1168:2-8). It is like a field owner who takes fifty-five strong men to reap the field: he shows greater zeal and effort, tells them to keep taking, marks the boundary and prepares the way, and likewise volition, at the point of accumulating wholesome and unwholesome kamma, works with doubled zeal and effort together with the fifty-five wholesome states (As 1168:12-17). The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha lists volition among the seven mental factors universal to every consciousness (Abhidh-s 84:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Kammaṃ, bhikkhave, veditabbaṃ … pe … kammanirodhagāminī paṭipadā veditabbāti, iti kho panetaṃ vuttaṃ. Kiñcetaṃ paṭicca vuttaṃ? Cetanāhaṃ, bhikkhave, kammaṃ vadāmi. Cetayitvā kammaṃ karoti – kāyena vācāya manasā.**  
> “‘Kamma, monks, should be known… the path leading to the cessation of kamma should be known.’ Thus it has been stated. In reference to what was this stated? Monks, I say that intention is kamma. Having intended, one does kamma bodily, verbally, or mentally.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye cetanā hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajā cetanā sañcetanā cetayitattaṃ– ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cetanā hoti.**  
> 5. What is volition at that time? Whatever volition arisen from the contact of the corresponding mind-consciousness element (tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātusamphassajā), intention, or the state of having intended exists at that time—this is volition at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Dhs 395, §5) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NS4gS2F0YW3EgSB0YXNtaeG5gyBzYW1heWUgY2V0YW7EgSBob3RpPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Cetayatīti cetanā saddhiṃ attanā sampayuttadhamme ārammaṇe abhisandahatīti attho. Sā cetayitalakkhaṇā, cetanābhāvalakkhaṇāti attho. Āyūhanarasā.**  
> It is called volition (cetanā) because it intends; this means it unifies its co-arising phenomena with itself on the object. It is characterized by being conceived; this means it is characterized by the state of volition. Its function is to accumulate.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 1168) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=154&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2V0YXlhdMSrdGkgY2V0YW7EgSBzYWRkaGnhuYMgYXR0YW7EgSBzYW1wYXl1dHRhZGhhbW1lIMSBcmFtbWHhuYdl)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha)
> **Kathaṃ? Phasso vedanā saññā cetanā ekaggatā jīvitindriyaṃ manasikāro ceti sattime cetasikā sabbacittasādhāraṇā nāma.**  
> 2. How? Contact, feeling, perception, volition, one-pointedness, life faculty, and attention—these seven mental factors are universal to every consciousness.  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Abhidh-s 84) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=7&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=Mi4gS2F0aGHhuYM%2F)
