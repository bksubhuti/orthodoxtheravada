### Grammatical Analysis
**Sammāsati**: [f.] Right Mindfulness. Formed by *sammā* (rightly) + *[sati](/glossary/sati/)* (mindfulness). The Aṭṭhakathā derives *sati*: "they remember by means of it, or it itself remembers, or it is just the act of remembering" (*saranti tāya, sayaṃ vā sarati saraṇamattameva vā esā*, Vism-ii 437).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sammāsati* is the seventh limb of the Noble Eightfold Path. The Mūla defines it as the four foundations of mindfulness (*[satipaṭṭhāna](/glossary/satipatthana/)*), each "ardent, clearly comprehending, mindful, having removed covetousness and displeasure concerning the world":
1. Contemplating the body in the body (*kāye kāyānupassī*).
2. Contemplating feelings in feelings (*vedanāsu vedanānupassī*).
3. Contemplating the mind in the mind (*citte cittānupassī*).
4. Contemplating dhammas in dhammas (*dhammesu dhammānupassī*) (DN 22).

The Abhidhamma gives the same factor in the wholesome citta as *sati anussati paṭissati sati saraṇatā dhāraṇatā apilāpanatā asammussanatā sati satindriyaṃ satibalaṃ sammāsati*, and adds in the supramundane state "the mindfulness factor of awakening, a path factor, included in the path" (Dhs 413; Vibh 764). The Visuddhimagga says of *sati*: it has the characteristic of not slipping away (*apilāpanalakkhaṇā*), the function of non-forgetfulness (*asammosarasā*), it is manifested as protection (*ārakkha*) or as "the state of confronting the object" (*visayābhimukhabhāva*); its proximate cause is firm perception or the foundations of mindfulness (*kāyādisatipaṭṭhāna*); it is seen as a pillar, because it is firmly established on the object, and as a gatekeeper, because it guards the eye-door and the rest (Vism-ii 437). In the path (XVI) right mindfulness is "the non-forgetfulness of mind that shakes off wrong mindfulness": its characteristic is establishing (*upaṭṭhāna*), its function not forgetting, its manifestation the abandonment of wrong mindfulness (Vism-ii 787). The Papañcasūdanī says the "one-way path" (*ekāyanamagga*) and the four foundations of mindfulness are the same in meaning (Ps-i 1176).
### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **“Katamā ca, bhikkhave, sammāsati? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāye kāyānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ; vedanāsu vedanānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ; citte cittānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ; dhammesu dhammānupassī viharati ātāpī sampajāno satimā vineyya loke abhijjhādomanassaṃ. Ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, sammāsati.**  
> “Now what, monks, is right mindfulness? Here, monks, a monk dwells contemplating (the nature of) the body in the body, ardent, fully aware, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating (the nature of) feelings in feelings, ardent, fully aware, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating (the nature of) the mind in the mind, ardent, fully aware, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. He dwells contemplating (the nature of) things in (various) things, ardent, fully aware, and mindful, after removing covetousness and displeasure regarding the world. This, monks, is called right mindfulness.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 22, Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta, Magga-sacca) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=250&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtxIEgY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtbcSBc2F0aT8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamā sammāsati? Yā sati anussati … pe … sammāsati satisambojjhaṅgo maggaṅgaṃ maggapariyāpannaṃ – ayaṃ vuccati “sammāsati”.**  
> Therein, which (katamā) is right mindfulness (sammāsati)? Whatever is mindfulness (sati), continuous recollection (anussati) ... (pe) right mindfulness (sammāsati), the mindfulness enlightenment factor (satisambojjhaṅgo), a path factor (maggaṅgaṃ), included in the path (maggapariyāpannaṃ) — this (ayaṃ) is called (vuccati) ‘‘right mindfulness’’.  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Saccavibhaṅga, Abhidhammabhājanīya) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=113&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGthdGFtxIEgc2FtbcSBc2F0aT8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye sammāsati hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye sati anussati paṭissati sati saraṇatā dhāraṇatā apilāpanatā asammussanatā sati satindriyaṃ satibalaṃ sammāsati – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye sammāsati hoti.**  
> 23. What is right mindfulness at that time? Whatever mindfulness, continuous recollection, specific mindfulness, mindfulness, recollecting, retention, penetration of the object, not forgetting, mindfulness, faculty of mindfulness, the power of mindfulness, or right mindfulness exists at that time—this is right mindfulness at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 23) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=21&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjMuIEthdGFtxIEgdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIHNhbW3EgXNhdGkgaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XIV)
> **Saranti tāya, sayaṃ vā sarati saraṇamattameva vā esāti sati. Sā apilāpanalakkhaṇā, asammosarasā, ārakkhapaccupaṭṭhānā, visayābhimukhabhāvapaccupaṭṭhānā vā, thirasaññāpadaṭṭhānā, kāyādisatipaṭṭhānapadaṭṭhānā vā. Ārammaṇe daḷhapatiṭṭhitattā pana esikā viya, cakkhudvārādirakkhaṇato dovāriko viya ca daṭṭhabbā.**  
> 465. They remember by means of it, or it itself remembers, or it is just the mere act of remembering—thus it is mindfulness (sati). It has the characteristic of not-slipping, the function of non-forgetfulness, and is manifested as protection or as the state of confronting an object. Its proximate cause is firm perception or the foundations of mindfulness such as the body. Because it is firmly established on the object, it should be seen as being like a pillar; and because it guards the eye-door and so on, it should be seen as being like a gate-keeper.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIV, para. 465) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=94&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDY1LiBTYXJhbnRpIHTEgXlhLCBzYXlh4bmDIHbEgSBzYXJhdGk%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga XVI)
> **Tassevaṃ vāyamato taṃsampayuttova micchāsativiniddhunano cetaso asammoso sammāsati nāma. Sā upaṭṭhānalakkhaṇā, asammussanarasā, micchāsatippahānapaccupaṭṭhānā.**  
> For one thus making an effort, the non-forgetfulness of mind associated with that, which shakes off wrong mindfulness, is called right mindfulness (sammāsati). It has the characteristic of establishing, the function of not forgetting, and it is manifested as the abandonment of wrong mindfulness.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XVI, para. 562ff) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=141&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2V2YeG5gyB2xIF5YW1hdG8gdGHhuYNzYW1wYXl1dHRvdmEgbWljY2jEgXNhdGl2aW5pZGRodW5hbm8gY2V0YXNvIGFzYW1tb3Nv)
