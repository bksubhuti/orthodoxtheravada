### Grammatical Analysis
**Iddhividha**: kinds of psychic power. *Iddhi* (success, accomplishment, from *ijjhati*, to succeed) + *vidha* (kind, category). The Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā glosses *iddhividhāya* as "*iddhikoṭṭhāsāya, iddhivikappāya vā*" (for a category of success, or for a variety of success). In the Sāmaññaphala formula the mind is directed *iddhividhāya* (to the kinds of psychic power), and the monk then "*anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhoti*" (experiences the manifold kinds of psychic power).

### Orthodox Definition
*Iddhividha* is the first of the six higher knowledges (*[abhiññā](/glossary/abhinna/)*) in the Vibhaṅga list (*iddhividhe ñāṇaṃ*, then the divine ear, knowledge of others' minds, past lives, the passing of beings, and the destruction of the taints). In the Paṭisambhidāmagga it is "knowledge of the kinds of psychic power": understanding of the meaning of succeeding (*ijjhanaṭṭhe paññā*) by defining body and mind as one and steadying the perceptions of ease and lightness by determination (*adhiṭṭhāna*).

The standard canonical formula (DN 2) lists the feats: having been one, he becomes many, and having been many, one; he appears and vanishes; he goes unimpeded through walls, ramparts and mountains as if through space; he dives in and out of the earth as if it were water; he walks on water without sinking as if on earth; he travels cross-legged through the sky like a winged bird; he touches and strokes the sun and moon with his hand; he exercises mastery over the body as far as the Brahma-world.

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XII) explains *iddhi* by *ijjhanaṭṭhena* ("in the sense of succeeding"), that is, in the sense of production and obtainment. On "*udakepi abhijjamāne gacchati*" it says that one who wishes to go in this way enters the earth kasiṇa attainment, emerges, determines "in this much place let the water be earth," does the preliminary practice and resolves; the water in the place determined then becomes earth only (Vism-ii 110:3-4). It also says that only success by determination (*adhiṭṭhānā iddhi*) is meant in the word *iddhividhāya*, though transformation and mind-made body are also to be understood here (Vism-ii 55:1-2).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, Sāmaññaphala Sutta)
> **“So evaṃ samāhite citte parisuddhe pariyodāte anaṅgaṇe vigatūpakkilese mudubhūte kammaniye ṭhite āneñjappatte iddhividhāya cittaṃ abhinīharati abhininnāmeti. So anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhoti – ekopi hutvā bahudhā hoti, bahudhāpi hutvā eko hoti; āvibhāvaṃ tirobhāvaṃ tirokuṭṭaṃ tiropākāraṃ tiropabbataṃ asajjamāno gacchati seyyathāpi ākāse. Pathaviyāpi ummujjanimujjaṃ karoti seyyathāpi udake. Udakepi abhijjamāne gacchati seyyathāpi pathaviyā. Ākāsepi pallaṅkena kamati seyyathāpi pakkhī sakuṇo. Imepi candimasūriye evaṃmahiddhike evaṃmahānubhāve pāṇinā parāmasati parimajjati. Yāva brahmalokāpi kāyena vasaṃ vatteti.**  
> 238. When his mind is thus concentrated, pure and bright, unblemished, free from defects, malleable, wieldy, steady, and attained to imperturbability, he directs and inclines it to the modes of supernormal power. He exercises the various modes of supernormal power: having been one, he becomes many and having been many, he becomes one; he appears and vanishes; he goes unimpeded through walls, ramparts, and mountains as if through space; he dives in and out of the earth as if it were water; he walks on water without sinking as if it were earth; sitting cross-legged he travels through space like a winged bird; with his hand he touches and strokes the sun and the moon, so mighty and powerful; he exercises mastery over the body as far as the Brahma-world.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Kāyampi cittampi ekavavatthānatā sukhasaññañca lahusaññañca adhiṭṭhānavasena ijjhanaṭṭhe paññā iddhividhe ñāṇaṃ.**  
> 50. Understanding of the meaning of succeeding by defining body and cognizance as one and by steadying easy perception and quick perception by the power of the determination is knowledge of the kinds of success.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Iddhividhāya cittaṃ abhinīharati abhininnāmetīti ettha ijjhanaṭṭhena iddhi , nipphattiatthena paṭilābhaṭṭhena cāti vuttaṃ hoti. Yañhi nipphajjati paṭilabbhati ca, taṃ ijjhatīti vuccati.**  
> 369. Here, in the phrase, “He directs, he inclines, his mind to the kinds of supernormal powers (iddhividhāya cittaṃ abhinīharati abhininnāmeti),” success (iddhi) is so called in the sense of succeeding (ijjhanaṭṭhena); that is, in the sense of production and in the sense of obtainment. For what is produced and obtained is said to succeed.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. XII, Iddhividhaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=6&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=MzY5LiBJZGRoaXZpZGjEgXlhIGNpdHRh4bmDIGFiaGluxKtoYXJhdGkgYWJoaW5pbm7EgW1ldMSrdGkgZXR0aGE%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Kevaṭṭa Sutta* (DN 11, D-i 915) – the Buddha says he does not instruct monks to perform *iddhipāṭihāriya* for lay people (D-i 917:9) and, seeing the danger in it, he is "*aṭṭīyāmi harāyāmi jigucchāmi*" about the miracle of psychic power (D-i 926:3); the formula "*anekavihitaṃ iddhividhaṃ paccanubhoti*" occurs at D-i 922:2, and the instruction miracle (*anusāsanīpāṭihāriya*) is the third of the three miracles (D-i 921:3).
* **Canonical**: *Sāmaññaphala Sutta* (DN 2, D-i 346:1-8) – the standard list of the psychic powers; *Iddhividhañāṇaniddesa* (Paṭis 586-587) in the Paṭisambhidāmagga; *Ñāṇavibhaṅga* (Vibh 2447:2), the six *abhiññā*.
* **Commentary**: *Visuddhimagga* (Ch. XII, *Iddhividhaniddesa*, Vism-ii 4-149), Vism-ii 32:1-2 on *ijjhanaṭṭhena iddhi*, 110:1-4 on walking on water; *Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā* (Vism-mht-ii 52:1) on the word *iddhividhāya*.
