### Grammatical Analysis
**Pañcappakaraṇaṭṭhakathā**: [f.] The Commentary on the Five Treatises. Formed by *pañca* (five) + *pakaraṇa* (treatise, book) + *[aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/atthakatha/)* (commentary). The colophon spells it “pañcapakaraṇaṭṭhakathā” (Paṭṭh-a 2604:1) and the opening heading “Pañcapakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā” (Dhatuk-a 3:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Pañcappakaraṇaṭṭhakathā* is the commentary on the last five books of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka (*[Dhātukathā](/glossary/dhatukatha/)*, *[Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/)*, *[Kathāvatthu](/glossary/kathavatthu/)*, *[Yamaka](/glossary/yamaka/)* and *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)*). In TPR it is divided into one book for each: Dhatuk-a, Pp-a, Kv-a, Yam-a and Paṭṭh-a. It begins under the heading “Pañcapakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā” with the Dhātukathā-aṭṭhakathā (Dhatuk-a 3:1-4:1), which recalls that the Great Hero taught the Dhātukathā after the Vibhaṅga (Dhatuk-a 5:1-8:1). At the end of the Paṭṭhāna commentary the colophon says “Niṭṭhitā ca pañcapakaraṇaṭṭhakathā” (Paṭṭh-a 2604:1), and the closing section (*nigamanakathā*) names the author as the Elder Buddhaghosa of the Mahāvihāra, who made this commentary on the whole Abhidhammapiṭaka (Paṭṭh-a 2606:1; the last line is “Abhidhammapiṭaka-aṭṭhakathā niṭṭhitā”, Paṭṭh-a 2611:1).

The commentary explains the structure of each book. For the Dhātukathā it explains the five kinds of *mātikā* (*nayamātikā*, *abbhantaramātikā*, *nayamukhamātikā*, *lakkhaṇamātikā*, *bāhiramātikā*, Dhatuk-a 11:4-6). In the Kathāvatthu commentary the opposing positions of the debate are assigned to named schools: the first question is put by the one who holds *puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthena* (the *puggalavādī*, Kv-a 558:1-2), and a position on the connection of the mind to a single object is given as that of the Mahāsaṅghikas (Kv-a 1314:3). The last book commented on is the Paṭṭhāna, with its 24 conditions.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Dhātukathā-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Aṭṭhārasahi bhedehi, vibhaṅgaṃ mārabhañjano; Desayitvā mahāvīro, yaṃ tasseva anantaraṃ. Adesayi dhātukathaṃ, dhātubhedappakāsano; Tassatthaṃ dīpayissāmi, taṃ suṇātha samāhitāti.**  
> The Destroyer of Māra, with eighteen divisions, (taught) the Vibhaṅga; the great hero having taught that immediately after. The Illuminator of the Element-Distinctions taught the Dhātukathā; I will illustrate its meaning; listen to it with concentrated minds.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Paramavisuddhasaddhābuddhivīriyapaṭimaṇḍitena sīlācārajjavamaddavādiguṇasamudayasamuditena sakasamayasamayantaragahanajjhogāhaṇasamatthena paññāveyyattiyasamannāgatena tipiṭakapariyattippabhede sāṭṭhakathe satthusāsane appaṭihatañāṇappabhāvena mahāveyyākaraṇena karaṇasampattijanitasukhaviniggatamadhurodāravacanalāvaṇṇayuttena yuttamuttavādinā vādīvarena mahākavinā pabhinnapaṭisambhidāparivāre chaḷabhiññādippabhedaguṇapaṭimaṇḍite uttarimanussadhamme suppatiṭṭhitabuddhīnaṃ theravaṃsappadīpānaṃ therānaṃ mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena vipulavisuddhabuddhinā buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena katā ayaṃ sakalassapi abhidhammapiṭakassa aṭṭhakathā –**  
> This commentary on the entire Abhidhamma Piṭaka was composed by the Elder, known by the name Buddhaghosa, which was given to him by his teachers. He was adorned with supreme pure faith, wisdom, and energy; endowed with a collection of virtues such as morality, good conduct, uprightness, and gentleness; capable of thoroughly penetrating his own doctrine and other doctrines; possessed of profound wisdom and eloquence; his intellect's radiance was unimpeded with regard to the Teacher’s message, which comprises the Tipitaka classified into texts and accompanied by commentaries; a great expounder; possessing charm of sweet and sublime words that sprang forth happily from perfect execution; a speaker of fitting and excellent words, a supreme debater, and a great poet. His wisdom was well-established in the superior human qualities, adorned with qualities such as the six super-knowledges and surrounded by the analytical knowledges he had penetrated. He was a lamp to the lineage of elders and an ornament to the lineage of the elders residing in the Great Monastery, possessing vast and pure wisdom.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthenāti hi ayaṃ sakavādīpucchā. Tāya “ye atthi puggaloti evaṃladdhikā puggalavādino, te evaṃ pucchitabbā” ti dīpeti.**  
> For “Is the person known in the sense of a real and ultimate fact?” is the Theravādin’s question. With that, he indicates: “Those who are proponents of the person (Puggalavādins), having the doctrine ‘there is a person,’ should be questioned in this way.”  
>
