### Grammatical Analysis
**Dukkha**: [nt.; adj.] suffering; pain; unsatisfactoriness. The Visuddhimagga (Ch. XVI) derives it as *du* + *kha*: the word *du* is used in the sense of "contemptible" (*kucchita*, as in *dupputta*, a bad son), and *kha* in the sense of "empty" (*tuccha*, as space is called *kha*). "Therefore it is called *dukkha* because of being contemptible and because of being empty" (*kucchitattā tucchattā ca dukkhanti vuccati*).

### Orthodox Definition
*Dukkha* is the First Noble Truth (*dukkhaṃ ariyasaccaṃ*). The Mūla defines it as birth, aging, illness, death, union with the disliked, separation from the liked, not getting what one wants; "in brief, the five aggregates subject to clinging are suffering" (*saṃkhittena pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā*). The Vibhaṅga adds grief, lamentation, pain, sorrow and despair to the list.

The Saṅgīti Sutta (DN 33) gives three kinds of suffering (*tisso dukkhatā*). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī explains them:
1. *Dukkhadukkhatā*: suffering by way of being suffering; a name for painful feeling.
2. *Vipariṇāmadukkhatā*: suffering in change; a name for pleasant feeling, because suffering arises when happiness changes.
3. *Saṅkhāradukkhatā*: suffering by way of being formations; a name for neutral feeling, which is oppressed by arising, aging and dissolution because it is conditioned. Apart from painful and pleasant feeling, all phenomena of the three planes are to be understood as *saṅkhāradukkhatā*, on the authority of "all formations are suffering".

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya, Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta)
> **“Idaṃ kho pana, bhikkhave, dukkhaṃ ariyasaccaṃ – jātipi dukkhā, jarāpi dukkhā, byādhipi dukkho, maraṇampi dukkhaṃ, appiyehi sampayogo dukkho, piyehi vippayogo dukkho, yampicchaṃ na labhati tampi dukkhaṃ – saṃkhittena pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā.**  
> “Now this, monks, is the noble truth of suffering: birth is suffering, aging is suffering, illness is suffering, death is suffering; union with what is displeasing is suffering; separation from what is pleasing is suffering; not to obtain that which one longs for is suffering; in brief, the five aggregates subject to clinging are suffering.  
>

> **“Tisso dukkhatā – dukkhadukkhatā, saṅkhāradukkhatā, vipariṇāmadukkhatā.**  
> “Three sufferings: suffering as pain, suffering as formations, suffering as change.

### Quote (Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamaṃ dukkhaṃ ariyasaccaṃ? Jātipi dukkhā, jarāpi dukkhā, maraṇampi dukkhaṃ, sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsāpi dukkhā, appiyehi sampayogo dukkho, piyehi vippayogo dukkho, yaṃ picchaṃ na labhati tampi dukkhaṃ, saṃkhittena pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā.**  
> 190. Which therein (tattha) is the Noble Truth of Suffering (dukkhaṃ ariyasaccaṃ)? Birth is suffering (jātipi dukkhā), old age is suffering (jarāpi dukkhā), death is suffering (maraṇampi dukkhaṃ), grief, lamentation, pain, sorrow, and despair are suffering (sokaparidevadukkhadomanassupāyāsāpi dukkhā), being joined to what is not liked is suffering (appiyehi sampayogo dukkho), being parted from what is liked is suffering (piyehi vippayogo dukkho), not to obtain that which one longs for is suffering (yaṃ picchaṃ na labhati tampi dukkhaṃ), in short, the five constituents (of mind and body) that provide fuel for attachment are suffering (saṃkhittena pañcupādānakkhandhā dukkhā).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Saccavibhaṅga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=104&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTkwLiBUYXR0aGEga2F0YW1h4bmDIGR1a2toYeG5gyBhcml5YXNhY2Nh4bmDPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Nibbacanalakkhaṇādippabhedatoti ettha pana nibbacanato tāva idha du -iti ayaṃ saddo kucchite dissati. Kucchitaṃ hi puttaṃ dupputtoti vadanti. Khaṃ -saddo pana tucche. Tucchaṃ hi ākāsaṃ “khan” ti vuccati. Idañca paṭhamasaccaṃ kucchitaṃ anekaupaddavādhiṭṭhānato. Tucchaṃ bālajanaparikappitadhuvasubhasukhattabhāvavirahitato.**  
> 530. Herein, as to ‘by way of derivation, characteristics, and other divisions’: first, by way of derivation (nibbacanato), here the word du is seen in the sense of ‘contemptible’. For they call a contemptible son a ‘bad son’ (dupputta). But the word kha is in the sense of ‘empty’. For the empty space is called ‘kha’. And this first truth is contemptible because it is the base for many afflictions. It is empty because it is devoid of the state of permanence, beauty, happiness, and self as imagined by foolish people.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. XVI, Indriyasaccaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=125&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NTMwLiBOaWJiYWNhbmFsYWtraGHhuYfEgWRpcHBhYmhlZGF0b3RpIGV0dGhhIHBhbmEgbmliYmFjYW5hdG8gdMSBdmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Dukkhadukkhatāti dukkhabhūtā dukkhatā. Dukkhavedanāyetaṃ nāmaṃ. Saṅkhāradukkhatāti saṅkhārabhāvena dukkhatā. Adukkhamasukhāvedanāyetaṃ nāmaṃ. Sā hi saṅkhatattā uppādajarābhaṅgapīḷitā, tasmā aññadukkhasabhāvavirahato saṅkhāradukkhatāti vuttā. Vipariṇāmadukkhatāti vipariṇāme dukkhatā. Sukhavedanāyetaṃ nāmaṃ. Sukhassa hi vipariṇāme dukkhaṃ uppajjati, tasmā sukhaṃ vipariṇāmadukkhatāti vuttaṃ. Apica ṭhapetvā dukkhavedanaṃ sukhavedanañca sabbepi tebhūmakā dhammā “sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā” ti vacanato saṅkhāradukkhatāti veditabbā.**  
> Suffering as pain (Dukkhadukkhatā) means suffering by way of being suffering. This is a name for painful feeling. Suffering as formations (Saṅkhāradukkhatā) means suffering by way of being formations. This is a name for neutral feeling. Because it is conditioned, it is oppressed by arising, aging, and dissolution; therefore, due to the absence of any other suffering state, it is called suffering as formations. Suffering as change (Vipariṇāmadukkhatā) means suffering in transformation. This is a name for pleasant feeling. For when happiness transforms, suffering arises; therefore, happiness is called suffering as change. Furthermore, setting aside painful and pleasant feelings, all phenomena belonging to the three spheres should be understood as suffering as formations due to the statement ‘all volitional formations are suffering’.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, DN 33) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_03&page_number=174&name=p%C4%81thikavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RHVra2hhZHVra2hhdMSBdGkgZHVra2hhYmjFq3TEgSBkdWtraGF0xIEu)
