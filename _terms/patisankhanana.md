### Grammatical Analysis
**Paṭisaṅkhāñāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of reflective observation; knowledge of re-observation. Formed by *paṭisaṅkhā* (reflection, careful consideration, re-evaluation) + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). The Paṭisambhidāmagga uses the words *paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ* (Paṭis 1246:1-6); the Visuddhimagga calls the stage *paṭisaṅkhāñāṇaṃ* (Vism-ii 1762:1, also *paṭisaṅkhānupassanāñāṇa*, Vism-ii 1761:3).

### Orthodox Definition
Having formed the intense desire for deliverance (*[muñcitukāmyatāñāṇa](/glossary/muncitukamyatanana/)*), the meditator realizes that escape cannot be achieved through sheer physical effort or wishful thinking. To find the exit, the mind must double down on the very method that brought it this far. Thus, *Paṭisaṅkhāñāṇa* arises.

In this stage, the practitioner returns to contemplating the formations, applying the Three Characteristics (impermanence, suffering, non-self) in order to find the means of release. The Visuddhimagga puts it as "the working on a means to release it" (*muñcanassa upāyakaraṇaṃ*), that is, "applying the three characteristics to formations through the knowledge of contemplation of reflection" (Vism-ii 1761:3). In the Paṭisambhidāmagga the same text then says: attending as inconstant, knowledge arises after reflecting on the sign (*nimittaṃ*); as suffering, after reflecting on the occurrence (*pavattaṃ*); as not-self, after reflecting on both (Paṭis 1246:4-6). In the Paṭisambhidāmagga's list of knowledges, desire for deliverance, reflection and composure are stated together as one knowledge, "knowledge of equanimity about formations" (Paṭis 14:1; Paṭis-a 126:1-8).

### Quote
> **Aniccato manasikaroto kiṃ paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati? Dukkhato manasikaroto kiṃ paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati? Anattato manasikaroto kiṃ paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati? Aniccato manasikaroto nimittaṃ paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. Dukkhato manasikaroto pavattaṃ paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati. Anattato manasikaroto nimittañca pavattañca paṭisaṅkhā ñāṇaṃ uppajjati.**  
> For one attending as inconstant, after reflecting on what does knowledge arise? For one attending as suffering, after reflecting on what does knowledge arise? For one attending as not-self, after reflecting on what does knowledge arise? For one attending as inconstant, knowledge arises after reflecting on the sign. For one attending as suffering, knowledge arises after reflecting on the occurrence. For one attending as not-self, knowledge arises after reflecting on the sign and the occurrence.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (Vimokkhakathā, Paṭis 1246) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=259&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=QW5pY2NhdG8gbWFuYXNpa2Fyb3RvIGtp4bmDIHBh4bmtaXNh4bmFa2jEgSDDscSB4bmHYeG5gyB1cHBhamphdGk%2F)
