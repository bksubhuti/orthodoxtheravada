### Grammatical Analysis
**Tilakkhaṇa**: [nt.] The Three Characteristics; the Three Marks. Formed by *ti* (three) + *lakkhaṇa* (mark, characteristic; the Aṭṭhakathā glosses *aniccalakkhaṇa*, *dukkhalakkhaṇa*, *anattalakkhaṇa* as "the characteristic of impermanence, of suffering, of non-self", Cūḷaniddesa-aṭṭhakathā, Nidd-a-ii 538:1). The compound *tilakkhaṇa* is not found in the Mūla (0 hits); the Mūla gives the three marks as separate statements, "sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā, sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā, sabbe dhammā anattā" (Dhp 277-279; AN 3.137, A-iii 1194).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Tilakkhaṇa* are the three characteristics of conditioned things, taught in the Mūla as: "All formations (*saṅkhārā*) are impermanent", "All formations are suffering", "All dhammas are not-self" (Dhp 277-279). Seeing them "with wisdom" (*paññāya*) leads to disenchantment with suffering, "this is the path to purity" (*esa maggo visuddhiyā*). The Buddha says the same three whether or not a Tathāgata arises: "that element stands, this steadfastness of the Dhamma", which a Tathāgata awakens to and declares (AN 3.137, A-iii 1194). The Paṭisambhidāmagga expands the contemplation of the five aggregates into forty aspects (Paṭis 37), which the Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā counts, for each aggregate, as ten of impermanence, twenty-five of suffering and five of not-self (Paṭis-a 2479:5-7).

The Aṭṭhakathā define each characteristic (Nidd-a-ii 538):
1. **[Anicca](/glossary/anicca/)-lakkhaṇa** (the characteristic of impermanence): the five aggregates are impermanent "because of arising, vanishing and alteration (*uppādavayaññathatta*), or because of being non-existent after having been (*hutvā abhāvato*)"; alteration is old age. That arising, vanishing and alteration is the characteristic of impermanence.
2. **[Dukkha](/glossary/dukkha/)-lakkhaṇa** (the characteristic of suffering): "what is impermanent is suffering"; the same five aggregates are suffering "because of continuous oppression" (*abhiṇhaṃ paṭipīḷanato*), and that manner of continuous oppression is the characteristic of suffering.
3. **[Anattā](/glossary/anatta/)-lakkhaṇa** (the characteristic of non-self): "what is suffering is non-self"; the aggregates are non-self "because of not being subject to control" (*avasavattanato*). In the Mūla, "sabbe dhammā anattā" speaks of all dhammas, not only the conditioned (Dhp 279).

The three characteristics become objects of insight (*[vipassanā](/glossary/vipassana/)*) for one who sees arising and vanishing (Nidd-a-ii 538:15). The Visuddhimagga explains why they do not appear to the untrained mind: impermanence is concealed by continuity (*[santati](/glossary/santati/)*) because rise and fall are not attended to; suffering is concealed by the postures (*iriyāpatha*) because continuous oppression is not attended to; non-self is concealed by compactness (*ghana*) because the resolution into the various elements is not attended to. When continuity is broken by grasping rise and fall, when the postures are exposed by attending to oppression, and when compactness is resolved into the elements, each characteristic "appears truly as it is" (Vism XXI §739).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammapada)
> **“Sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā” ti, yadā paññāya passati; Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā. 278. “Sabbe saṅkhārā dukkhā” ti, yadā paññāya passati; Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā. 279. “Sabbe dhammā anattā” ti, yadā paññāya passati; Atha nibbindati dukkhe, esa maggo visuddhiyā.**  
> “All volitional formations are impermanent,” when one sees with wisdom, Then one wearies of suffering; this is the path to purity. “All volitional formations are suffering,” when one sees with wisdom, Then one wearies of suffering; this is the path to purity. “All things are without self,” when one sees with wisdom, Then one wearies of suffering; this is the path to purity.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Cūḷaniddesa-aṭṭhakathā, the three marks defined)
> **Ettha ca aniccaṃ aniccalakkhaṇaṃ dukkhaṃ dukkhalakkhaṇaṃ anattā anattalakkhaṇanti ayaṃ vibhāgo veditabbo. Tattha aniccanti khandhapañcakaṃ. Kasmā? Uppādavayaññathattabhāvā, hutvā abhāvato vā. Aññathattaṃ nāma jarā. Uppādavayaññathattaṃ aniccalakkhaṇaṃ , hutvā abhāvasaṅkhāto vā ākāravikāro. “Yadaniccaṃ, taṃ dukkhan” ti vacanato tadeva khandhapañcakaṃ dukkhaṃ. Kasmā? Abhiṇhaṃ paṭipīḷanato. Abhiṇhaṃ paṭipīḷanākāro dukkhalakkhaṇaṃ. “Yaṃ dukkhaṃ, tadanattā” ti (saṃ. ni.3.15-16) vacanato tadeva khandhapañcakaṃ anattā. Kasmā? Avasavattanato. Avasavattanākāro anattalakkhaṇaṃ. Imāni tīṇipi lakkhaṇāni udayabbayaṃ passantasseva ārammaṇāni honti.**  
> Here, this distinction should be understood: impermanent, characteristic of impermanence; suffering, characteristic of suffering; non-self, characteristic of non-self. In this context, impermanent refers to the five aggregates. Why? Because of arising, vanishing, and alteration, or because of being non-existent after having been. Alteration is old age. Arising, vanishing, and alteration are the characteristic of impermanence, or the change in form defined as non-existence after having been. According to the statement, 'What is impermanent is suffering,' those same five aggregates are suffering. Why? Because of continuous oppression. The manner of continuous oppression is the characteristic of suffering. According to the statement, 'What is suffering is non-self,' those same five aggregates are non-self. Why? Because of not being subject to control. The manner of not being subject to control is the characteristic of non-self. These three characteristics become the objects for one who sees the arising and vanishing.  
>  
> — *Cūḷaniddesa-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_17&page_number=98&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7aniddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=RXR0aGEgY2EgYW5pY2Nh4bmDIGFuaWNjYWxha2toYeG5h2HhuYMgZHVra2hh4bmDIGR1a2toYWxha2toYeG5h2HhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the three marks concealed)
> **Lakkhaṇāni pana kissa amanasikārā kena paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhahanti? Aniccalakkhaṇaṃ tāva udayabbayānaṃ amanasikārā santatiyā paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhāti. Dukkhalakkhaṇaṃ abhiṇhasampaṭipīḷanassa amanasikārā iriyāpathehi paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhāti. Anattalakkhaṇaṃ nānādhātuvinibbhogassa amanasikārā ghanena paṭicchannattā na upaṭṭhāti. Udayabbayampana pariggahetvā santatiyā vikopitāya aniccalakkhaṇaṃ yāthāvasarasato upaṭṭhāti. Abhiṇhasampaṭipīḷanaṃ manasikatvā iriyāpathe ugghāṭite dukkhalakkhaṇaṃ yāthāvasarasato upaṭṭhāti. Nānādhātuyo vinibbhujitvā ghanavinibbhoge kate anattalakkhaṇaṃ yāthāvasarasato upaṭṭhāti.**  
> 739. But by not attending to what do the characteristics not appear, and by what are they concealed? Firstly, the characteristic of impermanence does not appear because, by not attending to rise and fall, it is concealed by continuity. The characteristic of suffering does not appear because, by not attending to continuous oppression, it is concealed by the postures. The characteristic of not-self does not appear because, by not attending to the resolution into the various elements, it is concealed by compactness. But when continuity is disrupted by grasping rise and fall, the characteristic of impermanence appears truly as it is. When the postures are exposed by attending to continuous oppression, the characteristic of suffering appears truly as it is. When compactness is resolved by separating the various elements, the characteristic of not-self appears truly as it is.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, §739) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=276&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzM5LiBMYWtraGHhuYfEgW5pIHBhbmEga2lzc2EgYW1hbmFzaWvEgXLEgSBrZW5h)
