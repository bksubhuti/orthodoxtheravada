### Grammatical Analysis
**Aniccatā**: [f.] Impermanence; the state of being impermanent; dissolution. Formed by negative prefix *a* (not) + *nicca* (permanent, constant) + *tā* (suffix, -ness).

### Orthodox Definition
In orthodox Theravāda, a clear distinction is made between **[Anicca](/glossary/anicca/)** (the impermanent thing) and **Aniccatā** (impermanence itself):

1. **[Anicca](/glossary/anicca/)**: Refers to the five aggregates (*pañcakkhandhā*), which are called impermanent because of their nature of arising, vanishing, and alteration (*uppādavayaññathattabhāvā*).
2. **Aniccatā**: Is the *characteristic* of impermanence itself—defined as the arising, vanishing, and alteration of those aggregates; their non-existence after having come into being (*hutvā abhāvo*); or their destruction by momentary dissolution (*khaṇabhaṅgena bhedo*).

As a material phenomenon (*lakkhaṇarūpa*) in the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) sequence ("Rūpassa upacayo santati jaratā aniccatā lakkhaṇarūpaṃ nāma", Abhidh-s 523:1), *aniccatā* represents the terminal boundary of physical matter. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it: "Yo rūpassa khayo vayo bhedo paribhedo aniccatā antaradhānaṃ – idaṃ taṃ rūpaṃ rūpassa aniccatā" (Dhs 1374:2, §644). The Atthasālinī analyses the words (*hutvā abhāvaṭṭhena, na niccanti aniccaṃ; tassa bhāvo aniccatā*, As 2126:4-5) and gives the characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause: it has the characteristic of breaking up (*paribhedalakkhaṇā*), the function of sinking down (*saṃsīdanarasā*), the manifestation of decay and vanishing (*khayavayapaccupaṭṭhānā*), and form that is breaking up as its proximate cause (*paribhijjamānarūpapadaṭṭhānā*) (As 2126:10). It adds that this applies to all five aggregates, not only form (As 2126:8-9). If *[upacaya](/glossary/upacaya/)* is the birth of matter, and *[jaratā](/glossary/jarata/)* is its aging, *aniccatā* is its microscopic death and dissolution. The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. VIII, in the fourth tetrad of mindfulness of breathing) distinguishes the impermanent (*anicca*), which is the five aggregates, from impermanence (*aniccatā*), their arising, vanishing and alteration (Vism-i 1706:5-1708:4). Repeatedly witnessing this constant dissolution (*aniccānupassanā*) triggers the higher stages of insight, causing the mind to recoil from the instability of all formed things.

### Quote

> 644. Which form is the impermanence of form? That which is the end, vanishing, breaking up, complete breaking up, impermanence, or disappearance of form – this is that impermanence of form.  
>

### Quote
> **Hutvā abhāvaṭṭhena, na niccanti aniccaṃ. Tassa bhāvo aniccatā. Antaradhāyati etthāti antaradhānaṃ. Maraṇañhi patvā rūpaṃ antaradhāyati, adassanaṃ gacchati. Na kevalañca rūpameva, sabbepi pañcakkhandhā. Tasmā pañcannampi khandhānaṃ aniccatāya idameva lakkhaṇanti veditabbaṃ. Lakkhaṇādito pana paribhedalakkhaṇā rūpassa aniccatā , saṃsīdanarasā, khayavayapaccupaṭṭhānā, paribhijjamānarūpapadaṭṭhānāti veditabbā.**  
> By reason of not existing after having been, that which is not permanent is impermanent. The state of that is impermanence (aniccatā). It disappears in this, therefore it is disappearance (antaradhānaṃ). Indeed, having reached death, form disappears and goes out of sight. And not only form, but all five aggregates (disappear). Therefore, this very characteristic should be understood as the impermanence of even the five aggregates. Furthermore, as to characteristic, etc., the impermanence of form (rūpassa aniccatā) has the characteristic of complete breaking up; its function is subsiding; its manifestation is ending and vanishing; and its proximate cause is forms that are completely breaking up; this should be understood.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (Aniccatāniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=365&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SHV0dsSBIGFiaMSBdmHhua3hua1oZW5hLCBuYSBuaWNjYW50aSBhbmljY2HhuYMu)

### Quote
> **Evaṃ cittānupassanāvasena idaṃ catukkaṃ bhāsitanti veditabbaṃ. Aniccatā veditabbā. Tattha aniccanti pañcakkhandhā. Aniccatāti tesaṃyeva uppādavayaññathattaṃ, hutvā abhāvo vā, nibbattānaṃ tenevākārena aṭṭhatvā khaṇabhaṅgena bhedoti attho.**  
> Thus, this four-group should be understood to have been spoken by way of the contemplation of mind (cittānupassanāvasa). Impermanence (aniccatā) should be understood. Herein, the impermanent (aniccaṃ) refers to the five aggregates. Impermanence (aniccatā) is the arising, fall, and change of those very aggregates; or, it is their non-existence after having arisen (hutvā abhāvo); meaning, the breakup of arisen things through momentary dissolution due to their not remaining in that same mode.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Ch. VIII, the fourth tetrad) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=281&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RXZh4bmDIGNpdHTEgW51cGFzc2FuxIF2YXNlbmEgaWRh4bmDIGNhdHVra2HhuYMgYmjEgXNpdGFudGkgdmVkaXRhYmJh4bmDLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Apadāna* (Ap-iii 8728:1-8729:1) – "eseva dhammo yadidaṃ aniccatā"; and Ap-iii 7772:1 – "palujjeyya aniccatā".
* **Abhidhamma**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Dhs 1374:1-2, §644, the definition; Dhs 1373:1, §643, jaratā) and *[Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (Abhidh-s 523:1, the four lakkhaṇarūpa).
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 2126:1-10) – the analysis and characteristic of aniccatā; *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-i 1706:3-1708:5, in the explanation of the fourth tetrad of mindfulness of breathing) – the impermanent thing (*anicca*) and impermanence (*aniccatā*).
