### Grammatical Analysis
**Sīlabbataparāmāsa**: [m.] Clinging to rites and rituals; attachment to rules and vows. Formed by *sīla* (moral habit, rule) + *vata/bbata* (vow, ascetic practice, rite) + *parāmāsa* (clinging, grasping, "touching on"). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī gives the analysis: *sīlañca vatañca parāmasatīti sīlabbataparāmāso*, "he clings to virtue and practice, thus it is *sīlabbataparāmāsa*" (Sv-iii 692), and the Samantapāsādikā: "the mere act of holding that has been grasped, having clung to both virtue and vow (*sīlañca vatañca parāmasitvā gahitagahaṇamattaṃ*)" (Sp-iii 603).

### Orthodox Definition
*Sīlabbataparāmāsa* is the third of the five lower fetters (*orambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni*: *sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso, kāmacchando, byāpādo*, A-x 71) and one of the three fetters abandoned at stream-entry. The Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists it among the states "to be abandoned by vision" (*dassanena pahātabbā*): "the three fetters: identity view, doubt and adherence to rites and rituals" (Dhs 2056).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it as a view: "outside this teaching, among ascetics and brahmins, such a view as 'purification by virtue, purification by observance, purification by virtue and observance'" (*ito bahiddhā samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ 'sīlena suddhi, vatena suddhi, sīlabbatena suddhī' ti yā evarūpā diṭṭhi*), with the string of synonyms *diṭṭhigata, diṭṭhigahana, diṭṭhikantāra, … gāha, paṭiggāha, abhinivesa, parāmāsa, kummagga, micchāpatha, micchatta, titthāyatana, vipariyāsaggāha* (Dhs 1777, §1009).

The Atthasālinī explains: "the grasping and undertaking that 'it is possible to be purified by virtue, it is possible to be purified by observance, it is possible to be purified by virtue and observance' is called *sīlabbataparāmāsa*" (As 2204). It adds that this adherence does not insist "this alone is true" (*idameva saccaṃ*), but only insists "purification by virtue" and the like (As 2309).

In the Mūla the Kukkuravatika Sutta (MN 57) shows the practice: the ox-duty (*govata*), ox-habit (*gosīla*), ox-mind and ox-behaviour, and the dog-duty ascetic Seniya, and the Buddha says that if the practitioner holds the view "by this virtue or observance or asceticism or holy life I shall become a god or one of the gods", that is wrong view (M-ii 238).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, the five lower fetters)
> **Pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni? Sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso, kāmacchando, byāpādo – imāni pañcorambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni.**  
> The five lower fetters and the five higher fetters. What are the five lower fetters? Embodiment view, doubt, wrong grasp of behavior and observances, sensual desire, and ill will. These are the five lower fetters.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 10.13, Saṃyojana Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_10&page_number=269&name=dasakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=UGHDsWNvcmFtYmjEgWdpecSBbmkgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBbmksIHBhw7FjdWRkaGFtYmjEgWdpecSBbmkgc2HhuYN5b2phbsSBbmku)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, the definition)
> **Tattha katamo sīlabbataparāmāso? Ito bahiddhā samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṃ ‘ sīlena suddhi, vatena suddhi, sīlabbatena suddhī ’ ti yā evarūpā diṭṭhi diṭṭhigataṃ diṭṭhigahanaṃ diṭṭhikantāro diṭṭhivisūkāyikaṃ diṭṭhivipphanditaṃ diṭṭhisaṃyojanaṃ gāho paṭiggāho abhiniveso parāmāso kummaggo micchāpatho micchattaṃ titthāyatanaṃ vipariyāsaggāho – ayaṃ vuccati sīlabbataparāmāso.**  
> 1009. What is adherence to rites and rituals (sīlabbataparāmāso) there? Whatever such view, view-point, thicket of views, wilderness of views, perplexity of views, agitation of views, fetter of views, grasping, holding fast, misinterpretation, misinterpretation, wrong path, wrong way, wrongness, sectarian fold, grasping of perversion — this is called adherence to rites and rituals (sīlabbataparāmāso) among ascetics and brahmins outside this teaching who hold the view: ‘Purification comes from virtuous behavior, purification comes from observances, purification comes from virtuous behavior and observances.’  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1009) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=208&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTAwOS4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyBzxKtsYWJiYXRhcGFyxIFtxIFzbz8%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī, abandoned by vision)
> **Katame dhammā dassanena pahātabbā? Tīṇi saṃyojanāni – sakkāyadiṭṭhi, vicikicchā, sīlabbataparāmāso.**  
> 1261. What mental phenomena are eradicated by the First Path? The three fetters: identity view, uncertainty, and adherence to rites and rituals.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1261) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=249&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTI2MS4gS2F0YW1lIGRoYW1txIEgZGFzc2FuZW5hIHBhaMSBdGFiYsSBPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **Idha, seniya, ekacco govataṃ bhāveti paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gosīlaṃ bhāveti paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gocittaṃ bhāveti paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gavākappaṃ bhāveti paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ. So govataṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gosīlaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gocittaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ, gavākappaṃ bhāvetvā paripuṇṇaṃ abbokiṇṇaṃ kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā gunnaṃ sahabyataṃ upapajjati. Sace kho panassa evaṃdiṭṭhi hoti – ‘ imināhaṃ sīlena vā vatena vā tapena vā brahmacariyena vā devo vā bhavissāmi devaññataro vā ’ ti, sāssa hoti micchādiṭṭhi.**  
> Here, Seniya, someone develops the ox-duty fully and uninterruptedly; he develops the ox-habit fully and uninterruptedly; he develops the ox-mind fully and uninterruptedly; he develops ox-behaviour fully and uninterruptedly. Having developed the ox-duty fully and uninterruptedly, the ox-habit fully and uninterruptedly, the ox-mind fully and uninterruptedly, and ox-behaviour fully and uninterruptedly, on the dissolution of the body, after death, he reappears in the company of oxen. But if he has such a view as this: ‘By this virtue or observance or asceticism or holy life I shall become a great god or some lesser god,’ that is wrong view in his case.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 57, Kukkuravatika Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_02&page_number=51&name=majjhimapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=SWRoYSwgc2VuaXlhLCBla2FjY28gZ292YXRh4bmDIGJoxIF2ZXRpIHBhcmlwdeG5h%2BG5h2HhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Sīlena sujjhituṃ sakkā, vatena sujjhituṃ sakkā, sīlavatehi sujjhituṃ sakkāti gahitasamādānaṃ pana sīlabbataparāmāso nāma.**  
> But the grasping and undertaking (gahitasamādānaṃ) that states 'it is possible to be purified (sujjhituṃ) by virtue, it is possible to be purified by observance, it is possible to be purified by virtuous practices and observances (sīlavatehi)' is called adherence to rites and rituals (sīlabbataparāmāso).  
>  
> — *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Dhammasaṅgaṇī-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=383&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U8SrbGVuYSBzdWpqaGl0deG5gyBzYWtrxIEsIHZhdGVuYSBzdWpqaGl0deG5gyBzYWtrxIEs)

> **Sīlañca vatañca parāmasatīti sīlabbataparāmāso .**  
> Clinging to virtue and practice is clinging to rules and observances (sīlabbataparāmāso).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Sīlabbataparāmāsanti sīlañca vatañca parāmasitvā gahitagahaṇamattaṃ.**  
> “Backsliding to attachment to rules and rituals” means merely the act of holding onto virtue and observances having touched them.  
>  
> — *Samantapāsādikā* (Mahāvagga-aṭṭhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_03&page_number=366&name=mah%C4%81vaggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=U8SrbGFiYmF0YXBhcsSBbcSBc2FudGkgc8SrbGHDsWNhIHZhdGHDsWNhIHBhcsSBbWFzaXR2xIEgZ2FoaXRhZ2FoYeG5h2FtYXR0YeG5gy4%3D)
