### Grammatical Analysis
**Nirodhasamāpatti**: [f.] the attainment of cessation. Formed by *nirodha* (cessation, stopping) + *samāpatti* (attainment, meditative entry). 

### Orthodox Definition
*Nirodhasamāpatti* is the attainment of the cessation of perception and feeling (*saññāvedayitanirodha*). The Visuddhimagga defines it as “yā anupubbanirodhavasena cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ appavatti”: the non-occurrence of consciousness and mental factors by way of successive cessation (Vism XXIII 868). In the Mūla, the Mahāvedalla Sutta distinguishes the one who has entered it from the dead. For both, the bodily, verbal and mental formations have ceased and subsided; but in the dead one life (*āyu*) is exhausted, warmth (*usmā*) is stilled and the faculties are broken up, while in the one in cessation life is not exhausted, warmth is not stilled and the faculties are very clear (M-i 1545:1-3). In the Cūḷavedalla Sutta Dhammadinnā says that, on attaining it, verbal formation ceases first, then bodily formation, then mental formation (M-i 1594:2).

Who attains it: the Visuddhimagga says that all worldlings, Stream-enterers and Once-returners do not attain it, nor do Non-returners and Arahants who are dry-insight practitioners (*sukkhavipassaka*); only Non-returners and Arahants who have obtained the eight attainments do (Vism-ii 2093:2-3, XXIII 868). It rests on two powers (serenity and insight), the calming of the three formations, sixteen kinds of knowledge-conduct and nine of concentration-conduct, and mastery (Vism-ii 2093:4, quoting Paṭis I 34). “Sixteen kinds of knowledge-conduct” is the eminent designation; the Non-returner has it by fourteen. The Stream-enterer and Once-returner cannot, because their lust for the five cords of sensual pleasure is not abandoned and the strength of serenity is incomplete (Vism XXIII 870). It is attained only in the five-constituent realm (Vism XXIII 871), and its purpose is that, weary of the occurrence of formations, one lives happily here and now without consciousness, having reached the cessation that is Nibbāna (Vism XXIII 872).

How it is entered: the monk attains the first jhāna, emerges and sees its formations as impermanent, suffering and not-self, and does the same with each attainment up to the sphere of nothingness. There he performs four preliminary duties (*pubbakicca*): protecting belongings that are not bound to him from fire, water, wind, thieves and rats (*nānābaddhaavikopana*), noting the summons of the Saṅgha (*saṅghapaṭimānana*) and of the Teacher (*satthupakkosana*), and determining the limit of his life-span (*addhānapariccheda*) (Vism XXIII 874-878, Vism-ii 2107:3). Then he attains the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and after one or two moments of consciousness he is without consciousness (*acittako*) and touches cessation (Vism XXIII 879). The resolution protects the belongings for seven days (*sattāhaṃ*, Vism-ii 2109:1-2), and the attainment may last only as long as the life-span, the Saṅgha's and the Teacher's summons permit (Vism XXIII 880). Emergence is the fruit of Non-returning for the Non-returner and the fruit of Arahantship for the Arahant (Vism XXIII 881).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato, yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno – imesaṃ kiṃ nānākaraṇan” ti? “Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato tassa kāyasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, vacīsaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, cittasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, āyu parikkhīṇo, usmā vūpasantā, indriyāni paribhinnāni. Yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno tassapi kāyasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, vacīsaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, cittasaṅkhārā niruddhā paṭippassaddhā, āyu na parikkhīṇo, usmā avūpasantā, indriyāni vippasannāni. Yvāyaṃ, āvuso, mato kālaṅkato, yo cāyaṃ bhikkhu saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpanno – idaṃ nesaṃ nānākaraṇan” ti.**  
> “What is the difference, friend, between one who is dead and has passed away, and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling?” “Friend, in one who is dead and has passed away, the physical processes have ceased and subsided, the verbal processes have ceased and subsided, the mental processes have ceased and subsided, vitality is exhausted, warmth is stilled, and the faculties have disintegrated.” “But in a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling, the physical processes have ceased and subsided, the verbal processes have ceased and subsided, the mental processes have ceased and subsided, but vitality is not exhausted, warmth is not stilled, and the faculties are very clear.” “This, friend, is the difference between one who is dead and has passed away, and a bhikkhu who has entered the cessation of perception and feeling.”  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 43, Mahāvedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=370&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFl2xIF5YeG5gywgxIF2dXNvLCBtYXRvIGvEgWxh4bmFa2F0bywgeW8%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpajjantassa panāyye, bhikkhuno katame dhammā paṭhamaṃ nirujjhanti – yadi vā kāyasaṅkhāro, yadi vā vacīsaṅkhāro, yadi vā cittasaṅkhāro” ti? “Saññāvedayitanirodhaṃ samāpajjantassa kho, āvuso visākha, bhikkhuno paṭhamaṃ nirujjhati vacīsaṅkhāro, tato kāyasaṅkhāro, tato cittasaṅkhāro” ti.**  
> “But for a bhikkhu who is attaining the cessation of perception and feeling, Noble Lady, which qualities cease first: bodily volitional formation, verbal volitional formation, or mental volitional formation?” “For a bhikkhu who is attaining the cessation of perception and feeling, friend Visākha, first verbal volitional formation ceases, then bodily volitional formation, then mental volitional formation.”  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 44, Cūḷavedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=376&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFNhw7HDscSBdmVkYXlpdGFuaXJvZGhh4bmDIHNhbcSBcGFqamFudGFzc2EgcGFuxIF5eWUsIGJoaWtraHVubyBrYXRhbWU%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tattha kā nirodhasamāpattīti yā anupubbanirodhavasena cittacetasikānaṃ dhammānaṃ appavatti. Ke taṃ samāpajjanti , ke na samāpajjantīti sabbepi puthujjanā, sotāpannā, sakadāgāmino, sukkhavipassakā ca anāgāmino, arahanto na samāpajjanti. Aṭṭhasamāpattilābhino pana anāgāmino, khīṇāsavā ca samāpajjanti.**  
> 868. In that context, what is the attainment of cessation? It is the non-occurrence (appavatti) of mental states (consciousness and mental factors) by way of successive cessation. Who attain it, who do not attain it? All ordinary people, stream-enterers, once-returners, and non-returners and Arahants who are dry-insight practitioners do not attain it. But non-returners and Arahants who have obtained the eight attainments do attain it.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XXIII, 868, Nirodhasamāpattikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=344&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODY4LiBUYXR0aGEga8SBIG5pcm9kaGFzYW3EgXBhdHTEq3RpIHnEgSBhbnVwdWJiYW5pcm9kaGF2YXNlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **So evaṃ ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samāpajjitvā vuṭṭhāya imaṃ pubbakiccaṃ katvā nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ samāpajjati. Athekaṃ vā dve vā cittavāre atikkamitvā acittako hoti, nirodhaṃ phusati.**  
> 879. Thus, having entered the dimension of nothingness and emerged from it, and having performed this preliminary duty, he enters the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception. ### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahāvedalla Sutta* (MN 43, M-i 1543-1545) – the difference between a dead body and a monk in cessation (life, warmth and faculties remain); *Cūḷavedalla Sutta* (MN 44, M-i 1594-1596) – Dhammadinnā on the order in which the formations cease and on how emergence occurs.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXIII, 867-881, Vism-ii 2090-2122) – who attains it, why, how it is entered, how long it lasts, how one emerges.
