### Grammatical Analysis
**Nibbidāñāṇa**: [nt.] Knowledge of disenchantment. Formed by *nibbidā* (disenchantment, weariness; the noun of the verb *nibbindati*, "becomes disenchanted") + *ñāṇa* (knowledge). In the Visuddhimagga the same knowledge is called *nibbidānupassanāñāṇa*, the knowledge of contemplation of disenchantment.

### Orthodox Definition
In the *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* the *nibbidāñāṇa* is the knowledge in which one who contemplates (for example as impermanent) knows and sees the in-breath and the out-breath "as it really is"; the text counts eight such knowledges (Paṭis 1002).

The *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* places it after the knowledge of danger (*ādīnavānupassanāñāṇa*). Seeing all formations as danger, the meditator "becomes disenchanted with, dissatisfied with, and does not delight in" the manifold formations of every kind of becoming, destiny and abode. The commentary gives the simile of the elephant king Chaddanta, who does not delight in the midst of a city but only in the Chaddanta lake in the Himalayas; so the yogi does not delight in any *saṅkhāra*, but only in the state of peace seen through the method beginning "non-arising is security".

The *Visuddhimagga* then says that this knowledge is one in meaning with the two preceding knowledges: the one knowledge receives three names, *bhayatupaṭṭhāna* (it saw all formations as fearful), *ādīnavānupassanā* (it brings forth danger in those formations) and *nibbidānupassanā* (disenchantment arose in them). It cites the Paṭisambhidāmagga: the wisdom in appearance as terror, the knowledge of danger and disenchantment have one meaning, only the phrasing differs.

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga)
> **Katamāni aṭṭha nibbidāñāṇāni? Aniccānupassī assāsaṃ yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passatīti – nibbidāñāṇaṃ, aniccānupassī passāsaṃ yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passatīti – nibbidāñāṇaṃ … pe … paṭinissaggānupassī assāsaṃ yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passatīti – nibbidāñāṇaṃ, paṭinissaggānupassī passāsaṃ yathābhūtaṃ jānāti passatīti – nibbidāñāṇaṃ. Imāni aṭṭha nibbidāñāṇāni.**  
> What are the eight kinds of knowledge of disenchantment? One contemplating as inconstant knows and sees the in-breath as it really is—this is the knowledge of disenchantment; one contemplating as inconstant knows and sees the out-breath as it really is—this is the knowledge of disenchantment … pe … one contemplating relinquishment knows and sees the in-breath as it really is—this is the knowledge of disenchantment; one contemplating relinquishment knows and sees the out-breath as it really is—this is the knowledge of disenchantment. These are the eight kinds of knowledge of disenchantment.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭisambhidāmagga, nibbidā as one in meaning)
> **Yā ca bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā yañca ādīnave ñāṇaṃ yā ca nibbidā, ime dhammā nānatthā ceva nānābyañjanā ca, udāhu ekatthā, byañjanameva nānanti? Yā ca bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā yañca ādīnave ñāṇaṃ yā ca nibbidā, ime dhammā ekatthā, byañjanameva nānaṃ.**  
> Understanding of appearance as terror, knowledge of danger, and disenchantment—are these things different in meaning and different in phrasing, or are they one in meaning and only the phrasing is different? Understanding of appearance as terror, knowledge of danger, and disenchantment—these things have one meaning, and only the phrasing is different.  
>  
> — [Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/) (§227) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_18&page_number=259&name=pa%E1%B9%ADisambhid%C4%81maggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=WcSBIGNhIGJoYXlhdHVwYeG5reG5rWjEgW5lIHBhw7HDscSBIHlhw7FjYSDEgWTEq25hdmU%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **So evaṃ sabbasaṅkhāre ādīnavato passanto sabbabhavayonigativiññāṇaṭṭhitisattāvāsagate sabhedake saṅkhāragate nibbindati ukkaṇṭhati nābhiramati. Yathā ca sabbaseto sattapatiṭṭho iddhimā vehāsaṅgamo chaddanto nāgarājā nagaramajjhe nābhiramati, himavati chaddantadahagahaneyeva abhiramati, evamayaṃ yogīvaravāraṇo sabbasmimpi saṅkhāragate nābhiramati, anuppādo khemantiādinā nayena diṭṭhe santipadeyeva abhiramati, tanninnatappoṇatappabbhāramānaso hotīti. Nibbidānupassanāñāṇaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ. 755. Taṃ panetaṃ purimena ñāṇadvayena atthato ekaṃ. Tenāhu porāṇā – “Bhayatupaṭṭhānaṃ ekameva tīṇi nāmāni labhati, sabbasaṅkhāre bhayato addasāti bhayatupaṭṭhānaṃ nāma jātaṃ. Tesuyeva saṅkhāresu ādīnavaṃ uppādetīti ādīnavānupassanā nāma jātaṃ. Tesuyeva saṅkhāresu nibbindamānaṃ uppannanti nibbidānupassanā nāma jātan” ti. Pāḷiyampi vuttaṃ – “yā ca bhayatupaṭṭhāne paññā, yañca ādīnave ñāṇaṃ, yā ca nibbidā, ime dhammā ekatthā, byañjanameva nānan” ti (paṭi. ma.1.227).**  
> 754. Seeing all formations in this way as danger, he becomes disenchanted with, dissatisfied with, and does not delight in (nābhiramati) the manifold categories of formations belonging to any kind of becoming, generation, destiny, station of consciousness, or abode of beings. And just as Chaddanta, the naga king (nāgarājā)—all-white, standing upon seven points (sattapatiṭṭho), wonderful in power (iddhimā), and flying through the sky—finds no delight in the middle of a city (nagaramajjhe), but finds delight only in the Chaddanta lake and forest in the Himalayas, just so this noble meditator-elephant finds no delight in any category of formations, but finds delight only in the state of peace (santipade) seen by the method beginning with, “Non-arising is safety,” and his mind tends, inclines, and leans toward it. Knowledge of contemplation of disenchantment is concluded. 755. This knowledge is one in meaning (atthato ekaṃ) with the two previous kinds of knowledge. Therefore the Ancients said: “The knowledge of appearance as terror is one, yet it receives three names: it saw all formations as peril (bhayato), thus the name ‘appearance as terror’ arose; it brings to light the danger in those very formations, thus the name ‘contemplation of danger’ arose; it arose while becoming disenchanted with those very formations, thus the name ‘contemplation of disenchantment’ arose.” In the canonical text (pāḷi) as well it was said: “The wisdom regarding appearance as terror, the knowledge regarding danger, and disenchantment—these things have one meaning (ekatthā), only the phrasing is different.”  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, Nibbidānupassanāñāṇakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=287&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NzU0LiBTbyBldmHhuYMgc2FiYmFzYeG5hWtoxIFyZSDEgWTEq25hdmF0byBwYXNzYW50bw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* (Ñāṇarāsichakkaniddesa, Paṭis 1002) – "What are the eight kinds of knowledge of disenchantment?"; and §227 (Paṭis 1244) – disenchantment, the knowledge of danger and appearance as terror are one in meaning.
* **Sutta**: *Alagaddūpama Sutta* (MN 22) – the well-taught noble disciple "becomes disenchanted with material form ... with consciousness" (*rūpasmiṃ nibbindati ... viññāṇasmiṃ nibbindati*); the verb *nibbindati*, not the term *nibbidāñāṇa*.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XXI, Nibbidānupassanāñāṇakathā) – the Chaddanta simile and the three names of one knowledge.
