### Grammatical Analysis
**Jātaka**: [nt.] birth story; a story of a past life. The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī-ṭīkā explains: "it is called *jātaka* because by it the Blessed One proclaims and makes known his previous conduct, what was born, what occurred, what lived in the past (*jātaṃ bhūtaṃ purāvutthaṃ bhagavato pubbacaritaṃ kāyati katheti pakāseti etenāti jātakaṃ*)" (Sv-nt-i 427:1). So *jāta* + the verb *kāyati* (to tell).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Jātaka* is the seventh of the nine aṅgas (*suttaṃ, geyyaṃ, veyyākaraṇaṃ, gāthaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, jātakaṃ, abbhutadhammaṃ, vedallaṃ*, A-iv 985:2). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī explains the Jātaka aṅga as "the five hundred and fifty Jātakas beginning with the Apaṇṇaka Jātaka" (Sv-i 190:9). In the Khuddaka Nikāya, it is the tenth book in the list of the Paramatthajotikā (Pj-i 24:5). The CST edition numbers the stories up to 547, the Vessantara Jātaka (Ja-ii 9034:1).

The Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā (the commentary on the Jātaka) begins with the Nidānakathā, which tells the story in three introductions (*nidāna*): the remote (*dūrenidāna*), from the Great Being's aspiration at the feet of the Buddha Dīpaṅkara until his rebirth in Tusita; the not-too-remote (*avidūrenidāna*), from his passing away from Tusita until his attainment of omniscience at the Bodhi-maṇḍa; and the immediate (*santikenidāna*), obtained in each place where the Buddha dwelled (Ja-a-i 29:1, 30). See *[Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā](/glossary/jatakatthavannana/)*.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya, Dutiyavalāhaka Sutta)
> **Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco puggalo dhammaṃ pariyāpuṇāti – suttaṃ, geyyaṃ, veyyākaraṇaṃ, gāthaṃ, udānaṃ, itivuttakaṃ, jātakaṃ, abbhutadhammaṃ, vedallaṃ.**  
> Here, monks, someone masters the Dhamma—discourse, stanzas, expositions, verses, exclamations, sayings, birth stories, marvels, and answers to questions.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Apaṇṇakajātakādīni paññāsādhikāni pañcajātakasatāni ‘ jātakan ’ti veditabbaṃ.**  
> The five hundred and fifty Jātakas, such as the Apaṇṇaka Jātaka, should be known as Jātaka.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Dīghanikāya-aṭṭhakathā, Ganthārambhakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=25&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QXBh4bmH4bmHYWthasSBdGFrxIFkxKtuaSBwYcOxw7HEgXPEgWRoaWvEgW5pIHBhw7FjYWrEgXRha2FzYXTEgW5pIOKAmCBqxIF0YWthbiDigJl0aQ%3D%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī-abhinavaṭīkā)
> **Jātaṃ bhūtaṃ purāvutthaṃ bhagavato pubbacaritaṃ kāyati katheti pakāseti etenāti jātakaṃ , taṃ pana imānīti dassetuṃ “apaṇṇakajātakādīnī”tiādimāha.**  
> It is called Jātaka because by this, the Blessed One proclaims and makes known his previous existences, what was born, what occurred, and what happened in the past. Wishing to show that it comprises the Apaṇṇaka Jātaka and so on, he said “Apaṇṇakajātakādīni” and so on.  
>  
> — Sīlakkhandhavaggaabhinavaṭīkā (Sumaṅgalavilāsinī-ṭīkā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=tika_di_01_02&page_number=132&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavaggaabhinava%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=SsSBdGHhuYMgYmjFq3Rh4bmDIHB1csSBdnV0dGhh4bmDIGJoYWdhdmF0byBwdWJiYWNhcml0YeG5gyBrxIF5YXRp)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā, Nidānakathā)
> **Sā panāyaṃ jātakassa atthavaṇṇanā dūrenidānaṃ, avidūrenidānaṃ, santikenidānanti imāni tīṇi nidānāni dassetvā vaṇṇiyamānā ye naṃ suṇanti, tehi samudāgamato paṭṭhāya viññātattā yasmā suṭṭhu viññātā nāma hoti, tasmā taṃ tāni nidānāni dassetvā vaṇṇayissāma.**  
> Now, this commentary on the Jātaka, being expounded after showing these three categories of introduction—the distant introduction, the intermediate introduction, and the immediate introduction—is called 'well-understood' because those who hear it know it well from its origin. Therefore, having shown those introductions, I shall expound the commentary.  
>  
> — *[Jātakaṭṭhavaṇṇanā](/glossary/jatakatthavannana/)* (Nidānakathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_zat_01&page_number=2&name=j%C4%81takaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U8SBIHBhbsSBeWHhuYMgasSBdGFrYXNzYSBhdHRoYXZh4bmH4bmHYW7EgSBkxatyZW5pZMSBbmHhuYMsIGF2aWTFq3JlbmlkxIFuYeG5gyw%3D)
