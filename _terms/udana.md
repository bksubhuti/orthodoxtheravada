### Grammatical Analysis
**Udāna**: [nt.] Inspired utterance; joyful exclamation. Derived from *udāneti*; the commentary explains it as a word of joy that the heart cannot contain: *yaṃ pītivacanaṃ hadayaṃ gahetuṃ na sakkoti, adhikaṃ hutvā anto asaṇṭhahitvā bahi nikkhamati, taṃ udānanti vuccati* (Sv-i 656:3; Mp-v 397:8).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Udāna* is the third book of the Khuddaka Nikāya, after the Khuddakapāṭha and Dhammapada (Pj-i 24:5; Sv-i 108:1). The Udāna-aṭṭhakathā says it consists of eight chapters (*Bodhivaggo, Mucalindavaggo, Nandavaggo, Meghiyavaggo, Soṇavaggo, Jaccandhavaggo, Cūḷavaggo, Pāṭaligāmiyavaggo*), **eighty discourses** and ninety-five verses of inspired utterance (Ud-a 44:2-3).

In the Mūla the formula is *atha kho bhagavā etamatthaṃ viditvā tāyaṃ velāyaṃ imaṃ udānaṃ udānesi*, "then, having understood this matter, the Blessed One on that occasion uttered this inspired utterance" (Ud 8:1). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī explains: as oil that a measure cannot hold overflows (*avaseka*) and water that a pond cannot hold overflows (*ogha*), so a word of joy (*pītivacanaṃ*) that the heart cannot hold, which becomes excessive and does not stay within but comes out, is called *udāna* (Sv-i 656:1-3). The Udāna-aṭṭhakathā limits the meaning in this book to the utterances spoken personally by the Fully Self-Awakened One, which the Blessed One referred to when he listed the Dhamma in nine parts as "Udāna" (Ud-a 39:3-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Nibbāna Sutta)
> **“Atthi, bhikkhave, ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ. No cetaṃ, bhikkhave, abhavissa ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ, nayidha jātassa bhūtassa katassa saṅkhatassa nissaraṇaṃ paññāyetha. Yasmā ca kho, bhikkhave, atthi ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ, tasmā jātassa bhūtassa katassa saṅkhatassa nissaraṇaṃ paññāyatī” ti.**  
> “There is, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned. If, monks, there did not exist that unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned, then no escape from the born, become, made, and conditioned would be discerned here. But because there is, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned, therefore an escape from the born, become, made, and conditioned is discerned.”  
>  
> — [Udāna](/glossary/udana/) (Ud 8.1, Paṭhamanibbānapaṭisaṃyuttasutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_03&page_number=178&name=ud%C4%81nap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEF0dGhpLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGFqxIF0YeG5gyBhYmjFq3Rh4bmDIGFrYXRh4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Udānaṃ udānesīti udāhāraṃ udāhari, yathā hi yaṃ telaṃ mānaṃ gahetuṃ na sakkoti, vissanditvā gacchati, taṃ avasekoti vuccati. Yañca jalaṃ taḷākaṃ gahetuṃ na sakkoti, ajjhottharitvā gacchati, taṃ oghoti vuccati; evameva yaṃ pītivacanaṃ hadayaṃ gahetuṃ na sakkoti, adhikaṃ hutvā anto asaṇṭhahitvā bahinikkhamati, taṃ udānanti vuccati.**  
> Udānaṃ udānesī: He uttered an exclamation; just as oil that cannot be held in a measuring vessel, but spills out, is called ‘overflowing’. And water that cannot be held by a pond, but overflows, is called a ‘flood’; so too, that utterance born of joy which cannot be held in the heart, but which, having become excessive, does not stay within but exits—that is called an ‘exclamation’.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (Sv-i 656, Udānaṃ udānesi) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_01&page_number=129&name=s%C4%ABlakkhandhavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VWTEgW5h4bmDIHVkxIFuZXPEq3RpIHVkxIFoxIFyYeG5gyB1ZMSBaGFyaSwgeWF0aMSBIGhp)
