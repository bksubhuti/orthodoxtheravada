### Grammatical Analysis
**Khanti**: [f.] patience; forbearance; enduring. The Paramatthajotikā explains it in the Maṅgala Sutta verse: "in the passage *khantī ca*, *khanti* means enduring (*khamanaṃ khanti*)" (Pj-i 718:1). Its related verb is *khamati* (to endure, bear).

### Orthodox Definition
*Khanti* is patience or forbearance. The Buddha's teaching, as recited by the Buddha Vipassī and in the Dhammapada, calls it "the highest austerity" (*khantī paramaṃ tapo titikkhā*, D-ii 210:1; Dhp 184). The Maṅgala Sutta counts "forbearance and being easy to admonish, and seeing ascetics" (*khantī ca sovacassatā, samaṇānañca dassanaṃ*) among the highest blessings (Khp 75:1).

The Paramatthajotikā explains the word as *khamanaṃ*, enduring (Pj-i 718:1). The Ṭīkā to the Aṅguttara (Mp-t-v 438:2) glosses *adhivāsanakkhanti*, the patience of endurance, as taking upon oneself the evil conduct and harsh speech of others, without opposing them.

The Khantīvādī Jātaka (Ja 313) illustrates it in the Bodhisatta's past life as an ascetic preaching patience (*samaṇo khantidīpano*), who stayed steady in patience (*khantiyāyeva ṭhitaṃ*) even when the king of Kāsi had him cut down (Ja-i 2512:1, 2513:1). In the commentary he answers "I am a preacher of patience" (*khantivādī*) as his hands and feet are cut off (Ja-a-iii 323). As a perfection, see *[khanti-pāramī](/glossary/khanti-parami/)*.
