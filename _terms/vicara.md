### Grammatical Analysis
**Vicāra**: [m.] sustained thought; sustained application. From *vi* + *car* (to move, wander): "vicaraṇaṃ vicāro, anusañcaraṇanti vuttaṃ hoti", sustained thinking is vicāra, "continued moving about" is what is meant (Vism-i 937:1); "ārammaṇe tena cittaṃ vicaratīti vicāro", it is that by which the mind moves about on the object (As 1178:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Vicāra* is the second factor of the first jhāna, "savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ" (DN 2, D-i 328:1), and one of the six occasional mental factors (*pakiṇṇaka cetasika*): vitakka, vicāra, adhimokkha, vīriya, pīti, chanda (Abh-s II §3). It arises in the sixty-six consciousnesses consisting of the fifty-five with vitakka and the eleven of the second jhāna (Abh-s 104:1, §14). The Mūla's definition: "whatever is wandering, moving about, sustained thought, sub-investigation, close investigation, continuous application of mind, continuous observation of mind: this is called vicāra" (Vibh 1735:2; Dhs 398:2, §8). In the Majjhima Nikāya, "vitakkavicārā vacīsaṅkhāro", initial and sustained thought are the verbal formation (MN 44, M-i 1589:1).

The Visuddhimagga gives its characteristic, function and manifestation: "characteristic, continued pressure on the object (*ārammaṇānumajjanalakkhaṇo*); function, keeping conascent states occupied with that (*sahajātānuyojanaraso*); manifestation, keeping the mind anchored (*cittassa anuppabandhanapaccupaṭṭhāno*)" (Vism IV §71, Vism-i 937:2; As 1178:4-6). It contrasts vicāra with *[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)*: "although they are inseparable in some states, vitakka, by its grossness and being a forerunner, is the first impact of the mind, like the striking of a bell; vicāra, by its subtlety and its nature of continued pressure, is the continuous succession, like the ringing of a bell." Vitakka is like a bird's flapping of its wings when about to soar, or a bee's diving towards a lotus; vicāra is of calm conduct, like the bird gliding with outspread wings, or the bee circling above the lotus (Vism-i 938:1-4). The commentary on the Book of Twos has another simile, the large bird that takes the wind with both wings and then keeps them still (vitakka) and the bird flapping its wings to catch the wind (vicāra) (Vism-i 938:5-6). Further similes: the hand holding a dirty bronze vessel and the hand rubbing it; the potter's hand pressing and the hand moving hither and thither; the fixed pin and the revolving pin of a compass (Vism-i 939:1-3).

As a jhāna factor it is the opponent of doubt: "vicāro vicikicchāya", as the Peṭaka says (Vism-i 934:3; As 1415:9; see [vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)).

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha, the first jhāna)
> **“So vivicceva kāmehi, vivicca akusalehi dhammehi savitakkaṃ savicāraṃ vivekajaṃ pītisukhaṃ paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati.**  
> 226. Quite secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, he enters and dwells in the first jhāna, which is accompanied by applied and sustained thought and filled with the rapture and happiness born of seclusion.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima, vitakkavicārā as verbal formation)
> **“Assāsapassāsā kho, āvuso visākha, kāyasaṅkhāro, vitakkavicārā vacīsaṅkhāro, saññā ca vedanā ca cittasaṅkhāro” ti.**  
> “In-breathing and out-breathing, friend Visākha, is bodily volitional formation; applied and sustained thought is verbal volitional formation; perception and feeling is mental volitional formation.”  
>  
> — *[Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/)* (MN 44, Cūḷavedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=375&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEFzc8SBc2FwYXNzxIFzxIEga2hvLCDEgXZ1c28gdmlzxIFraGEsIGvEgXlhc2HhuYVraMSBcm8s)

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Vibhaṅga)
> **Tattha katamo vicāro? Yo cāro anucāro vicāro anuvicāro upavicāro cittassa anusandhanatā anupekkhanatā – ayaṃ vuccati “vicāro”.**  
> What is sustained thought (vicāro) there? That which is wandering, sub-movement, sustained thought, sub-investigation, close investigation, continuous application of mind, continuous observation of mind—this is called sustained thought (vicāro).  
>  
> — *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Jhānavibhaṅga) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=268&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGthdGFtbyB2aWPEgXJvPw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Yo tasmiṃ samaye cāro vicāro anuvicāro upavicāro cittassa anusandhānatā anupekkhanatā – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye vicāro hoti.**  
> Whatever wandering (cāra), investigation, sub-investigation, close investigation, sustained application of mind, or continuous observation exists at that time—this is investigation at that time.  
>  
> — *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§8, first jhāna consciousness) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=18&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=WW8gdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGPEgXJvIHZpY8SBcm8gYW51dmljxIFybw%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, characteristic etc.)
> **Vicaraṇaṃ vicāro , anusañcaraṇanti vuttaṃ hoti. Svāyaṃ ārammaṇānumajjanalakkhaṇo, tattha sahajātānuyojanaraso, cittassa anuppabandhanapaccupaṭṭhāno.**  
> Sustained thinking is sustained thought (vicāro); "continued sustainment" is what is meant. It has the characteristic of continued pressure on the object, the function of keeping conascent states occupied with that, and is manifested as keeping the mind anchored.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IV, §71) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=138&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VmljYXJh4bmHYeG5gyB2aWPEgXJvICwgYW51c2HDsWNhcmHhuYdhbnRpIHZ1dHRh4bmDIGhvdGku)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the similes)
> **Santepi ca nesaṃ katthaci avippayoge oḷārikaṭṭhena pubbaṅgamaṭṭhena ca ghaṇḍābhighāto viya cetaso paṭhamābhinipāto vitakko. Sukhumaṭṭhena anumajjanasabhāvena ca ghaṇḍānuravo viya anuppabandho vicāro. Vipphāravā cettha vitakko paṭhamuppattikāle paripphandanabhūto cittassa ākāse uppatitukāmassa pakkhino pakkhavikkhepo viya padumābhimukhapāto viya ca gandhānubandhacetaso bhamarassa. Santavutti vicāro nātiparipphandanabhāvo cittassa ākāse uppatitassa pakkhino pakkhappasāraṇaṃ viya, paribbhamanaṃ viya ca padumābhimukhapatitassa bhamarassa padumassa uparibhāge. Dukanipātaṭṭhakathāyaṃ pana “ākāse gacchato mahāsakuṇassa ubhohi pakkhehi vātaṃ gahetvā pakkhe sannisīdāpetvā gamanaṃ viya ārammaṇe cetaso abhiniropanabhāvena pavatto vitakko. Vātaggahaṇatthaṃ pakkhe phandāpayamānassa gamanaṃ viya anumajjanabhāvena pavatto vicāro” ti vuttaṃ, taṃ anuppabandhena pavattiyaṃ yujjati.**  
> And although they are inseparable in some states, applied thought is the first impact of the mind, like the striking of a bell, because of its grossness and because of its being a forerunner. Sustained thought is a continuous succession, like the ringing of a bell, because of its subtleness and its nature of continued pressure. And here, applied thought is energetic, acting as a vibration of the mind at the time of its first arising, like a bird’s flapping of its wings when about to soar into the sky, and like a bee’s diving towards a lotus when its mind is drawn to the scent. Sustained thought is of calm conduct, without much vibration of the mind, like a bird gliding with outspread wings after soaring into the sky, and like a bee buzzing above the lotus after diving towards it. But in the commentary to the Book of Twos it is said: "Applied thought occurs as a state of directing the mind onto an object, like the flight of a large bird flying in the sky, taking the wind with both wings and then keeping them still. Sustained thought occurs with the nature of continued pressure, like the flight of a bird flapping its wings to catch the wind." This explanation fits its occurrence as a continuous succession.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (IV, §71) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=138&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=U2FudGVwaSBjYSBuZXNh4bmDIGthdHRoYWNpIGF2aXBwYXlvZ2Ugb%2BG4t8SBcmlrYeG5reG5rWhlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha)
> **Vitakko vicāro adhimokkho vīriyaṃ pīti chando cāti cha ime cetasikā pakiṇṇakā nāma.**  
> 3. Applied thought, sustained thought, decision, energy, zest, and zeal—these six mental factors are called occasionals.  
>  
> — *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (II, §3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=8&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=My4gVml0YWtrbyB2aWPEgXJvIGFkaGltb2traG8gdsSrcml5YeG5gyBwxKt0aQ%3D%3D)
