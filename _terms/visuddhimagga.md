### Grammatical Analysis
**Visuddhimagga**: [m.] The Path of Purification. Formed by *visuddhi* (purification) + *magga* (path, way). The author himself explains it: *visuddhīti sabbamalavirahitaṃ accantaparisuddhaṃ nibbānaṃ veditabbaṃ*, "purification" is Nibbāna, free of all stains and utterly pure; *tassā visuddhiyā maggoti visuddhimaggo*, the path to that purification is the Visuddhimagga; and *maggoti adhigamūpāyo vuccati*, "path" means the means of attainment (Vism-i 27:1-3).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Visuddhimagga* is the work of the elder **Buddhaghosa**, of the Mahāvihāra lineage; TPR places it in the Aṭṭhakathā layer. The colophon names him: *buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena … kato visuddhimaggo nāma*, "the Path of Purification was made by the Elder who bears the name Buddhaghosa given by his teachers", "of Moraṇḍakheṭaka" (Vism-ii 2184:1). He composed it at the request of the Elder Saṅghapāla, from the ways of exposition of the Mahāvihāra residents (*Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ desanānayanissitaṃ*, Vism-i 24:1; Vism-ii 2171:1-2176:1). It gathers, for the most part (*yebhuyyena*), the explanations in the commentaries on the five Nikāyas, free of the faults of mixing in the views of other schools (Vism-ii 2164:1-2167:1; Ṭīkā Vism-mht-ii 1826:1).

It is built on the verse the Buddha spoke in the Jaṭā Sutta (SN 1.23): *sīle patiṭṭhāya naro sapañño, cittaṃ paññañca bhāvayaṃ, ātāpī nipako bhikkhu, so imaṃ vijaṭaye jaṭaṃ* (S-i 219:1-220:1; explained in Vism-i 11-14). The author takes *sīla*, *citta* (concentration, by the word "mind") and *paññā* as the three parts (Vism-i 650:1; Vism-ii 275:1). It has twenty-three chapters (*paricchedas*; the last is Vism-ii 2145:1):
* **Virtue (*[Sīla](/glossary/sila/)*)**: Chapter 1, *Sīlaniddeso* (Vism-i 60:1) and Chapter 2, *Dhutaṅganiddeso*, the ascetic practices, which cleanse the virtue (Vism-i 417:1, 418:1-4).
* **Concentration (*[Samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)*)**: Chapters 3 to 13, from *Kammaṭṭhānaggahaṇaniddeso* (Vism-i 649:1) through the forty meditation subjects (*Cattālīsakammaṭṭhānavaṇṇanā*, Vism-i 768:1), the Brahmavihāras (Chapter 9), *Āruppaniddeso* (Chapter 10), *Samādhiniddeso* (Chapter 11), *Iddhividhaniddeso* (Chapter 12) and *Abhiññāniddeso* (Chapter 13, Vism-ii 151:1).
* **Wisdom (*[Paññā](/glossary/panna/)*)**: Chapters 14 to 23. Chapter 14 (*Khandhaniddeso*, Vism-ii 273:1) opens the *Paññākathā* (Vism-ii 274:1, 275:1). The chapters cover the aggregates (14), bases and elements (15), faculties and truths (16), the grounds of wisdom including dependent origination (17, *Paññābhūminiddeso*, Vism-ii 829:1), and the five purifications from the purification of view to the purification by knowledge and vision (Chapters 18 to 22), ending with *Paññābhāvanānisaṃsaniddeso* (Chapter 23).

For the orthodox tradition the *Visuddhimagga* is the standard explanation of the three trainings, drawing on the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) and the commentaries of the [Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/). The Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā, named *Paramatthamañjūsā*, was made by the Ācariya Dhammapāla of the Badaratittha Monastery (Vism-mht-ii 1849:1-1850:1).

### Quote (Mūla: the verse on which the Visuddhimagga is built)
> **“Sīle patiṭṭhāya naro sapañño, cittaṃ paññañca bhāvayaṃ; Ātāpī nipako bhikkhu, so imaṃ vijaṭaye jaṭaṃ.**  
> “A person established on virtue, wise, developing the mind and wisdom, An ardent and prudent bhikkhu: he can disentangle this tangle.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the aim)
> **Sīlādisaṅgahaṃ khemaṃ, ujuṃ maggaṃ visuddhiyā. Yathābhūtaṃ ajānantā, suddhikāmāpi ye idha; Visuddhiṃ nādhigacchanti, vāyamantāpi yogino. Tesaṃ pāmojjakaraṇaṃ, suvisuddhavinicchayaṃ; Mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ, desanānayanissitaṃ. Visuddhimaggaṃ bhāsissaṃ, taṃ me sakkacca bhāsato; Visuddhikāmā sabbepi, nisāmayatha sādhavoti.**  
> And the safe, straight path to purity, which comprises virtue and the rest, those here who, though desiring purity, are not knowing as it actually is, these practitioners, though they strive, do not attain purity. To bring them joy, with highly purified decisions, Relying on the teaching method of the dwellers in the Great Monastery, I shall expound the Path of Purification. As I carefully expound it, Let all good people who desire purity listen to it attentively.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the meaning of the name)
> **Tattha visuddhīti sabbamalavirahitaṃ accantaparisuddhaṃ nibbānaṃ veditabbaṃ. Tassā visuddhiyā maggoti visuddhimaggo. Maggoti adhigamūpāyo vuccati. Taṃ visuddhimaggaṃ bhāsissāmīti attho. So panāyaṃ visuddhimaggo katthaci vipassanāmattavaseneva desito.**  
> 3. Therein, purification (visuddhi) should be understood as Nibbāna, which, being devoid of all stains, is utterly pure. The path to that purification is the path of purification. Path (maggo) means the means of attainment. The meaning is: I shall expound that path of purification. Now this path of purification is in some places taught by way of insight alone.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (Nidānādikathā, 3) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=2&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=My4gVGF0dGhhIHZpc3VkZGjEq3RpIHNhYmJhbWFsYXZpcmFoaXRh4bmDIGFjY2FudGFwYXJpc3VkZGhh4bmDIG5pYmLEgW5h4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the author)
> **Parama visuddha saddhā buddhi vīriya paṭimaṇḍitena sīlācārajjava maddavādiguṇasamudayasamuditena sakasamaya samayantaragahanajjhogāhaṇasamatthena paññāveyyattiyasamannāgatena tipiṭakapariyattibhede sāṭṭhakathe satthusāsane appaṭihatañāṇappabhāvena mahāveyyākaraṇena karaṇasampattijanitasukhaviniggatamadhurodāravacanalāvaṇṇayuttena yuttamuttavādinā vādīvarena mahākavinā chaḷabhiññāpaṭisambhidādi bhedaguṇapaṭimaṇḍite uttarimanussadhamme appaṭihatabuddhīnaṃ theravaṃsappadīpānaṃ therānaṃ mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena vipulavisuddhabuddhinā buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena mudantakhedakavattabbena kato visuddhimaggo nāma.**  
> 895. This called the Path of Purification was made by the Elder who is adorned with supreme and purified faith, understanding, and energy; who is endowed with a collection of qualities such as virtue, conduct, uprightness, and gentleness; who is capable of delving into the thicket of one's own doctrine and the doctrines of others; who is possessed of wisdom and proficiency; who, with the power of unimpeded knowledge with regard to the Teacher’s message consisting of the divisions of the Tipiṭaka scriptures with their commentaries, is a great grammarian; who is gifted with the beauty of sweet and noble speech that flows easily from the perfection of his vocal instrument; who is a speaker of what is appropriate, a supreme speaker, and a great poet; who is an ornament to the lineage of the Elders dwelling in the Great Monastery—those shining lights of the Elder-lineage who possess unimpeded understanding in the superhuman states embellished with qualities such as the six direct-knowledges and the discriminations; who is of vast and purified understanding; who bears the name Buddhaghosa (buddhaghosa) conferred by his teachers; and who should be called 'of Moraṇḍakheṭaka'.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XXIII colophon, 895) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=355&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=ODk1LiBQYXJhbWEgdmlzdWRkaGEgc2FkZGjEgSBidWRkaGkgdsSrcml5YQ%3D%3D)
