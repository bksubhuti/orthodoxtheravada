### Grammatical Analysis
**Uggahanimitta**: [nt.] learning sign. Formed by *uggaha* (grasping, from *uggaṇhāti*, "to grasp, learn"; prefix *ud* + root *gah*) + *[nimitta](/glossary/nimitta/)* (sign). The Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī gives two analyses: the sign "to be grasped by the mind as if seen by the eye" (*cakkhunā diṭṭhaṃ viya manasā uggahetabbaṃ nimittaṃ*), or "the sign of one who grasps" (*uggaṇhantassa vā nimittaṃ*) (Abhidh-s-t 1723:2). The word does not occur in the Mūla (0 hits for *uggahanimitta* in the Mūla); it belongs to the commentaries, where it is found 26 times in the Aṭṭhakathā, mostly in the Visuddhimagga (Vism-i, 23).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Uggahanimitta* is the second of the three signs (*nimitta*) of meditation: *parikammanimitta*, *uggahanimitta* and *paṭibhāganimitta* (Abhidh-s IX §5). The *[parikammanimitta](/glossary/parikammanimitta/)* is the sign of the preparatory work, such as the kasiṇa disk (Abhidh-s-t 1723:1). The Visuddhimagga says that, as the meditator develops the earth kasiṇa, "when it comes into focus as he adverts with his eyes shut exactly as it does with his eyes open, then the learning sign is said to have been produced" (Vism IV §57). The Saṅgaha puts it the same way: when the sign has been apprehended by the mind and comes to the mind-door "as if seen by the eye", that object is called the *uggahanimitta* (Abh-s IX §29).

The Visuddhimagga gives the distinguishing mark between the learning sign and the next sign: "in the learning sign, any fault in the kasiṇa is apparent" (*uggahanimitte kasiṇadoso paññāyati*), whereas the *[paṭibhāganimitta](/glossary/patibhaganimitta/)* appears "a hundred times, a thousand times more purified", without colour or shape, a mere mode of appearance born of perception for one who has obtained concentration (Vism IV §57). In foulness meditation the learning sign appears "distorted, repulsive and terrifying" (Vism VI §117). Once it arises the meditator is told to go back to his dwelling and develop it there (Vism IV §57). The hindrances are suppressed, and access concentration reached, when the counterpart sign arises (Vism IV §57, §58), not at the learning sign.

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, earth kasiṇa)
> **Tassevaṃ bhāvayato yadā nimīletvā āvajjantassa ummīlitakāle viya āpāthamāgacchati, tadā uggahanimittaṃ jātaṃ nāma hoti. Tatrāyaṃ purimassa ca uggahanimittassa imassa ca viseso, uggahanimitte kasiṇadoso paññāyati, paṭibhāganimittaṃ thavikato nihatādāsamaṇḍalaṃ viya sudhotasaṅkhathālaṃ viya valāhakantarā nikkhantacandamaṇḍalaṃ viya meghamukhe balākā viya uggahanimittaṃ padāletvā nikkhantamiva tato sataguṇaṃ sahassaguṇaṃ suparisuddhaṃ hutvā upaṭṭhāti. Tañca kho neva vaṇṇavantaṃ, na saṇṭhānavantaṃ. Yadi hi taṃ īdisaṃ bhaveyya, cakkhuviññeyyaṃ siyā oḷārikaṃ sammasanupagaṃ tilakkhaṇabbhāhataṃ, na panetaṃ tādisaṃ. Kevalañhi samādhilābhino upaṭṭhānākāramattaṃ saññajametanti.**  
> 57. As he is developing it in this way, when it comes into focus as he adverts with his eyes shut exactly as it does with his eyes open, then the learning sign is said to have been produced. Herein, this is the difference between the earlier learning sign and this counterpart sign: in the learning sign, any fault in the kasiṇa is apparent; but the counterpart sign appears as if breaking out, having pierced through the learning sign, a hundred times, a thousand times more purified than that—like a mirror disk drawn from its bag, like a well-washed mother-of-pearl dish, like the moon's disk emerging from behind a cloud, or like cranes against a thunder cloud. But indeed, it has neither color nor shape. For if it were such, it would be cognizable by the eye, gross, susceptible to comprehension, and marked by the three characteristics; but it is not like that. For it is merely a mode of appearance born of perception (saññaja) for one who has obtained concentration.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, foulness)
> **Tassevaṃ karoto paṭibhāganimittaṃ uppajjati. Tatridaṃ nimittadvayassa nānākaraṇaṃ, uggahanimittaṃ virūpaṃ bībhacchaṃ bheravadassanaṃ hutvā upaṭṭhāti. Paṭibhāganimittaṃ pana yāvadatthaṃ bhuñjitvā nipanno thūlaṅgapaccaṅgapuriso viya.**  
> 117. As he does so, the counterpart sign arises. Here is the difference between the two signs: the learning sign appears as a distorted, repulsive, and terrifying sight. But the counterpart sign is like a stout-limbed man lying down after having eaten as much as he wanted.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, the three signs)
> **Parikammanimittaṃ uggahanimittaṃ paṭibhāganimittañceti tīṇi nimittāni ca veditabbāni.**  
> 5. The three signs should also be understood: the preliminary sign, the learning sign, and the counterpart sign.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha, the learning sign)
> **Yadā pana taṃ nimittaṃ cittena samuggahitaṃ hoti, cakkhunā passantasseva manodvārassa āpāthamāgataṃ, tadā tamevārammaṇaṃ uggahanimittaṃ nāma, sā ca bhāvanā samādhiyati.**  
> 29. When that sign has been thoroughly apprehended by the mind and has come into range of the mind door just as if one were seeing it with the eye, then that same object is called the learning sign (uggahanimitta), and that development becomes concentrated.  
>  
> — *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (IX, §29, Bhāvanābheda) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=61&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=MjkuIFlhZMSBIHBhbmEgdGHhuYMgbmltaXR0YeG5gyBjaXR0ZW5h)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Parikammassa nimittaṃ ārammaṇattāti parikammanimittaṃ , kasiṇamaṇḍalādi. Tadeva cakkhunā diṭṭhaṃ viya manasā uggahetabbaṃ nimittaṃ, uggaṇhantassa vā nimittanti uggahanimittaṃ.**  
> 5. The preliminary sign (parikammanimittaṃ) is the sign of preliminary work, such as the kasiṇa-disk and so on, because it is the object. That very sign, which should be grasped by the mind just as if it were seen with the eye, or the sign for one who is grasping it mentally, is the learning sign (uggahanimittaṃ).  
>  
> — *Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī* (on Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha IX §5) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=258&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NS4gUGFyaWthbW1hc3NhIG5pbWl0dGHhuYMgxIFyYW1tYeG5h2F0dMSBdGkgcGFyaWthbW1hbmltaXR0YeG5gyAs)
