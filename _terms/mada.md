### Grammatical Analysis
**Mada**: [m.] vanity; intoxication; pride; infatuation. From root *mad* (to be intoxicated, to revel in).

### Orthodox Definition
*Mada* is one of the sixteen defilements of the mind (*cittassa upakkilesa*) listed in the Vatthasutta (MN 7, M-i 191:2) and represents a state of psychological intoxication. It is not intoxication via alcohol, but the deep, blinding vanity that arises when a person becomes obsessed with their own worldly advantages.

The Vibhaṅga defines it as intoxication, conceit and elation of mind (Vibh 2551:2); the Papañcasūdanī gives its characteristic as the state of being intoxicated (*mattabhāvalakkhaṇo*), its function as taking up intoxication, its manifestation as madness (Ps-i 565:1), and says conceit and over-conceit give rise to *mada* of birth and the like, and the intoxicated one then falls into heedlessness (Ps-i 566:11-12).

The Buddha isolated **three specific types of intoxication** (AN 3.39):
1. *Yobbana-mada*: Intoxication with youth (forgetting that the body will inevitably age).
2. *Ārogya-mada*: Intoxication with health (forgetting that the body is entirely vulnerable to sickness).
3. *Jīvita-mada*: Intoxication with life (forgetting that death can strike at any moment).

When the mind is swollen with *mada*, it abandons urgency (*saṃvega*) and slides directly into heedlessness (*[pamāda](/glossary/pamada/)*), completely neglecting the practice of the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) until disaster strikes.

### Quote
> **Tattha katamo mado? Yo mado majjanā majjitattaṃ māno maññanā maññitattaṃ unnati unnāmo dhajo sampaggāho ketukamyatā cittassa – ayaṃ vuccati “mado”.**  
> 845. Which is infatuation (mado) there? Whatever infatuation, state of infatuation, condition of being infatuated, conceit, conceiving, state of conceiving, elevation, exaltation, standard, self-exaltation, or the mind’s desire for the standard there is—this is called infatuation (mado).  
>

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Sukhumāla Sutta* (AN 3.39, A-iii 309 "9. Sukhumālasuttaṃ") – The Buddha reflecting on his own life as a prince, demonstrating how he shattered these three specific intoxications by contemplating old age, sickness, and death.
* **Sutta**: *Vatthasutta* (MN 7) – *mado cittassa upakkileso* (M-i 191:2).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Khuddakavatthuvibhaṅga, Vibh §845 "Tattha katamo mado?").
* **Commentary**: *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* on the Vatthasutta (Ps-i 565:1; 566:11-12; 855:5, *mada* is abandoned by the arahant path).
