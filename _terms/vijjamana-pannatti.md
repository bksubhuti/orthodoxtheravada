### Grammatical Analysis
**Vijjamāna-paññatti**: [f.] concept of what exists. Formed by *vijjamāna* (existing, found) + *paññatti* (concept, designation). The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha says *paññāpanato paññatti*: the concept "as what makes known" is expressed by terms such as name (*nāma*), name-making (*nāmakamma*) and so on.

### Orthodox Definition
The term is not in the Mūla: *vijjamānapaññatti* has 0 hits in the Mūla and occurs in the Aṭṭhakathā (for example the Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā, 15 times), the Ṭīkā and the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha. The Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā introduces "six other designations according to the method of the commentary, apart from the Pāḷi": *vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti*.

It defines the first: the making known (*paññāpanā*) of a dhamma that really exists (*vijjamānassa sato sambhūtassa dhammassa*) in the ultimate sense, such as the wholesome and the unwholesome. The Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha (§42) gives the same: when they make known by this concept what exists in the ultimate sense (*paramatthato vijjamānaṃ*), such as matter and feeling (*rūpavedanādiṃ*), that is *vijjamānapaññatti*; when they make known what does not exist in the ultimate sense, such as land and mountains, it is *avijjamānapaññatti*.

The mixed kinds are illustrated by *chaḷabhiñño* ("one with the six supernormal powers": the powers exist, the person does not; *vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti*), *itthisaddo* ("woman's sound": the sound exists, the woman does not; *avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti*), *cakkhuviññāṇaṃ* (both existing) and *rājaputto* ("king's son": both not existing).

### Quote
> **Pāḷimuttakena pana aṭṭhakathānayena aparāpi cha paññattiyo – vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena avijjamānapaññattīti. Tattha kusalākusalasseva saccikaṭṭhaparamatthavasena vijjamānassa sato sambhūtassa dhammassa paññāpanā vijjamānapaññatti nāma. Tathā avijjamānassa lokaniruttimattasiddhassa itthipurisādikassa paññāpanā avijjamānapaññatti nāma.**  
> But according to the teacher's method, independent of the Pāli (text), there are six other designations: concept of what is existent, concept of what is non-existent, concept of what is non-existent by means of what is existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is non-existent, concept of what is existent by means of what is existent, and concept of what is non-existent by means of what is non-existent. Among these, the exposition of a quality that is actually existent and arisen, by way of ultimate reality and true meaning, such as the wholesome and unwholesome, is called a concept of what is existent. Likewise, the exposition of what is non-existent, such as women and men, and so on, which is established merely by worldly expression, is called a concept of what is non-existent.  
>

> **Paññāpanato paññatti pana nāmanāmakammādināmena paridīpitā, sā vijjamānapaññatti avijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, vijjamānena vijjamānapaññatti, avijjamānena avijjamānapaññatti ceti chabbidhā hoti. 42. Tattha yadā pana paramatthato vijjamānaṃ rūpavedanādiṃ etāya paññāpenti, tadāyaṃ vijjamānapaññatti. Yadā pana paramatthato avijjamānaṃ bhūmipabbatādiṃ etāya paññāpenti, tadāyaṃ avijjamānapaññattīti pavuccati.**  
> 41. But the concept as what makes known is expressed by terms like name and naming. It is of six kinds: a concept of what is existent, a concept of what is non-existent, a concept of what is non-existent by means of what is existent, a concept of what is existent by means of what is non-existent, a concept of what is existent by means of what is existent, and a concept of what is non-existent by means of what is non-existent. 42. Therein, when they make known through such a concept what exists in the ultimate sense, such as matter or feeling, that is a concept of what is existent (vijjamānapaññatti). But when they make known through such a concept what does not exist in the ultimate sense, such as land or mountains, that is called a concept of what is non-existent (avijjamānapaññatti).  
>  
> — [Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/) (Ch. VIII, Paññattibhedo, §41–42) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_05&page_number=58&name=abhidhammatthasa%E1%B9%85gaho&note=&selected_text=NDEuIFBhw7HDscSBcGFuYXRvIHBhw7HDsWF0dGkgcGFuYSBuxIFtYW7EgW1ha2FtbcSBZGluxIFtZW5hIHBhcmlkxKtwaXTEgSw%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/)* (Pp 6:1) – the Pāḷi's own six designations (*khandha-, āyatana-, dhātu-, sacca-, indriya-, puggalapaññatti*), which are a different set from the six of the commentary.
* **Commentary**: Puggalapaññatti-aṭṭhakathā (Pp-a 183) – the six designations by the commentary method and the definition of *vijjamānapaññatti*.
* **Historical**: *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (Chapter VIII, Paññattibhedo: Abhidh-s 742–746) – the six kinds with examples; Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Abhidh-s-t 1704) explains them.
