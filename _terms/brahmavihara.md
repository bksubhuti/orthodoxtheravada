### Grammatical Analysis
**Brahmavihāra**: [m.] divine abiding; sublime abiding. Formed by *brahma* (Brahmā; the supreme) + *vihāra* (abiding, dwelling). The Visuddhimagga explains the name in two ways: the abidings are supreme (*seṭṭha*) because they are the right attitude towards beings, and faultless (*niddosa*); and just as Brahmās abide with faultless minds, so a practitioner who is endowed with them abides like Brahmā (*brahmasamā hutvā viharanti*). They are also called *[Appamaññā](/glossary/appamanna/)* (immeasurables) because they occur with an immeasurable range, beings without limit being their object.

### Orthodox Definition
The *Brahmavihāras* are the four states *[mettā](/glossary/metta/)* (loving-kindness), *[karuṇā](/glossary/karuna/)* (compassion), *[muditā](/glossary/mudita/)* (altruistic joy) and *[upekkhā](/glossary/upekkha/)* (equanimity). In the Suttas they are the stock formula "with a mind accompanied by loving-kindness he abides pervading one quarter, then the second, the third, the fourth, above, below, across, everywhere" (for example in the Tevijja Sutta, DN 13, where this is called the way to companionship with Brahmā). The Visuddhimagga (IX) describes each by characteristic (*lakkhaṇa*), function (*rasa*), manifestation (*paccupaṭṭhāna*) and proximate cause (*padaṭṭhāna*):
1. *Mettā*: characterized by promoting the aspect of welfare; function, presenting welfare; manifested as the removal of resentment; proximate cause, seeing the lovable aspect of beings. Its success is the subsiding of ill will.
2. *Karuṇā*: characterized by promoting the aspect of allaying suffering; function, not bearing others' suffering; manifested as non-cruelty; proximate cause, seeing the helplessness of those overwhelmed by suffering. Its success is the subsiding of cruelty (*vihiṃsā*). The word is explained as that which, when there is suffering in others, makes the hearts of good people tremble (*hadayakampana*).
3. *Muditā*: characterized by rejoicing; function, being unenvious; manifested as the elimination of discontent (*arati*); proximate cause, seeing the success of beings. Its success is the subsiding of discontent.
4. *Upekkhā*: characterized by promoting the aspect of neutrality towards beings; function, seeing equality in beings; manifested as the quieting of resentment and approval; proximate cause, seeing that beings own their kamma (*kammassakatā*). Its success is the quieting of resentment and approval.

The Visuddhimagga also gives their order and their antidotes: loving-kindness is the path to purity for one full of ill will, compassion for one full of cruelty, altruistic joy for one full of discontent, and equanimity for one full of lust. In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī the four are counted as the four *brahmavihāra* jhānas, and the Visuddhimagga (III) assigns the first three to the three lower jhānas of the fourfold scheme and the fourth to the fourth jhāna (Vism-i 777).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Vuccate, seṭṭhaṭṭhena tāva niddosabhāvena cettha brahmavihāratā veditabbā. Sattesu sammāpaṭipattibhāvena hi seṭṭhā ete vihārā. Yathā ca brahmāno niddosacittā viharanti, evaṃ etehi sampayuttā yogino brahmasamā hutvā viharantīti seṭṭhaṭṭhena niddosabhāvena ca brahmavihārāti vuccanti.**  
> They should be understood as divine abidings, first of all, in the sense of being supreme and by reason of being faultless. For these abidings are supreme because they constitute the right attitude towards beings. And just as Brahmas dwell with faultless minds, so too, practitioners associated with these abidings dwell like Brahmas; thus, they are called divine abidings because of their supreme nature and their faultless state.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, lakkhaṇa and rasa)
> **Lakkhaṇādito panettha hitākārappavattilakkhaṇā mettā , hitūpasaṃhārarasā, āghātavinayapaccupaṭṭhānā, sattānaṃ manāpabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā. Byāpādūpasamo etissā sampatti, sinehasambhavo vipatti. Dukkhāpanayanākārappavattilakkhaṇā karuṇā , paradukkhāsahanarasā, avihiṃsāpaccupaṭṭhānā, dukkhābhibhūtānaṃ anāthabhāvadassanapadaṭṭhānā. Vihiṃsūpasamo tassā sampatti, sokasambhavo vipatti. Pamodanalakkhaṇā muditā , anissāyanarasā, arativighātapaccupaṭṭhānā, sattānaṃ sampattidassanapadaṭṭhānā. Arativūpasamo tassā sampatti, pahāsasambhavo vipatti. Sattesu majjhattākārappavattilakkhaṇā upekkhā , sattesu samabhāvadassanarasā, paṭighānunayavūpasamapaccupaṭṭhānā,“kammassakā sattā, te kassa ruciyā sukhitā vā bhavissanti, dukkhato vā muccissanti, pattasampattito vā na parihāyissantī” ti evaṃ pavattakammassakatādassanapadaṭṭhānā. Paṭighānunayavūpasamo tassā sampatti, gehasitāya aññāṇupekkhāya sambhavo vipatti.**  
> As to the characteristics and so forth (lakkhaṇādito) here, loving-kindness (mettā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of welfare; its function is presenting welfare; it is manifested as the removal of resentment; and its proximate cause is seeing the lovable aspect of beings. The subsiding of ill will is its success (sampatti), and the arising of affection is its failure (vipatti). Compassion (karuṇā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of allaying suffering; its function is not bearing others' suffering; it is manifested as non-cruelty; and its proximate cause is seeing the helplessness of those overwhelmed by suffering. The subsiding of cruelty is its success (sampatti), and the arising of sorrow is its failure (vipatti). Altruistic joy (muditā) is characterized by rejoicing; its function is being unenvious; it is manifested as the elimination of discontent; and its proximate cause is seeing the success of beings. The subsiding of discontent is its success (sampatti), and the arising of merriment is its failure (vipatti). Equanimity (upekkhā) is characterized by promoting the aspect of neutrality towards beings; its function is seeing equality in beings; it is manifested as the quieting of resentment and approval; and its proximate cause is seeing the ownership of deeds occurring thus: ‘Beings have each their own kamma; by whose preference will they become happy, or get free from suffering, or not fall away from the success they have reached?’ The quieting of resentment and approval is its success (sampatti), and the arising of the equanimity of unknowing based on the household life is its failure (vipatti).  
>

* **Mūla**: *Theragāthā* (Th 14.1, v. 649) – "Brahmavihāraṃ bhāvemi".
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Rūpāvacarakusala, "cattāri brahmavihārajhānāni") and *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Chapter 13, Appamaññāvibhaṅga: "catasso appamaññāyo", Vibh 1855).
