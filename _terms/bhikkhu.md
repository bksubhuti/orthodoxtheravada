### Grammatical Analysis
**Bhikkhu**: [m.] The Vinaya's word-analysis gives: "*bhikkhūti bhikkhakoti bhikkhu*", a bhikkhu is one who begs (*bhikkhako*); "*bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagatoti bhikkhu*", one who has entered the practice of begging; "*bhinnapaṭadharoti bhikkhu*", one who wears broken cloth (Vin-i 74:1). The Aṭṭhakathā adds the derivation "*saṃsāre bhayaṃ ikkhatīti bhikkhu*", "he sees fear (*bhayaṃ*) in saṃsāra, therefore he is a bhikkhu" (It-a 305:2; Nidd-a-i 460:1). The Abhidhamma (Vibhaṅga) has also "*bhindati pāpake akusale dhammeti bhikkhu*", one who breaks (pierces) evil unwholesome states (Vibh 1633:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The Vinaya lists the senses in which one is called a bhikkhu: by convention (*samaññāya*), by declaration (*paṭiññāya*), by the call "come, bhikkhu", by ordination through the three refuges, the good (*bhadro*), the essential (*sāro*), the trainee (*sekho*), the adept (*asekho*), and "one who has been fully ordained by a harmonious Saṅgha, by a formal act with one motion and three announcements, unshakable and fit for the case"; this last is the bhikkhu intended in the rule (Vin-i 74:1-2). The Vibhaṅga adds the one who abandons defilements partially or completely, and the one who is neither trainee nor adept (Vibh 1633:1).

On admission, the Pātimokkha rule says: if a bhikkhu knowingly gives the upasampadā to a person under twenty years of age, that person is not ordained and those bhikkhus are blameworthy, "this is a pācittiya for him" (Vin-ii 1242:1; Pātimokkha rule 65, Dvem-bhk 278:1). The Aṭṭhakathā to the Pārājika explains the word-analysis: one who begs by noble begging, whether he receives or not; and one who has taken up the practice of living on alms established by the Buddhas (Sp-i 1003:1-3). The Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā says there are two duties for bhikkhus, *ganthadhura* and *vipassanādhura* (Dhp-a 51:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya Piṭaka)
> ** Bhikkhūti bhikkhakoti bhikkhu, bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagatoti bhikkhu, bhinnapaṭadharoti bhikkhu, samaññāya bhikkhu, paṭiññāya bhikkhu, ehi bhikkhūti bhikkhu, tīhi saraṇagamanehi upasampannoti bhikkhu, bhadro bhikkhu, sāro bhikkhu, sekho bhikkhu, asekho bhikkhu, samaggena saṅghena ñatticatutthena kammena akuppena ṭhānārahena upasampannoti bhikkhu. Tatra yvāyaṃ bhikkhu samaggena saṅghena ñatticatutthena kammena akuppena ṭhānārahena upasampanno, ayaṃ imasmiṃ atthe adhippeto bhikkhūti.**  
> “Bhikkhu” refers to one who begs; one who has taken up the practice of begging; one who wears patched robes; one who is a bhikkhu by convention; one who is a bhikkhu by declaration; one who is called “Come, bhikkhu”; one who has received full ordination by taking the three refuges; a good bhikkhu; a substantial bhikkhu; a trainee bhikkhu; a fully trained bhikkhu; one who has received full ordination from the harmonious Sangha through a legal procedure consisting of one motion and three announcements, which is irreversible and fit to stand. This bhikkhu is meant in this instance. Here, the bhikkhu who has received full ordination from the harmonious Sangha through a legal procedure consisting of one motion and three announcements, which is irreversible and fit to stand—this bhikkhu is meant in this instance.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Pācittiya 65)
> **“Yo pana bhikkhu jānaṃ ūnavīsativassaṃ puggalaṃ upasampādeyya , so ca puggalo anupasampanno, te ca bhikkhū gārayhā , idaṃ tasmiṃ pācittiyan”ti.**  
> “Whatever monk should knowingly confer the upasampadā ordination on an individual under twenty years of age, both that individual is not ordained and these monks are blameworthy; this is for him an offence of expiation.”  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **“Bhikkhū” ti samaññāya bhikkhu, paṭiññāya bhikkhu, bhikkhatīti bhikkhu, bhikkhakoti bhikkhu, bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagatoti bhikkhu, bhinnapaṭadharoti bhikkhu, bhindati pāpake akusale dhammeti bhikkhu, bhinnattā pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ bhikkhu, odhiso kilesānaṃ pahānā bhikkhu, anodhiso kilesānaṃ pahānā bhikkhu, sekkho bhikkhu, asekkho bhikkhu, nevasekkhanāsekkho bhikkhu, aggo bhikkhu, bhadro bhikkhu, maṇḍo bhikkhu, sāro bhikkhu, samaggena saṅghena ñatticatutthena kammena akuppena ṭhānārahena upasampanno bhikkhu.**  
> “Bhikkhu” refers to a bhikkhu by convention, a bhikkhu by claim, a bhikkhu because he begs, a bhikkhu because he is a beggar, a bhikkhu because he has resorted to the practice of begging, a bhikkhu because he wears patched robes, a bhikkhu because he pierces evil unwholesome states, a bhikkhu because of the breaking of bad unwholesome states. It also refers to a bhikkhu because of the partial abandoning of defilements, a bhikkhu because of the complete abandoning of defilements, a trainee bhikkhu, a bhikkhu whose training is complete, a bhikkhu who is neither a trainee nor one whose training is complete, a foremost bhikkhu, a good bhikkhu, a purified bhikkhu, a substantial bhikkhu. Furthermore, it refers to a bhikkhu who has attained ordination through a formal act consisting of one motion and three announcements, by a harmonious Sangha, which is irreversible and fit to stand.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Samantapāsādikā)
> **Bhikkhuniddese bhikkhatīti bhikkhako ; labhanto vā alabhanto vā ariyāya yācanāya yācatīti attho. Buddhādīhi ajjhupagataṃ bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagatattā bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhupagato nāma.**  
> In the definition of 'bhikkhu', one who begs (bhikkhati) is a beggar (bhikkhako); the meaning is: one who begs by means of noble begging (ariyāya yācanāya), whether receiving or not. One who has entered upon the practice of living on alms (bhikkhācariyaṃ ajjhūpagato) is so called because he has undertaken the practice of living on alms established by the Buddhas and others.  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Sp-i 1003) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=204&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Qmhpa2todW5pZGRlc2UgYmhpa2toYXTEq3RpIGJoaWtraGFrbyA7)
