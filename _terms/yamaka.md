### Grammatical Analysis
**Yamaka**: [nt.] The Book of Pairs. The Aṭṭhakathā explains the name: *kenaṭṭhena yamakanti? Yugaḷaṭṭhena. Yugaḷañhi yamakanti vuccati – yamakapāṭihāriyaṃ, yamakasālā tiādīsu viya*, "in what sense is it called Yamaka? In the sense of a pair; for a pair is called *yamaka*, as in the twin miracle and the twin sāla trees" (Yam-a 1633:2-5). The whole treatise is called *yamaka* as the collection of the ten Yamakas (Yam-a 1633:7).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Yamaka* is the sixth treatise of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka in the traditional order: *Dhammasaṅgaho, vibhaṅgo, dhātukathā, puggalapaññatti, kathāvatthu, yamakaṃ, paṭṭhānaṃ* (Sp-i 133:1). Its own commentary calls it "the sixth Abhidhamma treatise" (*abhidhammappakaraṇaṃ chaṭṭhaṃ*; Yam-a 1627:1).

The Aṭṭhakathā says it is divided into ten: *Mūlayamakaṃ, khandhayamakaṃ, āyatanayamakaṃ, dhātuyamakaṃ, saccayamakaṃ, saṅkhārayamakaṃ, anusayayamakaṃ, cittayamakaṃ, dhammayamakaṃ, indriyayamakaṃ* (Yam-a 1633:1). The Mūla has these ten as its numbered headings: 1. Mūlayamaka (Yam-i 4:1), 2. Khandhayamaka (Yam-i 309:1), 3. Āyatanayamaka (Yam-i 1463:1), 4. Dhātuyamaka (Yam-i 3544:1), 5. Saccayamaka (Yam-i 3809:1), 6. Saṅkhārayamaka (Yam-ii 4:1), 7. Anusayayamaka (Yam-ii 822:1), 8. Cittayamaka (Yam-ii 3204:1), 9. Dhammayamaka (Yam-iii 4:1), 10. Indriyayamaka (Yam-iii 1122:1).

Each point is asked as a pair of questions, one the converse of the other. For example, in the Khandhayamaka: *rūpaṃ rūpakkhandho? rūpakkhandho rūpaṃ?* ("Is form the form aggregate? Is the form aggregate form?", Yam-i 315:1-316:1). In the Saccayamaka: *yo dukkhasaccaṃ parijānāti so samudayasaccaṃ pajahatīti? yo vā pana samudayasaccaṃ pajahati so dukkhasaccaṃ parijānātīti?* ("Does one who fully comprehends the truth of suffering abandon the truth of origin? Or does one who abandons the truth of origin fully comprehend the truth of suffering?", Yam-i 4738:1, 4739:1); the answer to each is "Yes" (*āmantā*, Yam-i 4738:2, 4739:2).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: the name and the ten Yamakas)
> **Mūlayamakaṃ, khandhayamakaṃ, āyatanayamakaṃ, dhātuyamakaṃ, saccayamakaṃ, saṅkhārayamakaṃ, anusayayamakaṃ, cittayamakaṃ, dhammayamakaṃ, indriyayamakanti imesaṃ dasannaṃ yamakānaṃ vasena idaṃ pakaraṇaṃ dasavidhena vibhattanti hi vuttaṃ. Tattha yesaṃ dasannaṃ yamakānaṃ vasena idaṃ pakaraṇaṃ dasavidhena vibhattaṃ, tesañceva imassa ca pakaraṇassa nāmattho tāva evaṃ veditabbo – kenaṭṭhena yamakanti? Yugaḷaṭṭhena. Yugaḷañhi yamakanti vuccati – ‘ yamakapāṭihāriyaṃ, yamakasālā ’ tiādīsu viya. Iti yugaḷasaṅkhātānaṃ yamakānaṃ vasena desitattā imesu dasasu ekekaṃ yamakaṃ nāma. Imesaṃ pana yamakānaṃ samūhabhāvato sabbampetaṃ pakaraṇaṃ yamakanti veditabbaṃ.**  
> 1. The Method of Roots, the Method of Aggregates, the Method of Sense Bases, the Method of Elements, the Method of Truths, the Method of Formations, the Method of Latent Tendencies, the Method of Consciousness, the Method of Mental Phenomena, and the Method of Faculties—it is said that this treatise is divided into ten types by way of these ten methods. There, regarding the ten methods by which this treatise is divided into ten types, the meaning of the name of both those methods and this treatise should first be understood in this way—in what sense is it called the ‘Pairs’? In the sense of a pair. For a pair is called a ‘pair’ (yamaka)— as in the ‘Twin Miracle,’ the ‘Twin Sal trees,’ and so on. Thus, because it is taught by way of the pairs that are counted as twins, each one among these ten is called a ‘pair.’ However, because of the collective nature of these pairs, this whole treatise should be known as the ‘Pairs’ (Yamaka).  
>  
> — [Yamaka-aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/yamaka-atthakatha/) (Yamakappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā, Mūlayamaka, Uddesavāravaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=288&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=MS4gTcWrbGF5YW1ha2HhuYMsIGtoYW5kaGF5YW1ha2HhuYMsIMSBeWF0YW5heWFtYWth4bmDLCBkaMSBdHV5YW1ha2HhuYMsIHNhY2NheWFtYWth4bmDLA%3D%3D)
