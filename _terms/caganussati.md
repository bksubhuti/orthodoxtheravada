### Grammatical Analysis
**Cāgānussati**: [f.] recollection of generosity. Formed by *cāga* (giving up, relinquishment, generosity) + *anussati* (recollection). The Visuddhimagga explains that it is reckoned as "recollection of generosity" because it arises by way of recollecting the qualities of generosity (*cāgaguṇānussaraṇavasena uppannattā cāgānussaticceva saṅkhaṃ gacchati*, Vism-i 1368:5).

### Orthodox Definition
*Cāgānussati* is the recollection of one's own generosity. In the Mahānāma Sutta the Buddha describes it thus: a noble disciple "recollects their own generosity" (*attano cāgaṃ anussarati*) with the formula "*lābhā vata me, suladdhaṃ vata me*…" ("How fortunate indeed I am…"), and at that time the mind is not obsessed by greed, hate or delusion but is "straight", focused on generosity (A-vi 42).

In the Visuddhimagga it is the fifth section of the recollections (Vism-i 1363, "5. Cāgānussatikathā"). The commentary says that one who wishes to develop it should be naturally intent on generosity and constantly devoted to giving and sharing; one may begin by resolving not to eat without first giving at least a morsel when a recipient is present, give to recipients of distinguished virtue, take the sign in that, and then recollect one's own generosity in solitude (Vism-i 1364:1-2). It explains the formula word by word, for example *muttacāgo* as giving without reservation, *payatapāṇi* as having purified hands, and *vossaggarato* as delighting in giving up (Vism-i 1367). The stain of miserliness (*[macchariya](/glossary/macchariya/)*) is one of the dark states that defile the luminous nature of the mind (Vism-i 1366:3).

As to the result, the Visuddhimagga says that the jhāna factors arise in a single moment for one whose hindrances are suppressed, but owing to the profundity of the qualities of generosity, or devotion to recollecting their various kinds, the jhāna reaches only access (*upacāra*) and not absorption (Vism-i 1368:3-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Puna caparaṃ, mahānāma, ariyasāvako attano cāgaṃ anussarati – ‘ lābhā vata me, suladdhaṃ vata me! Yohaṃ maccheramalapariyuṭṭhitāya pajāya vigatamalamaccherena cetasā agāraṃ ajjhāvasāmi muttacāgo payatapāṇi vossaggarato yācayogo dānasaṃvibhāgarato ’ ti. Yasmiṃ, mahānāma, samaye ariyasāvako cāgaṃ anussarati nevassa tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti; ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti cāgaṃ ārabbha. ‘ ariyasāvako visamagatāya pajāya samappatto viharati, sabyāpajjāya pajāya abyāpajjo viharati, dhammasotaṃ samāpanno cāgānussatiṃ bhāveti”’.**  
> “Furthermore, Mahānāma, a noble disciple recollects their own generosity: ‘How fortunate indeed I am, how very fortunate! For I dwell at home rid of the stain of avarice, with a mind cleansed of the stain of miserliness, freely generous, open-handed, delighting in relinquishment, committed to charity, delighting in giving and sharing.’” Mahānāma, at the time when a noble disciple recollects their generosity, at that time their mind is not obsessed by greed, not obsessed by hate, not obsessed by delusion; at that time their mind is simply straight, focused on generosity. ‘A noble disciple dwells in balance amid an unbalanced population, dwells unafflicted amid an afflicted population. As one who has entered the stream of the Dhamma, he develops the recollection of generosity.’”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Cāgānussatiṃ bhāvetukāmena pana pakatiyā cāgādhimuttena niccappavattadānasaṃvibhāgena bhavitabbaṃ. Atha vā pana bhāvanaṃ ārabhantena ito dāni pabhuti sati paṭiggāhake antamaso ekālopamattampi dānaṃ adatvā na bhuñjissāmīti samādānaṃ katvā taṃdivasaṃ guṇavisiṭṭhesu paṭiggāhakesu yathāsatti yathābalaṃ dānaṃ datvā tattha nimittaṃ gaṇhitvā rahogatena paṭisallīnena “lābhā vata me suladdhaṃ vata me, yohaṃ maccheramalapariyuṭṭhitāya pajāya vigatamalamaccherena cetasā viharāmi muttacāgo payatapāṇi vossaggarato yācayogo dānasaṃvibhāgarato” ti evaṃ vigatamalamaccheratādiguṇavasena attano cāgo anussaritabbo. 161. Tassevaṃ vigatamalamaccheratādiguṇavasena attano cāgaṃ anussarato neva tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti. Na dosa … pe … na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti. Ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti cāgaṃ ārabbhāti (a. ni.5.10) purimanayeneva vikkhambhitanīvaraṇassa ekakkhaṇe jhānaṅgāni uppajjanti. Cāgaguṇānaṃ pana gambhīratāya nānappakāracāgaguṇānussaraṇādhimuttatāya vā appanaṃ appatvā upacārappattameva jhānaṃ hoti. Tadetaṃ cāgaguṇānussaraṇavasena uppannattā cāgānussaticceva saṅkhaṃ gacchati.**  
> 160. One who wishes to develop the recollection of generosity, however, should be naturally intent on generosity and constantly devoted to giving and sharing. Alternatively, one starting the development of this meditation should make this undertaking: 'From now on, whenever a recipient is present, I will not eat even a single mouthful of food without first giving a gift.' Then, on that very day, after giving a gift according to his ability and strength to recipients of distinguished virtue, and having apprehended the sign in that, he should go into solitude and, having retreated, recollect his own generosity by way of its qualities such as being free from the stain of miserliness, in this way: 'How fortunate indeed for me! How well gained indeed is it for me, that among a generation obsessed with the stain of miserliness, I dwell with a mind cleansed of the stain of miserliness, freely generous, openhanded, delighting in relinquishment, devoted to charity, and delighting in giving and sharing!' 161. For one who is thus recollecting his own generosity by way of its special qualities of freedom from the stain of avarice and so forth, on that occasion his mind is not obsessed by greed. Nor is it obsessed by hate ... nor is it obsessed by delusion. On that occasion his mind is simply straight, being inspired by generosity (cāgaṃ ārabbha); then, in the same way as before, when the hindrances have been suppressed, the jhāna factors arise in a single moment. But owing to the profundity of the qualities of generosity, or because he is devoted to recollecting the various qualities of generosity, the jhāna is only access and does not reach absorption. And this is reckoned as 'recollection of generosity' (cāgānussati) because it arises by way of recollecting the qualities of generosity.  
>

### Textual References
* **Sutta**: *Mahānāma Sutta* (AN 6.10; A-vi 36 heading "10. Mahānāmasuttaṃ"), the recollection of generosity at A-vi 42.
* **Sutta**: *Dutiya Mahānāma Sutta* (AN 11.12; A-xi 104 heading "2. Dutiyamahānāmasuttaṃ"), where Mahānāma is told "attano cāgaṃ anussareyyāsi" (A-xi 109:1) and is to develop the recollection of the devas while walking, standing, sitting and lying down (A-xi 109:9).
