### Grammatical Analysis
**Sāvaka**: [m.] hearer; disciple; follower. Derived from root *su* (to hear, listen). Literally means "one who hears the Dhamma."

### Orthodox Definition
In the Theravāda tradition, *Sāvaka* designates a disciple of a Fully Enlightened Buddha. Unlike a *[Sammā-sambuddha](/glossary/samma-sambuddha/)* or a *[Paccekabuddha](/glossary/paccekabuddha/)*, who discover the path to [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/) independently without a teacher, a *Sāvaka* achieves awakening strictly by hearing, studying, and following the instructions laid down by a Buddha.

The term is formally divided into distinct categories of excellence:
1. *Ariya-sāvaka*: A noble disciple who has achieved at least Stream-entry (*[Sotāpanna](/glossary/sotapanna/)*).
2. *Mahā-sāvaka*: The great disciples (80 in number during Gotama's dispensation) who achieved Arahatship and possessed exceptional mastery, such as Venerable Mahākassapa or Venerable Ānanda.
3. *Agga-sāvaka*: The two chief disciples appointed by the Buddha. For Gotama Buddha, these were Venerable Sāriputta (foremost in wisdom) and Venerable Mahāmoggallāna (foremost in psychic power).

The awakening of a disciple is technically termed *Sāvaka-bodhi*. While their liberation from [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/) is identical to the Buddha's, their scope of omniscience and teaching capacity is vastly limited compared to the [Tathāgata](/glossary/tathagata/).

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahāpadāna Sutta* (DN 14) – Detailing the historical phenomenon that every Fully Enlightened Buddha appoints exactly two Chief Disciples.
* **Canonical**: *[Theragāthā](/glossary/theragatha/)* & *[Therīgāthā](/glossary/therigatha/)* – The inspired verses of the great male and female sāvakas upon reaching ultimate freedom.
* **Commentary**: *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* – Exploring the immense *[pāramī](/glossary/parami/)* required to become a chief or great disciple.
