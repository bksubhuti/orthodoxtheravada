### Grammatical Analysis
**Sāvaka**: [m.] hearer; disciple; follower. Derived from root *su* (to hear, listen). The Visuddhimagga explains: "They hear the Blessed One's advice and instruction attentively (*bhagavato ovādānusāsaniṃ sakkaccaṃ suṇantīti sāvakā*), thus they are disciples" (Vism-i 1335).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Theravāda tradition, *Sāvaka* designates a disciple of a Fully Enlightened Buddha. The Mūla draws the line between the Buddha and the disciple: the Tathāgata, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Awakened One "is the discoverer of a way that was unknown, the producer of a way that was unproduced, the declarer of a way that was undeclared; the knower of the way, the discerner of the way, the expert in the way; and his disciples now dwell following that way, becoming possessed of it afterwards" (*maggānugā ca, bhikkhave, etarahi sāvakā viharanti pacchāsamannāgatā*, S-iii 321, SN 22.58). A *sāvaka* thus attains the path by following the path found and taught by the Buddha.

The Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā divides the disciples into three kinds: chief disciples (*aggasāvakā*), great disciples (*mahāsāvakā*) and ordinary disciples (*pakatisāvakā*) (Th-a-ii 6587). It lists the great disciples, beginning with Aññāsikoṇḍañña and ending with Subhūti, among them Sāriputta, Mahāmoggallāna, Mahākassapa and Ānanda, and says that they are called "great" because of the greatness of their aspiration (*abhinīhārassa mahantabhāvato*); the two chief disciples are also counted among the great disciples (Th-a-ii 6588). The commentaries speak of "the eighty great disciples" (*asīti mahāsāvakā*, for example Mp-i 1555, Ap-a 713).

The two chief disciples (*sāvakayugaṃ aggaṃ bhaddayugaṃ*, "a chief and excellent pair of disciples") are named for each Buddha in the Mahāpadāna Sutta, and for Gotama they are Sāriputta and Moggallāna (D-ii 15). In the Etadagga chapter of the Aṅguttara, the Buddha names Sāriputta as foremost among his disciples of great wisdom (*mahāpaññānaṃ*) and Mahāmoggallāna as foremost of those with psychic power (*iddhimantānaṃ*) (A-i 165–166). The Mūla also speaks of the noble disciple (*ariyasāvako*), "well-taught, seeing the noble ones" (*sutavā ariyasāvako, ariyānaṃ dassāvī*, M-i 1578), who is distinct from the uninstructed worldling.

### Quote (Mūla: Saṃyutta Nikāya)
> **“Tathāgato, bhikkhave, arahaṃ sammāsambuddho anuppannassa maggassa uppādetā, asañjātassa maggassa sañjanetā, anakkhātassa maggassa akkhātā maggaññū, maggavidū, maggakovido; maggānugā ca, bhikkhave, etarahi sāvakā viharanti pacchāsamannāgatā. Ayaṃ kho, bhikkhave, viseso, ayaṃ adhippayāso, idaṃ nānākaraṇaṃ tathāgatassa arahato sammāsambuddhassa paññāvimuttena bhikkhunā” ti.**  
> “The Tathāgata, bhikkhus, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Awakened One, is the discoverer of a way that was unknown, the producer of a way that was unproduced, the declarer of a way that was undeclared; he is the Road-knower, the Road-discerner, the expert in the Path. And his disciples now dwell following that path, becoming possessed of it afterwards. This, bhikkhus, is the distinction, the variance, the difference between the Tathāgata, the Arahant, the Perfectly Self-Awakened One, and a bhikkhu liberated by wisdom.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya, the chief disciples)
> **Mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, etarahi sāriputtamoggallānaṃ nāma sāvakayugaṃ ahosi aggaṃ bhaddayugaṃ.**  
> For me now, monks, there was a pair of disciples named Sāriputta and Moggallāna, a chief and excellent pair.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Bhagavato ovādānusāsaniṃ sakkaccaṃ suṇantīti sāvakā. Sāvakānaṃ saṅgho sāvakasaṅgho , sīladiṭṭhisāmaññatāya saṅghātabhāvamāpanno sāvakasamūhoti attho.**  
> They hear the Blessed One’s advice and instruction attentively, thus they are disciples (sāvakā). The group of disciples is the Saṅgha of the disciples (sāvakasaṅgho); the meaning is that they are an assembly of disciples who have achieved unity through having virtue and view in common.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VII, Saṅghānussatikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=212&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=QmhhZ2F2YXRvIG92xIFkxIFudXPEgXNhbmnhuYMgc2Fra2FjY2HhuYMgc3XhuYdhbnTEq3RpIHPEgXZha8SBLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Aggasāvakā, mahāsāvakā, pakatisāvakāti tividhā. Kasmā pana te eva therā “mahāsāvakā” ti vuccantīti? Abhinīhārassa mahantabhāvato. Tathā hi dve aggasāvakāpi mahāsāvakesu antogadhā. Te hi sāvakapāramīñāṇassa matthakappattiyā sāvakesu aggadhammādhigamena aggaṭṭhāne ṭhitāpi abhinīhāramahantatāsāmaññena “mahāsāvakā” tipi vuccanti.**  
> There are three kinds: chief disciples, great disciples, and ordinary disciples. But why are only those Elders called “great disciples”? Due to the greatness of their aspiration. Because such is the case, even the two chief disciples are included among the great disciples. Indeed, even though they are established in the foremost place among disciples, having reached the culmination of a disciple’s perfection-knowledge by the attainment of the highest Dhamma, they are also called “great disciples” by the commonality of the greatness of aspiration.  
>  
> — *Theragāthā-aṭṭhakathā* · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_08_02&page_number=541&name=therag%C4%81th%C4%81a%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=QWdnYXPEgXZha8SBLCBtYWjEgXPEgXZha8SBLCBwYWthdGlzxIF2YWvEgXRpIHRpdmlkaMSBLg%3D%3D)
