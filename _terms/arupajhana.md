### Grammatical Analysis
**Arūpajhāna**: [nt.] immaterial absorption, a compound of *arūpa* (immaterial) + *[jhāna](/glossary/jhana/)* (absorption). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī uses the word in its heading "*Cattāri arūpajhānāni soḷasakkhattukāni*", "the four immaterial jhānas, sixteen times" (Dhs 735:1). The Visuddhimagga calls the same four *āruppā*, "immaterial states" (Vism-i 775:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *arūpajhānas* are the four immaterial absorptions: the dimension of infinite space (*[ākāsānañcāyatana](/glossary/akasanancayatana/)*), of infinite consciousness (*[viññāṇañcāyatana](/glossary/vinnanancayatana/)*), of nothingness (*[ākiñcaññāyatana](/glossary/akincannayatana/)*) and of neither perception nor non-perception (*[nevasaññānāsaññāyatana](/glossary/nevasannanasannayatana/)*) (Vism-i 775:1).

The Dhammasaṅgaṇī presents each as a wholesome consciousness of the one who "develops the path for rebirth in the immaterial realm", with "the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, non-attention to perceptions of diversity", and describes each as "having attained the fourth jhāna" accompanied by equanimity (Dhs 736:2, 737:2). The Ṭīkā says accordingly that the fourfold immaterial absorption is included in the fourth jhāna (Sp-t-i-a 1419:1).

The Visuddhimagga explains how the meditator comes to the first of them: although by the fourth fine-material jhāna he has already surmounted the physical body, he also wishes to surmount the kasiṇa materiality, since that is its counterpart (Vism-i 1982:1-1983:1). The Buddha, before his awakening, reached the dimension of nothingness under Āḷāra Kālāma and the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception under Uddaka Rāmaputta, and judged that this Dhamma "does not lead to disenchantment, to dispassion, to cessation, to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to Nibbāna" (M-i 920:9, 927:9).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Cattāri arūpajhānāni soḷasakkhattukāni 265. Katame dhammā kusalā? Yasmiṃ samaye arūpūpapattiyā maggaṃ bhāveti sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā ākāsānañcāyatanasaññāsahagataṃ sukhassa ca pahānā … pe … catutthaṃ jhānaṃ upasampajja viharati upekkhāsahagataṃ, tasmiṃ samaye phasso hoti … pe … avikkhepo hoti … pe … ime dhammā kusalā.**  
> The four immaterial jhānas are sixteen-fold. 265. What mental phenomena are wholesome? At the time when one develops the path for rebirth in the immaterial realm, with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, by non-attention to perceptions of diversity, having attained the fourth jhāna accompanied by the perception of the dimension of infinite space, having abandoned pleasure… and dwells accompanied by equanimity. At that time contact exists… non-distraction exists… these mental phenomena are wholesome.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Arūpāvacarakusala, §265) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=68&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Q2F0dMSBcmkgYXLFq3BhamjEgW7EgW5pIFthcsWrcGFqamjEgW7EgW5pIChzxKsuIHN5xIEuKV0gc2%2FhuLdhc2Fra2hhdHR1a8SBbmk%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ, viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ, ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ, nevasaññānāsaññāyatananti ime cattāro āruppā.**  
> The dimension of infinite space, the dimension of infinite consciousness, the dimension of nothingness, and the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception—these are the four immaterial states (āruppa).  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Sāratthadīpanī)
> **Rūpavirāgabhāvanāvasena pavattaṃ catubbidhampi arūpajjhānaṃ catutthajjhānasaṅgahamevāti āha “cattāri jhānānī”ti.**  
> Regarding 'four absorptions' (cattāri jhānāni), he said this because even the fourfold formless absorption, when occurring by way of the development of the fading of form, is included within the fourth absorption.  
>
