### Grammatical Analysis
**Āhārapaccaya**: [m.] Nutriment condition; Food condition. Formed by *āhāra* (food, nourishment, support) + *paccaya* (condition).

### Orthodox Definition
*Āhāra-paccaya* is the fifteenth of the 24 Conditions. The Paṭṭhāna states it: "Kabaḷīkāro āhāro imassa kāyassa āhārapaccayena paccayo. Arūpino āhārā sampayuttakānaṃ dhammānaṃ taṃsamuṭṭhānānañca rūpānaṃ āhārapaccayena paccayo" (Paṭṭh-i 48:1-2). The Visuddhimagga defines it: "the four nutriments that help material and immaterial phenomena in the sense of support are the nutriment condition" (*rūpārūpānaṃ upatthambhakaṭṭhena upakārakā cattāro āhārā āhārapaccayo*, Vism-ii 937:1), and gives physical food the characteristic of nutritive essence (*ojālakkhaṇo*), the function of feeding materiality (*rūpāharaṇaraso*) and the manifestation of sustaining (*upatthambhanapaccupaṭṭhāno*) (Vism-ii 356:1).

In Theravāda doctrine, there are four nutriments, as in the Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhaya Sutta (M-i 1314:1-3):
1. **Physical Edible Food** (*Kabaḷīkārāhāra*): The physical nutrients that sustain the material body.
2. **Contact** (*[Phassa](/glossary/phassa/)*): The mental nutriment that sustains feelings (pleasure, pain, neutral).
3. **Mental Volition** (*Manosañcetanā*): The [kamma](/glossary/kamma/)/volition that sustains continued rebirth and existence.
4. **Consciousness** (*[Viññāṇa](/glossary/vinnana/)*): The mind itself, which sustains the co-nascent mind and body (*[nāmarūpa](/glossary/namarupa/)*).

The sub-commentary explains that the operational core of nutriment lies in its capacity to draw forth, generate, or sustain its specific fruit for either genesis or continued existence. While physical food functions through its essential nutritive essence (*ojā*), the mental nutriments operate by structurally driving experience: contact actively links onto an object through the mode of touching, volition integrates associated states with the object, and consciousness cognizes specifically by conceiving and constructing the framework for ongoing rebirth.

### Quote
> **Tattha āharatīti āhārapaccayasaṅkhātena uppattiyā, ṭhitiyā vā paccayabhāvena attano phalaṃ āneti nibbatteti pavatteti cāti attho. Kabaḷaṃ karīyatīti kabaḷīkāro, vatthuvasena cetaṃ vuttaṃ, lakkhaṇato pana ojālakkhaṇo veditabbo, kabaḷīkāro ca so yathāvuttenatthena āhāro cāti kabaḷīkārāhāro. Esa nayo sesesupi. Phusatīti phasso. Ayaṃ hi arūpadhammopi samāno ārammaṇe phusanākāreneva pavattati. Tathā hi so phusanalakkhaṇoti vuccati. Cetayatīti cetanā, attano sampayuttadhammehi saddhiṃ ārammaṇe abhisandahatīti attho, manosannissitā cetanā manosañcetanā. Upapattiparikappanavasena vijānātīti viññāṇaṃ. Evamettha sāmaññatthato, visesatthato ca āhārā veditabbā.**  
> Herein, “it brings on” (āharati) means that by way of being a condition called nutriment, it brings, produces, or maintains its fruit by way of the condition for arising or remaining. That which is made into a mouthful is “nutriment” (kabaḷīkāra); this was spoken by way of subject-matter, but by way of characteristic, it should be known as nutriment with the characteristic of essence; it is what is made into a mouthful, and because it brings by the meaning stated, it is nutriment. This method applies to the rest as well. “Touching” (phusati) is why it is called “contact” (phasso). For this contact, even though it is a mental phenomenon, proceeds only by the mode of touching in the object. That is why it is called contact, having the characteristic of touching. “Intending” (cetayati) is why it is called “volition” (cetanā); it connects along with associated phenomena in the object—this is the meaning. Volition relying on the mind is “mental intention” (manosañcetanā). “Cognizing” (vijānātī) by way of determining rebirth is “consciousness” (viññāṇaṃ). Thus, here, the nutriments should be known from their common meaning and distinctive meaning.  
>  
> — *Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā* (Āhārepaṭikkūlabhāvanāvaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_03&page_number=413&name=visuddhimaggamah%C4%81%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIMSBaGFyYXTEq3RpIMSBaMSBcmFwYWNjYXlhc2HhuYVraMSBdGVuYSB1cHBhdHRpecSBLCDhua1oaXRpecSBIHbEgQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhaya Sutta* (MN 38; heading "8. Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhayasuttaṃ", M-i 1273:1) – the four nutriments: physical food, contact, mental volition and consciousness (M-i 1314:1-3). The same four are in the SN 12 Āhāra Sutta (S-ii 87:1 and parallels).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (Paṭṭh-i 48:1-2; the list at Paṭṭh-i 8:1 and Abhidh-s 709:1 puts it fifteenth) – "Kabaḷīkāro āhāro imassa kāyassa āhārapaccayena paccayo. Arūpino āhārā sampayuttakānaṃ dhammānaṃ taṃsamuṭṭhānānañca rūpānaṃ āhārapaccayena paccayo."
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 937:1-5; the physical nutriment at Vism-ii 356:1) and the *Paṭṭhānaṭṭhakathā* (Paṭṭh-a 1935:1-3).
* **Handbook**: *[Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (Abhidh-s 729:1) – nutriment condition is of two kinds: physical food for this body, and the immaterial nutriments for co-arisen mind and matter.
* **Sub-commentary**: *Visuddhimagga-mahāṭīkā* (*Āhārepaṭikkūlabhāvanāvaṇṇanā*, Vism-mht-i 1470:1-11) – the derivation of *āhāra* ("it brings on") and the sense of each of the four.
