### Grammatical Analysis
**Bodhisatta**: [m.] a being intent on awakening, a compound of *bodhi* (awakening) + *satta* (being). The Aṭṭhakathā gives the senses: "*bodhisattasseva satoti bujjhanakasattasseva sammāsambodhiṃ adhigantuṃ arahasattasseva sato, bodhiyā vā sattasseva laggasseva sato*", "while he was still a being who was to awaken, a being worthy to attain perfect self-awakening, or a being attached to awakening" (Ps-i 588:3). Another commentary: "here *bodhi* means knowledge; one who has awakening is a *bodhisatta*, that is, one who has knowledge, who is wise" (Spk-ii 76:5).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla the word is used by the Buddha of himself before his awakening: "Monks, before my awakening, while I was still unawakened, still a *bodhisatta*, this thought occurred to me: 'What is the gratification in the world, what is the danger, what is the escape?'" (A-iii 1032:1-2). The Aṭṭhakathā explains "*anabhisambuddhassa*" as one who had not yet penetrated the four truths and "*pubbeva sambodhā*" as before attaining the noble path (Ps-i 588:1-2).

The Aṭṭhakathā shows the bodhisatta's career: having made his resolve (*abhinīhāra*) at the feet of Dīpaṅkara, he searched for the qualities that make a Buddha, saw the perfection of giving first and the others after it, and fulfilled them down to his existence as Vessantara (As 759:1; Bv-a 3875:1). The Sāratthappakāsinī says that while the bodhisatta was fulfilling the perfections he was still subject to lust, hatred and delusion, and gave away his head, his eyes, his son and his wife to beggars (Spk-i 902:3).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi – ‘ ko nu kho loke assādo, ko ādīnavo, kiṃ nissaraṇan ’ ti?**  
> “Monks, before my enlightenment, while I was still a bodhisatta (a being intent on awakening) and not yet fully awakened, this thought occurred to me: ‘What is the gratification in the world, what is the danger, and what is the escape?’  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Papañcasūdanī)
> **Tattha pubbeva sambodhāti sambodhato pubbeva, ariyamaggappattito aparabhāgeyevāti vuttaṃ hoti. Anabhisambuddhassāti appaṭividdhacatusaccassa. Bodhisattasseva satoti bujjhanakasattasseva sammāsambodhiṃ adhigantuṃ arahasattasseva sato, bodhiyā vā sattasseva laggasseva sato.**  
> Therein, pubbeva sambodhā means before the full awakening, before attaining the Noble Path. Anabhisambuddhassā means to one who has not pierced the four truths. Bodhisattasseva sato means while he was still a being worthy to attain perfect enlightenment, or while he was still a being bound to enlightenment.  
>  
> — Papañcasūdanī (Ps-i 588) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ma_01_01&page_number=117&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81saa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHB1YmJldmEgc2FtYm9kaMSBdGkgc2FtYm9kaGF0byBwdWJiZXZhLCBhcml5YW1hZ2dhcHBhdHRpdG8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Ime aṭṭha dhamme samodhānetvā dīpaṅkarapādamūle katābhinīhārena “handa buddhakare dhamme vicināmi ito cito” ti ussāhaṃ katvā “vicinanto tadādakkhiṃ, paṭhamaṃ dānapāramin” ti dānapāramitādayo buddhakarā dhammā diṭṭhā, te pūrento yāva vessantarattabhāvā āgami.**  
> Having combined these eight factors, he made his resolve at the feet of Dīpaṅkara, and with the effort: “Come, I will search hither and thither for the qualities that lead to Buddhahood,” he searched and beheld them, starting with: “Searching then, I saw the first: the perfection of giving.” Thus, the qualities that lead to Buddhahood, beginning with the perfection of giving, were seen; and fulfilling them, he arrived at his existence as Vessantara.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 759) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=71&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=SW1lIGHhua3hua1oYSBkaGFtbWUgc2Ftb2RoxIFuZXR2xIEgZMSrcGHhuYVrYXJhcMSBZGFtxatsZSBrYXTEgWJoaW7Eq2jEgXJlbmE%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sāratthappakāsinī)
> **Pāramīpūraṇakāle pana sarāgasadosasamohasseva sato āgatāgatānaṃ yācakānaṃ alaṅkatapaṭiyattaṃ sīsaṃ kantitvā galalohitaṃ nīharitvā suañjitāni akkhīni uppāṭetvā kulavaṃsappadīpaṃ puttaṃ manāpacāriniṃ bhariyanti evamādīni dentassa aññāni ca khantivādisadisesu attabhāvesu chejjabhejjādīni pāpuṇantassa āgamanīyapaṭipadaṃ sandhāyetaṃ vuttaṃ.**  
> But at the time of fulfilling the perfections, even while having passion, hatred, and delusion, the Bodhisatta was one who gave to approaching beggars—with a head adorned and well-prepared, cut off, dripping blood, eyes plucked out, having given away his son, the light of the lineage, and his wife, a virtuous companion—and in other existences like that of the patience-sage, he experienced being cut and broken; it is with reference to this strenuous practice that this was said.  
>  
> — [Sāratthappakāsinī](/glossary/saratthappakasini/) (Sagāthāvagga-aṭṭhakathā, Spk-i 902) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_sa_01&page_number=180&name=sag%C4%81th%C4%81vaggaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=UMSBcmFtxKtwxatyYeG5h2FrxIFsZSBwYW5hIHNhcsSBZ2FzYWRvc2FzYW1vaGFzc2V2YSBzYXRvIMSBZ2F0xIFnYXTEgW5h4bmDIHnEgWNha8SBbmHhuYM%3D)
