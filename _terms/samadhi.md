### Grammatical Analysis
**Samādhi**: [m.] concentration; one-pointedness of mind. The Visuddhimagga: "it is *samādhi* in the sense of *samādhāna*"; *samādhāna* is "the even and right placing (*samaṃ sammā ca ādhānaṃ*) of consciousness and its concomitants on a single object" (Vism-i 653).

### Orthodox Definition
The Mūla (MN 44) defines it: "whatever is one-pointedness of mind (*cittassa ekaggatā*), that is *samādhi*; the four foundations of mindfulness are its signs (*nimitta*), the four right efforts its requisites (*parikkhāra*)". In the Dhammasaṅgaṇī, *cittassekaggatā* is explained as stability of mind, non-scattering, non-distraction, tranquillity (*samatha*), the faculty and the power of concentration, and right concentration (Dhs 401).

The Visuddhimagga gives the characteristic (*lakkhaṇa*), function (*rasa*), manifestation (*paccupaṭṭhāna*) and footing (*padaṭṭhāna*): "concentration has non-distraction as its characteristic (*avikkhepalakkhaṇo*), the destruction of distraction as its function (*vikkhepaviddhaṃsanaraso*), non-wavering as its manifestation (*avikampanapaccupaṭṭhāno*); happiness is its footing, since 'the mind of one who is happy becomes concentrated'" (Vism-i 654).

In the Eightfold Path *samādhi* is the third division (MN 44: right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration are included in the aggregate of concentration). The Visuddhimagga names three kinds of concentration: momentary (*khaṇika*), access (*upacāra*) and absorption (*appanā*) (Vism-i 944); the Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha speaks of development by "preliminary concentration" (*parikammasamādhi*, para. 30). In access concentration "the mind becomes concentrated by the abandonment of the hindrances" and "the factors are not strong"; in absorption "the factors are strong", and the mind stays for a whole night or day (Vism-i 849-850).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Yā kho, āvuso visākha, cittassa ekaggatā ayaṃ samādhi; cattāro satipaṭṭhānā samādhinimittā; cattāro sammappadhānā samādhiparikkhārā. Yā tesaṃyeva dhammānaṃ āsevanā bhāvanā bahulīkammaṃ, ayaṃ ettha samādhibhāvanā” ti.**  
> “Whatever is one-pointedness of mind, friend Visākha, that is concentration. The four ways of attending to mindfulness are the causes of concentration. The four right efforts are the accessories to concentration. Whatever repetition, development, and cultivation there is of these same qualities—this is the development of concentration herein.”  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 44, Cūḷavedalla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=375&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFnEgSBraG8sIMSBdnVzbyB2aXPEgWtoYSwgY2l0dGFzc2E%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamā tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti? Yā tasmiṃ samaye cittassa ṭhiti saṇṭhiti avaṭṭhiti avisāhāro avikkhepo avisāhaṭamānasatā samatho samādhindriyaṃ samādhibalaṃ sammāsamādhi – ayaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittassekaggatā hoti.**  
> 11. What is unification of mind at that time? Whatever stability of mind (cittassa ṭhiti), firm stability, established stability, non-scattering, non-distraction, non-scatteredness of mind, tranquility, faculty of concentration, the power of concentration, or right concentration exists at that time—this is unification of mind at that time.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (Cittuppādakaṇḍa, para. 11) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=19&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEuIEthdGFtxIEgdGFzbWnhuYMgc2FtYXllIGNpdHRhc3Nla2FnZ2F0xIEgaG90aT8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga III)
> **Kenaṭṭhena samādhīti samādhānaṭṭhena samādhi. Kimidaṃ samādhānaṃ nāma? Ekārammaṇe cittacetasikānaṃ samaṃ sammā ca ādhānaṃ, ṭhapananti vuttaṃ hoti. Tasmā yassa dhammassānubhāvena ekārammaṇe cittacetasikā samaṃ sammā ca avikkhipamānā avippakiṇṇā ca hutvā tiṭṭhanti, idaṃ samādhānanti veditabbaṃ.**  
> Regarding ‘In what sense is it concentration?’: it is concentration in the sense of ‘holding together’. What is this ‘holding together’? It is the placing—that is to say, the establishing—of consciousness and its concomitants evenly and rightly on a single object. Therefore, the state by whose power consciousness and its concomitants remain on a single object evenly and rightly, being undistracted and unscattered, should be understood as ‘holding together’.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (III, Vism-i 653) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=81&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=S2VuYeG5reG5rWhlbmEgc2FtxIFkaMSrdGkgc2FtxIFkaMSBbmHhua3hua1oZW5hIHNhbcSBZGhpLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga III, lakkhaṇa etc.)
> **Kānassa lakkhaṇarasapaccupaṭṭhānapadaṭṭhānānīti ettha pana avikkhepalakkhaṇo samādhi, vikkhepaviddhaṃsanaraso, avikampanapaccupaṭṭhāno. “Sukhino cittaṃ samādhiyatī” ti vacanato pana sukhamassa padaṭṭhānaṃ.**  
> Regarding ‘What are its characteristic, function, manifestation, and footing?’: here, concentration has non-distraction as its characteristic, the elimination of distraction as its function, and non-wavering as its manifestation. And because of the statement, ‘The mind of one who is happy becomes concentrated,’ happiness is its footing.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (III, Vism-i 654) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=81&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=S8SBbmFzc2EgbGFra2hh4bmHYXJhc2FwYWNjdXBh4bmt4bmtaMSBbmFwYWRh4bmt4bmtaMSBbsSBbsSrdGkgZXR0aGEgcGFuYSBhdmlra2hlcGFsYWtraGHhuYdvIHNhbcSBZGhpLA%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga IV, access and absorption)
> **Duvidho hi samādhi upacārasamādhi ca appanāsamādhi ca. Dvīhākārehi cittaṃ samādhiyati upacārabhūmiyaṃ vā paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ vā. Tattha upacārabhūmiyaṃ nīvaraṇappahānena cittaṃ samāhitaṃ hoti. Paṭilābhabhūmiyaṃ aṅgapātubhāvena.**  
> For concentration is of two kinds: access concentration and absorption concentration. In two ways the mind becomes concentrated: either on the plane of access or on the plane of obtainment. Herein, on the plane of access, the mind becomes concentrated by the abandonment of the hindrances. And on the plane of obtainment, by the manifestation of the factors.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (IV, Vism-i 849) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=123&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=RHV2aWRobyBoaSBzYW3EgWRoaSB1cGFjxIFyYXNhbcSBZGhpIGNhIGFwcGFuxIFzYW3EgWRoaQ%3D%3D)
