### Grammatical Analysis
**Macchariya**: [nt.] stinginess; avarice; miserliness. The commentaries derive it from *maccharabhāva* / *maccherabhāva*: "the state of being miserly is *macchariya*" (*Maccharabhāvo macchariyaṃ*, Vism XIV, 487; *Maccherabhāvo macchariyaṃ*, As on the ninth citta).

### Orthodox Definition
The *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§1127) defines it as "miserliness, the act of being a miser, the state of being a miser, avarice, harshness and contraction, closed-fistedness of mind", and calls it the fetter of stinginess (*macchariyasaṃyojana*). The Mūla (AN 9.69; Dhs §1127) names five kinds:
1. *Āvāsa-macchariya*: stinginess with dwellings.
2. *Kula-macchariya*: stinginess with families.
3. *Lābha-macchariya*: stinginess with gains.
4. *Vaṇṇa-macchariya*: stinginess with praise.
5. *Dhamma-macchariya*: stinginess as to the *[Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/)*.

In the *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (As 1811, 1815) *macchariya* is one of the inconstant factors (*aniyatayevāpanakā*) that arise, one at a time with *issā* or *kukkucca*, in the ninth citta, the mind accompanied by mental pain (*domanassa*) and associated with aversion (*paṭigha*). The *Atthasālinī* and the *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* give the same definition by characteristic and so on:
* **Lakkhaṇa** (Characteristic): concealing one's own possessions, whether gained or to be gained (*laddhānaṃ vā labhitabbānaṃ vā attano sampattīnaṃ nigūhanalakkhaṇaṃ*).
* **Rasa** (Function): not bearing that others share those same possessions (*tāsaṃyeva parehi sādhāraṇabhāvaakkhamanarasaṃ*).
* **Paccupaṭṭhāna** (Manifestation): shrinking, or bitterness and closed-fistedness (*saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, kaṭukañcukatāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ vā*).
* **Padaṭṭhāna** (Proximate Cause): one's own possessions (*attasampattipadaṭṭhānaṃ*); it should be seen as a deformity of the mind (*cetaso virūpabhāvo*).

The Mūla also shows its danger: "That which the miser fears when he does not give is the very danger that comes to the non-giver" (SN 1.32, Maccharī Sutta).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, macchariyāni. Katamāni pañca? Āvāsamacchariyaṃ, kulamacchariyaṃ, lābhamacchariyaṃ, vaṇṇamacchariyaṃ, dhammamacchariyaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañca macchariyāni.**  
> “Mendicants, there are these five kinds of stinginess. What five? Stinginess with dwellings, stinginess with families, stinginess with gains, stinginess with praise, stinginess as to the Dhamma— these indeed, mendicants, are the five kinds of stinginess.  
>

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Tattha katamaṃ macchariyasaṃyojanaṃ? Pañca macchariyāni – āvāsamacchariyaṃ, kulamacchariyaṃ, lābhamacchariyaṃ, vaṇṇamacchariyaṃ, dhammamacchariyaṃ. Yaṃ evarūpaṃ maccheraṃ maccharāyanā maccharāyitattaṃ vevicchaṃ kadariyaṃ kaṭukañcukatā aggahitattaṃ cittassa – idaṃ vuccati macchariyasaṃyojanaṃ.**  
> 1127. Which among these is the fetter of stinginess? There are five forms of stinginess: stinginess as to one’s monastery, stinginess as to one’s family, stinginess as to one’s gains, stinginess as to one’s status, and stinginess as to the Dhamma. Whatever such miserliness, act of being a miser, state of being a miser, avarice, miser, harshness and contraction, or closed-fistedness of mind there is — this is called the fetter of stinginess.  
>  
> — [Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/) (§1127, macchariyasaṃyojana) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_01&page_number=226&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABp%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEyNy4gVGF0dGhhIGthdGFtYeG5gyBtYWNjaGFyaXlhc2HhuYN5b2phbmHhuYM%2F)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Maccherabhāvo ‘ macchariyaṃ ’. Taṃ laddhānaṃ vā labhitabbānaṃ vā attano sampattīnaṃ nigūhanalakkhaṇaṃ, tāsaṃyeva parehi sādhāraṇabhāvaakkhamanarasaṃ, saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ kaṭukañcukatāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ vā, attasampattipadaṭṭhānaṃ. Cetaso virūpabhāvoti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> The state of a miser (maccherabhāvo) is called ‘avarice’ (macchariyaṃ). That (taṃ) (avarice) has the characteristic of concealing (nigūhanalakkhaṇaṃ) one’s own (attano) possessions (sampattīnaṃ), whether gained or to be gained; its function is intolerance of sharing (sādhāraṇabhāvaakkhamanarasaṃ) those very possessions by others (parehi); its manifestation is contraction/withholding (saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ) or its manifestation is bitterness and miserliness (kaṭukañcukatāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ); and its proximate cause is one’s own possessions (attasampattipadaṭṭhānaṃ). It should be understood (daṭṭhabbaṃ) as the mind’s perversion (cetaso virūpabhāvo).  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on the ninth citta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=299&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TWFjY2hlcmFiaMSBdm8g4oCYIG1hY2NoYXJpeWHhuYMg4oCZLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Maccharabhāvo macchariyaṃ. Taṃ laddhānaṃ vā labhitabbānaṃ vā attano sampattīnaṃ nigūhanalakkhaṇaṃ, tāsaṃyeva parehi sādhāraṇabhāvaakkhamanarasaṃ, saṅkocanapaccupaṭṭhānaṃ, kaṭukañcukatāpaccupaṭṭhānaṃ vā, attasampattipadaṭṭhānaṃ, cetaso virūpabhāvoti daṭṭhabbaṃ.**  
> 487. The state of being stingy is avarice (macchariyaṃ). It has the characteristic of concealing one's own success, whether already obtained or yet to be obtained; the function of not bearing for others to share in that same success; is manifested as shrinking or as bitterness; has one's own success as its proximate cause; and should be seen as a deformity of the mind.  
>  
> — [Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/) (XIV, 487) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=100&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=NDg3LiBNYWNjaGFyYWJoxIF2byBtYWNjaGFyaXlh4bmDLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Macchariya Sutta* (AN 9.69) – the five kinds of stinginess; *Maccharī Sutta* (SN 1.32) – the miser who does not give ("Yasseva bhīto na dadāti maccharī").
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (§1127) – the fetter of stinginess, with the five kinds and the definition.
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (on the ninth citta) and *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (XIV, 487) – the definition by characteristic, function, manifestation and proximate cause.
