### Grammatical Analysis
**Samantapāsādikā**: [f.] The All-Pleasing; "Inspiring in All Respects". The Ṭīkā glosses: *samantato sabbaso pasādaṃ janetīti samantapāsādikā*, "it generates confidence on all sides, in every way" (Sp-t-i-a 1654). The Aṭṭhakathā itself says the name is justified because "for those who look carefully, nothing here is seen that is displeasing" (Sp-i 855-856).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Samantapāsādikā* is the commentary (*[Aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/atthakatha/)*) on the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) Piṭaka. It states its own origin. The earlier explanation had been composed in the language of the island of Sīhaḷa and so "did not benefit the community of monks in other lands" (Sp-i 33-36); therefore, at the request of the Elder Buddhasiri, the author rendered it "in accordance with the method of the Pāḷi" (Sp-i 37-39), taking "the Great Commentary (*mahāaṭṭhakathā*) as its body", and likewise the *Mahāpaccarī* and the *Kurundī* and the other renowned commentaries (Sp-i 41-44), and incorporating the teachings of the Elders (Sp-i 46-47).

The colophon names the author: "this Samantapāsādikā, the explanation of the Vinaya, was made by the elder who received the name Buddhaghosa from his teachers, an ornament to the lineage of the Elders who dwell in the Mahāvihāra" (*mahāvihāravāsīnaṃ vaṃsālaṅkārabhūtena … buddhaghosoti garūhi gahitanāmadheyyena therena katā ayaṃ samantapāsādikā nāma vinayasaṃvaṇṇanā*, Sp-v 655). The closing verses give the grounds for the name: the lineage of teachers (*ācariyaparamparā*); showing the divisions of the origin-stories and subject matter; avoiding other doctrines; purity of its own tradition; clarification of the wording; the meaning of the words and the method of applying the Pāḷi; determination of the training rules; and showing the methods of the Vibhaṅga (Sp-i 850-858).

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: the name explained)
> **Tatridaṃ samantapāsādikāya samantapāsādikattasmiṃ – Ācariyaparamparato, nidānavatthuppabhedadīpanato; Parasamayavivajjanato, sakasamayavisuddhito ceva. Byañjanaparisodhanato, padatthato pāḷiyojanakkamato; Sikkhāpadanicchayato, vibhaṅganayabhedadassanato. Sampassataṃ na dissati, kiñci apāsādikaṃ yato ettha; Viññūnamayaṃ tasmā, samantapāsādikātveva. Saṃvaṇṇanā pavattā, vinayassa vineyyadamanakusalena; Vuttassa lokanāthena, lokamanukampamānenāti.**  
> Here, regarding the status of the Samantapāsādikā as being "Inspiring in All Respects": Because of the lineage of teachers, and the explanation of the various types of origins and subject matter; Because of the rejection of other doctrines, and the purification of one's own doctrine; Because of the refinement of phrasing, and the explanation of the meaning of terms and the sequence of Pāli constructions; Because of the determination of training rules, and the showing of the various methods of analysis; Since no displeasing element is seen here by those who see into the truth; Therefore, for the wise, it is truly “Inspiring in All Respects.” This explanation of the Vinaya proceeds, which was taught by the Lord of the World, skilled in taming those to be trained, And who, out of compassion for the world, spoke it.  
>  
> — [Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/) (Verañjakaṇḍavaṇṇanā, closing verses, Sp-i 850-858) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_vi_01_01&page_number=168&name=p%C4%81r%C4%81jikaka%E1%B9%87%E1%B8%8Daa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGF0cmlkYeG5gyBzYW1hbnRhcMSBc8SBZGlrxIF5YSBzYW1hbnRhcMSBc8SBZGlrYXR0YXNtaeG5gyDigJM%3D)

> This Vinaya explanation called Samantapāsādikā was made by the Elder, who is known by the teachers by the name Buddhaghosa, who is adorned with perfectly pure faith, wisdom, and energy; who is possessed of a collection of virtues such as ethical conduct, uprightness, and gentleness; who is capable of delving into the dense forests of his own doctrine and other doctrines; who is possessed of analytical wisdom; who has the diverse categories of the Tipiṭaka and their Aṭṭhakathā teachings; who is possessed of the radiance of unobstructed knowledge; who is a great grammarian; who is possessed of the charm of sweet and noble speech produced by the excellence of his articulation; who speaks what is fitting and right; who is the best of debaters; who is a great poet; who is possessed of the diverse analytical knowledges; who is adorned with virtues such as the six higher knowledges; who has a mind well-established in the noble human states; who is a lamp in the lineage of Elders; and who is a decoration to the lineage of the Mahāvihāra Elders, who have widely pure wisdom –  
>

### Textual References
* **Subject Matter**: Comments comprehensively on the [Suttavibhaṅga](/glossary/suttavibhanga/), the [Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/), and the [Parivāra](/glossary/parivara/).
* **Historical Significance**: Its introduction (*Bāhiranidānakathā*, Sp-i 69-489) contains the accounts of the First Council (*Paṭhamamahāsaṅgītikathā*, ending Sp-i 222), the Second, and the Third (*Tatiyasaṅgītikathā*, Sp-i 259-308), and the arrival of the Elder Mahinda in the island (Sp-i 285, 396).
* **Sub-commentary**: *Sāratthadīpanī* (Sp-t-i-a 1654) – on the name.
