### Grammatical Analysis
**[Citta](/glossary/citta/)**: [nt.] consciousness; mind; active thought. (See main entry for *[Citta](/glossary/citta/)*).

### Orthodox Definition
While *[Citta](/glossary/citta/)* broadly refers to consciousness, in the specific context of the 37 wings to awakening, it designates the **Third Base of Spiritual Power** (*Cittasamādhi-padhānasaṅkhāra*).

When *[Citta](/glossary/citta/)* operates as an *[iddhipāda](/glossary/iddhipada/)*, it means that the practitioner's natural, dominant mental strength is neither sheer will (*[chanda](/glossary/chanda/)*), heroic physical energy (*[viriya](/glossary/viriya/)*), nor sharp analytical intellect (*[vīmaṃsā](/glossary/vimamsa/)*). Instead, their success is driven by an overwhelmingly powerful, concentrated, and unwavering focus of the mind itself. 

The Vibhaṅga defines it thus: if a monk, making *citta* the leader (*adhipati*), gains concentration and one-pointedness of mind, this is called *cittasamādhi*; joined with the four right efforts (*padhānasaṅkhārā*) it is called *cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhāra*. The Visuddhimagga adds that the mundane iddhipādas are those gained by making *chanda* and the others dominant (*adhipati*), while the four as such are supramundane.

### Quote
> **Kathañca bhikkhu cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti? Cittaṃ ce bhikkhu adhipatiṃ karitvā labhati samādhiṃ labhati cittassekaggataṃ – ayaṃ vuccati “cittasamādhi”.**  
> 438. And how (kathañca) does a bhikkhu (bhikkhu) develop (bhāveti) the basis of psychic power that has immersion due to cognizance and the volitional formation to endeavour (cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ)? If (ce) a bhikkhu (bhikkhu), having taken mind as his authority (cittaṃ adhipatiṃ karitvā), obtains concentration (labhati samādhiṃ) and obtains one-pointedness of mind (labhati cittassekaggataṃ) — this (ayaṃ) is called ‘concentration due to mind’ (cittasamādhi).  
>
