### Grammatical Analysis
**Avigatapaccaya**: [m.] Non-disappearance condition, a compound of *avigata* (not disappeared; *a* + *vigata*, gone away) + *paccaya* (condition). The Aṭṭhakathā: "*atthipaccayadhammā eva avigatabhāvena upakārakattā avigatapaccayoti veditabbā*", "the very states of the presence condition, because they are helpful by way of the state of non-disappearance, should be understood as the non-disappearance condition" (Paṭṭh-a 1944:1).

### Orthodox Definition
*Avigata-paccaya* is the twenty-fourth and last of the conditions of the *[Paṭṭhāna](/glossary/patthana/)* (Paṭṭh-i 61:1; the list at Paṭṭh-i 8:1). The Mūla states it with the same examples as the presence condition: the four immaterial aggregates are a condition for one another, the four great elements for one another, name-and-form at the moment of conception, consciousness and mental factors for mind-born matter, and the great elements for derived matter (Paṭṭh-i 61:1-5).

The Aṭṭhakathā says that the states of the presence condition are, by being helpful through non-disappearance, the non-disappearance condition; and that this pair (*duka*) was stated "through the elegance of the teaching (*desanāvilāsena*), or according to those who are to be guided in that way", just as the *hetusampayutta* pair was stated even after the *sahetuka* pair (Paṭṭh-a 1944:1-3). Just before, the Aṭṭhakathā defines the disappearance condition (*vigatapaccaya*) as the same states being helpful through their having disappeared (Paṭṭh-a 1943:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Paṭṭhāna)
> **Avigatapaccayoti – cattāro khandhā arūpino aññamaññaṃ avigatapaccayena paccayo. Cattāro mahābhūtā aññamaññaṃ avigatapaccayena paccayo. Okkantikkhaṇe nāmarūpaṃ aññamaññaṃ avigatapaccayena paccayo. Cittacetasikā dhammā cittasamuṭṭhānānaṃ rūpānaṃ avigatapaccayena paccayo. Mahābhūtā upādārūpānaṃ avigatapaccayena paccayo.**  
> 24. Non-disappearance condition: the four immaterial aggregates are mutually related to one another by non-disappearance condition. The four great elements are mutually related to one another by non-disappearance condition. At the moment of conception, name-and-form are mutually related by non-disappearance condition. The phenomena consciousness and its mental factors are related to mind-born matter by non-disappearance condition. The great elements are related to derived matter by non-disappearance condition.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Paṭṭhānappakaraṇa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Atthipaccayadhammā eva avigatabhāvena upakārakattā avigatapaccayoti veditabbā. Desanāvilāsena pana tathā vinetabbaveneyyavasena vā ayaṃ duko vutto; sahetukadukaṃ vatvāpi hetusampayuttaduko viyāti.**  
> The phenomena of the presence condition (atthipaccayadhammā) themselves, because of being helpful due to the state of not having disappeared (avigatabhāvena), should be understood as non-disappearance condition (avigatapaccayo). But this dual (duko) was stated by the elegance of the teaching (desanāvilāsena), or according to those who are to be guided in that way; just like having stated the dual of ‘with roots’ (sahetukadukaṃ), the dual of ‘associated with roots’ (hetusampayuttaduko).  
>
