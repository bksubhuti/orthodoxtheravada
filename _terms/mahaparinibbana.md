### Grammatical Analysis
**Mahāparinibbāna**: [nt.] The Great Final Nibbāna; The Great Passing Away. Formed by *mahā* (great) + *[parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/)* (final extinguishment). In the Mūla the word is the title of the sutta (“3. Mahāparinibbānasuttaṃ”, D-ii 287); the event itself is called *parinibbāna* (“Parinibbute bhagavati”).

### Orthodox Definition
While *[parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/)* applies to the death of any [Arahat](/glossary/arahat/), the term *Mahāparinibbāna* is reserved almost exclusively for the physical death and final extinguishment of a Fully Enlightened Buddha (*[Sammā-sambuddha](/glossary/samma-sambuddha/)*).

For Gotama Buddha, this monumental event occurred at the age of eighty (“Āsītiko me vayo vattati”) at Kusinārā, in the Upavattana sāla grove of the Mallas, between twin Sāla trees (“kusinārāyaṃ upavattane mallānaṃ sālavane antarena yamakasālānaṃ”, DN 16). It marks the most critical historical pivot in the Theravāda tradition—the moment the physical Teacher vanished, leaving the *[Dhamma-Vinaya](/glossary/dhamma-vinaya/)* as the sole guide. The event was accompanied by a great earthquake (“saha parinibbānā mahābhūmicālo ahosi”), the weeping of the monks not yet free from passion (*avītarāgā*), and the mindful, clearly comprehending endurance of those free from passion (*vītarāgā*, “satā sampajānā adhivāsenti”). It also initiated the process of compiling the [Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/) at the First Buddhist Council.

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Ahaṃ kho panānanda, etarahi jiṇṇo vuddho mahallako addhagato vayoanuppatto. Āsītiko me vayo vattati.**  
> I, Ānanda, at present, am old, elderly, of great age, far gone, advanced in years. I am eighty years old.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Dīgha Nikāya)
> **Parinibbute bhagavati saha parinibbānā mahābhūmicālo ahosi bhiṃsanako salomahaṃso.**  
> When the Gracious One attained Final Emancipation, along with the Emancipation, there was a great earthquake, and an awful, horrifying crash of the Divinities’ thunder drum.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 16, Mahāparinibbāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_02&page_number=128&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjIwLiBQYXJpbmliYnV0ZSBiaGFnYXZhdGkgc2FoYSBwYXJpbmliYsSBbsSBIG1haMSBYmjFq21pY8SBbG8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Nibbānapuraṃ pavisanto pana bhagavā dhammassāmī sabbāpi catuvīsatikoṭisatasahassasaṅkhyā samāpattiyo pavisitvā videsaṃ gacchanto ñātijanaṃ āliṅgetvā viya sabbasamāpattisukhaṃ anubhavitvā paviṭṭho.**  
> The Blessed One, the Lord of the Dhamma, entering the city of Nibbāna, entered all these twenty-four hundred million meditative attainments; just as someone going to a foreign country might embrace their relatives before leaving, he experienced the bliss of all these attainments before passing on.  
>  
> — [Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/) (on the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_02&page_number=186&name=mah%C4%81vagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TmliYsSBbmFwdXJh4bmDIHBhdmlzYW50byBwYW5hIGJoYWdhdsSBIGRoYW1tYXNzxIFtxKsgc2FiYsSBcGk%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* (DN 16) – Quoted above: his age (D-ii 409), the passing and earthquake (D-ii 593). A sweeping, epic narrative detailing the Buddha's final months, his last teachings, his final meal from Cunda, his passing away, the cremation of his body, and the subsequent division of his relics.
* **Commentary**: *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* – Explains the sequence of jhānas and attainments the Buddha entered and emerged from just before his final breath (Sv-ii 740, on D-ii 592): the first jhāna in twenty-four places, the second and third in thirteen, the fourth in fifteen, and then all the attainments, as one who “embraces his relatives” before leaving. The text itself is DN 16, D-ii 287–662 (Mūla), Sv-ii 420–816 (Aṭṭhakathā).
