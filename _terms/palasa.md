### Grammatical Analysis
**Paḷāsa**: [m.] insolence; rivalry; competitiveness. The Vibhaṅga commentary glosses it by its own verb: *paḷāsatīti paḷāso* ("it rivals, therefore it is paḷāsa"), meaning that one shows the virtues of another and makes them equal to one's own (Vibh-a 2174:1-2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Paḷāsa* is a defilement of the mind (*[upakkilesa](/glossary/upakkilesa/)*) that the texts regularly pair with contempt (*[makkha](/glossary/makkha/)*): "makkho ca paḷāso ca" (M-i 191:2, MN 7; Vibh 2647:1). The Vibhaṅga and the Puggalapaññatti define it as rivaling (*yugaggāho*) and non-relinquishment (*appaṭinissaggo*), a cause of dispute.

The *[Papañcasūdanī](/glossary/papancasudani/)* (on MN 7) says *makkha* has the characteristic of smearing the virtues of others, whereas *paḷāsa* has the characteristic of rivalry (*yugaggāhalakkhaṇo*): its function is to make one's own virtues equal to those of others, and it appears as presenting oneself in terms of the scale of others' virtues (Ps-i 560:1-2). The same commentary shows the sequence in a monk who is angry at another monk's gains: he disparages the other's real virtues (*makkha*) and takes up a competitive attitude, thinking "I also have such virtues" (*paḷāsa*) (Ps-i 566:2).

### Quote
> **(Kha) tattha katamo paḷāso? Yo paḷāso paḷāsāyanā paḷāsāhāro vivādaṭṭhānaṃ yugaggāho appaṭinissaggo – ayaṃ vuccati “paḷāso”.**  
> (B) What is insolence (paḷāso) there? Whatever is insolence, the act of insolence, the nourishment for insolence, a cause of dispute, rivaling, non-relinquishment—this is called insolence (paḷāso).  
>
