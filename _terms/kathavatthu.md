### Grammatical Analysis
**Kathāvatthu**: [nt.] Points of Controversy; Subjects of Discussion. A compound of *kathā* (discourse, discussion) + *vatthu* (basis, subject). The Aṭṭhakathā calls it "the treatise that crushes the doctrines of others" (*parappavādamathanaṃ … kathāvatthuppakaraṇaṃ*, Kv-a 544:10).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Kathāvatthu* is a book of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Piṭaka. The Aṭṭhakathā list is: Dhammasaṅgaha, Vibhaṅga, Dhātukathā, Puggalapaññatti, Kathāvatthu, Yamaka, Paṭṭhāna (Sv-i 120:5), so it is the fifth.

The Atthasālinī records that the Buddha laid down the mātikā beforehand: two hundred and eighteen years after his parinibbāna, the elder Moggaliputtatissa would, seated among a thousand bhikkhus, combine five hundred suttas from his own school and five hundred from other schools, and expound the *Kathāvatthu*, equal in size to the Dīgha Nikāya (As 55:1; also As 50:13). The Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā tells how, after the king's remorse over the troubles in the Sāsana, Moggaliputtatissa purified the Sāsana and held the third council (*tatiyasaṅgīti*, Kv-a 545:2), and at that gathering, to refute the points that had arisen and would arise, he expounded the treatise according to the mātikā set down by the Tathāgata (Kv-a 544:10).

The book proceeds as a dialogue between a Theravādin and an opponent. Its first controversy, the *Puggalakathā*, begins with the Theravādin asking whether a person is found in the sense of a real and ultimate fact (Kv 7:1-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Kathāvatthu, Puggalakathā)
> **Puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthenāti? Āmantā. Yo saccikaṭṭho paramattho, tato so puggalo upalabbhati saccikaṭṭhaparamatthenāti? Na hevaṃ vattabbe.**  
> Theravādin: Is the person known in the sense of a real and ultimate fact? Puggalavādin: Yes. Theravādin: Is the person known in the same way as a real and ultimate fact is known? Puggalavādin: Nay, that cannot truly be said.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Mama parinibbānato aṭṭhārasavassādhikānaṃ dvinnaṃ vassasatānaṃ matthake moggaliputtatissatthero nāma bhikkhu bhikkhusahassamajjhe nisinno sakavāde pañca suttasatāni paravāde pañcāti suttasahassaṃ samodhānetvā dīghanikāyappamāṇaṃ kathāvatthuppakaraṇaṃ bhājessatīti.**  
> “Two hundred and eighteen years after my final extinguishment, a bhikkhu named the Elder Moggaliputtatissa, seated in the midst of a thousand bhikkhus, will combine five hundred suttas from his own school and five hundred suttas from other schools—a thousand suttas in all—and analyze the treatise Points of Controversy, which is equal in size to the Long Discourses.”  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Tasmiṃ samāgame moggaliputtatissatthero yāni ca tadā uppannāni vatthūni, yāni ca āyatiṃ uppajjissanti, sabbesampi tesaṃ paṭibāhanatthaṃ satthārā dinnanayavaseneva tathāgatena ṭhapitamātikaṃ vibhajanto sakavāde pañca suttasatāni paravāde pañcāti suttasahassaṃ āharitvā imaṃ parappavādamathanaṃ āyatilakkhaṇaṃ kathāvatthuppakaraṇaṃ abhāsi.**  
> At that gathering, for the sake of suppressing all those points that had arisen then and those that would arise in the future, Elder Moggaliputta Tissa, detailing the summary (mātikaṃ) established by the Realized One according to the method given by the Teacher, related (āharitvā) a thousand suttas—five hundred suttas on our own doctrine (sakavāde) and five hundred on other doctrines—and declared (abhāsi) this Kathāvatthu treatise, which crushes the doctrines of others and serves as a characteristic for the future.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Puggalakathā* (Kv 7) – the first controversy, on whether a person exists in the ultimate sense.
* **Commentary**: *Atthasālinī* (As 55:1) – the Buddha's prediction of the elder Moggaliputtatissa and the *Kathāvatthu*.
* **Commentary**: *Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā* (Kv-a 543-545) – the third council and the origin of the treatise.
* **Commentary**: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* (Sv-i 120:5) – the seven books of the Abhidhamma Piṭaka in order.
