### Grammatical Analysis
**Sekha**: [m.; adj.] a learner; a noble disciple undergoing training; trainee. From root *sikkh* (to train, study, learn). The Buddha derives it from *sikkhati*: "he trains, therefore he is called a trainee" (*sikkhatīti kho, bhikkhu, tasmā sekhoti vuccati*, A-iii 889:3).

### Orthodox Definition
In the Mūla the Buddha defines the *sekha* by his training: he trains in heightened virtue, heightened mind and heightened wisdom (*adhisīlampi sikkhati, adhicittampi sikkhati, adhipaññampi sikkhati*, A-iii 889:3-5). The Puggalapaññatti defines the person: the four persons who possess the path and the three persons who possess the fruition are *sekkhā*; the Arahant is *asekkha* (Pp 209:2-3).

The sekha taxonomy therefore encompasses **seven of the eight noble individuals** (*ariya-puggala*), since the arahatta-phala holder is *asekha*:
1. The one standing on the path of Stream-entry (*sotāpatti-maggaṭṭho*).
2. The Fruition of Stream-entry (*sotāpatti-phalaṭṭho*).
3. The Path of Once-Return (*sakadāgāmi-maggaṭṭho*).
4. The Fruition of Once-Return (*sakadāgāmi-phalaṭṭho*).
5. The Path of Non-Return (*anāgāmi-maggaṭṭho*).
6. The Fruition of Non-Return (*anāgāmi-phalaṭṭho*).
7. The Path of Arahatship (*arahatta-maggaṭṭho*).

The Aṭṭhakathā says the same: one who trains in higher virtue and so on is a *sekkha*, a stream-enterer, once-returner or non-returner (Nidd-a-i 962:6; similarly Paṭis-a 1783:3, It-a 294:1). The Papañcasūdanī speaks of "the seven *sekkhā*" living the holy life together with the *puthujjanakalyāṇaka* (Ps-i 654:2).

### Quote (Mūla: Sekkha Sutta)
> **‘“Sekho, sekho ’ ti, bhante, vuccati. Kittāvatā nu kho, bhante, sekho hotī” ti? “Sikkhatīti kho, bhikkhu, tasmā sekhoti vuccati. Kiñca sikkhati? Adhisīlampi sikkhati, adhicittampi sikkhati, adhipaññampi sikkhati. Sikkhatīti kho, bhikkhu, tasmā sekhoti vuccatī” ti.**  
> “Venerable sir, it is said, ‘a trainee, a trainee’. In what way, venerable sir, is a trainee defined?” “He trains, bhikkhu, therefore he is called a trainee. And how does he train? He trains in heightened virtue, he trains in heightened mind, and he trains in heightened discernment. He trains, bhikkhu, therefore he is called a trainee.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 3.86, Sekkha Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_03&page_number=232&name=tikanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCY4oCYIFNla2hvLCBzZWtobyDigJkgdGksIGJoYW50ZSw%3D)

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Puggalapaññatti)
> **Katamo ca puggalo sekkho? Cattāro maggasamaṅgino tayo phalasamaṅgino puggalā “sekkhā”. Arahā asekkho.**  
> 19. What sort of person is a learner? The four persons who possess the path and the three persons who possess the fruition are learners. An Arahant is a non-learner.  
>  
> — [Puggalapaññatti](/glossary/puggalapannatti/) (Ekaka-niddesa, §19) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_04&page_number=117&name=puggalapa%C3%B1%C3%B1attip%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTkuIEthdGFtbyBjYSBwdWdnYWxvIHNla2tobz8%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā)
> **Adhisīlādīni sikkhatīti sekkho , tassa sekkhassa vā sotāpannassa vā sakadāgāmino vā anāgāmino vā.**  
> “A trainee” is one who trains in higher ethics and so on, of that trainee—whether a stream-enterer, a once-returner, or a non-returner.  
>  
> — Mahāniddesa-aṭṭhakathā (Nidd-a-i 962:6) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_ku_16&page_number=222&name=mah%C4%81niddesaa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=QWRoaXPEq2zEgWTEq25pIHNpa2toYXTEq3RpIHNla2tobyAsIHRhc3NhIHNla2toYXNzYQ%3D%3D)
