### Grammatical Analysis
**Senāsana**: [nt.] Bed and seat; lodging; accommodation. Formed by *sena* (from *seti*, "he lies down, sleeps") + *āsana* (from *āsati*, "he sits"). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says: *seti ceva āsati ca etthāti senāsanaṃ*, "because one lies down and sits there, it is a lodging; this is a name for a bed, a bench and so on" (Sv-i 916).

### Orthodox Definition
*Senāsana* is the term for **lodging**, one of the four requisites (robes, almsfood, lodging and medicine) of a bhikkhu. The Mūla, in the Sabbāsava Sutta (MN 2), says that a bhikkhu uses lodging only after wise reflection: "just to ward off cold, to ward off heat, to ward off the touch of gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, sun and creeping things, and just to remove the perils of climate and for enjoying seclusion" (M-i 77).

The Vibhaṅga defines the word by a list: a bed, a bench, a cushion, a pillow, a monastery (*vihāra*), a half-yoke house (*aḍḍhayoga*), a mansion (*pāsāda*), a fortified building (*aṭṭa*), a hall (*māḷa*), a rock-shelter (*leṇa*), a cave (*guhā*), the foot of a tree (*rukkhamūla*), a bamboo thicket (*veḷugumba*) (Vibh 1658, §527; the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī quotes it and adds the pavilion, *maṇḍapa*, and "wherever bhikkhus retire", Sv-i 916). So it covers anything from a bed to the foot of a tree.

The Vinaya has many rules on it. The Pātimokkha has, among the Pācittiya rules, the training rules on lodgings (*senāsanasikkhāpada*): 14, leaving a bed, bench, mattress or stool of the Saṅgha spread in the open without putting it away or asking leave; 15, the same with bedding in a monastery of the Saṅgha; 16, knowingly encroaching on the sleeping place of a bhikkhu who arrived earlier; 17, evicting a bhikkhu from a monastery of the Saṅgha out of anger (Dvem-bhk 170–177). The *Senāsanakkhandhaka* of the Cūḷavagga (Vin-iv 1505–1822) is the chapter on lodgings. In it the Buddha allows the Saṅgha to appoint a lodging-assigner (*senāsanaggāhāpaka*), a bhikkhu with five qualities: he does not act from desire, aversion, delusion or fear, and knows what has been taken and what has not been taken (Vin-iv 1649). The Dīgha Nikāya says that a bhikkhu abstains from high and luxurious beds (*uccāsayanamahāsayanā paṭivirato*, D-i 286).

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Paṭisaṅkhā yoniso senāsanaṃ paṭisevati – ‘ yāvadeva sītassa paṭighātāya, uṇhassa paṭighātāya, ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīṃsapasamphassānaṃ paṭighātāya, yāvadeva utuparissayavinodanapaṭisallānārāmatthaṃ ’.**  
> ‘‘ Reflecting wisely, a mendicant makes use of lodgings. ‘Just for warding off cold, for warding off heat, for warding off the touch of gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, and creeping things, and just for warding off the perils of climate and for enjoying seclusion.’

### Quote (Abhidhamma: Vibhaṅga)
> **“Senāsanan” ti mañcopi senāsanaṃ, pīṭhampi senāsanaṃ, bhisipi senāsanaṃ, bibbohanampi senāsanaṃ, vihāropi senāsanaṃ, aḍḍhayogopi senāsanaṃ, pāsādopi senāsanaṃ, aṭṭopi senāsanaṃ, māḷopi senāsanaṃ, leṇampi senāsanaṃ, guhāpi senāsanaṃ, rukkhamūlampi senāsanaṃ, veḷugumbopi senāsanaṃ.**  
> 527. What is “lodging” (senāsanaṃ)? A bed (mañco) is a lodging; a chair (pīṭhaṃ) is a lodging; a cushion (bhisī) is a lodging; a pillow (bibbohanaṃ) is a lodging; a monastery (vihāro) is a lodging; a half-yoke house (aḍḍhayogo) is a lodging; a mansion (pāsādo) is a lodging; a fortified building (aṭṭo) is a lodging; a hall (māḷo) is a lodging; a rock-shelter (leṇaṃ) is a lodging; a cave (guhā) is a lodging; the foot of a tree (rukkhamūlaṃ) is a lodging; a bamboo thicket (veḷugumbo) is a lodging.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Vinaya, Cūḷavagga)
> **‘ kena nu kho senāsanaṃ gāhetabban” ti? Bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ … pe …“anujānāmi, bhikkhave, pañcahaṅgehi samannāgataṃ bhikkhuṃ senāsanaggāhāpakaṃ sammannituṃ – yo na chandāgatiṃ gaccheyya, na dosāgatiṃ gaccheyya, na mohāgatiṃ gaccheyya, na bhayāgatiṃ gaccheyya, gahitāgahitañca jāneyya.**  
> “By whom indeed should lodgings be assigned?” They reported this matter to the Blessed One ... “Bhikkhus, I allow you to designate as a lodging assigner a bhikkhu who is endowed with five qualities: one who would not follow bias based on desire, would not follow bias based on aversion, would not follow bias based on delusion, would not follow bias based on fear, and who knows what has been taken and what has not been taken.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) ([Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/), Senāsanakkhandhaka, §317) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_04&page_number=317&name=c%C5%AB%E1%B8%B7avaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCYIGtlbmEgbnUga2hvIHNlbsSBc2FuYeG5gyBnxIFoZXRhYmJhbg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Mūla: Bhikkhupātimokkha, Pācittiya 14-17)
> **Paṭhamasenāsanasikkhāpadaṃ 14. Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghikaṃ mañcaṃ vā pīṭhaṃ vā bhisiṃ vā kocchaṃ vā ajjhokāse santharitvā vā santharāpetvā vā taṃ pakkamanto neva uddhareyya, na uddharāpeyya, anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya, pācittiyaṃ. Dutiyasenāsanasikkhāpadaṃ 15. Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghike vihāre seyyaṃ santharitvā vā santharāpetvā vā taṃ pakkamanto neva uddhareyya, na uddharāpeyya, anāpucchaṃ vā gaccheyya, pācittiyaṃ. Anupakhajjasikkhāpadaṃ 16. Yo pana bhikkhu saṅghike vihāre jānaṃ pubbupagataṃ bhikkhuṃ anupakhajja seyyaṃ kappeyya “yassa sambādho bhavissati, so pakkamissatī” ti etadeva paccayaṃ karitvā anaññaṃ, pācittiyaṃ. Nikkaḍḍhanasikkhāpadaṃ 17. Yo pana bhikkhu bhikkhuṃ kupito anattamano saṅghikā vihārā nikkaḍḍheyya vā nikkaḍḍhāpeyya vā, pācittiyaṃ.**  
> The first training rule on dwellings 14. If a bhikkhu, having set out or having caused to be set out in the open air a couch, or a chair, or a mattress, or a stool belonging to the Sangha, and when departing, does not put it away or have it put away, or if he should go without asking for permission, he commits an offence of Expiation. The second training rule on dwellings 15. If a bhikkhu, having set out or having caused to be set out bedding in a dwelling-place belonging to the Sangha, and when departing, does not put it away or have it put away, or if he should go without asking for permission, he commits an offence of Expiation. The training rule on encroaching 16. If a bhikkhu, in a dwelling-place belonging to the Sangha, knowing that another bhikkhu arrived there before him, should share a sleeping place by encroaching on him, thinking: ‘Whoever feels crowded will depart,’ and doing so only for this reason and no other, he commits an offence of Expiation. The training rule on eviction 17. If a bhikkhu, in anger and displeased, should evict a bhikkhu from a dwelling-place belonging to the Sangha or have him evicted, he commits an offence of Expiation.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī)
> **Seti ceva āsati ca etthāti senāsanaṃ mañcapīṭhādīnametaṃ adhivacanaṃ. Tenāha – “senāsananti mañcopi senāsanaṃ, pīṭhampi, bhisipi, bimbohanampi, vihāropi, aḍḍhayogopi, pāsādopi, hammiyampi, guhāpi, aṭṭopi, māḷopi leṇampi, veḷugumbopi, rukkhamūlampi, maṇḍapopi, senāsanaṃ, yattha vā pana bhikkhū paṭikkamanti, sabbametaṃ senāsanan” ti (vibha.527).**  
> Because one sleeps and sits therein, it is a lodging; this is a designation for a bed, a bench, and so on. Therefore he said— “A lodging is a bed, a bench, a mattress, a pillow, a mansion, a half-yoke house, a palace, a turreted building, a cave, a turret, a hall, a rock dwelling, a bamboo clump, the foot of a tree, or a pavilion; or indeed, wherever bhikkhus dwell, all of that is a lodging.”  
>
