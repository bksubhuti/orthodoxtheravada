### Grammatical Analysis
**Nimitta**: [nt.] sign; mark; image; object; cause; defining characteristic. In compounds it names the kind of sign: *samādhinimitta*, *paggahanimitta*, *upekkhānimitta*, *parikammanimitta*, *uggahanimitta*, *paṭibhāganimitta*, *nimittaggāhī* (“one who grasps the sign”), *animitta* (signless).

### Orthodox Definition
*Nimitta* is a wide word, and the texts give it several senses. The Aṭṭhakathā glosses *tīṇi nimittāni* (AN 3.103) as *tīṇi kāraṇāni*, “three causes” (Mp-iii 876:2), and says of *samādhinimitta*: “samādhiyeva nimittaṃ samādhinimittaṃ”, concentration itself is the sign, namely one-pointedness (Mp-iii 876:5-6). In the same sutta the monk practising the higher mind attends in turn to the signs of concentration, exertion (*paggaha*) and equanimity, so that the mind is neither lazy nor restless nor unconcentrated (A-iii 1020).

In the sense of a defiling sign it is *nimittaggāhī*: grasping the sign of man or woman, or of beauty and so on, which is the footing of defilement, through desire and passion (As 2377:8). The opposite is the guarding of the sense-door (MN 33). Its end is the signless (*animittā cetovimutti*): by not attending to any signs the monk enters the signless concentration of mind (MN 43, M-i 1563). The Paṭisambhidāmagga commentary adds that all *saṅkhāra*-signs are called *nimitta* because they appear to the meditator as if having a shape (Paṭis-a 414:5-6).

In samatha practice the Visuddhimagga and the Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha tradition distinguish three signs of the meditation object: the preparatory sign (*parikammanimitta*, e.g. the kasiṇa disc), the learning sign (*uggahanimitta*, seen with the eyes shut as if seen with the eyes open), and the counterpart sign (*paṭibhāganimitta*), which arises together with access concentration and is the object of access and absorption (Vism IV 57; Abh-s IX). These three terms are post-canonical; the Mūla uses *samādhinimitta* and the other signs above.

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara Nikāya)
> **“Adhicittamanuyuttena, bhikkhave, bhikkhunā tīṇi nimittāni kālena kālaṃ manasi kātabbāni – kālena kālaṃ samādhinimittaṃ manasi kātabbaṃ, kālena kālaṃ paggahanimittaṃ manasi kātabbaṃ, kālena kālaṃ upekkhānimittaṃ manasi kātabbaṃ. Sace, bhikkhave, adhicittamanuyutto bhikkhu ekantaṃ samādhinimittaṃyeva manasi kareyya, ṭhānaṃ taṃ cittaṃ kosajjāya saṃvatteyya.**  
> 103. “Monks, a bhikkhu pursuing the higher mind should attend to three signs at the right time: he should attend to the object of concentration at the right time; he should attend to the sign of exertion at the right time; he should attend to the sign of equanimity at the right time. If a bhikkhu pursuing the higher mind were to attend solely to the object of concentration, it is possible that his mind would tend to laziness.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Majjhima Nikāya)
> **“Idhāvuso, bhikkhu sabbanimittānaṃ amanasikārā animittaṃ cetosamādhiṃ upasampajja viharati. Ayaṃ vuccatāvuso, animittā cetovimutti.**  
> “Here, friend, a bhikkhu, by not attending to all signs, enters and remains in the signless concentration of mind. This, friend, is called the signless liberation of mind.  
>

> Here, monks, on seeing a form with the eye, a bhikkhu grasps at its signs and grasps at its features.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Manorathapūraṇī)
> **Tīṇi nimittānīti tīṇi kāraṇāni. Kālena kālanti kāle kāle, yuttakāleti attho. Kālena kālaṃ samādhinimittaṃ manasikātabbantiādīsu taṃ taṃ kālaṃ sallakkhetvā ekaggatāya yuttakāle ekaggatā manasikātabbā. Ekaggatā hi idha samādhinimittanti vuttā. Tatra vacanattho – samādhiyeva nimittaṃ samādhinimittaṃ.**  
> 'Three signs' (tīṇi nimittānī) means three causes. 'At the right time' (kālena kālan) means from time to time; it means at an appropriate time. In 'one should attend to the object of concentration at the right time' (kālena kālaṃ samādhinimittaṃ manasikātabbaṃ) and so on, it means that having noted the proper time, one should attend to the one-pointedness of mind when it is the time for one-pointedness. For here, the Buddha has stated that one-pointedness is the 'object of concentration'. The meaning of the words there is: concentration itself is the sign—that is the object of concentration.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/manorathapurani/) (Manorathapūraṇī, 11. Nimittasuttavaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_an_02&page_number=228&name=duk%C4%81dinip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VMSr4bmHaSBuaW1pdHTEgW7Eq3RpIHTEq%2BG5h2kga8SBcmHhuYfEgW5pLg%3D%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Nimittaggāhīti itthipurisanimittaṃ vā subhanimittādikaṃ vā kilesavatthubhūtaṃ nimittaṃ chandarāgavasena gaṇhāti, diṭṭhamatteyeva na saṇṭhāti.**  
> ‘Grasping at signs (nimittaggāhī)’ means one grasps at a sign of female or male, or a sign of beauty and so forth, or a sign that is a basis for defilements, by way of lust and attachment; one does not become established merely upon seeing.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (on nimittaggāhī) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=428&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=TmltaXR0YWdnxIFoxKt0aSBpdHRoaXB1cmlzYW5pbWl0dGHhuYMgdsSBIHN1YmhhbmltaXR0xIFkaWth4bmDIHbEgSBraWxlc2F2YXR0aHViaMWrdGHhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga)
> **Tassevaṃ bhāvayato yadā nimīletvā āvajjantassa ummīlitakāle viya āpāthamāgacchati, tadā uggahanimittaṃ jātaṃ nāma hoti.**  
> 57. As he is developing it in this way, when it comes into focus as he adverts with his eyes shut exactly as it does with his eyes open, then the learning sign is said to have been produced.

### Quote (Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha)
> **Parikammanimittaṃ uggahanimittaṃ paṭibhāganimittañceti tīṇi nimittāni ca veditabbāni.**  
> 5. The three signs should also be understood: the preliminary sign, the learning sign, and the counterpart sign.  
>

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Parikammassa nimittaṃ ārammaṇattāti parikammanimittaṃ , kasiṇamaṇḍalādi. Tadeva cakkhunā diṭṭhaṃ viya manasā uggahetabbaṃ nimittaṃ, uggaṇhantassa vā nimittanti uggahanimittaṃ. Tappaṭibhāgaṃ vaṇṇādikasiṇadosarahitaṃ nimittaṃ upacārappanānaṃ ārammaṇattāti paṭibhāganimittaṃ.**  
> 5. The preliminary sign (parikammanimittaṃ) is the sign of preliminary work, such as the kasiṇa-disk and so on, because it is the object. That very sign, which should be grasped by the mind just as if it were seen with the eye, or the sign for one who is grasping it mentally, is the learning sign (uggahanimittaṃ). The sign that is the counterpart of that, free from the defects of the kasiṇa such as color and so on, is the counterpart sign (paṭibhāganimittaṃ), because it is the object of access and absorption meditations.  
>  
> — Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Nimittabhedavaṇṇanā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_06&page_number=258&name=abhidhammatthavibh%C4%81vin%C4%AB%E1%B9%AD%C4%ABk%C4%81&note=&selected_text=NS4gUGFyaWthbW1hc3NhIG5pbWl0dGHhuYMgxIFyYW1tYeG5h2F0dMSBdGkgcGFyaWthbW1hbmltaXR0YeG5gyAs)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Nimitta Sutta* (AN 3.103, A-iii 1019–1020) – the three signs of concentration, exertion and equanimity; *Mahāvedalla Sutta* (MN 43, M-i 1563) – the signless liberation of mind; *Mahāgopālaka Sutta* (MN 33, M-i 1146) – the monk who grasps signs (*nimittaggāhī*) and does not guard the eye faculty.
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-iii 876:2-6) on the Nimitta Sutta; *Atthasālinī* (As 2377:8) on *nimittaggāhī*; *Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā* (Paṭis-a 414:5-6); *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* IV, 57 (Vism-i 846:1) on the learning sign.
* **Abhidhamma tradition**: *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha* IX, Nimittabhedo (Abh-s 763:1) – the three signs, with the Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī-ṭīkā (Abh-s-t 1723:1-3) giving the definition of each.
