### Grammatical Analysis
**Suttapiṭaka**: [nt.] The Basket of Discourses (the Aṭṭhakathā spells it *suttantapiṭaka*). Formed by *sutta* (thread, discourse) + *piṭaka* (basket, collection). The Aṭṭhakathā says the word *piṭaka* has two meanings, learning (*pariyatti*) and container of the respective meanings (*bhājana*), and so "the Suttanta, and it is a basket, thus the Suttantapiṭaka" (As 174:2-3; Sv-i 143:1).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Sutta-piṭaka* is the second of the three great divisions of the Pāḷi Canon (*[Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/)*). It contains the discourses delivered by the Buddha, and some by his chief disciples, and the Aṭṭhakathā says that everything the Buddha spoke from his awakening until his final Nibbāna, forty-five years, has a single taste, the taste of liberation (Sv-i 110:2). 

In Orthodox Theravāda, the *Sutta-piṭaka* is classified as *vohāra-desanā* (teaching by convention), since the Blessed One, skilled in convention, taught it mostly in conventional terms (As 181:2; Sv-i 149:3). The Buddha spoke to a vast array of people, from kings to farmers. He tailored his language to fit their specific minds and backgrounds. To do this, he freely used conventional terms like "I," "you," "man," "woman," "[deva](/glossary/deva/)," and "self." 

This makes the *Sutta-piṭaka* very accessible. However, to understand these teachings deeply, a student must rely on the *[Abhidhamma-piṭaka](/glossary/abhidhamma-pitaka/)*. The Abhidhamma takes these conventional teachings and explains them in terms of ultimate realities (*paramattha-dhamma*).

The Commentaries (*[Aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/atthakatha/)*) are also absolutely necessary. The suttas usually do not explain their own history. The Commentaries provide the background story for why a discourse was given. They identify the specific people involved. Most importantly, they preserve the exact definitions of the words used. Without the Commentaries, the deep context of the *Sutta-piṭaka* would be lost.

Structurally, the *Sutta-piṭaka* is divided into five collections called *Nikāyas*: *Dīgha*, *Majjhima*, *Saṃyutta*, *Aṅguttara*, and *Khuddaka* (Sv-i 173:2). At the First Council they were recited in turn and entrusted to elders and their pupils: the Dīgha to Ānanda, the Majjhima to the pupils of Sāriputta, the Saṃyutta to Mahākassapa and the Aṅguttara to Anuruddha (Sv-i 103-106). The Dīgha reciters say that the Khuddaka collection was included in the Abhidhamma Piṭaka, while the Majjhima reciters say that it belongs to the Suttanta Piṭaka (Sv-i 108).

### Textual References
* **Historical**: *[Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/)* (Pañcasatikakkhandhaka, Vin-iv 2676-2733) – The account of the First Council, where Mahākassapa put the questions on the Dhamma to Venerable Ānanda (Vin-iv 2696-2698). The Mūla does not use the word *suttantapiṭaka* (searched); the division of the Sutta Piṭaka among the reciters is in the Sumaṅgalavilāsinī (Sv-i 100-108).
* **Commentary**: *[Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/)* (Nidāna, As 174-181) – The meaning of *piṭaka* and the difference between the conventional teaching of the suttas and the ultimate teaching of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) (As 181:2-3).
