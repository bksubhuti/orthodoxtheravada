### Grammatical Analysis
**Citta**: [nt.] consciousness, mind. The Aṭṭhakathā gives the derivations: "*cittanti ārammaṇaṃ cintetīti cittaṃ; vijānātīti attho*", it is *citta* because it cognizes (*cinteti*) an object, that is, it knows; or the mundane wholesome, unwholesome and functional *citta* is so called because it builds up (*cinoti*) its own continuum by way of the impulsion process; resultant *citta* because it is accumulated (*cita*) by kamma and defilements; and all *citta* because of its variegatedness (*cittatā*) (As 945:1-6).

### Orthodox Definition
*Citta* is the first of the four ultimate things (*paramattha*) of the Abhidhamma: "the things stated there are fourfold in the ultimate sense" (Abhidh-s 7:1); they are *cittaṃ cetasikaṃ rūpaṃ nibbānaṃ* (Abhidh-s 8:1; Abhidh-s-t 898:2). The Dhammasaṅgaṇī defines it, for the first wholesome consciousness, by a string of synonyms: "*cittaṃ mano mānasaṃ hadayaṃ paṇḍaraṃ mano manāyatanaṃ manindriyaṃ viññāṇaṃ viññāṇakkhandho tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātu*" (Dhs 396:2). In the Mūla, the Buddha says in the Citta Sutta: "the world is led around by the mind (*cittena nīyati loko*), by the mind it is dragged here and there" (S-i 735:1).

The Ṭīkā adds that the characteristic of *citta* is "cognizing an object" (*visayavijānanalakkhaṇaṃ cittaṃ*), since consciousness does not arise without an object (Abhidh-s-t 899:2-3). The Visuddhimagga counts the mind-base as eighty-nine kinds by way of wholesome, unwholesome, resultant and functional consciousness, or one hundred and twenty-one (Vism-ii 552:4); the Ṭīkā likewise says that by the consciousnesses of the four planes the wise analyse the mind as eighty-nine (Abhidh-s-t 1006:1).

### Quote (Mūla: Dhammasaṅgaṇī)
> **Katamaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti? Yaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ mano mānasaṃ hadayaṃ paṇḍaraṃ mano manāyatanaṃ manindriyaṃ viññāṇaṃ viññāṇakkhandho tajjāmanoviññāṇadhātu – idaṃ tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti.**  
> 6. What is the mind at that time? Whatever mind (cittaṃ), mind (mano), heart (mānasaṃ), heart (hadayaṃ), lucidity, mind (mano), mind sense base, controlling power of mind, consciousness, consciousness aggregate, or the corresponding mind-consciousness element exists at that time—this is the mind at that time.  
>

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Atthasālinī)
> **Cittanti ārammaṇaṃ cintetīti cittaṃ; vijānātīti attho. Yasmā vā ‘ cittan ’ ti sabbacittasādhāraṇo esa saddo, tasmā yadettha lokiyakusalākusalakiriyacittaṃ, taṃ javanavīthivasena attano santānaṃ cinotīti cittaṃ. Vipākaṃ kammakilesehi citanti cittaṃ. Apica sabbampi yathānurūpato cittatāya cittaṃ. Cittakaraṇatāya cittanti evampettha attho veditabbo.**  
> Mind (citta) is so called because it cognizes (cinteti) an object; the meaning is that it cognizes (vijānāti). Or, since the word 'mind' (citta) is common to all consciousness, therefore, whatever mundane wholesome, unwholesome, and functional consciousness there is here, it is called 'mind' because it builds up (cinoti) its own continuum by way of cognitive processes of impulsion (javanavīthi). Resultant consciousness (vipāka) is called 'mind' (citta) because it is accumulated (cita) by deeds and defilements. Furthermore, all indeed is called 'mind' (citta) due to its variegatedness (cittatā) in accordance with its nature. It is called 'mind' (citta) because it produces variegatedness (cittakaraṇatā)—this is how the meaning here should be understood.  
>  
> — [Atthasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 945) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=106&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q2l0dGFudGkgxIFyYW1tYeG5h2HhuYMgY2ludGV0xKt0aSBjaXR0YeG5gzs%3D)

### Quote (Ṭīkā: Abhidhammatthavibhāvinī)
> **Cintetīti cittaṃ , ārammaṇaṃ vijānātīti attho. Yathāha “visayavijānanalakkhaṇaṃ cittan” ti (dha. sa. aṭṭha.1 dhammudesavāraphassapañcamakarāsivaṇṇanā).**  
> It is called consciousness (cittaṃ) because it thinks; the meaning is that it cognizes an object. As it was said: “Mind has the characteristic of cognizing an object.”  
>
