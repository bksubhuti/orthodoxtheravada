### Grammatical Analysis
**Upasamānussati**: [f.] recollection of peace. Formed by *upasama* (peace, stilling) + *anussati* (recollection). The Visuddhimagga defines the *upasama* recollected here as "Nibbāna, defined as the stilling of all suffering" (*sabbadukkhūpasamasaṅkhātassa nibbānassa*, Vism-i 1724:2).

### Orthodox Definition
*Upasamānussati* is the tenth of the ten recollections: the Mūla lists "Dhammānussati ... maraṇassati ... kāyagatāsati ... upasamānussati" at its close (AN 1.297, A-i 291:3), and the Visuddhimagga treats it right after mindfulness of breathing (Vism-i 1724:1). The "peace" is Nibbāna (Vism-i 1724:2, 1726:1).

The meditator, in solitude, recollects the qualities of Nibbāna in the Buddha's words: "Of all states, conditioned or unconditioned, dispassion is declared the foremost, that is, *madanimmadano pipāsavinayo ālayasamugghāto vaṭṭupacchedo taṇhakkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānaṃ*" (AN 4.34, quoted at Vism-i 1724:2). The Visuddhimagga explains each name: the crushing of intoxication (all vanities are destroyed on reaching it), the removal of thirst, the uprooting of the attachments to the five cords of sensual pleasure, the cutting off of the round of the three realms, and the ending of craving (Vism-i 1725:6-10). He may also recollect the other qualities of peace spoken of by the Buddha, such as the unconditioned, the truth, the other shore and the deathless (Vism-i 1726:3).

For one recollecting thus, the mind is not obsessed by lust, hatred or delusion; the jhāna factors arise in a single moment for one who has suppressed the hindrances, but "owing to the profundity of the qualities of peace, or because of being intent on recollecting various kinds of qualities, the jhāna is only access, without reaching absorption (*appanā*)" (Vism-i 1727:1-3). The Visuddhimagga adds that, like the six recollections, this also "succeeds only for a noble disciple" (*ariyasāvakasseva ijjhati*), but "it should be brought to mind even by a worldling who values peace, for even by hearsay (*sutavasena*) the mind acquires confidence in peace" (Vism-i 1728:1-2). A bhikkhu devoted to it sleeps and awakens happily, is tranquil in senses and mind, and, if he does not penetrate further, is headed for a happy destination (Vism-i 1728:3-4).

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, the ten recollections)
> **“Ekadhammo, bhikkhave, bhāvito bahulīkato ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati. Katamo ekadhammo? Dhammānussati … pe … saṅghānussati … sīlānussati … cāgānussati … devatānussati … ānāpānassati … maraṇassati … kāyagatāsati … upasamānussati. Ayaṃ kho, bhikkhave, ekadhammo bhāvito bahulīkato ekantanibbidāya virāgāya nirodhāya upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattatī” ti.**  
> “One thing, monks, when developed and cultivated, leads to dispassion, for the fading away of passion, for cessation, peace, having directly known, enlightenment, and for Nibbāna. What one thing? Recollection of the Dhamma … ... … recollection of the Saṅgha … recollection of moral conduct … recollection of generosity … recollection of deities … mindfulness of breathing … mindfulness of death … mindfulness of the body … recollection of peace. The Blessed One, monks, is this one thing that, when developed and cultivated, leads to dispassion, for the fading away of passion, for cessation, peace, having directly known, enlightenment, and for Nibbāna.”  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Aṅguttara, the qualities of Nibbāna)
> **“Yāvatā, bhikkhave, dhammā saṅkhatā vā asaṅkhatā vā, virāgo tesaṃ aggamakkhāyati, yadidaṃ madanimmadano pipāsavinayo ālayasamugghāto vaṭṭupacchedo taṇhākkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānaṃ.**  
> To whatever extent there are mental phenomena, whether conditioned or unconditioned, dispassion is declared the foremost among them, that is to say, the crushing of pride, the removal of thirst, the uprooting of attachment, the termination of the round, the destruction of craving, dispassion, cessation, Nibbāna.  
>  
> — *[Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/)* (AN 4.34, Aggappasāda Sutta; = Itivuttaka 90) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=343&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFnEgXZhdMSBLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGRoYW1txIEgc2HhuYVraGF0xIEgdsSB)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, the practice)
> **Ānāpānassatiyā anantaraṃ uddiṭṭhaṃ pana upasamānussatiṃ bhāvetukāmena rahogatena paṭisallīnena – “yāvatā, bhikkhave, dhammā saṅkhatā vā asaṅkhatā vā virāgo tesaṃ dhammānaṃ aggamakkhāyati, yadidaṃ madanimmadano pipāsavinayo ālayasamugghāto vaṭṭupacchedo taṇhakkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānan” ti (a. ni.4.34; itivu.90) evaṃ sabbadukkhūpasamasaṅkhātassa nibbānassa guṇā anussaritabbā.**  
> 239. One who wants to develop the recollection of peace, which is mentioned immediately after mindfulness of breathing, having gone into solitude and solitary retreat— should recollect the qualities of Nibbāna, which is defined as the stilling of all suffering, in this way: “Mendicants, in so far as there are states, whether conditioned or unconditioned, dispassion is declared the foremost of them, that is to say, the disillusionment of vanity, the removal of thirst, the uprooting of attachment, the cutting off of the round, the ending of craving, dispassion, cessation, Nibbāna.”  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VIII, §239, Upasamānussatikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=284&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=MjM5LiDEgG7EgXDEgW5hc3NhdGl5xIEgYW5hbnRhcmHhuYMgdWRkaeG5reG5rWhh4bmDIHBhbmEgdXBhc2FtxIFudXNzYXRp4bmD)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, access only)
> **Tassevaṃ madanimmadanatādiguṇavasena upasamaṃ anussarato neva tasmiṃ samaye rāgapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti, na dosa … na mohapariyuṭṭhitaṃ cittaṃ hoti. Ujugatamevassa tasmiṃ samaye cittaṃ hoti upasamaṃ ārabbhāti buddhānussatiādīsu vuttanayeneva vikkhambhitanīvaraṇassa ekakkhaṇe jhānaṅgāni uppajjanti. Upasamaguṇānaṃ pana gambhīratāya nānappakāraguṇānussaraṇādhimuttatāya vā appanaṃ appatvā upacārappattameva jhānaṃ hoti. Tadetamupasamaguṇānussaraṇavasena upasamānussaticceva saṅkhyaṃ gacchati.**  
> For one who thus recollects peace by way of its qualities beginning with the disillusionment of vanity, at that time his mind is not obsessed by lust, nor obsessed by hatred, nor obsessed by delusion. At that time his mind is simply straight, focusing on peace (upasama); and in the very manner stated under the recollection of the Buddha and so on, the jhāna factors arise in a single moment for one who has suppressed the hindrances. But owing to the profundity of the qualities of peace, or because of being intent on recollecting various kinds of qualities, the jhāna is only access, without reaching absorption. This very jhāna is reckoned as “recollection of peace” because it arises by way of recollecting the qualities of peace.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VIII, Upasamānussatikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=285&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=VGFzc2V2YeG5gyBtYWRhbmltbWFkYW5hdMSBZGlndeG5h2F2YXNlbmEgdXBhc2FtYeG5gyBhbnVzc2FyYXRvIG5ldmEgdGFzbWnhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Visuddhimagga, who it succeeds for)
> **Cha anussatiyo viya ca ayampi ariyasāvakasseva ijjhati, evaṃ santepi upasamagarukena puthujjanenāpi manasi kātabbā. Sutavasenāpi hi upasame cittaṃ pasīdati.**  
> And like the six recollections, this too succeeds only for a noble disciple; even if it is so, it should nevertheless be brought to mind by an ordinary person who values peace. For even by hearsay, the mind acquires confidence in peace.  
>  
> — *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (VIII, Upasamānussatikathā) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_01&page_number=286&name=visuddhimaggo+%28pa%29&note=&selected_text=Q2hhIGFudXNzYXRpeW8gdml5YSBjYSBheWFtcGkgYXJpeWFzxIF2YWthc3NldmE%3D)
