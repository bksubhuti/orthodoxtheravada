### Grammatical Analysis
**Vīmaṃsā**: [f.] investigation; inquiry. In the Vibhaṅga it is defined by a string of synonyms: wisdom (*paññā*), understanding (*pajānanā*), non-delusion (*amoho*), investigation of phenomena (*dhammavicayo*) and right view (*sammādiṭṭhi*).

### Orthodox Definition
*Vīmaṃsā* is the fourth of the four bases of psychic power (*[iddhipādā](/glossary/iddhipada/)*), after desire (*chanda*), energy (*vīriya*) and mind (*citta*). The Mūla (SN, Iddhipādasaṃyutta) says: if a bhikkhu, relying on investigation (*vīmaṃsaṃ nissāya*), obtains concentration and unification of mind, this is called *vīmaṃsāsamādhi*; with the volitional formations of striving (*padhānasaṅkhārā*) it makes up the *vīmaṃsāsamādhippadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgato iddhipādo*.

The Vibhaṅga (Iddhipādavibhaṅga, §442) asks "Tattha katamā vīmaṃsā?" and answers with the synonyms above, so in the Abhidhamma it is wisdom; and it lists the four: *chandiddhipādo, vīriyiddhipādo, cittiddhipādo, vīmaṃsiddhipādo*.

The Visuddhimagga (Ch. XII, Iddhividhaniddesa) gives the passage with the four bases, and the eight steps (*aṭṭha padāni*), where it says the investigation is not the concentration, and the concentration is not the investigation: they are different things. The Sammohavinodanī (Vibh-a 1436) says that desire, concentration and the volitional formation of striving are both "success" and bases, and likewise energy, mind and investigation with concentration and striving, while the four conjoined aggregates are the base (*iddhipādo*).

### Quote
> **“Vīmaṃsaṃ ce, bhikkhave, bhikkhu nissāya labhati samādhiṃ, labhati cittassa ekaggataṃ – ayaṃ vuccati ‘ vīmaṃsāsamādhi ’. So anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati … pe … uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Ime vuccanti ‘ padhānasaṅkhārā ’ ti. Iti ayañca vīmaṃsā, ayañca vīmaṃsāsamādhi, ime ca padhānasaṅkhārā – ayaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave,‘ vīmaṃsāsamādhippadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgato iddhipādo”’ ti.**  
> “Monks, if a bhikkhu, relying on investigation, obtains concentration, obtains unification of mind—this is called concentration due to investigation. He generates desire, endeavors, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort for the non-arising of bad, unwholesome states that have not yet arisen … for the endurance, non-confusion, multiplication, extension, development, and fulfilment of wholesome states that have already arisen. These are called volitional formations of striving. Thus, this investigation, this concentration due to investigation, and these volitional formations of striving—this is called, monks, the basis of psychic power possessing concentration due to investigation, with energy and volitional formations.”  
>  
> — [Saṁyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (Iddhipādasaṃyutta, Chandasamādhi Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=235&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFbEq21h4bmDc2HhuYMgY2UsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgYmhpa2todSBuaXNzxIF5YQ%3D%3D)

> **Cattāro iddhipādā – chandiddhipādo, vīriyiddhipādo, cittiddhipādo, vīmaṃsiddhipādo.**  
> The four bases for psychic potency (cattāro iddhipādā) are: the base of psychic power of will (chandiddhipādo), the base of psychic power of energy (vīriyiddhipādo), the base of psychic power of consciousness (cittiddhipādo), the base of psychic power of investigation (vīmaṃsiddhipādo).  
>  
> — [Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/) (Iddhipādavibhaṅga, §457) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_bi_02&page_number=232&name=vibha%E1%B9%85gap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDU3LiBDYXR0xIFybyBpZGRoaXDEgWTEgSDigJMgY2hhbmRpZGRoaXDEgWRvLCB2xKtyaXlpZGRoaXDEgWRvLA%3D%3D)

> **Idha bhikkhu chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti. Vīriya … citta … vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti. Vīriyañce bhikkhu … cittañce bhikkhu … vīmaṃsañce bhikkhu nissāya labhati samādhiṃ, labhati cittassekaggataṃ. Vīmaṃsā na samādhi, samādhi na vīmaṃsā. Aññā vīmaṃsā, añño samādhi.**  
> Here, a bhikkhu develops the basis for psychic power that has immersion due to zeal and the volitional formation to endeavour. He develops the basis for psychic power that has immersion due to energy... mind... investigation and the volitional formation to endeavour. If a bhikkhu... relying on energy... mind... investigation, obtains concentration, obtains one-pointedness of mind. Then the investigation is not the concentration; the concentration is not the investigation. Investigation is one thing, concentration is another.  
>

XII, Iddhividhaniddesa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_bi_02&page_number=13&name=visuddhimaggo+%28du%29&note=&selected_text=SWRoYSBiaGlra2h1IGNoYW5kYXNhbcSBZGhpcGFkaMSBbmFzYeG5hWtoxIFyYXNhbWFubsSBZ2F0YeG5gyBpZGRoaXDEgWRh4bmDIGJoxIF2ZXRpLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **[Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)**: *Chandasamādhi Sutta* (Iddhipādasaṃyutta, S-v 1609–1613) – the four *samādhi* by way of desire, energy, mind and investigation. (The *Vīmaṃsaka Sutta*, MN 47, M-i 1662, is about a bhikkhu who is an investigator, *vīmaṃsako*, examining the Tathāgata; it is a related use of the word, not the iddhipāda.)
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Iddhipādavibhaṅga, §441–442; Vibh 1461–1462) – the definition of *vīmaṃsā* as wisdom; Vibh 1503:1 (§457) lists the four *iddhipādā*.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Ch. XII, Vism-ii 4–149; §382–383 at Vism-ii 58–60); *Sammohavinodanī* (Vibh-a 1436).
