### Grammatical Analysis
**Pātimokkha**: [nt.] The code of monastic discipline. The Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī gives two explanations of the word: *atipamokkhaṃ atiseṭṭhaṃ atiuttamaṃ*, "the most foremost, most excellent, most supreme"; and, for the virtue (*sīla*), "whoever guards (*pāti*) it, it frees (*mokkheti*) him from the woeful states and from fears such as self-reproach". It says the word is used in two senses: virtue (*sīlapātimokkha*, as in *pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto*) and the text (*ganthapātimokkha*, the code recited).

### Orthodox Definition
The *Pātimokkha* is the code of monastic rules laid down by the Buddha. The Buddha allowed its recitation (*pātimokkhuddesa*) as the Uposatha observance of the monks (Vin-iii 770), and explained the word: "*Pātimokkha* means: this is the beginning, the gateway, the foremost of wholesome states" (Mahāvagga 135). The Vibhaṅga defines it as ethical conduct, the foundation, the beginning, restraint, "for the attainment of wholesome states" (Vibh 511). 

The Aṭṭhakathā (Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī) distinguishes the text-Pātimokkha into the Bhikkhu-Pātimokkha, arranged in five sections of recitation (*nidāna, pārājika, saṅghādisesa, aniyata, vitthāra*), and the Bhikkhunī-Pātimokkha, arranged in four (Kkh 1231). The Dīghanikāya commentary (Sv-i 90-91) counts the Bhikkhus' rules as **227** (4 *pārājika*, 13 *saṅghādisesa*, 2 *aniyata*, 30 *nissaggiya pācittiya*, 92 *pācittiya*, 4 *pāṭidesanīya*, 75 *sekhiya*, and the 7 *adhikaraṇasamatha*), and the Bhikkhunīs' rules as 304 (8, 17, 30, 166, 8, 75) with the same 7 *adhikaraṇasamatha* set down separately, which gives **311**.

The recitation is made on the Uposatha day by a Saṅgha of at least four: "I allow you to recite the Pātimokkha when there are four of you"; with three or two, the Uposatha is made by way of purity (*pārisuddhi*) (Vin-iii 907, 914). A monk who has fallen into an offence on the Uposatha day must first confess it to another monk, since "the Uposatha should not be carried out by an offender" (Vin-iii 917).

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, the Pātimokkha allowed)
> **“yaṃnūnāhaṃ yāni mayā bhikkhūnaṃ paññattāni sikkhāpadāni, tāni nesaṃ pātimokkhuddesaṃ anujāneyyaṃ. So nesaṃ bhavissati uposathakamman” ti. Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, pātimokkhaṃ uddisituṃ.**  
> “What if I were to allow for those training rules laid down by me for the monks a recitation of the Pātimokkha for them? That would be their Uposatha observance.” I allow you, monks, to recite the Pātimokkha.  
>

### Quote (Mūla: Mahāvagga, the quorum)
> **Tena kho pana samayena aññatarasmiṃ āvāse tadahuposathe cattāro bhikkhū viharanti. Atha kho tesaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ etadahosi – “bhagavatā paññattaṃ ‘ uposatho kātabbo ’ ti, mayañcamhā cattāro janā, kathaṃ nu kho amhehi uposatho kātabbo” ti? Bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ. Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, catunnaṃ pātimokkhaṃ uddisitunti.**  
> 168. Now at that time in a certain residence four bhikkhus were staying on an Uposatha day. Then it occurred to these bhikkhus— “It is laid down by the Blessed One that ‘the Uposatha should be carried out,’ but we are four persons; how should the Uposatha be carried out by us?” They told this matter to the Blessed One. “I allow you, monks, to recite the Pātimokkha when there are four of you.”  
>  
> — [Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/) (Uposathakkhandhaka, 168) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_03&page_number=166&name=mah%C4%81vaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTY4LiBUZW5hIGtobyBwYW5hIHNhbWF5ZW5hIGHDscOxYXRhcmFzbWnhuYM%3D)

### Quote (Aṭṭhakathā: Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī)
> **Tattha pātimokkhanti paatimokkhaṃ atipamokkhaṃ atiseṭṭhaṃ atiuttamanti attho. Tathā hi “pātimokkhasaṃvarasaṃvuto viharatī” ti (ma. ni.1.69;3.75; vibha.508) ca “ādimetaṃ mukhametaṃ pamukhametaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ, tena vuccati pātimokkhan” ti (mahāva.135) ca ādīsu sīlaṃ pātimokkhanti vuccati,“ubhayāni kho panassa pātimokkhāni vitthārena svāgatāni hontī” tiādīsu (pāci.147; a. ni.8.52;10.33) gantho pātimokkhanti vuccati. Tattha sīlaṃ yo naṃ pāti rakkhati, taṃ mokkheti mocayati āpāyikādīhi dukkhehi, attānuvādādīhi vā bhayehīti pātimokkhaṃ. Gantho pana tassa pātimokkhassa jotakattā pātimokkhanti vuccati.**  
> Therein, pātimokkha means the most foremost, most excellent, most supreme. For it is said in such passages as, “He dwells restrained by the Pātimokkha restraint,” and “This is the beginning, this is the gateway, this is the foremost of skillful mental phenomena; therefore it is called the Pātimokkha,” virtue is called the Pātimokkha. In such passages as, “Both his monastic codes have been well transmitted in detail,” the text is called the Pātimokkha. Therein, virtue (sīla) is the Pātimokkha because whoever guards it is liberated from woeful states, or from fears such as self-reproach. The text (gantha), however, is called the Pātimokkha because it illuminates that Pātimokkha.  
>  
> — [Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī](/glossary/kankhavitarani/) (Nidānavaṇṇanā, Kkh 1230) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=annya_vi_01&page_number=84&name=dvem%C4%81tik%C4%81+ka%E1%B9%85kh%C4%81vitara%E1%B9%87%C4%AB&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHDEgXRpbW9ra2hhbnRpIHBhYXRpbW9ra2hh4bmDIGF0aXBhbW9ra2hh4bmDIGF0aXNl4bmt4bmtaGHhuYMgYXRpdXR0YW1hbnRp)
