### Overview
While [kamma](/glossary/kamma/) is commonly understood as a simple binary of "good" and "bad," the Buddha introduced a fourfold classification to account for the complexity of worldly actions and, most importantly, the specific type of action that leads to liberation and the end of all kamma.

### The List
1. **Kaṇhaṃ kammaṃ kaṇhavipākaṃ** - Dark kamma with a dark result: one generates afflictive (*sabyābajjha*) bodily, verbal and mental activities, is reborn in an afflictive world, is touched by afflictive contacts and feels utterly painful feeling, like the beings in hell (AN 4.233, A-iv 1760).
2. **Sukkaṃ kammaṃ sukkavipākaṃ** - Bright kamma with a bright result: one generates unafflictive (*abyābajjha*) bodily, verbal and mental activities, is reborn in an unafflictive world and feels utterly pleasant feeling, like the Subhakiṇhā devas (AN 4.233, A-iv 1761). Another sutta of the series (A-iv 1774) describes it as abstaining from killing, stealing, sexual misconduct, false speech and liquor.
3. **Kaṇhasukkaṃ kammaṃ kaṇhasukkavipākaṃ** - Dark-and-bright kamma with a dark-and-bright result: one generates both afflictive and unafflictive activities, is reborn in a world of both, and feels mixed pleasure and pain, like some humans, some devas and some in the lower realms (*vinipātikā*) (A-iv 1762).
4. **Akaṇhaṃ asukkaṃ kammaṃ akaṇhaasukkavipākaṃ** - Neither-dark-nor-bright kamma with a neither-dark-nor-bright result, which leads to the ending of kamma: in the Vitthāra Sutta it is the volition for abandoning the first three kinds (A-iv 1763); in the Ariyamagga Sutta (AN 4.237) it is right view up to right concentration, the Noble Eightfold Path (A-iv 1788).

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, kammāni mayā sayaṃ abhiññā sacchikatvā paveditāni. Katamāni cattāri? Atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṃ kaṇhaṃ kaṇhavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṃ sukkaṃ sukkavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṃ kaṇhasukkaṃ kaṇhasukkavipākaṃ; atthi, bhikkhave, kammaṃ akaṇhaasukkaṃ akaṇhaasukkavipākaṃ kammakkhayāya saṃvattati.**  
> “Monks, there are these four actions proclaimed by me, having realized them for myself with direct knowledge. What are the four? There is, monks, dark action with a dark result; there is, monks, bright action with a bright result; there is, monks, dark-and-bright action with a dark-and-bright result; there is, monks, action that is neither dark nor bright with a neither-dark-nor-bright result, which leads to the ending of kamma.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 4.232, Saṃkhitta Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=553&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjMyLuKAmOKAmCBDYXR0xIFyaW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGthbW3EgW5pIG1hecSBIHNheWHhuYM%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Saṃkhitta [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 4.232, A-iv 1756–1757) – The four named, in brief. *Vitthāra Sutta* (AN 4.233, A-iv 1759–1763) – The four defined in detail. *Ariyamagga Sutta* (AN 4.237, A-iv 1783–1788) – The fourth defined as the path.
