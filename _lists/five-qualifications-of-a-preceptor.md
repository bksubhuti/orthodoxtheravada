### Overview
In the Theravāda monastic system, a newly ordained monk must live under the strict guidance of a senior teacher (*Upajjhāya*) for at least five years (Vin-iii 574:6). The Buddha laid down requirements for who is allowed to give ordination and provide this dependency (*nissaya*). Besides requiring ten years of seniority (Vin-iii 417:18), the preceptor must possess the five *asekkha* qualities.

### The List
1. **Asekkha [Sīla](/glossary/sila/)kkhandha** - The Adept's Body of Virtue: The senior monk is possessed of the *asekkha* (beyond training) aggregate of virtue.
2. **Asekkha [Samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)kkhandha** - The Adept's Body of Concentration: The senior monk is possessed of the *asekkha* aggregate of concentration.
3. **Asekkha [Paññā](/glossary/panna/)kkhandha** - The Adept's Body of Wisdom: The senior monk is possessed of the *asekkha* aggregate of wisdom.
4. **Asekkha Vimuttikkhandha** - The Adept's Body of Liberation: The senior monk is possessed of the *asekkha* aggregate of liberation.
5. **Asekkha Vimuttiñāṇadassanakkhandha** - The Adept's Body of Knowledge and Vision of Liberation: The senior monk is possessed of the *asekkha* aggregate of knowledge and vision of liberation.

### Quote
> **“Pañcahi, bhikkhave, aṅgehi samannāgatena bhikkhunā upasampādetabbaṃ, nissayo dātabbo, sāmaṇero upaṭṭhāpetabbo. Asekkhena sīlakkhandhena samannāgato hoti, asekkhena samādhikkhandhena samannāgato hoti, asekkhena paññākkhandhena samannāgato hoti, asekkhena vimuttikkhandhena samannāgato hoti, asekkhena vimuttiñāṇadassanakkhandhena samannāgato hoti – imehi kho, bhikkhave, pañcahaṅgehi samannāgatena bhikkhunā upasampādetabbaṃ, nissayo dātabbo, sāmaṇero upaṭṭhāpetabbo.**  
> “Monks, if a monk is possessed of five qualities he may ordain, he may give guidance, a novice may attend him. If he is possessed of an adept’s body of moral habit, if he is possessed of an adept’s body of concentration, if he is possessed of an adept’s body of wisdom, if he is possessed of an adept’s body of freedom, if he is possessed of an adept’s body of vision and knowledge of freedom—monks, if a monk is possessed of these five qualities he may ordain, he may give guidance, a novice may attend him.”  
>
