### Overview
Living in the wilderness is only spiritually fruitful if driven by the right internal motivation. The Buddha described five kinds of forest dweller: one through stupidity and folly, one through corrupt wishes and greed, one through madness and mental distraction, one because it is praised by the Buddhas and their disciples, and one for the sake of five wholesome motives. These five motives, taken together, mark the foremost kind (A-v 1256:5).

### The List
1. **Appicchataṃyeva nissāya** - For the sake of having few wishes.
2. **Santuṭṭhiṃyeva nissāya** - For the sake of contentment.
3. **Sallekhaṃyeva nissāya** - For the sake of self-effacement.
4. **Pavivekaṃyeva nissāya** - For the sake of seclusion.
5. **Idamatthitaṃyeva nissāya** - For the sake of simplicity (*idamatthitā*): he relies only on this noble practice and not on any worldly gain (Mp-v 377:7).

### Quote
> **“Pañcime, bhikkhave, āraññikā. Katame pañca? Mandattā momūhattā āraññiko hoti, pāpiccho icchāpakato āraññiko hoti, ummādā cittakkhepā āraññiko hoti, vaṇṇitaṃ buddhehi buddhasāvakehīti āraññiko hoti, appicchataṃyeva nissāya santuṭṭhiṃyeva nissāya sallekhaṃyeva nissāya pavivekaṃyeva nissāya idamatthitaṃyeva nissāya āraññiko hoti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca āraññikā.**  
> “Mendicants, there are these five kinds of wilderness dwellers. What five? One may be a wilderness dweller because of stupidity and folly; or because of corrupt wishes, being overwhelmed by greed; or because of madness and mental distraction; or because it is praised by the Buddhas and their disciples; or for the sake of having few wishes, for the sake of contentment, for the sake of self-effacement, for the sake of seclusion, and for the sake of simplicity. These indeed, mendicants, are the five kinds of wilderness dwellers.  
>
