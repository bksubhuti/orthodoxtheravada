### Overview
Directly related to the mental barrennesses, the "bondages" or "shackles" of the mind (*cetasovinibandha*) are deep-seated attachments that prevent a practitioner from putting forth full effort. While the barrennesses relate to doubt and anger, the bondages relate to subtle lust, comfort, and wrong spiritual ambition.

### The List
1. **Lust for sensual pleasures:** Not being free from desire and affection for the five strands of sensual pleasure.
2. **Lust for the body:** Not being free from lust, desire, affection, thirst, passion and craving for the body (*kāye*).
3. **Lust for form:** Not being free from lust, desire, affection, thirst, passion and craving for forms (*rūpe*).
4. **Indulgence in eating and sleeping:** Eating as much as one wants until the belly is full, and then indulging in the pleasure of sleeping, lying down, and drowsiness.
5. **Practicing for heavenly rebirth:** Living the holy life directed to rebirth in a certain order of devas: "By this virtue or observance or austerity or holy life, may I become a god or one of the gods!" (M-i 643:1-2).

### Quote
> **“Katamāssa pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu kāme avītarāgo hoti avigatacchando avigatapemo avigatapipāso avigatapariḷāho avigatataṇho.**  
> 186. What are the five shackles of the heart that he has not eradicated? Here, mendicants, a bhikkhu is not free from lust for sensual pleasures, not free from desire, not free from affection, not free from thirst, not free from passion, and not free from craving.

### Textual References
