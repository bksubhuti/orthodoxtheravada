### Overview
There are specific limits to human intellect. The Buddha said that anyone who conjectures about these four would partake of madness and vexation (*ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī*). These four subjects are *acinteyya* (unthinkable/inconceivable).

### The List
1. **Buddhavisayo** - The domain (range) of the Buddhas: the Aṭṭhakathā says it is the occurrence and power of the Buddha-qualities such as the knowledge of omniscience (*sabbaññutaññāṇādīnaṃ buddhaguṇānaṃ pavatti ca ānubhāvo ca*, Mp-iv 1321).
2. **Jhānavisayo** - The jhāna-range of one in jhāna: the Aṭṭhakathā says it is the range of the abhiññā-jhāna (Mp-iv 1321).
3. **Kammavipāko** - The result of kamma: the Aṭṭhakathā says it is the result of kamma such as that to be felt in this very life (*diṭṭhadhammavedanīya*) and the rest (Mp-iv 1321).
4. **Lokacintā** - Speculation about the world: the Aṭṭhakathā gives the questions "By whom were the moon and sun made? By whom the great earth, the great ocean? By whom were beings produced? By whom the mountains, the mango, palm and coconut trees?" (Mp-iv 1321).

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, acinteyyāni, na cintetabbāni; yāni cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa. Katamāni cattāri? Buddhānaṃ, bhikkhave, buddhavisayo acinteyyo, na cintetabbo; yaṃ cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa. Jhāyissa, bhikkhave, jhānavisayo acinteyyo, na cintetabbo; yaṃ cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa. Kammavipāko, bhikkhave, acinteyyo, na cintetabbo; yaṃ cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa. Lokacintā, bhikkhave, acinteyyā, na cintetabbā; yaṃ cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assa. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, cattāri acinteyyāni, na cintetabbāni; yāni cintento ummādassa vighātassa bhāgī assā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these four unconjecturables that are not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about them would partake of madness and vexation. What are the four? The Buddha-range (budhavisaya) of the Buddhas, monks, is an unconjecturable that is not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about it would partake of madness and vexation. The jhana-range (jhānavisaya) of one in jhana, monks, is an unconjecturable that is not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about it would partake of madness and vexation. The result of kamma (kammavipāka), monks, is an unconjecturable that is not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about it would partake of madness and vexation. Speculation about the world (lokacintā), monks, is an unconjecturable that is not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about it would partake of madness and vexation. These indeed, monks, are the four unconjecturables that are not to be conjectured about; anyone who conjectured about them would partake of madness and vexation.”  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Acinteyya [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 4.77) – The Buddha strictly warns practitioners not to obsess over these four subjects, as they lead to madness and vexation.
