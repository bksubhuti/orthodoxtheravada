### Overview
In the Dhammakathika Sutta (AN 4.139) the Buddha divides speakers on the Dhamma (*dhammakathikā*) into four by two pairs of qualities: whether they speak little (*appaṃ*) or much (*bahuṃ*), and whether what they say is on topic (*sahitaṃ*) or off topic (*asahitaṃ*). Each case also says whether the assembly is skilled in telling what is on topic from what is off topic. In all four the sutta says the speaker "counts as a speaker on the Dhamma" to such an assembly.

### The List
1. **Appañca bhāsati asahitañca** - Speaks little and off topic; the assembly is not skilled in what is on topic and off topic (A-iv 1182).
2. **Appañca bhāsati sahitañca** - Speaks little but stays on topic; the assembly is skilled in what is on topic and off topic (A-iv 1183).
3. **Bahuñca bhāsati asahitañca** - Speaks much but off topic; the assembly is not skilled in what is on topic and off topic (A-iv 1184).
4. **Bahuñca bhāsati sahitañca** - Speaks much and stays on topic; the assembly is skilled in what is on topic and off topic (A-iv 1185).

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, dhammakathikā. Katame cattāro? Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco dhammakathiko appañca bhāsati asahitañca; parisā cassa na kusalā hoti sahitāsahitassa. Evarūpo, bhikkhave, dhammakathiko evarūpāya parisāya dhammakathikotveva saṅkhaṃ gacchati. “Idha pana, bhikkhave, ekacco dhammakathiko appañca bhāsati sahitañca; parisā cassa kusalā hoti sahitāsahitassa.**  
> “Monks, there are these four speakers on the Dhamma. What four? Here, monks, someone who is a speaker on the Dhamma speaks little and off topic. And their assembly is not skilled in what is on topic and what is off topic. Such a speaker on the Dhamma, to such an assembly, counts as a speaker on the Dhamma. But here, monks, someone who is a speaker on the Dhamma speaks little but stays on topic. And their assembly is skilled in what is on topic and what is off topic.  
>
