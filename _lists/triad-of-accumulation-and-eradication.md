### Overview
The tenth triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā classifies phenomena based on their directionality regarding [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). It asks: is this action building up the cycle of rebirth, is it tearing down the cycle of rebirth, or is it doing neither?

### The List
1. **Ācayagāmino dhammā** - States leading to accumulation: the wholesome and unwholesome states with outflows (*sāsavā kusalākusalā*) of the sense, fine-material and immaterial planes (Dhs 1788:2). The Aṭṭhakathā: accumulation (*ācaya*) is built up by deeds and defilements and is a name for rebirth, death, destiny and continuance (As 860:1-2); this is a designation for wholesome and unwholesome states with outflows (As 860:4).
2. **Apacayagāmino dhammā** - States leading to dispersal (*apacaya*): the four supramundane paths (*[magga](/glossary/magga/)*) (Dhs 1789:2). The Aṭṭhakathā: Nibbāna is apacaya, being without accumulation, and the noble paths lead to dispersal by taking it as object (As 860:5-7); they disperse the continuance, as a man destroys piled-up bricks (As 860:9).
3. **Nevācayagāmināpacayagāmino dhammā** - States leading to neither accumulation nor dispersal: the results of wholesome and unwholesome states (including the supramundane ones), the functional states (*kiriyā*) that are neither wholesome, unwholesome nor kamma-result, all matter, and the unconditioned element ([Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)) (Dhs 1790:2-5). The third term is stated by rejecting both (As 860:10).

### Quote
> **(Ka) ācayagāmino dhammā. (Kha) apacayagāmino dhammā. (Ga) nevācayagāmināpacayagāmino dhammā.**  
> 10.(Ka) Mental phenomena leading to accumulation. (Kha) Mental phenomena leading to dispersal. (Ga) Mental phenomena neither leading to accumulation nor leading to dispersal.  
>
