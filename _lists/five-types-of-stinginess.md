### Overview
Stinginess or meanness (*[macchariya](/glossary/macchariya/)*) is described in the Visuddhimagga-aṭṭhakathā as arising in the manner of not tolerating that others share in these five things (*aññesaṃ sādhāraṇabhāvaṃ asahanākārena pavattāni*; Vism-ii 1952:1). The Buddha names the five in AN 5.254, and the Vibhaṅga and Aṭṭhasālinī (As 2294-2299) explain each.

### The List
1. **Āvāsamacchariya** - Stinginess regarding dwellings: The dwelling means a whole monastery, a cell, a room, a night-quarters or day-quarters. A monk does not want a well-behaved, amiable monk to come there, and thinks of one who has come, "Let him leave quickly"; wanting troublemakers not to stay is not this stinginess (As 2295:1-6).
2. **Kulamacchariya** - Stinginess regarding families/supporters: "Family" means a supporting family or a family of relatives. Not wanting another to approach it is family-stinginess; not wanting an evil person to approach is not (As 2296:1-5).
3. **Lābhamacchariya** - Stinginess regarding gains: "Gain" means gain of the four requisites. Thinking, "May a virtuous monk not receive it" is gain-stinginess (As 2297:1-2).
4. **Vaṇṇamacchariya** - Stinginess regarding praise/reputation: *Vaṇṇa* is either bodily appearance or praise of virtue. The stingy person does not want to speak of another's good looks, or of another's virtue, ascetic practice, conduct and behaviour (As 2298:1-3).
5. **Dhammamacchariya** - Stinginess regarding the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/): Dhamma-stinginess belongs to the Dhamma of the texts (*tantidhamme*): one does not want another to learn a secret text or line of exposition that one knows. Noble disciples are not stingy with the Dhamma of penetration, and one who withholds after examining the person or the Dhamma is not stingy (As 2299:1-6). The Buddha called this the meanest (*paṭikuṭṭhaṃ*) of the five (A-v 1560:4).

### Quote
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, macchariyāni. Katamāni pañca? Āvāsamacchariyaṃ, kulamacchariyaṃ, lābhamacchariyaṃ, vaṇṇamacchariyaṃ, dhammamacchariyaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañca macchariyāni.**  
> “Monks, there are these five forms of stinginess. What are the five? Stinginess as to one’s monastery, stinginess as to one’s family, stinginess as to one’s gains, stinginess as to one’s status, and stinginess as to the Dhamma. These, monks, are the five forms of stinginess.  
>
