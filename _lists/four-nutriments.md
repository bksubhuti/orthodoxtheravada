### Overview
"All beings subsist on nutriment." The Buddha elevated the concept of "food" far beyond the physical realm. To understand how suffering continues, one must understand how the cycle of birth and death is continually "fed." These four nutriments sustain both biological life and the continuation of [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/).

### The List
1. **Kabaḷīkārāhāra** - Physical Food: Gross or subtle material food. The Buddha compared it to a couple eating the flesh of their only son to cross a desert, eating only to cross the desert; when it is fully understood, lust for the five cords of sensual pleasure is fully understood (S-ii 469-470).
2. **Phassāhāra** - Contact: The meeting of sense organ, object, and consciousness. The Buddha compared it to a flayed cow nibbled at by the creatures wherever she stands; when it is fully understood, the three kinds of feeling (*[vedanā](/glossary/vedana/)*) are fully understood.
3. **Manosañcetanāhāra** - Mental Volition: The intention or will (*[cetanā](/glossary/cetana/)*). The Buddha compared it to a man dragged by two strong men towards a pit of glowing coals; when it is fully understood, the three kinds of craving are fully understood (S-ii 472).
4. **Viññāṇāhāra** - Consciousness: The Buddha compared it to a bandit struck with a hundred spears in the morning, at noon and in the evening; when it is fully understood, name-and-form (*[nāmarūpa](/glossary/namarupa/)*) is fully understood.

### Quote
> **Sāvatthiyaṃ … pe …“cattārome, bhikkhave, āhārā bhūtānaṃ vā sattānaṃ ṭhitiyā sambhavesīnaṃ vā anuggahāya. Katame cattāro? Kabaḷīkāro āhāro oḷāriko vā sukhumo vā, phasso dutiyo, manosañcetanā tatiyā, viññāṇaṃ catutthaṃ.**  
> At Sāvatthī. “Bhikkhus, there are these four kinds of nutriment for the maintenance of beings that have already come to be and for the assistance of those about to come to be. What four? The nutriment edible food, gross or subtle; second, contact; third, mental volition; fourth, consciousness.  
>
