### Overview
The Aṭṭhakathā divide the thirteen ascetic practices (*dhutaṅga*, Vism-i 419:2) by the requisite they concern: "two connected with robes, five connected with almsfood, five connected with lodging, one connected with energy" (Vism-i 642:1). The two robe practices are the refuse-rag wearer's and the triple-robe wearer's (Sv-iii 804:1). The sitter's practice (*nesajjikaṅga*) is the one connected with energy (Vism-i 642:2), and the tree-root and open-air practices belong to lodging.

### The List
1. **Paṃsukūlikaṅga** - The rag-robe wearer's practice: undertaken with the words "I refuse robes given by householders" (*gahapatidānacīvaraṃ paṭikkhipāmi*) or "I undertake the rag-robe wearer's practice" (Vism-i 439:2).
2. **Tecīvarikaṅga** - The triple-robe wearer's practice: undertaken with the words "I refuse a fourth robe" (*catutthakacīvaraṃ paṭikkhipāmi*) or "I undertake the triple-robe wearer's practice" (Vism-i 456:1).

### Quote
> **Cīvarapaṭisaṃyuttāni dhutaṅgāni nāma paṃsukūlikaṅgañceva tecīvarikaṅgañca. Tesaṃ vitthārakathā visuddhimaggato veditabbā.**  
> The ascetic practices connected with robes (cīvarapaṭisaṃyuttāni dhutaṅgāni) are both the practice of wearing rag-robes and the practice of having only three robes. Their elaboration should be understood from the Visuddhimagga (visuddhimaggato).  
>  
> — Sumaṅgalavilāsinī (Dīgha-aṭṭhakathā, Aṭṭhakathā layer) on the Saṅgīti Sutta (DN 33), the Ariyavaṃsa of contentment with robes · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_di_03&page_number=195&name=p%C4%81thikavagga%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=Q8SrdmFyYXBh4bmtaXNh4bmDeXV0dMSBbmkgZGh1dGHhuYVnxIFuaSBuxIFtYSBwYeG5g3N1a8WrbGlrYeG5hWdhw7FjZXZhIHRlY8SrdmFyaWth4bmFZ2HDsWNhLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* (Sv-iii 804:1-2, on the Saṅgīti Sutta, DN 33) – Names the two robe practices and sends the reader to the Visuddhimagga for the details.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter II, Dhutaṅganiddeso, Vism-i 417 "2. Dhutaṅganiddeso") – Lists the thirteen (Vism-i 419:2), classes them as two robe, five almsfood, five lodging and one energy practice (Vism-i 642:1), and gives the undertaking of each (Vism-i 439:2 and 456:1).
