### Overview
The Ten Recollections (*Anussati*) are core meditation subjects designed to inspire faith, generate joy, and overcome fear. According to the Visuddhimagga (Vism-i 776:1), all of these except mindfulness of the body and mindfulness of breathing bring access concentration (*upacāra-samādhi*) only; the rest bring absorption: mindfulness of the body leads to the first jhāna and mindfulness of breathing to all four jhānas (Vism-i 777:1-2).

### The List
1. **[Buddhānussati](/glossary/buddhanussati/)** - Recollection of the Buddha: Reflecting on the 9 supreme qualities of the Awakened One.
2. **[Dhammānussati](/glossary/dhammanussati/)** - Recollection of the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/): Reflecting on the perfect, timeless nature of the Teaching.
3. **[Saṅghānussati](/glossary/sanghanussati/)** - Recollection of the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/): Reflecting on the purity and worthiness of the Noble Disciples.
4. **[Sīlānussati](/glossary/silanussati/)** - Recollection of Virtue: Reflecting on the unbroken, unblemished purity of one's own moral precepts.
5. **[Cāgānussati](/glossary/caganussati/)** - Recollection of Generosity: Reflecting on one's own acts of selfless giving.
6. **[Devatānussati](/glossary/devatanussati/)** - Recollection of Deities: Reflecting on the faith and virtue that caused devas to be reborn in heaven, and verifying that those same qualities exist within oneself.
7. **Maraṇānussati** - Mindfulness of Death: Reflecting on the absolute certainty and unpredictability of one's own death.
8. **[Kāyagatāsati](/glossary/kayagatasati/)** - Mindfulness of the Body: Meditating on the parts of the body (hair, nails, teeth, skin, etc.) to destroy lust.
9. **[Ānāpānasati](/glossary/anapanasati/)** - Mindfulness of Breathing: Meditating on the in-breath and out-breath.
10. **[Upasamānussati](/glossary/upasamanussati/)** - Recollection of Peace: Reflecting on the supreme, cooling, unconditioned peace of [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Ekadhamma Suttas* (AN 1.296-297) – The Buddha names each of these ten recollections as a single thing that, when developed and cultivated, leads to dispassion, cessation, peace and Nibbāna. The Pāḷi text lists them in the order Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha, sīla, cāga, devatā, ānāpāna, maraṇa, kāyagatā, upasama (A-i 291:3); the order on this page is that of the Visuddhimagga (Vism-i 773:1).
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapters VII & VIII) – The complete manual for practicing all ten recollections.
