### Overview
In Buddhist cosmology, the biological process of birth is not limited to mammalian reproduction. [Kamma](/glossary/kamma/) is powerful enough to generate life through four distinct mechanisms, as set out by the Buddha in the Mahāsīhanāda Sutta.

### The List
1. **Aṇḍajā yoni** - Egg-born generation: beings born by breaking out of the shell of an egg (M-i 472:5).
2. **Jalābujā yoni** - Womb-born generation: beings born by breaking out from the caul (*vatthikosa*, M-i 472:7).
3. **Saṃsedajā yoni** - Moisture-born generation: beings born in a rotten fish, a rotten corpse, rotten porridge, a cesspit or a sewer (M-i 472:9). The commentary adds that this only points to undesirable places; beings are also born in ghee, oil, honey and molasses (Ps-i 1601).
4. **Opapātikā yoni** - Spontaneous generation: devas, denizens of hell, certain human beings and some beings in the lower worlds (M-i 472:11). The commentary explains that they appear "as if by springing up", without these causes; the devas from the Four Great Kings upwards are exclusively of this kind, while some humans and the rest of the lower-world beings are mixed (Ps-i 1601).

### Quote
> **“Catasso kho imā, sāriputta, yoniyo. Katamā catasso? Aṇḍajā yoni, jalābujā yoni, saṃsedajā yoni, opapātikā yoni.**  
> “Sāriputta, there are these four kinds of generation. What are the four? Egg-born generation, womb-born generation, moisture-born generation, and spontaneous generation.  
>
