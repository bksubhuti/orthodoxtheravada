### Overview
In the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) an accusation or reproof (*codanā*) has two roots (with a root or rootless), three grounds or matters (*vatthu*), five bases (*bhūmi*) and is made in two ways, by body or by speech (Vin-v 2566:3-2567:2). The three grounds (*codanāvatthūni*) are what is seen, heard and suspected (Vin-v 1976:22; 2566:7-8).

### The List
1. **Diṭṭhena** - By Seeing: The ground of what has been seen.
2. **Sutena** - By Hearing: The ground of what has been heard.
3. **Parisaṅkitena** - By Suspecting / Ground for Suspicion: The ground of what is suspected.

### Quote
> **Codanāya kati mūlāni, kati vatthūni, kati bhūmiyo, katihākārehi codeti? Codanāya dve mūlāni, tīṇi vatthūni, pañca bhūmiyo, dvīhākārehi codeti. Codanāya katamāni dve mūlāni? Samūlikā vā amūlikā vā – codanāya imāni dve mūlāni. Codanāya katamāni tīṇi vatthūni? Diṭṭhena sutena parisaṅkāya – codanāya imāni tīṇi vatthūni.**  
> How many roots of reproving, how many matters, how many (mental) planes, in how many ways does one reprove? Two roots of reproving, three matters, five (mental) planes; he reproves in two ways. What are the two roots of reproving? With root or rootless—these are the two roots of reproving. What are the three matters for reproving? About the seen, the heard, the suspected—these are the three matters for reproving.  
>  
> — [Vinaya Piṭaka](/glossary/vinaya-pitaka/) (Parivāra, Codanākaṇḍa) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_vi_05&page_number=282&name=pariv%C4%81rap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=Q29kYW7EgXlhIGthdGkgbcWrbMSBbmksIGthdGkgdmF0dGjFq25pLCBrYXRp)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Codanākaṇḍa* (Parivāra, Vin-v 2553:1; the three matters at Vin-v 2566:7-8; also the list of threes at Vin-v 1976:22 "Tīṇi codanāvatthūni – diṭṭhena, sutena, parisaṅkāya").
* **Canonical**: *[Pavāraṇā](/glossary/pavarana/) [Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/)* ([Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/), "4. Pavāraṇākkhandhako" Vin-iii 1226:1) – "Anujānāmi, bhikkhave, vassaṃvuṭṭhānaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ tīhi ṭhānehi pavāretuṃ – diṭṭhena vā sutena vā parisaṅkāya vā" (Vin-iii 1231:9): at the end of the rains each monk invites the Saṅgha to speak to him in regard to what has been seen, heard or suspected (invitation formula Vin-iii 1233:2).
* **Commentary**: Samantapāsādikā quotes the Parivāra list: "tīṇi vatthūni – diṭṭhaṃ, sutaṃ, parisaṅkitaṃ" (Sp-i 2364:3), and adds that the accuser should be established in fifteen qualities and the accused in truth and non-anger (Sp-i 2364:5).
