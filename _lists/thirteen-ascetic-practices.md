### Overview
The *Dhutaṅgas* are extreme ascetic practices voluntarily undertaken by Theravāda monastics to ruthlessly shake off defilements (specifically craving for comfort, food, and robes). They are not mandatory [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) rules, but the Buddha highly praised monks like Venerable Mahākassapa who observed them. 

### The List
**Regarding Robes:**
1. **Paṃsukūlik'aṅga** - Wearing only robes made from discarded rags (refuse-rag wearer).
2. **Tecīvarik'aṅga** - Possessing and wearing only three robes, never keeping a fourth.

**Regarding Food:**
3. **Piṇḍapātik'aṅga** - Eating only food collected on alms round, refusing invitations to laypeoples' houses.
4. **Sapadānacārik'aṅga** - Begging for alms strictly from house to house in order, not skipping poor houses.
5. **Ekāsanik'aṅga** - Eating only one meal a day at a single sitting.
6. **Pattapiṇḍik'aṅga** - Eating only from the alms bowl, mixing all food together, refusing plates.
7. **Khalupacchābhattik'aṅga** - Refusing all food offered after one has already started eating, even if still hungry.

**Regarding Dwellings:**
8. **Āraññik'aṅga** - Living exclusively in the forest, far from villages.
9. **Rukkhamūlik'aṅga** - Living exclusively at the root of a tree (not in a hut).
10. **Abbhokāsik'aṅga** - Living exclusively in the open air, with no roof or tree cover.
11. **Sosānik'aṅga** - Living exclusively in a charnel ground or cemetery.
12. **Yathāsanthatik'aṅga** - Sleeping in whatever bed/location is assigned, without complaining or requesting a better one.

**Regarding Posture:**
13. **Nesajjik'aṅga** - The Sitter's Practice: Never lying down. Sleeping only in a seated posture.

### Quote
> **Bhagavatā hi pariccattalokāmisānaṃ kāye ca jīvite ca anapekkhānaṃ anulomapaṭipadaṃyeva ārādhetukāmānaṃ kulaputtānaṃ terasadhutaṅgāni anuññātāni. Seyyathidaṃ – paṃsukūlikaṅgaṃ, tecīvarikaṅgaṃ, piṇḍapātikaṅgaṃ, sapadānacārikaṅgaṃ, ekāsanikaṅgaṃ, pattapiṇḍikaṅgaṃ, khalupacchābhattikaṅgaṃ, āraññikaṅgaṃ, rukkhamūlikaṅgaṃ, abbhokāsikaṅgaṃ, sosānikaṅgaṃ, yathāsanthatikaṅgaṃ, nesajjikaṅganti.**  
> For thirteen ascetic practices have been allowed by the Blessed One for clansmen who have renounced worldly attachments, who are indifferent to body and life, and who desire to accomplish the conforming practice. That is to say: the rag-robe wearer’s practice, the triple-robe wearer’s practice, the almsfood eater’s practice, the house-to-house alms round practice, the one-sitting eater’s practice, the bowl-food eater’s practice, the refusal of after-meal food practice, the forest dweller’s practice, the tree-root dweller’s practice, the open-air dweller’s practice, the cemetery dweller’s practice, the as-available-bed-and-seat dweller’s practice, and the sitter’s practice.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical (Mūla layer in TPR)**: *Milindapañha* (Mil 1805:2-3) – "Terasimāni, mahārāja, dhutaṅgāni … Katame terasa?" followed by the same thirteen in the same order.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter II) – Buddhaghosa provides the absolute definitive manual detailing exactly how to undertake, maintain, and grade the strictness of these 13 practices.
