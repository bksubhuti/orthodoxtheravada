### Overview
Within the 13 strict Ascetic Practices (*Dhutaṅga*) outlined in the Theravāda commentarial tradition, exactly five are connected with almsfood (*piṇḍapātapaṭisaṃyutta*). By undertaking these five voluntary rules, a monk breaks his reliance on luxury meals, simplifies his lifestyle, and guards his meditation time.

### The List
1. **Piṇḍapātik'aṅga** - The Alms-round Wearer's Practice: Consuming only food gathered directly inside one's bowl during the daily walk, completely refusing formal house invitations from lay supporters.
2. **Sapadānacārik'aṅga** - The House-to-house Walker's Practice: Walking sequentially down a street house by house without skipping any family based on their poverty or wealth, avoiding favoritism.
3. **Ekāsanik'aṅga** - The One-session Eater's Practice: Consuming food only at a single sitting per day. Once the monk rises from his seat, his meal is legally finished for the day.
4. **Pattapiṇḍik'aṅga** - The Bowl-eater's Practice: Eating from the bowl alone: refusing a second vessel (*dutiyakabhājana*); even a leaf is not allowed (Vism-i 513:1, 514:8).
5. **Khalupacchābhattik'aṅga** - The Later-food Refuser's Practice: Not eating again, after he has declined further food (*pavāretvā*), even if more food is made allowable (Vism-i 525:1).

### Quote
> **Piṇḍapātapaṭisaṃyuttāni pana pañca dhutaṅgāni – piṇḍapātikaṅgaṃ, sapadānacārikaṅgaṃ, ekāsanikaṅgaṃ, pattapiṇḍikaṅgaṃ, khalupacchābhattikaṅganti. Tesaṃ vitthārakathā visuddhimagge vuttā.**  
> There are five ascetic practices connected with almsfood: the almsfood-eater’s practice, the house-to-house walker’s practice, the one-sitting eater’s practice, the bowl-only eater’s practice, and the later-food refuser’s practice. Their detailed account is stated in the Visuddhimagga. (tr. Claude)
