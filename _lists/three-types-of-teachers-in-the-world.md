### Overview
In the Bharaṇḍukālāma Sutta the Buddha told Mahānāma the Sakyan that there are three kinds of teachers in the world, distinguished by what they declare the full understanding (*pariññā*) of: sensual pleasures, material forms, and feelings (A-iii 1140:4-10). The Aṭṭhakathā explains *pariññā* here as going beyond (*samatikkamo*) the thing (Mp-iii 934:11).

### The List
1. **Kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti; na rūpānaṃ, na vedanānaṃ** - A teacher who declares the full understanding of sensual pleasures (*kāma*), but not of material forms (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*) and not of feelings. The Aṭṭhakathā: he declares going beyond sensual pleasures (the first jhāna) but not going beyond forms (the formless attainments) or feelings (Nibbāna) (Mp-iii 934:11-13).
2. **Kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti; na vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti** - A teacher who declares the full understanding of sensual pleasures and of material forms, but not of feelings.
3. **Kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti** - A teacher who declares the full understanding of sensual pleasures, of material forms and of feelings. Explaining the same formula elsewhere (Mp-x 497:8), the Aṭṭhakathā says the Blessed One declares them by the first jhāna, the formless attainments and the Nibbāna of non-clinging respectively.

### Quote
> **“tayo khome, mahānāma, satthāro santo saṃvijjamānā lokasmiṃ. Katame tayo? Idha, mahānāma, ekacco satthā kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti; na rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, na vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti. Idha pana, mahānāma, ekacco satthā kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti; na vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti. Idha pana, mahānāma, ekacco satthā kāmānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, rūpānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti, vedanānaṃ pariññaṃ paññāpeti.**  
> “Mahānāma, there are these three kinds of teachers found existing in the world. What three? Here, Mahānāma, someone who is a teacher describes the full understanding of sensual pleasures; but he does not describe the full understanding of material forms, nor the full understanding of feelings. But then, Mahānāma, someone who is a teacher describes the full understanding of sensual pleasures and describes the full understanding of material forms; but he does not describe the full understanding of feelings. But then, Mahānāma, someone who is a teacher describes the full understanding of sensual pleasures, describes the full understanding of material forms, and describes the full understanding of feelings.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 3.127, Bharaṇḍukālāma Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_03&page_number=281&name=tikanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIHRheW8ga2hvbWUsIG1haMSBbsSBbWEsIHNhdHRoxIFybyBzYW50bw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Bharaṇḍukālāma [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 3.127; A-iii 1135:1 "4. Bharaṇḍukālāmasuttaṃ") – Addressed to Mahānāma the Sakyan at Kapilavatthu; the Buddha gives the three teachers, and then says their goal (*niṭṭhā*) is various, not one (A-iii 1140:12-1141:4).
* **Commentary**: Manorathapūraṇī on AN 3.127 (Mp-iii 933:1 "4. Bharaṇḍukālāmasuttavaṇṇanā"; Mp-iii 934:11-13). Other, different triads called "tayo satthāro" exist (DN 12, D-i 986:13; Pp 336-338).
