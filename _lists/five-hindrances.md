### Overview
The Five Hindrances (*[Nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*) are the primary psychological obstacles to meditation and clear understanding. They paralyze the mind, blind wisdom, and prevent the attainment of the jhānas. The Buddha compared a mind infected by these hindrances to water that is dyed, boiling, overgrown with algae, stirred by wind, or murky with mud.

### The List
1. **[Kāmacchanda](/glossary/kamachanda/)** - Sensual Desire: The craving for pleasant sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and touches. It pulls the mind away from the meditation object into worldly fantasies.
2. **[Byāpāda](/glossary/byapada/)** - Ill-will: Aversion, anger, and resentment. It burns the mind, making concentration impossible.
3. **[Thīna-middha](/glossary/thina-middha/)** - Sloth and Torpor: Mental dullness, laziness, and physical drowsiness. It causes the mind to sink, lose focus, and eventually fall asleep.
4. **[Uddhacca-kukkucca](/glossary/uddhacca-kukkucca/)** - Restlessness and Remorse: The mind's inability to settle. Restlessness is agitation about the present or future, while remorse is guilt and worry over past unwholesome deeds.
5. **[Vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)** - Doubt: Skeptical, paralyzing indecision regarding the Buddha, the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/), the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/), or the training. It halts all spiritual effort.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Nīvaraṇa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (SN 46.55) – The Buddha provides the vivid water similes, explaining how the hindrances prevent a person from seeing their own true benefit.
