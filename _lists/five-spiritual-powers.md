### Overview
The Five Spiritual Powers (*[Bala](/glossary/bala/)*) are identical in nature to the Five Spiritual Faculties (*[Indriya](/glossary/indriya/)*). The distinction lies in their degree of development. While faculties act as guiding forces, they become "powers" when they have grown so strong that they cannot be shaken or overcome by their opposing defilements.

### The List
1. **Saddhābala** - The Power of Faith: Faith that is not shaken by faithlessness (*assaddhiye na kampati*; Paṭis 1729:2, Mp-i 1521:3).
2. **Viriyabala** - The Power of Energy: Energy that is not shaken by laziness (*kosajje na kampati*; Paṭis 1730:2).
3. **Satibala** - The Power of Mindfulness: Mindfulness that is not shaken by unmindfulness (*muṭṭhassacca*; Mp-i 1521:3).
4. **Samādhibala** - The Power of Concentration: Concentration that is not shaken by restlessness (*uddhacca*; Mp-i 1521:3).
5. **Paññābala** - The Power of Wisdom: Wisdom that is not shaken by ignorance (*avijjā*; Mp-i 1521:3).

### Quote
> **705-716. “Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, balāni. Katamāni pañca? Saddhābalaṃ, vīriyabalaṃ, satibalaṃ, samādhibalaṃ, paññābalaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañca balānīti.**  
> “Mendicants, there are these five powers. What five? The power of faith, the power of energy, the power of mindfulness, the power of concentration, and the power of wisdom—these indeed, mendicants, are the five powers.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Bala Saṃyutta* (SN 50) – The Buddha says that a bhikkhu who develops and cultivates these five powers slants, slopes and inclines towards Nibbāna, as the Ganges slants towards the east (S-v 1498:4-5; the sutta group is "1-12. Balādisuttadvādasakaṃ", S-v 1497:1). Commentary: Mp-i 1521:3 calls them powers because they are unshakable (*akampiyaṭṭhena*).
