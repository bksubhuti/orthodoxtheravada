### Overview
In the chain of Dependent Origination (*[Paṭiccasamuppāda](/glossary/paticcasamuppada/)*), Craving (*[taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/)*) intensifies into Clinging (*[upādāna](/glossary/upadana/)*). While craving is the initial thirst, clinging is the aggressive grasping and holding onto the object. This gripping guarantees the formation of a new existence (*[bhava](/glossary/bhava/)*).

### The List
1. **Kāmupādāna** - Clinging to Sensuality: Lust, longing and craving for sensual pleasures (Dhs 1220).
2. **Diṭṭhupādāna** - Clinging to Views: All wrong view, such as 'there is no fruit of what is given', leaving aside clinging to rules and observances and clinging to a doctrine of self (Dhs 1221).
3. **Sīlabbatupādāna** - Clinging to Rules and Observances: The view that outside ascetics and brahmins have purification by virtue and observance (Dhs 1222).
4. **Attavādupādāna** - Clinging to a Doctrine of Self: The view of an untaught ordinary person who sees form, feeling, perception, volitional formations or consciousness as self (Dhs 1223).

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, upādānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Kāmupādānaṃ, diṭṭhupādānaṃ, sīlabbatupādānaṃ, attavādupādānaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these four kinds of clinging. What four? Clinging to sensual pleasures, clinging to views, clinging to rules and observances, and clinging to a doctrine of self.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 11, Cūḷasīhanāda Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=95&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTQzLuKAmOKAmCBDYXR0xIFyaW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIHVwxIFkxIFuxIFuaS4%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Cūḷasīhanāda [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 11) – Teaching the four clingings (M-i 431), the Buddha says that some ascetics and brahmins who claim to describe the full understanding of all clinging describe it only for some of the four: first only for clinging to sensual pleasures, then also for views, then also for rules and observances, but never for clinging to a doctrine of self (M-i 431–433). The same four are in the Upādāna Sutta (SN, S-v 386).
