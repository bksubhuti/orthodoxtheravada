### Overview
The term *bāla* (fool) in Pāḷi does not mean someone lacking in worldly intelligence or academic education. Rather, it refers to an individual who is morally blind. In AN 2.22 the Buddha names two kinds of fool.

### The List
1. **One who does not see a transgression as a transgression:** Having offended, he does not see his own offense and does not ask for forgiveness by making amends (Mp-ii 122:1).
2. **One who does not rightfully accept the confession of another:** When another says “I will not do this again, please forgive me,” he does not accept the transgression according to the Dhamma and does not say “I forgive you” (Mp-ii 122:3-4).

### Quote
> **‘“Dveme, bhikkhave, bālā. Katame dve? Yo ca accayaṃ accayato na passati, yo ca accayaṃ desentassa yathādhammaṃ nappaṭiggaṇhāti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, dve bālā ’ ti.**  
> “Monks, these two are fools. Which two? The one who doesn’t see a transgression as a transgression, and the one who doesn’t rightfully pardon another who has confessed a transgression. These two are fools.”  
>
