### Overview
The mind justifies anger by clinging to specific narratives. The Buddha lists nine grounds on which one binds resentment (*āghātaṃ bandhati*): harm done, being done or to be done to oneself, to one who is dear, and help given to one who is not pleasing.

### The List
1. **"He has harmed me"** (Past)
2. **"He is harming me"** (Present)
3. **"He will harm me"** (Future)
4. **"He has harmed someone dear to me"** (Past)
5. **"He is harming someone dear to me"** (Present)
6. **"He will harm someone dear to me"** (Future)
7. **"He has helped someone not pleasing to me"** (Past)
8. **"He is helping someone not pleasing to me"** (Present)
9. **"He will help someone not pleasing to me"** (Future)

### Quote
> **“Navayimāni, bhikkhave, āghātavatthūni. Katamāni nava? ‘ Anatthaṃ me acarī ’ ti āghātaṃ bandhati; ‘ anatthaṃ me caratī ’ ti āghātaṃ bandhati; ‘ anatthaṃ me carissatī ’ ti āghātaṃ bandhati; ‘ piyassa me manāpassa anatthaṃ acarī ’ ti … pe …‘ anatthaṃ caratī ’ ti … pe …‘ anatthaṃ carissatī ’ ti āghātaṃ bandhati; ‘ appiyassa me amanāpassa atthaṃ acarī ’ ti … pe …‘ atthaṃ caratī ’ ti … pe …‘ atthaṃ carissatī ’ ti āghātaṃ bandhati. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, nava āghātavatthūnī” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these nine grounds for resentment (āghātavatthu). What are the nine? Thinking: ‘They did what is not worthwhile to me,’ one binds resentment. Thinking: ‘They are doing what is not worthwhile to me,’ one binds resentment. Thinking: ‘They will do what is not worthwhile to me,’ one binds resentment. Thinking: ‘They did what is not worthwhile to one who is pleasing to me’ … ‘They are doing what is not worthwhile’ … ‘They will do what is not worthwhile,’ one binds resentment. Thinking: ‘They did what is worthwhile to one who is not pleasing to me’ … ‘They are doing what is worthwhile’ … ‘They will do what is worthwhile,’ one binds resentment. These indeed, monks, are the nine grounds for resentment.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 9.29, Āghātavatthu Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_09&page_number=208&name=navakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MjkuW3ZpYmhhLjk2MDsgZMSrLiBuaS4zLjM0MDsgYS4gbmkuMTAuNzld4oCY4oCYIE5hdmF5aW3EgW5pLA%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Āghātavatthu [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 9.29) – The Buddha lists these nine grounds.
* **Canonical**: *Āghātapaṭivinaya Sutta* (AN 9.30) – Immediately follows, the nine ways of removing resentment (*āghātapaṭivinayā*), each answering one ground with "taṃ kutettha labbhā" ("how could it be otherwise?"; A-ix 235).
