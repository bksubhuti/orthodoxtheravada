### Overview
In the Pañcapubbanimitta Sutta (Iti 83) the Buddha says that when a deva is due to pass away from the deva-host (*devakāyā cavanadhammo*), five signs appear (*pañcassa pubbanimittāni pātubhavanti*). The Itivuttaka Aṭṭhakathā says these arise for a deva whose death is at hand, about seven days by human reckoning (It-a 1241:1, 1245:2).

### The List
1. **Mālā milāyanti** - The garlands wither: the garland he wears becomes faded and loses its beauty, as if cast out in the midday heat (It-a 1241:2).
2. **Vatthāni kilissanti** - The robes become soiled: his bright garments become dull and stained at that very moment, as if trampled in mud (It-a 1242:1).
3. **Kacchehi sedā muccanti** - Sweat is released from the armpits: a body that was free of sweat and dirt now streams with sweat from both armpits and all over (It-a 1243:1-2).
4. **Kāye dubbaṇṇiyaṃ okkamati** - Ugliness enters the body: the body that shone for one to twelve yojanas, free of broken teeth and grey hair, becomes without radiance and lustre (It-a 1244:1).
5. **Sake devo devāsane nābhiramati** - The deva takes no delight in his own divine seat: he finds no pleasure in the seat where he played with his companions (It-a 1245:1).

### Quote
> **“Yadā, bhikkhave, devo devakāyā cavanadhammo hoti, pañcassa pubbanimittāni pātubhavanti – mālā milāyanti, vatthāni kilissanti, kacchehi sedā muccanti, kāye dubbaṇṇiyaṃ okkamati, sake devo devāsane nābhiramatīti.**  
> “Bhikkhus, when a deva is due to pass away from a deva-host, five prognostic signs appear: his flower-garlands wither, his robes become soiled, sweat is released from his armpits, his bodily radiance fades, and the deva takes no delight in his heavenly throne.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Pañcapubbanimitta [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Iti 83, It 792 "4. Pañcapubbanimittasuttaṃ") – After the five signs, the other devas congratulate the departing *devaputta* with three wishes: go to a good destination, gain a good gain, be well established (It 794:2-3). When a monk asks what these mean, the Buddha explains them as human birth, gaining faith in the Dhamma and Vinaya of the Tathāgata, and that faith being firmly established (It 796:1-5).
* **Commentary**: *Paramatthadīpanī* (Itivuttaka Aṭṭhakathā, It-a 1240-1245) – Explains each of the five signs.
