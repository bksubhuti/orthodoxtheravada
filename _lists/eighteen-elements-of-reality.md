### Overview
To completely shatter the illusion of a unified "self" or "soul," the Buddha broke down the entirety of existence—both physical and mental, internal and external—into eighteen ultimate Elements (*[Dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)*). Everything that can possibly be experienced in the universe is simply the interaction of these eighteen irreducible, conditioned realities.

### The List
**The 6 Sense Organs (Internal):**
1. **Cakkhudhātu** - Eye element
2. **Sotadhātu** - Ear element
3. **Ghānadhātu** - Nose element
4. **Jivhādhātu** - Tongue element
5. **Kāyadhātu** - Body element
6. **Manodhātu** - Mind element (The cognitive faculty)

**The 6 Sense Objects (External):**
7. **Rūpadhātu** - Visible form element
8. **Saddadhātu** - Sound element
9. **Gandhadhātu** - Odor element
10. **Rasadhātu** - Taste element
11. **Phoṭṭhabbadhātu** - Tangible element
12. **Dhammadhātu** - Mental object element

**The 6 Classes of Consciousness:**
13. **Cakkhuviññāṇadhātu** - Eye-consciousness element
14. **Sotaviññāṇadhātu** - Ear-consciousness element
15. **Ghānaviññāṇadhātu** - Nose-consciousness element
16. **Jivhāviññāṇadhātu** - Tongue-consciousness element
17. **Kāyaviññāṇadhātu** - Body-consciousness element
18. **Manoviññāṇadhātu** - Mind-consciousness element

### Quote
> **“Katamañca, bhikkhave, dhātunānattaṃ? Cakkhudhātu rūpadhātu cakkhuviññāṇadhātu, sotadhātu saddadhātu sotaviññāṇadhātu, ghānadhātu gandhadhātu ghānaviññāṇadhātu, jivhādhātu rasadhātu jivhāviññāṇadhātu, kāyadhātu phoṭṭhabbadhātu kāyaviññāṇadhātu, manodhātu dhammadhātu manoviññāṇadhātu – idaṃ vuccati, bhikkhave, dhātunānattan” ti.**  
> “And what, mendicants, is the diversity of elements? The eye element, form element, and eye-consciousness element; the ear element, sound realm, and ear-consciousness element; the nose element, odour element, and nose-consciousness element; the tongue element, flavour element, and tongue-consciousness element; the body element, touch realm, and body-consciousness element; the mind element, principle of truth, and mind-consciousness element—this is called, mendicants, the diversity of elements.”  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 14.1, Dhātunānatta Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_02&page_number=358&name=nid%C4%81navaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEthdGFtYcOxY2EsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgZGjEgXR1bsSBbmF0dGHhuYM%2F)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Dhātu Saṃyutta* (SN 14) – The Buddha teaches the diversity of elements (*dhātunānatta*) as the eighteen, in six triads of sense-organ, object and consciousness.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* – Extensively analyzes the precise nature of these 18 elements.
