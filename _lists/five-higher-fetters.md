### Overview
The Ten Fetters bind beings to [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). The Non-returner (*[Anāgāmī](/glossary/anagami/)*) has ended the five lower fetters (*pañcannaṃ orambhāgiyānaṃ saṃyojanānaṃ parikkhayā*, A-iii 978:2); he is reborn spontaneously in the Pure Abodes, attains final Nibbāna there and does not return from that world (Pp-a 257:5-8). The five higher fetters are called "higher" because they bring about rebirth in the form and formless realms (*uddhaṃ vuccati rūpārūpadhātu*, Vibh-a 2183:8), and the Aṭṭhakathā also calls them the "external" fetters, the desire and lust for form and formless existence being outside the sense-sphere (Mp-ii 151:8-9). The breaking of all ten, the five lower and the five higher, establishes the monk in Arahatship (Dhp-a 3930:1).

### The List
1. **[Rūparāga](/glossary/ruparaga/)** - Desire for fine-material existence: the desire and lust for the form realms.
2. **[Arūparāga](/glossary/aruparaga/)** - Desire for immaterial existence: the desire and lust for the formless realms (Mp-ii 151:8).
3. **[Māna](/glossary/mana/)** - Conceit.
4. **[Uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)** - Restlessness.
5. **[Avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)** - Ignorance: not knowing suffering, its origin, its cessation and the way to its cessation, and the further kinds of not-knowing listed in the Dhammasaṅgaṇī (Dhs 1839:2).


### Quote
> **“Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, uddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni. Katamāni pañca? Rūparāgo, arūparāgo, māno, uddhaccaṃ, avijjā – imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañcuddhambhāgiyāni saṃyojanāni.**  
> “Mendicants, there are five higher fetters. What five? Lust for form, lust for the formless, conceit, restlessness, and ignorance—these indeed are the five higher fetters.  
>
