### Overview
Dīghajāṇu the Koliyan told the Buddha that he and his fellow householders enjoy sensual pleasures and live crowded with children, and asked for teachings for welfare and happiness in this visible life and in the next (A-viii 715:5-6). In reply the Buddha gave four things for welfare and happiness in this visible life (*diṭṭhadhammahitāya*, A-viii 716:1), then four for the world beyond (A-viii 722:1).

### The List
1. **Uṭṭhānasampadā** - Accomplishment in persistent effort: In whatever work a clansman earns his living (farming, trade, cattle-keeping, archery, royal service or some other craft) he is skilled and not lazy, endowed with the means of investigation, able to do it and to arrange it (A-viii 716:5).
2. **Ārakkhasampadā** - Accomplishment in protection: His wealth, gained by energetic effort, by the strength of his arm, by the sweat of his brow, lawful and lawfully gained, he guards, so that kings, thieves, fire, water or unloved heirs may not take it (A-viii 717:2-4).
3. **Kalyāṇamittatā** - Good friendship: In the village or town where he lives he associates and converses with householders, young or old, who are advanced in virtue and endowed with faith, virtue, generosity and wisdom, and he learns from each (A-viii 718:2-3). Elsewhere in the sutta the "leaks" of wealth are: a womanizer, a drunkard, a gambler, a bad friend (A-viii 720:1), and the "inlets" the opposite, with a good friend (A-viii 721:1).
4. **Samajīvitā** - Balanced living: Knowing his income and expenditure, he lives a balanced livelihood, neither too extravagant nor too frugal, so that his income will outlast his expenditure (A-viii 719:2-3), as a weigher knows the balance of the scale (719:4-6). If he spends lavishly on small income, people say he eats his wealth like an *udumbarakhādī* (fig eater); if he lives meanly on a large income, they say, "This clansman will die of starvation" (*ajeṭṭhamaraṇaṃ*) (A-viii 719:8-11).

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, byagghapajja, dhammā kulaputtassa diṭṭhadhammahitāya saṃvattanti diṭṭhadhammasukhāya. Katame cattāro? Uṭṭhānasampadā, ārakkhasampadā, kalyāṇamittatā, samajīvitā.**  
> “TigerPaw, there are these four things that lead to a clansman’s welfare and happiness in this visible life. What four? Being consummate in initiative, being consummate in vigilance, admirable friendship, and maintaining one’s livelihood in tune.
