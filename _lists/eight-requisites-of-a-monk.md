### Overview
While a monastic is formally allowed four broad categories of requisites (robes, food, shelter, medicine), the commentarial and later canonical tradition standardized the personal inventory of a fully ordained [bhikkhu](/glossary/bhikkhu/) into eight specific items. These represent the absolute minimum possessions required to live the homeless life independently.

### The List
The source gives the list as "the three robes, the bowl, a knife for cutting tooth-sticks, a needle, a girdle, and a water filter" (Manorathapūraṇī), so the three robes count as three of the eight.

1. **Saṅghāṭi:** The outer robe.
2. **Uttarāsaṅga:** The upper robe.
3. **Antaravāsaka:** The lower robe.
4. **Patta:** The alms bowl.
5. **Vāsi:** A knife for cutting tooth-sticks (*dantakaṭṭhacchedanavāsi*).
6. **Sūci:** A needle.
7. **Kāyabandhana:** A girdle or waistband.
8. **Parissāvana:** A water filter.

### Quote
> **Iminā pana dvādasavidhena itarītarapaccayasantosena samannāgatassa bhikkhuno aṭṭha parikkhārā vaṭṭanti – tīṇi cīvarāni, patto, dantakaṭṭhacchedanavāsi, ekā sūci, kāyabandhanaṃ parissāvananti.**  
> For a monk endowed with this twelvefold contentment with whatever requisites are available, eight requisites are suitable: the three robes, the bowl, a knife for cutting tooth-sticks, a needle, a girdle, and a water filter.  
>
