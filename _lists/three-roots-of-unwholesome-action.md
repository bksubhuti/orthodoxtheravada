### Overview
In Buddhist psychology, these three mental factors (*cetasikas*) are the fundamental toxins that poison the mind. Every unwholesome thought, word, or physical action can be traced back to one or more of these three roots. They are the driving forces that generate bad [kamma](/glossary/kamma/).

### The List
1. **[Lobha](/glossary/lobha/)** - Greed: The desire to pull things toward oneself. It manifests as attachment, craving, lust, covetousness, and clinging to sensual pleasures or existence.
2. **[Dosa](/glossary/dosa/)** - Hatred: The desire to push things away. It manifests as aversion, anger, ill-will, irritation, resentment, and a destructive disposition toward objects or beings.
3. **[Moha](/glossary/moha/)** - Delusion: The darkness of the mind that prevents seeing things as they truly are. It is synonymous with ignorance (*[avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)*) and is present in every single unwholesome state of consciousness.

### Quote
> **“Tīṇimāni, bhikkhave, akusalamūlāni. Katamāni tīṇi? Lobho akusalamūlaṃ, doso akusalamūlaṃ, moho akusalamūlaṃ – imāni kho, bhikkhave, tīṇi akusalamūlānī” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these three unwholesome roots. What are the three? Greed is an unwholesome root, hatred is an unwholesome root, delusion is an unwholesome root—these indeed, monks, are the three unwholesome roots.”  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Mūla [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Iti 50) – The Buddha names the three unwholesome roots: greed, hatred and delusion (It 467:1-3). The Aṅguttara has a longer discourse on the same three, the Akusalamūla Sutta (AN 3.70 in TPR's numbering, A-iii 720-722), which says that many evil unwholesome states are born of each root.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha](/glossary/abhidhammatthasangaha/)* (Abhidh-s 16:1, 115:1) – There are twelve unwholesome states of consciousness, and delusion (moha) is found in all twelve. (Dhs 1754:2 states the triad "Tīṇi akusalamūlāni – lobho, doso, moho".)
