### Overview
The Four Bases of Sympathy (*Saṅgahavatthu*) are the four "means of sustaining a favorable relationship" taught in AN 4.32 (A-iv 347:1-3). The Buddha says they are in the world like the linchpin of a rolling chariot (A-iv 350:1), and that without them a mother would not receive honour or reverence from her child (A-iv 351:1). The commentary explains that different people are drawn together by different ones of the four (Mp-iv 1184:2-15).

### The List
1. **[Dāna](/glossary/dana/)** - Giving / Generosity: Giving. Some person is to be drawn by giving alone, and to him just a gift is to be given (Mp-iv 1184:2).
2. **Peyyavajjā** - Endearing Speech: *Peyyavajja* is endearing speech (*piyavacanaṃ*). Some person looks for no gift, only for kind speech (Mp-iv 1184:3-8).
3. **Atthacariyā** - Beneficial Action: *Atthacariyā* is speech that promotes welfare (*atthavaḍḍhanakathā*): telling the person what should and should not be done, and whom to follow or not (Mp-iv 1184:9-11).
4. **Samānattatā** - Impartiality / Equal Participation: *Samānattatā* is sharing the same happiness and suffering (*samānasukhadukkhabhāvo*): sitting on the same seat, lying on the same couch, eating together; it is done "as is suitable in each case" (*tattha tattha yathārahaṃ*) (A-iv 349:1; Mp-iv 1184:12-15). Note that the TPR translation renders *samānattatā ca dhammesu* as "impartiality"; the commentary's gloss is the sharing of the same condition.

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, saṅgahavatthūni. Katamāni cattāri? Dānaṃ, peyyavajjaṃ, atthacariyā, samānattatā – imāni kho, bhikkhave, cattāri saṅgahavatthūnī” ti. “Dānañca peyyavajjañca, atthacariyā ca yā idha; Samānattatā ca dhammesu, tattha tattha yathārahaṃ;**  
> “Monks, there are these four means of sustaining a favorable relationship. What are the four? A gift, endearing speech, beneficent conduct, and impartiality.
