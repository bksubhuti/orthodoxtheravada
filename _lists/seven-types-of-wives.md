### Overview
One day the Buddha went to the house of the householder [Anāthapiṇḍika](/glossary/anathapindika/), where there was great noise, "like fishermen in a fish haul" (A-vii 461:1-462:1). Anāthapiṇḍika explained that his daughter-in-law Sujātā, brought from a wealthy family, did not respect her mother-in-law, her father-in-law or her husband, and did not honour even the Buddha (A-vii 462:2-3). The Manorathapūraṇī says that Sujātā was Visākhā's younger sister (Mp-vii 925:2). The Buddha called her and taught that a man has seven kinds of wife, three who go to hell and four who go to a good destination (A-vii 464:1-4; verses 499-506).

### The List
**The Unwholesome Wives (the verse says they go to hell):**
1. **Vadhakasamā** - The Slayer-like wife: one with a corrupt mind, without compassion for his welfare, who loves other men, despises her husband, and is eager to kill the man whom she was bought with wealth (A-vii 465:1-467:1; Mp-vii 926:1-5).
2. **Corīsamā** - The Thief-like wife: one who wishes to take away even a little of the wealth that her husband gains by craft, trade or farming (A-vii 470:1-472:1; Mp-vii 926:6-7).
3. **Ayyasamā** - The Master-like wife: one who is not fond of work, lazy, a big eater, harsh, fierce and foul-spoken, who dominates her energetic husband (A-vii 475:1-477:1; Mp-vii 926:8-11).

**The Wholesome Wives (the verse says they go to a good destination):**
4. **Mātāsamā** - The Mother-like wife: one who is always compassionate for his welfare, guards her husband as a mother guards her son, and protects the wealth he has gathered (A-vii 480:1-482:1).
5. **Bhaginīsamā** - The Sister-like wife: one who respects her husband as a younger sister respects her elder, modest and obedient to her husband's will (A-vii 485:1-487:1).
6. **Sakhīsamā** - The Friend-like wife: one who rejoices on seeing her husband as a friend rejoices at one long absent, of good family, virtuous and devoted to her husband (A-vii 489:1-491:1; Mp-vii 926:12-14).
7. **Dāsīsamā** - The Slave-like wife: one who, though threatened with the rod, is calm and without anger, bears her husband's treatment with a pure mind, and follows his will without wrath (A-vii 494:1-496:1).

The sutta ends with Sujātā's reply: "From today, Venerable Sir, may the Blessed One regard me as a wife who is like a slave to her husband" (A-vii 507:3).


### Quote
> **“Satta kho imā, sujāte, purisassa bhariyāyo. Katamā satta? Vadhakasamā, corīsamā, ayyasamā, mātāsamā, bhaginīsamā, sakhīsamā, dāsīsamā. Imā kho, sujāte, satta purisassa bhariyāyo.**  
> “Sujātā, there are seven kinds of wives for a man. Which seven? A wife like a killer, a wife like a thief, a wife like a lord, a wife like a mother, a wife like a sister, a wife like a companion, a wife like a servant. Sujātā, these are the seven kinds of wives for a man.  
>
