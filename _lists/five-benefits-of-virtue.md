### Overview
The Buddha detailed the advantages of maintaining moral discipline (*[sīla](/glossary/sila/)*) to the householders of Pāṭaligāma, on his last journey before his [Parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/) (DN 16).

### The List
1. **Bhogakkhandhaṃ adhigacchati** - One obtains a great mass of riches: the virtuous person, accomplished in virtue, obtains it because of being heedful (*appamādādhikaraṇa*).
2. **Kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggacchati** - A good reputation spreads: A good report (*kalyāṇo kittisaddo*) of the virtuous person goes round.
3. **Visārado upasaṅkamati** - One approaches any assembly without fear: Whatever assembly he approaches, whether of khattiyas, brahmins, householders or ascetics, he approaches with confidence and without confusion (*amaṅkubhūta*).
4. **Asammūḷho kālaṃ karoti** - One dies unconfused: The virtuous person dies without bewilderment.
5. **Saggaṃ lokaṃ upapajjati** - Rebirth in a heavenly realm: At the break-up of the body, after death, he arises in a fortunate destiny (*sugati*), in a heavenly world.

### Quote
> **“Pañcime, gahapatayo, ānisaṃsā sīlavato sīlasampadāya. Katame pañca?**  
> 150. There are these five advantages, householders, for one who is virtuous (sīlavā), for one accomplished in virtue (sīlasampanna). What are the five?
