### Overview
The Uposatha is the day on which the monks gather to recite the Pāṭimokkha. The Mahāvagga allows the recitation "once a fortnight, on the fourteenth or the fifteenth" (*sakiṃ pakkhassa cātuddase vā pannarase vā pātimokkhaṃ uddisituṃ*, Kkh 1236:5, quoting Mahāvagga). The Parivāra and the commentaries count three kinds of Uposatha by the day on which it is held (*divasavasena tayo uposathā*, Kkh 1236:3): the fourteenth-day, the fifteenth-day and the Uposatha of harmony. Only the fifteenth-day Uposatha falls on the full moon (or new moon) night; the fourteenth-day one does not, which is why this page speaks of "Uposatha days", not "full-moon days".

### The List
1. **Cātuddasiko Uposatha** - Fourteenth-day Uposatha: of the Uposathas of a year, twenty-four in all, six are of the fourteenth day: two in the third and seventh fortnights of each of the three seasons, winter, summer and rains (Kkh 1236:4). With a fitting reason, an Uposatha may be held on another fourteenth day (Kkh 1236:5).
2. **Paṇṇarasiko Uposatha** - Fifteenth-day Uposatha: the remaining eighteen of the twenty-four are of the fifteenth day (Kkh 1236:4); this is the regular rule (*pakaticārittaṃ*).
3. **Sāmaggī-uposatha** - Harmony Uposatha: when, as told in the Kosambaka section, a split Saṅgha is reconciled, "the Uposatha is to be held at once, the Pātimokkha is to be recited" (*tāvadeva uposatho kātabbo, pātimokkhaṃ uddisitabbaṃ*, Vin-iii 2698:1). Any day other than the fourteenth and the fifteenth can be the day of the Uposatha of harmony (Kkh 1236:8). The Ṭīkā defines it as "the Uposatha to be held on the day of the Saṅgha's harmony" (Vin-alaṅke 1035:4).


### Quote
> **Tayo uposathā – cātuddasiko, pannarasiko, sāmaggiuposatho.**  
> There are three Uposathas: the fourteenth day, the fifteenth day, and the harmonious Uposatha.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Uposatha [Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/)* ([Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/), Vinaya) – Gives only two Uposatha days: "Dveme, bhikkhave, uposathā – cātuddasiko ca pannarasiko ca" (Vin-iii 817:4). The Parivāra (Vin-v 1982:1) is the Mūla text that adds the third, "sāmaggiuposatho".
* **Commentary**: *Kaṅkhāvitaraṇī* (Kkh 1236:3-8) – "Divasavasena hi tayo uposathā cātuddasiko pannarasiko sāmaggiuposatho"; six fourteenth-day Uposathas fall in each year (the third and seventh fortnights of each season) and the other eighteen are fifteenth-day; the Uposatha of harmony is held on the very day a split Saṅgha is reconciled (Kosambaka section, "tāvadeva uposatho kātabbo", Vin-iii 2698:1).
* **Ṭīkā**: *Vinayālaṅkāra-ṭīkā* (Vin-alaṅke 1035:2-4) – the same three, and the Uposatha of harmony is "the Uposatha to be done on the day of the Saṅgha's harmony".
