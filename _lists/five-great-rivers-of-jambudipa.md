### Overview
In the Gaṅgāpeyyāla of the Saṃyutta Nikāya (SN 45.96) the Buddha names five great rivers (*mahānadiyo*) that all slope, slant and incline to the east, as a simile for a monk who develops the noble eightfold path and so inclines to Nibbāna. The Majjhimapaṇṇāsa Aṭṭhakathā says the five come from the Anotatta lake: its water, after flowing underground, breaks out at the Viñjha mountain in five streams, which are counted as the Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Aciravatī, Sarabhū and Mahī (Ps-ii 125:5-11).

### The List
1. **Gaṅgā**
2. **Yamunā**
3. **Aciravatī**
4. **Sarabhū**
5. **Mahī**

The texts give only the names. The modern identifications of these rivers are not in the Mūla or the Aṭṭhakathā passages seen.

### Quote
> **“Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, yā kācimā mahānadiyo, seyyathidaṃ – gaṅgā, yamunā, aciravatī, sarabhū, mahī, sabbā tā pācīnaninnā pācīnapoṇā pācīnapabbhārā; evameva kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhu ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ bhāvento ariyaṃ aṭṭhaṅgikaṃ maggaṃ bahulīkaronto nibbānaninno hoti nibbānapoṇo nibbānapabbhāro.**  
> “Bhikkhus, just as whatever great rivers there are—namely, the Ganges, the Yamunā, the Aciravatī, the Sarabhū, the Mahī—all slant, slope, and incline towards the east; even so, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu who develops and cultivates the Noble Eightfold Path slants, slopes, and inclines towards Nibbāna.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Gaṅgāpeyyāla* (SN 45.96, S-v 235) – "Just as whatever great rivers there are, the Gaṅgā, Yamunā, Aciravatī, Sarabhū and Mahī, all slope, slant and incline to the east, so a monk who develops the noble eightfold path inclines to Nibbāna." The same five rivers are named in the Milindapañha (Mil 442:4) as those that flow into the great ocean without filling it.
* **Commentary**: *Papañcasūdanī* (Ps-ii 125:11) – the five rivers as five streams from the Viñjha mountain.
