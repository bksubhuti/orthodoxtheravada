### Overview
The Paṭisambhidāmagga sets out the kinds of concentration (*[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)*) from one to ten, fifty-five in all (*pañcapaññāsa samādhi*). Its fivefold kind is the five "pervadings" and the reviewing sign. The same five appear in the Dasuttara Sutta (DN 34) as the five-factored right concentration (*pañcaṅgiko [sammāsamādhi](/glossary/sammasamadhi/)*), one of the five things to be developed, and the Vibhaṅga (§804) defines each one. The Vibhaṅga-aṭṭhakathā compares the five to a man's body: the first two are the feet, the next two the hands, the reviewing sign the head, and the [jhāna](/glossary/jhana/) that is the basis for direct knowledge the trunk.

The stages of concentration found in the commentaries are a different classification. The Aṭṭhasālinī names three kinds, momentary, access and absorption concentration (*khaṇikasamādhi, [upacārasamādhi](/glossary/upacarasamadhi/), [appanāsamādhi](/glossary/appanasamadhi/)*; As 1183), and the Visuddhimagga divides concentration into access and absorption (Vism-i 657).

### The List
1. **Pītipharaṇatā** - Pervading with rapture (*[pīti](/glossary/piti/)*): "Wisdom in two jhānas" (*dvīsu jhānesu paññā*).
2. **Sukhapharaṇatā** - Pervading with happiness (*[sukha](/glossary/sukha/)*): "Wisdom in three jhānas" (*tīsu jhānesu paññā*).
3. **Cetopharaṇatā** - Pervading of minds: knowledge of the minds of others (*paracitte ñāṇaṃ*; [cetopariyañāṇa](/glossary/cetopariyanana/)).
4. **Ālokapharaṇatā** - Pervading with light: the [divine eye](/glossary/dibbacakkhu/) (*dibbacakkhu*).
5. **Paccavekkhaṇānimitta** - The reviewing sign: the [reviewing knowledge](/glossary/paccavekkhananana/) of one who has emerged from that concentration (*tamhā tamhā samādhimhā vuṭṭhitassa paccavekkhaṇāñāṇaṃ*).

### Quote
> **Pañca samādhī – pītipharaṇatā, sukhapharaṇatā, cetopharaṇatā, ālokapharaṇatā, paccavekkhaṇānimittaṃ.**  
> Five kinds of concentration: extension of joy, extension of pleasure, extension of mind, extension of light, and the object of reviewing.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* (Paṭis 258) – The kinds of concentration from one to ten; the five are its fivefold kind. Paṭis 179 also names the five-factored concentration (*pañcaṅgiko samādhi*) as the five things to be developed.
* **Canonical**: *Dasuttara [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 34, D-iii 1721) – The five-factored right concentration, the five things to be developed. (The Saṅgīti Sutta, DN 33, does not have this list.)
* **Canonical**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (§804, Vibh 2443) – Defines each of the five, as given in The List above.
* **Commentary**: *[Saddhammappakāsinī](/glossary/saddhammappakasini/)* (Paṭis-a 512) – Calls the five-factored concentration the concentration of the fourth jhāna (*catutthajjhānasamādhi*) and explains each factor as in the Vibhaṅga.
* **Commentary**: *[Sammohavinodanī](/glossary/sammohavinodani/)* (Vibh-a 1902) – The simile of the body with its limbs.
