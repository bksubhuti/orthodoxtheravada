### Overview
In the mechanism of [kamma](/glossary/kamma/), these five acts are the heaviest a human being can commit. They are called *ānantariya* (immediate) because their result follows at once: a person who has done one is reborn in hell right after death (Ud-a 926:3, *cutianantaraṃ niraye uppajjīti*), and, as the Manorathapūraṇī says, no gift or merit, however great, can ward off the result (Mp-i 1368:1). AN 5.129 calls the one who does them *āpāyika, nerayika, parikuppa, atekiccha*: bound for states of woe, for hell, "festering like old wounds" and with no remedy that can be applied (Mp-v 264:3-4). Only an ordinary worldling does these deeds, never a noble disciple (Mp-i 1365:4-5). Of the five, only the schism in the Saṅgha is *kappaṭṭhitika*, lasting for an eon; the other four are *ānantariya* but not eon-lasting (Mp-i 1374:1-5).

### The List
1. **Mātughāta** - Matricide: Intentionally killing one's own mother. The act is *ānantariya* when a human kills his or her human mother; killing an animal mother, or an animal killing, is heavy kamma but not *ānantariya* (Mp-i 1368:1-2).
2. **Pitughāta** - Patricide: Intentionally killing one's own father, by the same rule (Mp-i 1368:1-2).
3. **Arahantaghāta** - Killing an [Arahat](/glossary/arahat/): Intentionally depriving a human Arahat of life. Killing a yakkha Arahat, or a noble person of a lower stage, is heavy kamma "like an *ānantariya*" but not one (Mp-i 1369:6-10).
4. **Lohituppāda** - Causing the Tathāgata's blood to flow with a corrupt mind. The Tathāgata's body cannot be broken, so no skin is cut by another's attack; the blood only collects inside, as when Devadatta's rock splinter struck the Buddha's foot (Mp-i 1370:1-3). Jīvaka's cutting to let out bad blood was a meritorious act (Mp-i 1370:4-5).
5. **Saṅghabheda** - Causing a schism in the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/): dividing the community by presenting what is not Dhamma as Dhamma (the eighteen grounds of schism), and then holding a separate formal act or recitation of the Pātimokkha with a group of at least nine monks (Mp-i 1364:3, 1365:1-3, 1372:1-4).


### Quote
> **“Pañcime, bhikkhave, āpāyikā nerayikā parikuppā atekicchā. Katame pañca? Mātā jīvitā voropitā hoti, pitā jīvitā voropito hoti, arahaṃ jīvitā voropito hoti, tathāgatassa duṭṭhena cittena lohitaṃ uppāditaṃ hoti, saṅgho bhinno hoti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, pañca āpāyikā nerayikā parikuppā atekicchā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these five who are bound for states of woe, denizens of hell, who are in agony and incurable. What five? One who has deprived their mother of life, one who has deprived their father of life, one who has deprived an Arahant of life, one who—with a corrupted mind—has caused the blood of a Tathāgata to flow, and one by whom the Saṅgha is divided. These indeed, monks, are the five who are bound for states of woe, denizens of hell, who are in agony and incurable.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 5.129, Parikuppa Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=129&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTI5LuKAmOKAmCBQYcOxY2ltZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCDEgXDEgXlpa8SBIG5lcmF5aWvEgSBwYXJpa3VwcMSB)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Parikuppa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 5.129, "9. Parikuppasuttaṃ") - the five who are bound for states of woe, denizens of hell, in agony and incurable. The [Kathāvatthu](/glossary/kathavatthu/) discusses the same five as a proposition (*ruhiruppādako* is its word for the fourth).
* **Commentary**: Manorathapūraṇī on AN 5.129 (Mp-v 264:3-4) and on the *ānantariya* deeds by kamma, door and eon-duration (Mp-i 1364-1374). Devadatta committed the shedding of blood (Mp-i 1370:2) and the schism (Ud-a 1246:5).
