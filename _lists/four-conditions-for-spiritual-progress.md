### Overview
In the same discourse where he taught wealth management, the Buddha immediately balanced the teaching by reminding the layperson that physical wealth ends at death. To ensure happiness in the *next* life (and movement toward ultimate liberation), a householder must simultaneously develop these four spiritual assets.

### The List
1. **Saddhāsampadā** - Accomplishment in faith: A clansman is endowed with faith: he has faith in the Tathāgata's enlightenment, "Indeed, the Blessed One is worthy … the Teacher of gods and humans, enlightened, blessed" (A-viii 722:5-6).
2. **Sīlasampadā** - Accomplishment in virtue: Abstaining from killing living beings … and from liquor, wine and intoxicants, the occasion for heedlessness (A-viii 723:2, abridged in the text).
3. **Cāgasampadā** - Accomplishment in generosity: Living at home with a heart free from the stain of stinginess, freely generous, open-handed, delighting in relinquishment, one to be asked, delighting in giving and sharing (A-viii 724:2).
4. **Paññāsampadā** - Accomplishment in wisdom: A clansman is wise, possessed of wisdom regarding arising and passing away that is noble, penetrating, and leads to the complete ending of suffering (A-viii 725:2).

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, byagghapajja, dhammā kulaputtassa samparāyahitāya saṃvattanti samparāyasukhāya. Katame cattāro? Saddhāsampadā, sīlasampadā, cāgasampadā, paññāsampadā.**  
> TigerPaw, there are these four things that lead to a clansman’s welfare and happiness in the world beyond. What four? Acquisition of faith, acquisition of virtuous behavior, acquisition of generosity, and the acquisition of wisdom.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Dīghajāṇu [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 8.54) – The Buddha states that these four qualities lead to a clansman's welfare and happiness in the world beyond (A-viii 722:1; ending at 725:4). The sutta is "4. Dīghajāṇusuttaṃ" (A-viii 714:1), addressed to Dīghajāṇu (Byagghapajja); the four conditions for the present life come earlier in the same sutta.
