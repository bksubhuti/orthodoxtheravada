### Overview
Proclaiming the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) is a highly sensitive moral action. In the Udāyī Sutta (AN 5.159) the Buddha says: "It is not easy to teach the Dhamma to others" (*na kho, ānanda, sukaraṃ paresaṃ dhammaṃ desetuṃ*). One who teaches should first establish five qualities within himself (*pañca dhamme ajjhattaṃ upaṭṭhāpetvā*).

### The List
1. **Anupubbiṃ kathaṃ kathessāmi** - I will speak a graduated discourse: The Aṭṭhakathā explains it as the order of teaching, virtue after giving and heaven after virtue, or a discourse fitting whatever sutta or verse passage is taken up (Mp-v 325:1).
2. **Pariyāyadassāvī kathaṃ kathessāmi** - I will speak showing the reasons: The Aṭṭhakathā explains that here *pariyāya* means reason (*kāraṇa*): showing the reason for each point (Mp-v 325:2-3).
3. **Anuddayataṃ paṭicca kathaṃ kathessāmi** - I will speak out of compassion: The Aṭṭhakathā says: "I will free beings who have reached great suffering from their suffering" (Mp-v 325:4).
4. **Na āmisantaro kathaṃ kathessāmi** - I will speak not for material reward: The Aṭṭhakathā explains that one does not hope for the four requisites for oneself (Mp-v 325:5).
5. **Attānañca parañca anupahacca kathaṃ kathessāmi** - I will speak without harming myself or others: The Aṭṭhakathā explains it as not harming the good qualities of oneself or others by exalting oneself and disparaging others (Mp-v 325:6).

### Quote
> **“Na kho, ānanda, sukaraṃ paresaṃ dhammaṃ desetuṃ. Paresaṃ, ānanda, dhammaṃ desentena pañca dhamme ajjhattaṃ upaṭṭhāpetvā paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo. Katame pañca? ‘ Anupubbiṃ kathaṃ kathessāmī ’ ti paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo; ‘ pariyāyadassāvī kathaṃ kathessāmī ’ ti paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo; ‘ anuddayataṃ paṭicca kathaṃ kathessāmī ’ ti paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo; ‘ na āmisantaro kathaṃ kathessāmī ’ ti paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo; ‘ attānañca parañca anupahacca kathaṃ kathessāmī ’ ti paresaṃ dhammo desetabbo.**  
> “Indeed, Ānanda, it is not easy to teach the Dhamma to others. One who teaches the Dhamma to others, Ānanda, should establish five qualities within oneself, and then the Dhamma should be taught to others. What five? The Dhamma should be taught to others with the thought: ‘I will speak a graduated discourse.’ The Dhamma should be taught to others with the thought: ‘I will speak explaining the sequence.’ The Dhamma should be taught to others with the thought: ‘I will speak out of compassion.’ The Dhamma should be taught to others with the thought: ‘I will speak not for the purpose of material reward.’ The Dhamma should be taught to others with the thought: ‘I will speak without harming himself or others.’  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 5.159, Udāyī Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_05&page_number=162&name=pa%C3%B1cakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIE5hIGtobywgxIFuYW5kYSwgc3VrYXJh4bmDIHBhcmVzYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Udāyī [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 5.159, A-v 1080-1082) – Venerable Ānanda sees Udāyī teaching a large assembly of householders and reports it to the Buddha, who then gives the five qualities. *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-v 325) explains each.
