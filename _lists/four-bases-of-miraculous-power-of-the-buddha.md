### Overview
The four *iddhipādā* are the same four bases of spiritual power taught to monks. In the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta the Buddha says to Ānanda that the Tathāgata has developed them, made much of them, made them a vehicle and a basis, and so, if he wished, the [Tathāgata](/glossary/tathagata/) could remain for a *kappa* or for what is left of a *kappa* (D-ii 413:3-4; also S-v 1574:3 in the Cetiya Sutta). The Aṭṭhakathā explains that *kappa* here means the human lifespan (*āyukappaṃ*), the full measure of life at that time (Sv-ii 561:2-3).

### The List
1. **Chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ** - The base of power endowed with concentration due to desire (*chanda*) and the volitional formations of exertion (D-iii 252:2).
2. **Vīriyasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ** - The base of power endowed with concentration due to energy (*vīriya*) and the volitional formations of exertion.
3. **Cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ** - The base of power endowed with concentration due to mind (*citta*) and the volitional formations of exertion.
4. **Vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ** - The base of power endowed with concentration due to investigation (*vīmaṃsā*) and the volitional formations of exertion.


### Quote
> **“Kiñca, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno āyusmiṃ? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu chandasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīriyasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, cittasamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti, vīmaṃsāsamādhipadhānasaṅkhārasamannāgataṃ iddhipādaṃ bhāveti. So imesaṃ catunnaṃ iddhipādānaṃ bhāvitattā bahulīkatattā ākaṅkhamāno kappaṃ vā tiṭṭheyya kappāvasesaṃ vā.**  
> ‘And what is the meaning of length of years to a brother? Herein that a brother practises the Four Roads to Iddhi, to wit: action, effort, and concentration applied to desire, to energy, to consciousness, and to investigation. From practising and developing these Four Roads, he may, if he so desires, live on for an aeon, or the remainder of an aeon.  
>  
> — [Dīgha Nikāya](/glossary/digha-nikaya/) (DN 26, Cakkavatti Sutta, CST "3. Cakkavattisuttaṃ") · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_di_03&page_number=64&name=p%C4%81thikavaggap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIEtpw7FjYSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBiaGlra2h1bm8gxIF5dXNtaeG5gz8%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Mahāparinibbāna](/glossary/mahaparinibbana/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 16, D-ii 413:3-4, and again at D-ii 461:5, 463:5, 464:4) – The Buddha says to Venerable Ānanda that the Tathāgata has developed these four bases of power, so that if he wished he could remain for a *kappa* or the remainder of a *kappa*. The four are named in the quoted passage (D-iii 252:2) and in the Iddhipāda Saṃyutta (SN 51). Commentary: Sumaṅgalavilāsinī, Sv-ii 561:2 (*kappaṃ* = *āyukappaṃ*).
