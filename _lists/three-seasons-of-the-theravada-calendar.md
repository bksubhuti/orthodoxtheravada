### Overview
The regulatory and administrative laws of the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) operate strictly on a three-season tropical calendar layout. The Buddha divided the complete year into three distinct four-month blocks (*utūni*). Each season dictates specific modifications in monastic behavior, robe storage laws, and mandatory residential retreats.

### The List
1. **Hemanta** - The Cold Season: Spanning exactly four months from the day following the full moon of October/November through March, characterized by dry, cool weather.
2. **Gimhāna** - The Hot Season: Spanning exactly four months from March through the full moon night of June/July, characterized by intense heat and drought.
3. **Vassāna** - The Rainy Season: Spanning exactly four months from the day following the full moon of June/July through October/November, during which monastics are legally restricted from wandering and must enter the rains retreat (*[vassa](/glossary/vassa/)*).

### Quote
> **Vassasataṃ kho pana, bhikkhave, jīvanto tīṇiyeva utusatāni jīvati – utusataṃ hemantānaṃ, utusataṃ gimhānaṃ, utusataṃ vassānaṃ. Tīṇi kho pana, bhikkhave, utusatāni jīvanto dvādasa yeva māsasatāni jīvati – cattāri māsasatāni hemantānaṃ, cattāri māsasatāni gimhānaṃ, cattāri māsasatāni vassānaṃ.**  
> Indeed, monks, living for a hundred years, one lives for three hundred seasons: one hundred seasons of cold, one hundred seasons of heat, one hundred seasons of rain. Indeed, monks, living for three hundred seasons, one lives for twelve hundred months: four hundred months of cold, four hundred months of heat, four hundred months of rain.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 7.70, Arakasutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_07&page_number=503&name=sattakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=VmFzc2FzYXRh4bmDIGtobyBwYW5hLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGrEq3ZhbnRvIHTEq%2BG5h2l5ZXZh)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Vassūpanāyika [Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/)* ([Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/), Vinaya) – Before the rains residence was prescribed, the monks "wandered on tour during the cold weather, the hot weather, and the rains" ("hemantampi gimhampi vassampi cārikaṃ caranti"), and people criticised them (Vin-iii 1062:3-4); this led to the rains retreat.
* **Ṭīkā**: *Vinayālaṅkāra-ṭīkā* (Vin-alaṅke 1035:5-7) – Fixes the three seasons as four months each: winter from the first day of the waning moon of the second Kattika month to the Phagguna full moon, summer from the first day of the waning moon of Phagguna to the Āsāḷha full moon, rains from the first day of the waning moon of Āsāḷha to the second Kattika full moon (TPR translation wording).
