### Overview
Five of the thirteen *dhutaṅgas* are associated with lodging (*senāsanappaṭisaṃyutta*): the Manorathapūraṇī names them in one sentence (Mp-iv 1152:3) and refers to the Visuddhimagga for their detailed explanation (Mp-iv 1152:4). They concern where the monastic resides.

### The List
1. **Āraññik'aṅga** - Forest Dweller's Practice: Residing in the forest, away from the village (the Visuddhimagga gives the minimum distance as five hundred bow-lengths: Vism-i 539:3 "pañcadhanusatikaṃ pacchimaṃ").
2. **Rukkhamūlik'aṅga** - Tree-root Dweller's Practice: Living at the root of a tree, not in a hut.
3. **Abbhokāsik'aṅga** - Open-air Dweller's Practice: Living in the open air, with no roof or tree cover.
4. **Sosānik'aṅga** - Cemetery Dweller's Practice: Dwelling in a charnel ground or cemetery, keeping the reality of death in view.
5. **Yathāsanthatik'aṅga** - As-available-bed-and-seat Dweller's Practice: Contentment regarding accommodation, accepting whatever lodging is assigned, without complaint or preference.

### Quote
> **Senāsanappaṭisaṃyuttāni pana pañca dhutaṅgāni āraññikaṅgaṃ rukkhamūlikaṅgaṃ abbhokāsikaṅgaṃ sosānikaṅgaṃ yathāsanthatikaṅganti.**  
> The five ascetic practices associated with lodging are the forest dweller’s practice, the tree-root dweller’s practice, the open-air dweller’s practice, the cemetery dweller’s practice, and the as-available-bed-and-seat dweller’s practice (last item corrected, tr. Claude; TPR's translation here reads "sitter's practice").

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-iv 1152:3) – "Senāsanappaṭisaṃyuttāni pana pañca dhutaṅgāni …", the five lodging practices.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter II, Vism-i 419:2) – The full list of thirteen ascetic practices, in which these five are items 8 to 12. Other commentaries count the sitter's practice (*nesajjikaṅga*) with the lodging group too, making six (Pj-ii 1531:3).
* **Canonical (Mūla layer in TPR)**: *Milindapañha* (Mil 1805:3) – The thirteen *dhutaṅgas* in the same order.
