### Overview
In the Vaṇijjā Sutta (AN 5.177) the Buddha says that five kinds of trade (*vaṇijjā*) are not to be done by a lay follower (*upāsakena akaraṇīyā*). The Aṭṭhakathā says a lay follower here means one who has gone to the Three Refuges, and that he should neither do these trades himself nor urge others to do them (Mp-v 363:2, 363:8).

### The List
1. **Satthavaṇijjā** - Trade in Weapons: Having weapons made and selling them (Mp-v 363:3).
2. **Sattavaṇijjā** - Trade in Living Beings: Selling human beings (Mp-v 363:4).
3. **Maṃsavaṇijjā** - Trade in Meat: Raising pigs, deer and so on, and selling them (Mp-v 363:5).
4. **Majjavaṇijjā** - Trade in Intoxicants: Having any intoxicant made and selling it (Mp-v 363:6).
5. **Visavaṇijjā** - Trade in Poison: Having poison made and selling it (Mp-v 363:7).

### Quote
> **“Pañcimā, bhikkhave, vaṇijjā upāsakena akaraṇīyā. Katamā pañca? Satthavaṇijjā, sattavaṇijjā, maṃsavaṇijjā, majjavaṇijjā, visavaṇijjā – imā kho, bhikkhave, pañca vaṇijjā upāsakena akaraṇīyā” ti.**  
> “Monks, these five types of trade are beyond the reach of a lay follower. Which five? Trade in weapons, trade in living beings, trade in meat, trade in intoxicants, and trade in poison—these indeed, monks, are the five types of trade that are beyond the reach of a lay follower.”
