### Overview
The fourth triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā categorizes phenomena based on whether they were generated by past clinging/[kamma](/glossary/kamma/), and whether they can serve as the object of present clinging.

### The List
1. **Upādiṇṇupādāniyā dhammā** - States that are clung to (by kamma) and subject to clinging: the results of wholesome and unwholesome states with outflows of the three planes, the four mental aggregates, and the form that arises from kamma done (Dhs 1762:2-4). The Aṭṭhakathā: *upādinnā* = taken up by kamma that is endowed with craving and views, grasped as its fruit; *upādāniyā* = beneficial to clinging by becoming its object; it designates the form and formless states produced by kamma with outflows (As 854:1-5).
2. **Anupādiṇṇupādāniyā dhammā** - States that are not clung to by kamma but are subject to clinging: the wholesome and unwholesome states with outflows of the three planes, the four mental aggregates, the functional states (*kiriyā*), and the form that does not arise from kamma (Dhs 1763:2-5). The Aṭṭhakathā applies the same method with the negation (As 854:6).
3. **Anupādiṇṇaanupādāniyā dhammā** - States that are neither clung to by kamma nor subject to clinging: the supramundane paths, their fruits, and the unconditioned element ([Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)) (Dhs 1764:2).

### Quote
> **(Ka) upādiṇṇupādāniyā dhammā. (Kha) anupādiṇṇupādāniyā dhammā. (Ga) anupādiṇṇaanupādāniyā dhammā.**  
> 4.(Ka) Mental phenomena that are grasped and graspable. (Kha) Mental phenomena that are not grasped, but are graspable. (Ga) Mental phenomena that are not grasped and not graspable.  
>
