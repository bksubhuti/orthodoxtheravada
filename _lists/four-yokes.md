### Overview
Sharing the exact same doctrinal constituents as the Four Floods, the Four Yokes (*[Yoga](/glossary/yoga/)*) use an agricultural metaphor. Just as a heavy wooden yoke binds an ox to a cart, forcing it to endlessly pull burdens in a circle, these defilements yoke the mind to the cycle of birth and death. Liberation is frequently called *Yogakkhema* (Rest from the Yoke).

### The List
1. **Kāmayoga** - The Yoke of Sensuality: One does not understand as they are the origin, passing away, gratification, danger and escape in the case of sensual pleasures, and lust, delight, craving and clinging for sensual pleasures lie latent (AN 4.10, A-iv 114).
2. **Bhavayoga** - The Yoke of Existence: The same non-understanding in the case of becomings (*bhavā*), with craving for becoming lying latent (A-iv 115).
3. **Diṭṭhiyoga** - The Yoke of Views: The same non-understanding in the case of views (*diṭṭhi*), with craving for views lying latent (A-iv 116).
4. **Avijjāyoga** - The Yoke of Ignorance: One does not understand as they are the origin, passing away, gratification, danger and escape in the case of the six bases of contact, and ignorance and unknowing lie latent in them (A-iv 117).

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, yogā. Katame cattāro? Kāmayogo, bhavayogo, diṭṭhiyogo, avijjāyogo.**  
> “Monks, there are these four bonds (yoga). What four? The bond of sensuality, the bond of existence, the bond of views, and the bond of ignorance.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Yoga [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 4.10) – The Buddha details these four yokes (A-iv 114–117) and, in the same sutta, the four unyokings (*visaṃyoga*, A-iv 118 ff.), which come from understanding as it is what the yoked person does not. One yoked to all four is called *ayogakkhemī* (A-iv 117).
