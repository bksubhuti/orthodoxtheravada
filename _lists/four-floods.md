### Overview
The Buddha frequently used water metaphors to describe the overwhelming power of defilements. The Four Floods (*[Ogha](/glossary/ogha/)*) represent the powerful currents that sweep unenlightened beings away, drowning them in the ocean of [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/). The task of the meditator is to "cross the flood."

### The List
1. **Kāmogha** - The Flood of Sensuality: The overwhelming tide of desire for bodily pleasures—sights, sounds, smells, tastes, and touches.
2. **Bhavogha** - The Flood of Existence: The deep current of craving to be, to endure, to achieve status, or to be reborn in heavenly realms.
3. **Diṭṭhogha** - The Flood of Views: The swirling waters of dogmatic opinions, philosophical theories, and wrong views (eternalism, annihilationism).
4. **Avijjogha** - The Flood of Ignorance: The foundational, dark, murky water of delusion, the inability to see the Four Noble Truths.

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, oghā. Katame cattāro? Kāmogho, bhavogho, diṭṭhogho, avijjogho – ime kho, bhikkhave, cattāro oghā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, there are these four floods. What four? The flood of sensuality, the flood of existence, the flood of views, the flood of ignorance—these indeed, bhikkhus, are the four floods.  
>  
> — [Saṃyutta Nikāya](/glossary/samyutta-nikaya/) (SN 45.172, Ogha Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_sa_05&page_number=54&name=mah%C4%81vaggasa%E1%B9%83yuttap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJvbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgb2doxIEu)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Ogha [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (SN 45.172) – Lists the four floods: kāmogha, bhavogha, diṭṭhogha, avijjogha.
* **Canonical**: *Oghataraṇa Sutta* (SN 1.1) – The very first sutta of the Saṃyutta Nikāya, where a deity asks the Buddha how he crossed the flood. He replies: "By not halting and not straining." (It speaks of crossing the flood but does not itself list the four.)
