### Overview
A Theravāda [bhikkhu](/glossary/bhikkhu/) lives a life of absolute material simplicity, relying entirely on the generosity of the lay community. The Buddha allowed monastics to accept and use only four basic categories of material goods, and they must constantly reflect on the proper purpose of these items to prevent attachment.

### The List
1. **[Cīvara](/glossary/civara/)** - Robes: Used just to ward off cold, heat, and the touch of flies, mosquitoes, wind, sun and creeping things, and to cover the private parts to conceal shame (M-i 75:2).
2. **[Piṇḍapāta](/glossary/pindapata/)** - Almsfood: Eaten not for amusement, intoxication, adornment or beautification, but just for the maintenance and sustenance of the body, for the cessation of harm and for the support of the holy life (M-i 76:1).
3. **[Senāsana](/glossary/senasana/)** - Lodging / Shelter: Used just to ward off cold, heat, and the touch of gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, sun and creeping things, to ward off the perils of climate and to enjoy seclusion (M-i 77:1).
4. **Gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāra** - Medicines for the sick: Used just to ward off arisen painful feelings caused by bodily imbalance and for the ultimate freedom from affliction (M-i 78:1).

### Quote
> **Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu paṭisaṅkhā yoniso cīvaraṃ paṭisevati – ‘ yāvadeva sītassa paṭighātāya, uṇhassa paṭighātāya, ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīṃsapasamphassānaṃ paṭighātāya, yāvadeva hirikopīnappaṭicchādanatthaṃ ’. “Paṭisaṅkhā yoniso piṇḍapātaṃ paṭisevati – ‘ neva davāya, na madāya, na maṇḍanāya, na vibhūsanāya, yāvadeva imassa kāyassa ṭhitiyā yāpanāya, vihiṃsūparatiyā, brahmacariyānuggahāya, iti purāṇañca vedanaṃ paṭihaṅkhāmi navañca vedanaṃ na uppādessāmi, yātrā ca me bhavissati anavajjatā ca phāsuvihāro ca ’. “Paṭisaṅkhā yoniso senāsanaṃ paṭisevati – ‘ yāvadeva sītassa paṭighātāya, uṇhassa paṭighātāya, ḍaṃsamakasavātātapasarīṃsapasamphassānaṃ paṭighātāya, yāvadeva utuparissayavinodanapaṭisallānārāmatthaṃ ’. “Paṭisaṅkhā yoniso gilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhāraṃ paṭisevati – ‘ yāvadeva uppannānaṃ veyyābādhikānaṃ vedanānaṃ paṭighātāya, abyābajjhaparamatāya ’.**  
> Here, monks, a mendicant, reflecting wisely, makes use of robes (cīvaraṃ) – ‘just for warding off cold, for warding off heat, for warding off the touch of flies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, and creeping things, just for covering the private parts to conceal shame’. ‘‘ Reflecting wisely, a mendicant makes use of almsfood. ‘Not for amusement, not for intoxication, not for adornment, not for beautification, but just for the maintenance and sustenance of this body, for the cessation of harm, and for the support of the holy life. Thus, I will get rid of old feelings and not give rise to new feelings. And there will be for me sustenance, blamelessness, and comfortable living.’ ‘‘ Reflecting wisely, a mendicant makes use of lodgings. ‘Just for warding off cold, for warding off heat, for warding off the touch of gadflies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, and creeping things, and just for warding off the perils of climate and for enjoying seclusion.’ ‘‘ Reflecting wisely, a mendicant makes use of medicinal requisites for the sick. ‘Just for warding off arisen painful feelings caused by bodily imbalance, and for the ultimate freedom from affliction.’  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sabbāsava [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 2) – The Buddha instructs monks to reflect deeply on these four requisites as a method for abandoning the taints (*āsavas*) connected to use.
