### Overview
In the Mūla these three, as *saṅkappa*, are called right intention (*sammāsaṅkappa*) of the Noble Eightfold Path (S-v 51:2). In the Itivuttaka the three wholesome thoughts do not make one blind, make one see, aid wisdom and lead to Nibbāna (It 843:1).

### The List
1. **Nekkhammavitakka** - Thoughts of renunciation: Renunciation thought. It is the one given up by a monk who thinks and ponders sensual thoughts much (M-i 693:2).
2. **Abyāpādavitakka** - Thoughts of non-ill-will (Loving-kindness): Non-ill-will thought. Paired in the sutta with ill-will thought (M-i 690:3-4).
3. **Avihiṃsāvitakka** - Thoughts of non-cruelty (Compassion): Non-harming thought. It is the one given up by a monk who thinks and ponders harming thoughts much (M-i 693:3).

### Quote
> **“Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi – ‘ yaṃnūnāhaṃ dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā vitakke vihareyyan ’ ti. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, yo cāyaṃ kāmavitakko yo ca byāpādavitakko yo ca vihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ ekaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ; yo cāyaṃ nekkhammavitakko yo ca abyāpādavitakko yo ca avihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ dutiyaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ.**  
> “Monks, before my enlightenment, while I was still only an unenlightened Bodhisatta, this thought occurred to me: ‘What if I were to divide my thoughts into two classes?’ So, monks, I made these—sensual thought, ill-will thought, and harmful thought—one division. And I made these—renunciation thought, non-ill-will thought, and non-harmful thought—the second division.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 19, Dvedhāvitakka Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_01&page_number=161&name=m%C5%ABlapa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFB1YmJldmEgbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtYm9kaMSBIGFuYWJoaXNhbWJ1ZGRoYXNzYQ%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Dvedhāvitakka [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 19) – The Buddha recounts that, before his awakening, he divided thoughts into two classes (M-i 690:3-4); what a monk much thinks and ponders on becomes the inclination of his mind (M-i 693:1).
* **Canonical**: *Andhakaraṇa Sutta* (Iti 87; It 843:1-6) – the three wholesome thoughts lead to wisdom and [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).
* **Canonical**: *Saccavibhaṅga Sutta* (MN 141) – Ven. Sāriputta defines right intention (*sammāsaṅkappo*) as nekkhammasaṅkappo, abyāpādasaṅkappo, avihiṃsāsaṅkappo (M-iii 1193:2); the Mūla word there is *saṅkappa*, not *vitakka*. Also S-v 51:2.
