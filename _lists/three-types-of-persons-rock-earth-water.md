### Overview
The Buddha frequently used striking natural metaphors to describe the psychological traits of human beings. In assessing how individuals handle anger and resentment (*[dosa](/glossary/dosa/)*), he described three types of individual by how long their anger lasts and how they respond when spoken to harshly (A-iii 1176-1178).

### The List
1. **Pāsāṇalekhūpamo** - Like an inscription in rock: A person who is often angry, and whose anger underlies for a long time, like an inscription in rock that is not quickly effaced by wind or water.
2. **Pathavilekhūpamo** - Like an inscription in soil: A person who is often angry, but whose anger does not underlie for a long time, like an inscription in soil that is quickly effaced by wind or water.
3. **Udakalekhūpamo** - Like an inscription in water: A person who, even when spoken to roughly, harshly or in an unpleasing way, is nevertheless congenial, companionable and courteous, like an inscription in water that immediately disappears.

### Quote
> **“Tayome, bhikkhave, puggalā santo saṃvijjamānā lokasmiṃ. Katame tayo? Pāsāṇalekhūpamo puggalo, pathavilekhūpamo puggalo, udakalekhūpamo puggalo.**  
> “Monks, there are these three kinds of individuals found existing in the world. What three? An individual like an inscription in rock, an individual like an inscription in soil, and an individual like an inscription in water.  
>
