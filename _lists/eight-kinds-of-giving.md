### Overview
While the physical act of giving (*[dāna](/glossary/dana/)*) is always beneficial, the kammic result is heavily determined by the mental intention behind the gift. The Buddha outlined eight specific motivations that drive people to give.

### The List
1. **Āsajja dānaṃ deti** - Giving spontaneously: Giving when the recipient has arrived: on seeing him come, one has him sit down, honours him and gives (Manorathapūraṇī).
2. **Bhayā dānaṃ deti** - Giving out of fear: Giving from fear of blame ("this one is a non-giver") or fear of the lower worlds (Manorathapūraṇī).
3. **Adāsi me ti dānaṃ deti** - Giving thinking "He gave to me": Giving because "he gave to me before" (Manorathapūraṇī).
4. **Dassati me ti dānaṃ deti** - Giving thinking "He will give to me": Giving because "he will give to me in the future" (Manorathapūraṇī).
5. **Sāhu dānan ti dānaṃ deti** - Giving thinking "Giving is good": Giving because giving is good, praised by the wise such as the Buddha (Manorathapūraṇī).
6. **Ahaṃ pacāmi, ime na pacantī ti...** - Giving thinking "I cook, they do not": Giving because "I cook, they do not cook; it is not right that I, who cook, should not give to those who do not cook."
7. **Kittisaddo abbhuggacchatū ti...** - Giving to acquire a good reputation: Giving because "a good reputation will arise for me from giving this gift."
8. **Cittālaṅkāracittaparikkhāratthaṃ dānaṃ deti** - Giving to adorn and beautify the mind: Giving for the purpose of adorning and equipping the mind of serenity and insight (Manorathapūraṇī).

### Quote
> **“Aṭṭhimāni, bhikkhave, dānāni. Katamāni aṭṭha? Āsajja dānaṃ deti, bhayā dānaṃ deti,‘ adāsi me ’ ti dānaṃ deti,‘ dassati me ’ ti dānaṃ deti,‘ sāhu dānan ’ ti dānaṃ deti,‘ ahaṃ pacāmi, ime na pacanti; nārahāmi pacanto apacantānaṃ dānaṃ adātun ’ ti dānaṃ deti,‘ imaṃ me dānaṃ dadato kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggacchatī ’ ti dānaṃ deti, cittālaṅkāracittaparikkhāratthaṃ dānaṃ deti. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, aṭṭha dānānī” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these eight gifts. What are these eight? (1) One gives a gift prying. (2) One gives a gift through fear. (3) One gives a gift, thinking: ‘He gave to me.’ (4) One gives a gift, thinking: ‘He will give to me.’ (5) One gives a gift, thinking: ‘Giving is good.’ (6) One gives a gift, thinking: ‘I am well-off; these people are not well-off. It is not right that I, who am well-off, should not give a gift to those who are not well-off.’ (7) One gives a gift, thinking: ‘Because I have given this gift, a good reputation will arise for my giving.’ (8) One gives a gift for the purpose of ornamenting the mind, equipping the mind. These, indeed, monks, are the eight gifts.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 8.31, Paṭhamadāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_08&page_number=68&name=a%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzEuW2TEqy4gbmkuMy4zMzZd4oCY4oCYIEHhua3hua1oaW3EgW5pLCBiaGlra2hhdmUsIGTEgW7EgW5pLg%3D%3D)
