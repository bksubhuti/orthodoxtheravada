### Overview
The *Anupubbavihāra* are the nine progressive states of deep meditative absorption. They are the nine "step-by-step dwellings" of the Anupubbavihāra Sutta (AN 9.32), which runs from the first jhāna through the four formless attainments to the cessation of perception and feeling.

### The List
1. **Paṭhama [Jhāna](/glossary/jhana/)** - First Fine-Material Absorption.
2. **Dutiya Jhāna** - Second Fine-Material Absorption.
3. **Tatiya Jhāna** - Third Fine-Material Absorption.
4. **Catuttha Jhāna** - Fourth Fine-Material Absorption.
5. **[Ākāsānañcāyatana](/glossary/akasanancayatana/)** - The Base of Infinite Space (First Formless Absorption).
6. **[Viññāṇañcāyatana](/glossary/vinnanancayatana/)** - The Base of Infinite Consciousness (Second Formless Absorption).
7. **[Ākiñcaññāyatana](/glossary/akincannayatana/)** - The Base of Nothingness (Third Formless Absorption).
8. **[Nevasaññānāsaññāyatana](/glossary/nevasannanasannayatana/)** - The Base of Neither-Perception-Nor-Non-Perception (Fourth Formless Absorption).
9. **[Saññāvedayitanirodha](/glossary/sannavedayitanirodha/)** - The Cessation of Perception and Feeling: The temporary, complete halting of all mental activity and consciousness. The Paṭisambhidāmagga-aṭṭhakathā says it is attained only by non-returners and those with taints destroyed who have gained the eight attainments (Paṭis-a 1206:2).

### Quote
> **“Navayime, bhikkhave, anupubbavihārā. Katame nava? Paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ, dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ, tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ, catutthaṃ jhānaṃ, ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ, viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ, ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ, saññāvedayitanirodho – ime kho, bhikkhave, nava anupubbavihārā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these nine step-by-step dwellings. Which nine? The first absorption, the second absorption, the third absorption, the fourth absorption, the dimension of infinite space, the dimension of infinite consciousness, the dimension of nothingness, the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception, and the cessation of perception and feeling—these, monks, are the nine step-by-step dwellings.”  
>
