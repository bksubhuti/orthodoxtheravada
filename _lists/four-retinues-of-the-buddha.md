### Overview
The structural integrity and long-term survival of the Buddhist dispensation rely on a balanced, fourfold social configuration known as the *Catasso Parisā* (Four Retinues). The Buddha explicitly stated that his mission would only be complete when all four groups were deeply educated, morally pure, confident in the texts, and capable of defending the teaching from heretical distortion.

### The List
1. **[Bhikkhu](/glossary/bhikkhu/)** - Monks: The fully ordained male monastics who carry the burden of scriptural preservation (*[pariyatti](/glossary/pariyatti/)*) and intensive meditation.
2. **[Bhikkhunī](/glossary/bhikkhuni/)** - Nuns: The fully ordained female monastics who historically mirrored the monks in ascetic discipline and realization.
3. **[Upāsaka](/glossary/upasaka/)** - Laymen: The male householders who keep the five precepts and financially sustain the monastic community with the four requisites.
4. **[Upāsikā](/glossary/upasika/)** - Laywomen: The female householders who practice deep generosity, master the discourses, and provide vital support to the dispensation.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Mahāparinibbāna](/glossary/mahaparinibbana/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 16) – The Buddha tells Māra that he will not pass away until these four retinues have become perfectly accomplished, proficient, and well-trained in the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/).
