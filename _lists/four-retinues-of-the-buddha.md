### Overview
The structural integrity and long-term survival of the Buddhist dispensation rely on a balanced, fourfold social configuration known as the *Catasso Parisā* (Four Retinues). In the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta the Buddha tells Māra he will not attain parinibbāna until each of the four groups is accomplished, disciplined, confident, learned, bearers of the Teaching, and able to refute the doctrines of others.

### The List
1. **[Bhikkhu](/glossary/bhikkhu/)** - Monks (*bhikkhū*).
2. **[Bhikkhunī](/glossary/bhikkhuni/)** - Nuns (*bhikkhuniyo*).
3. **[Upāsaka](/glossary/upasaka/)** - Laymen, male lay followers (*upāsakā*).
4. **[Upāsikā](/glossary/upasika/)** - Laywomen, female lay followers (*upāsikāyo*).

In DN 16 the Buddha tells Māra he will not attain parinibbāna until each of these four, in turn, are "true disciples, accomplished, disciplined, confident, learned, bearers of the Teaching" and so on (monks D-ii 450:1, nuns 451:1, laymen 452:1, laywomen 453:1).

### Quote
> **“Ettāvatā, bhante uttara, manussesu catasso parisā – bhikkhū, bhikkhuniyo, upāsakā, upāsikāyo.**  
> “Venerable Uttara, there are four assemblies among human beings: bhikkhus, nuns, male lay followers, and female lay followers.  
>
