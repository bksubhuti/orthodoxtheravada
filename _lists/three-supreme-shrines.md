### Overview
The Jātaka commentary tells the occasion. When the Buddha went touring the countryside to help those ready to be taught, the people of Sāvatthī came to Jetavana with scents and garlands and, finding no other place of veneration, laid them at the door of the Perfumed Chamber and went away without joy (Ja-a-iv 2143:2). Anāthapiṇḍika told the elder Ānanda, who asked the Buddha how many shrines (*cetiya*) there are. "Three, Ānanda": the relic shrine, the shrine of use and the shrine of indication (Ja-a-iv 2143:3-7). *Cetiya* means that which is to be heaped up, to be worshipped, or that which is heaped up (*cayitabbanti cetiyaṃ, pūjetabbanti vuttaṃ hoti, citattā vā cetiyaṃ*, Pj-i 1068:1).

### The List
1. **Sārīrikacetiya** (*dhātukacetiya*) - The Shrine of Relics: the stūpa that has a relic chamber and contains relics (*dhātugabbhathūpā sadhātukā dhātukacetiyaṃ*, Pj-i 1068:3; the Ṭīkā: "made by depositing the relics", Sp-t-i-a 711:8). It cannot be made while the Buddha is alive, for it exists at the time of the Buddha's Parinibbāna (Ja-a-iv 2143:9-10).
2. **Pāribhogikacetiya** - The Shrine of Use: the Bodhi tree is the shrine of use (*bodhirukkho paribhogacetiyaṃ*, Pj-i 1068:3). The Great Bodhi Tree, used by the Buddhas, is a cetiya even while the Buddhas are alive (Ja-a-iv 2143:10). The Ṭīkā explains it as a shrine made by depositing the used requisites (Sp-t-i-a 711:5).
3. **Uddissakacetiya** - The Shrine of Indication: the Buddha image is the uddissakacetiya (*buddhapaṭimā uddissakacetiyaṃ*, Pj-i 1068:3); in the Buddha's words it is "without substance, merely an object of one's regard" (*avatthukaṃ mamāyanamattameva*, Ja-a-iv 2143:10). The Ṭīkā says that this threefold division fits best, because even the image is included in the uddissaka-cetiya (Sp-t-i-a 711:10-11).

When the Buddha agreed to Ānanda's request, Ānanda planted a seed from the Great Bodhi Tree at the gate of Jetavana; "in that way it will be as if I have a permanent residence in Jetavana" (Ja-a-iv 2143:11-12, 2144:1-2).


### Quote
> **So “sādhū” ti sampaṭicchitvā tathāgataṃ pucchi “kati nu kho, bhante, cetiyānī” ti? “Tīṇi ānandā” ti. “Katamāni, bhante, tīṇī” ti? “Sārīrikaṃ pāribhogikaṃ uddissakan” ti. “Sakkā pana, bhante, tumhesu dharantesuyeva cetiyaṃ kātun” ti. “Ānanda, sārīrikaṃ na sakkā kātuṃ. Tañhi buddhānaṃ parinibbānakāle hoti, uddissakaṃ avatthukaṃ mamāyanamattameva hoti, buddhehi paribhutto mahābodhirukkho buddhesu dharantesupi cetiyamevā” ti.**  
> Agreeing, he approached the Tathāgata and asked, “How many types of shrines are there, venerable sir?” “Three, Ānanda.” “What are the three, venerable sir?” “A bodily shrine, a shrine of use, and a commemorative shrine.” “Is it possible, venerable sir, to make a shrine while you are still alive?” “Ānanda, it is not possible to make a bodily shrine. That happens at the time of a Buddha’s parinibbāna. A commemorative shrine has no material basis and depends only on imagination. But the great Bo-tree used by the Buddhas is indeed a shrine even while the Buddhas are alive.”  
>
