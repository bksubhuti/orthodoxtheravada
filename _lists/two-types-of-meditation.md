### Overview
Theravāda mental development (*[bhāvanā](/glossary/bhavana/)*) is categorized into two distinct but complementary streams. While they cultivate different mental faculties and suppress different defilements, both are essential in the orthodox path, working in tandem to liberate the mind.

### The List
1. **[Samatha](/glossary/samatha/)** - Tranquility / Serenity: The development of deep, unified concentration (*[samādhi](/glossary/samadhi/)*). When tranquility is developed the mind is developed, and when the mind is developed lust (*rāga*) is abandoned (A-ii 71:4-7).
2. **[Vipassanā](/glossary/vipassana/)** - Insight: The direct observation of physical and mental phenomena to realize their impermanence, unsatisfactoriness, and non-self nature. When insight is developed wisdom (*[paññā](/glossary/panna/)*) is developed, and when wisdom is developed ignorance (*[avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)*) is abandoned (A-ii 71:8-11).

### Quote
> **“Dve me, bhikkhave, dhammā vijjābhāgiyā. Katame dve? Samatho ca vipassanā ca. Iti kho, bhikkhave, rāgavirāgā cetovimutti, avijjāvirāgā paññāvimuttī” ti.**  
> “Monks, these two mental phenomena are conducive to knowledge (vijjābhāgiyā). Which two? Tranquility (samatha) and insight (vipassanā). Thus, monks, the liberation of mind (cetovimutti) is through the fading away of lust, and the liberation by wisdom (paññāvimutti) is through the fading away of ignorance.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 2.32) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=62&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MzIu4oCY4oCYIER2ZSBtZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBkaGFtbcSBIHZpamrEgWJoxIFnaXnEgS4%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: Dukanipāta (AN 2.32, A-ii 71, CST para 32) – The two things that are conducive to knowledge (*vijjābhāgiyā*): samatha abandons lust, vipassanā abandons ignorance.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* – Serves as the ultimate manual detailing the specific meditation subjects (*kammaṭṭhāna*) suitable for each of these two developments.
