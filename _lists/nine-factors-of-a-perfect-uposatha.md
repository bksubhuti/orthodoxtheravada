### Overview
While laypeople normally observe the Eight [Uposatha](/glossary/uposatha/) Precepts on observance days, the Buddha introduced a ninth factor to elevate the practice from mere moral restraint to active mental purification. The sutta declares the Uposatha undertaken with these nine factors to be of great fruit, great profit, great splendor and widespread effect.

### The List
1. **Abstaining from killing.**
2. **Abstaining from stealing.**
3. **Abstaining from all sexual activity (abrahmacariya, unchastity).**
4. **Abstaining from false speech.**
5. **Abstaining from intoxicants.**
6. **Abstaining from eating at the wrong time (after noon).**
7. **Abstaining from entertainment and bodily adornment.**
8. **Abstaining from high and luxurious beds.**
9. **Pervading the world with Loving-kindness ([Mettā](/glossary/metta/)):** The practitioner sits and systematically pervades all four directions, above, and below, with a mind of loving-kindness that is abundant, exalted, and free from hostility.

### Quote
> **“Navahi, bhikkhave, aṅgehi samannāgato uposatho upavuttho mahapphalo hoti mahānisaṃso mahājutiko mahāvipphāro. Kathaṃ upavuttho ca, bhikkhave, navahaṅgehi samannāgato uposatho mahapphalo hoti mahānisaṃso mahājutiko mahāvipphāro? Idha, bhikkhave, ariyasāvako iti paṭisañcikkhati – ‘ yāvajīvaṃ arahanto pāṇātipātaṃ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭiviratā nihitadaṇḍā nihitasatthā lajjī dayāpannā sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampino viharanti; ahampajja imañca rattiṃ imañca divasaṃ pāṇātipātaṃ pahāya pāṇātipātā paṭivirato nihitadaṇḍo nihitasattho lajjī dayāpanno sabbapāṇabhūtahitānukampī viharāmi. Imināpaṅgena arahataṃ anukaromi; uposatho ca me upavuttho bhavissatī ’ ti.**  
> “Monks, when the Uposatha (observance day) is undertaken possessed of nine factors, it is of great fruit, of great profit, of great splendor, and of widespread effect. And how is the Uposatha observed so that, being possessed of nine factors, it is of great fruit, of great profit, of great splendor, and of widespread effect? Here, monks, a noble disciple considers thus: ‘As long as they live, the Arahants (perfected ones), abandoning the killing of living beings, abstain from killing living creatures; with rod laid aside, with weapon laid aside, they are conscientious and merciful, and they dwell compassionate for the welfare of all living beings. Today I too, for this day and night, abandoning the killing of living beings, will abstain from killing living creatures; with rod laid aside, with weapon laid aside, I am conscientious and merciful, and I dwell compassionate for the welfare of all living beings. By means of this factor I emulate the Arahants; and the Uposatha will be observed by me.’  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Navaṅguposatha [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 9.18) – The Buddha teaches this ninefold practice, in which the noble disciple, in each factor, follows the Arahants for a day and night; the Uposatha so observed is "mahapphalo … mahāvipphāro".
