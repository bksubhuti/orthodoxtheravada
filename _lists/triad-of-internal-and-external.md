### Overview
The twentieth triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā categorizes states as internal, external, or both (Dhs 63:1-65:1). The Aṭṭhakathā notes that the word *ajjhatta* has four senses and that here the sense "one's own" (*niyakajjhatta*) is intended: states occurring in one's own continuum, belonging to each individual, are internal; those that are outside that, whether bound to the faculties or not, are external (As 870:2, 870:9-11).

### The List
1. **Ajjhattā dhammā** - Internal states: those states of each being that are internal, personal, fixed, individual and clung to (*upādiṇṇā*): form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness (Dhs 1818:2).
2. **Bahiddhā dhammā** - External states: the same of other beings and other persons (Dhs 1819:2); and, in the later exposition, matter not bound to the faculties (*anindriyabaddharūpa*) and Nibbāna are external (Dhs 2235:2).
3. **Ajjhattabahiddhā dhammā** - Internal and external states: "both of those" (*tadubhayaṃ*) (Dhs 1820:2). The Aṭṭhakathā: the third term is stated by way of both (As 870:12).

### Quote
> **(Ka) ajjhattā dhammā. (Kha) bahiddhā dhammā. (Ga) ajjhattabahiddhā dhammā.**  
> 20.(Ka) Internal mental phenomena. (Kha) External mental phenomena. (Ga) Internally and externally mental phenomena.  
>
