### Overview
In the Vitthatadhana Sutta (AN 7.6) the Buddha names seven kinds of wealth (*dhanāni*), each defined as that of a noble disciple (*ariyasāvako*): faith, virtue, moral shame, moral concern, learning, generosity and wisdom. The closing verse says that whoever, woman or man, has these is called "adaliddo" (not poor), and that his life is not in vain (A-vii 53-55).

### The List
1. **Saddhādhana** - The Treasure of Faith: Deep, unshakeable confidence in the enlightenment of the Buddha.
2. **Sīladhana** - The Treasure of Virtue: Strict adherence to the moral precepts (not killing, stealing, etc.).
3. **Hirīdhana** - The Treasure of Moral Shame: An internal sense of dignity that recoils from unwholesome actions.
4. **Ottappadhana** - The Treasure of Moral Dread: A healthy fear of the kammic consequences of doing evil.
5. **Sutadhana** - The Treasure of Learning: Deep knowledge and memorization of the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/).
6. **Cāgadhana** - The Treasure of Generosity: A heart free from stinginess, delighting in giving and sharing.
7. **Paññādhana** - The Treasure of Wisdom: The penetrative insight into the arising and passing away of all conditioned things.

### Quote
> **“Sattimāni, bhikkhave, dhanāni. Katamāni satta? Saddhādhanaṃ, sīladhanaṃ, hirīdhanaṃ, ottappadhanaṃ, sutadhanaṃ, cāgadhanaṃ, paññādhanaṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these seven kinds of wealth. What are these seven? The wealth of faith, the wealth of virtuous behavior, the wealth of moral shame, the wealth of moral concern, the wealth of learning, the wealth of generosity, and the wealth of wisdom.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Vitthatadhana [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 7.6, A-vii 42) – each treasure is defined in turn (A-vii 44-), and the closing verse says that whoever has them, woman or man, is "adaliddo", and his life is "amogha" (A-vii 53-55). The same seven are in the Saṃkhittadhana Sutta (AN 7.5, A-vii 34).
