### Overview
The Buddha used a medical analogy to explain why the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) must be taught universally, even though some people will never understand it. Just as a doctor treats all patients because he cannot be certain who will benefit, a teacher proclaims the Dhamma for the sake of the middle group.

### The List
1. **One who will not recover:** A patient who, whether they receive medicine, nursing, and proper food or not, will not recover from their illness. (The sutta compares this to a person who will not enter the fixed course of rightness in wholesome states, whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata and hear the Dhamma and discipline he proclaims, A-iii 120:3).
2. **One who will recover anyway:** A patient who, whether they receive medicine and nursing or not, will recover naturally. (The sutta compares this to a person who will enter the fixed course whether or not they get to see the Tathāgata and hear his Dhamma and discipline, A-iii 121:1).
3. **One who recovers only with treatment:** A patient who will only recover *if* they receive medicine, nursing, and proper food; without it, they will die. (The sutta compares this to a person who will enter the fixed course *only if* they get to see the Tathāgata and hear the Dhamma and discipline he proclaims, A-iii 122:1; it is because of this person that the exposition of the Dhamma was allowed, A-iii 123:1).

### Quote
> **“Tayome, bhikkhave, gilānā santo saṃvijjamānā lokasmiṃ. Katame tayo? Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco gilāno labhanto vā sappāyāni bhojanāni alabhanto vā sappāyāni bhojanāni, labhanto vā sappāyāni bhesajjāni alabhanto vā sappāyāni bhesajjāni, labhanto vā patirūpaṃ upaṭṭhākaṃ alabhanto vā patirūpaṃ upaṭṭhākaṃ neva vuṭṭhāti tamhā ābādhā. “Idha pana, bhikkhave, ekacco gilāno labhanto vā sappāyāni bhojanāni alabhanto vā sappāyāni bhojanāni, labhanto vā sappāyāni bhesajjāni alabhanto vā sappāyāni bhesajjāni, labhanto vā patirūpaṃ upaṭṭhākaṃ alabhanto vā patirūpaṃ upaṭṭhākaṃ vuṭṭhāti tamhā ābādhā. “Idha pana, bhikkhave, ekacco gilāno labhantova sappāyāni bhojanāni no alabhanto, labhantova sappāyāni bhesajjāni no alabhanto, labhantova patirūpaṃ upaṭṭhākaṃ no alabhanto vuṭṭhāti tamhā ābādhā.**  
> “Monks, there are these three kinds of sick persons found existing in the world. What three? Here, monks, someone who is sick will not recover from that illness whether or not they receive suitable food, whether or not they receive suitable medicine, and whether or not they receive a proper attendant. “But here, monks, someone who is sick will recover from that illness whether or not they receive suitable food, whether or not they receive suitable medicine, and whether or not they receive a proper attendant. “But here, monks, someone who is sick will recover from that illness only if they receive suitable food, not if they fail to receive it; only if they receive suitable medicine, not if they fail to receive it; and only if they receive a proper attendant, not if they fail to receive one.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Gilāna [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 3.22) – The Buddha gives the three kinds of sick people (A-iii 116-119) and then compares three kinds of individuals to them (A-iii 120-123). Because of the third kind, and because of the third sick person, the sick-diet, sick-medicine and nursing allowances were permitted and the exposition of the Dhamma was allowed; and "because of this individual the Dhamma should also be taught to the others" (A-iii 123:1-2).
