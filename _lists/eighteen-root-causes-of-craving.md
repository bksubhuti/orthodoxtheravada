### Overview
The Buddha identified the exact psychological mechanism by which craving and ego-formation occur. The instant the mind makes the fundamental, delusional assumption "I am" (*asmi*), a cascade of eighteen specific internal psychological projections follows, creating the complex web of identity, desire, and anxiety. *(Note: The [sutta](/glossary/sutta/) lists 18 internal and 18 external, making 36, but the core internal framework is 18).*

### The List
Dependent on the five aggregates taken as internal, when there exists "I am" (*Asmīti sati*), the eighteen *taṇhāvicaritāni* are, in the Aṭṭhakathā's four groups of four plus two:
1. **Asmi** - "I am."
2. **Itthasmi** - "I am like this."
3. **Evaṃsmi** - "I am in this way."
4. **Aññathāsmi** - "I am in another way."
5. **Asasmi** - "I am permanent."
6. **Satasmi** - "I am impermanent."
7. **Santi** - "I might be."
8. **Itthaṃ santi** - "I might be like this."
9. **Evaṃ santi** - "I might be in this way."
10. **Aññathā santi** - "I might be in another way."
11. **Apihaṃ santi** - "May I be."
12. **Apihaṃ itthaṃ santi** - "May I be like this."
13. **Apihaṃ evaṃ santi** - "May I be in this way."
14. **Apihaṃ aññathā santi** - "May I be in another way."
15. **Bhavissanti** - "I will be."
16. **Itthaṃ bhavissanti** - "I will be like this."
17. **Evaṃ bhavissanti** - "I will be in this way."
18. **Aññathā bhavissanti** - "I will be in another way."

### Quote
> **“Katamāni aṭṭhārasa taṇhāvicaritāni ajjhattikassa upādāya? Asmīti, bhikkhave, sati itthasmīti hoti, evaṃsmīti hoti, aññathāsmīti hoti, asasmīti hoti, satasmīti hoti, santi hoti, itthaṃ santi hoti, evaṃ santi hoti, aññathā santi hoti, apihaṃ santi hoti, apihaṃ itthaṃ santi hoti, apihaṃ evaṃ santi hoti, apihaṃ aññathā santi hoti, bhavissanti hoti, itthaṃ bhavissanti hoti, evaṃ bhavissanti hoti, aññathā bhavissanti hoti. Imāni aṭṭhārasa taṇhāvicaritāni ajjhattikassa upādāya.**  
> “What are the 18 craving-verbalizations depending on what is internal? When there exists ‘I am,’ monks, there comes to be ‘I am like this,’ there comes to be ‘I am in this way,’ there comes to be ‘I am in another way,’ there comes to be ‘I am permanent,’ there comes to be ‘I am impermanent,’ there comes to be ‘I might be,’ there comes to be ‘I might be like this,’ there comes to be ‘I might be in this way,’ there comes to be ‘I might be in another way,’ there comes to be ‘May I be,’ there comes to be ‘May I be like this,’ there comes to be ‘May I be in this way,’ there comes to be ‘May I be in another way,’ there comes to be ‘I will be,’ there comes to be ‘I will be like this,’ there comes to be ‘I will be in this way,’ there comes to be ‘I will be in another way.’ These are the 18 craving-verbalizations depending on what is internal.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Taṇhā](/glossary/tanha/) Sutta* (AN 4.199) – The Buddha teaches craving as *jālinī* (the ensnarer), which keeps the world from overcoming [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/), and lists eighteen *taṇhāvicaritāni* for the internal and eighteen for the external (thirty-six; with past, future and present, 108). The Aṭṭhakathā (Mp-iv 1644-1645) groups the eighteen as four present, two of eternalism and annihilation, four of doubt, four of wishing and four of the future.
