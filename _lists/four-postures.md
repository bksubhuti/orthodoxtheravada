### Overview
The human body can only exist in one of four primary modes. In Theravāda meditation, specifically in the contemplation of the body (*Kāyānupassanā*), these four postures become the primary anchors for developing continuous mindfulness and clear comprehension (*satisampajañña*) throughout the day.

### The List
1. **Gacchanto** - Walking: "when walking, he understands: 'I am walking'" (*gacchanto vā 'gacchāmī' ti pajānāti*).
2. **Ṭhito** - Standing: "when standing, he understands: 'I am standing'" (*ṭhito vā 'ṭhitomhī' ti pajānāti*).
3. **Nisinno** - Sitting: "when sitting, he understands: 'I am sitting'" (*nisinno vā 'nisinnomhī' ti pajānāti*).
4. **Sayāno** - Lying down: "when lying down, he understands: 'I am lying down'" (*sayāno vā 'sayānomhī' ti pajānāti*). The section closes: "In whatever way his body is disposed, he understands it thus" (M-i 291:2).

### Quote
> **“Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, bhikkhu gacchanto vā ‘ gacchāmī ’ ti pajānāti, ṭhito vā ‘ ṭhitomhī ’ ti pajānāti, nisinno vā ‘ nisinnomhī ’ ti pajānāti, sayāno vā ‘ sayānomhī ’ ti pajānāti. Yathā yathā vā panassa kāyo paṇihito hoti tathā tathā naṃ pajānāti.**  
> “Again, bhikkhus, when walking, a bhikkhu understands: ‘I am walking’; when standing, he understands: ‘I am standing’; when sitting, he understands: ‘I am sitting’; or when lying down, he understands: ‘I am lying down.’ In whatever way his body is disposed, he understands it thus.  
>
