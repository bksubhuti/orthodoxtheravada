### Overview
Devotion and respect toward the Buddha are central to Theravāda life. In AN 2.157 the Buddha names two kinds of offering (*pūjā*) and calls offering of the Dhamma the foremost. Shortly before his passing (*[Parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/)*), when the Twin Sal Trees showered flowers on his body, he told Ānanda that he is honoured with the highest worship by the one who practises the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma (D-ii 540:8-9).

### The List
1. **Āmisa-pūjā** - Material Homage: Offering with material things (*āmisena pūjanaṃ*, Mp-ii 277:1).
2. **Dhamma-pūjā** - [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) Homage: Offering with the Dhamma (*dhammena pūjanaṃ*, Mp-ii 277:1). The Buddha calls it the foremost of the two (*etadaggaṃ*, A-ii 265:5). In DN 16 the one who lives *dhammānudhammappaṭipanno*, *sāmīcippaṭipanno*, *anudhammacārī* honours the Tathāgata “paramāya pūjāya” (D-ii 540:9).

### Quote
> **“Dvemā, bhikkhave, pūjā. Katamā dve? Āmisapūjā ca dhammapūjā ca. Imā kho bhikkhave, dve pūjā. Etadaggaṃ, bhikkhave, imāsaṃ dvinnaṃ pūjānaṃ yadidaṃ dhammapūjā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these two kinds of offering. Which two? Offering of material goods and offering of the Dhamma. These are the two kinds of offering. The foremost of these two kinds of offering is that is to say, offering of the Dhamma.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 2.157) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=92&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTU3LuKAmOKAmCBEdmVtxIEsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgcMWrasSBLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: Dukanipāta (AN 2.157, A-ii 265:1-5, CST para 157) – The two kinds of offering, *āmisapūjā* and *dhammapūjā*; the names occur in the Mūla only here.
* **Canonical**: *[Mahāparinibbāna](/glossary/mahaparinibbana/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 16, D-ii 540:1-9) – When divine flowers fall on the Buddha's body, he tells Ānanda that he is not honoured in this way; the monk, nun, layman or laywoman who practises the Dhamma in accordance with the Dhamma honours him with the highest worship. The words *āmisapūjā* and *dhammapūjā* do not occur in DN.
