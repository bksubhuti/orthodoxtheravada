### Overview
The 32 Marks of a Great Man (*Mahāpurisalakkhaṇa*) are not exclusive to the Buddha. A [Bodhisatta](/glossary/bodhisatta/)'s immense kammic merit dictates that if he chooses to remain a householder, he will inevitably become a *Cakkavatti* (a Wheel-Turning Monarch). He bears the exact same 32 physical marks as a Buddha, but utilizes them to rule the world righteously rather than to teach liberation.

### The List
*(DN 30 gives one set of 32 marks for the Great Man who becomes either a Cakkavatti or a Buddha; for the full list see [The 32 Marks of a Great Man](/lists/thirty-two-marks-of-a-great-man/). The numbers here follow the order of D-iii 474-477. Some of them:)*
1. Feet with level tread.
2. Wheels on the soles of the feet.
3. Long fingers and toes.
11. Complexion like gold.
9. Standing and without bending, he can touch and rub his knees with either hand.
27. A large, long tongue.
31. The white tuft of hair between the eyebrows (*uṇṇā*).
32. The turban-like protrusion on the head (*uṇhīsa*).

**The Difference:**
While the Buddha is attended by monks and realizes [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/), the Cakkavatti is attended by the Seven Royal Treasures (The Wheel, the Elephant, the Horse, the Jewel, the Woman, the Treasurer, the Advisor) and, in the words of DN 30, conquers this earth to its ocean-bounds, ruling not by the rod, not by the sword, but by righteousness (*dhammena*).

### Quote
> **“Dvattiṃsimāni, bhikkhave, mahāpurisassa mahāpurisalakkhaṇāni, yehi samannāgatassa mahāpurisassa dveva gatiyo bhavanti anaññā. Sace agāraṃ ajjhāvasati, rājā hoti cakkavattī dhammiko dhammarājā cāturanto vijitāvī janapadatthāvariyappatto sattaratanasamannāgato. Tassimāni satta ratanāni bhavanti; seyyathidaṃ, cakkaratanaṃ hatthiratanaṃ assaratanaṃ maṇiratanaṃ itthiratanaṃ gahapatiratanaṃ pariṇāyakaratanameva sattamaṃ. Parosahassaṃ kho panassa puttā bhavanti sūrā vīraṅgarūpā parasenappamaddanā. So imaṃ pathaviṃ sāgarapariyantaṃ adaṇḍena asatthena dhammena abhivijiya ajjhāvasati.**  
> 199. “There are, monks, these thirty-two marks of a Great Man, possessed of which a Great Man has only two careers, and no other. If he lives at home, he becomes a Wheel-turning Monarch, a righteous king who rules in righteousness, lord of the four quarters, who has reached stability in his realm, and is possessed of the seven treasures. He has these seven treasures; that is to say: the Wheel-treasure, the Elephant-treasure, the Horse-treasure, the Gem-treasure, the Woman-treasure, the Wonderful Treasurer, and the Councillor-treasure as the seventh. He has more than a thousand sons, heroes, champions, vigorous of frame, crushers of the hosts of the enemy. Having conquered this earth to its ocean-bounds, he resides there, ruling not by the rod, not by the sword, but by righteousness.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Lakkhaṇa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 30, D-iii 469-478) – The thirty-two marks, and the two careers (Cakkavatti or Buddha) of one who bears them.
* **Canonical**: *Cakkavattisīhanāda [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 26) – Details the righteous rule and destiny of the Wheel-Turning Monarch; the list of 32 marks is not in this sutta.
