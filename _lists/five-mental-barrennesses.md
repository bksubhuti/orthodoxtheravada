### Overview
In the Cetokhila Sutta (MN 16) the Buddha says that a monk who has not abandoned the five barrennesses of the mind (*cetokhilā*) and the five shackles of the heart (*cetasovinibandhā*) cannot reach growth, increase and maturity in this Dhamma and discipline (M-i 633:1). Each barrenness is a state in which the mind does not incline to ardor, effort, perseverance and striving (*ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya*).

### The List
1. **Doubt in the Teacher:** Having doubts, uncertainty, and lack of confidence in the Buddha.
2. **Doubt in the Dhamma:** Having doubts, uncertainty, and lack of confidence in the Teaching.
3. **Doubt in the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/):** Having doubts, uncertainty, and lack of confidence in the Community.
4. **Doubt in the Training:** Having doubts, uncertainty, and lack of confidence in the rules of training and meditation.
5. **Anger towards companions:** The monk is angry, displeased, hard-hearted and stubborn (*khilajāto*) towards his fellows in the holy life (M-i 638:1).

### Quote
> **“Yassa kassaci, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno pañca cetokhilā appahīnā, pañca cetasovinibandhā asamucchinnā, so vatimasmiṃ dhammavinaye vuddhiṃ virūḷhiṃ vepullaṃ āpajjissatīti – netaṃ ṭhānaṃ vijjati. “Katamāssa pañca cetokhilā appahīnā honti? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati. Yo so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu satthari kaṅkhati vicikicchati nādhimuccati na sampasīdati tassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya. Yassa cittaṃ na namati ātappāya anuyogāya sātaccāya padhānāya, evamassāyaṃ paṭhamo cetokhilo appahīno hoti.**  
> “Mendicants, when any bhikkhu has five kinds of mental barrenness that he has not abandoned and five shackles of the heart that are not eradicated, that he will achieve growth, increase, and maturity in this Dhamma and discipline—this is not possible. What are the five kinds of mental barrenness that he has not abandoned? Here, mendicants, a bhikkhu is perplexed about the Teacher; he is undecided, is undecided, and does not place confidence in him. When a bhikkhu is perplexed about the Teacher, is undecided, is undecided, and does not place confidence in him, his mind does not incline to ardor, effort, perseverance, and striving. Since his mind does not incline to ardor, effort, perseverance, and striving, this is the first kind of mental barrenness that he has not abandoned.  
>
