### Overview
Orthodox Theravāda cosmology maps the entirety of [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/) into three broad planes of existence (*[bhava](/glossary/bhava/)* or *loka*). Every living being, from the lowest hell-dweller to the highest [Brahma](/glossary/brahma/) god, resides within one of these three realms, bound by their [kamma](/glossary/kamma/) and craving.

### The List
1. **Kāmabhava** - The Sensuous Realm: The planes of existence dominated by five-sense desire. This includes the four lower realms (hell, ghosts, demons, animals), the human realm, and the six lower heavenly realms (*devalokas*).
2. **Rūpabhava** - The Fine-Material Realm: The higher celestial planes inhabited by the Brahma gods. Beings are reborn here through the attainment of the fine-material absorptions (*rūpajhāna*). They possess subtle physical bodies but are free from gross sensual desires.
3. **Arūpabhava** - The Immaterial Realm: The highest planes of existence where physical form (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*) does not exist at all. Beings here consist purely of mind and abide in states of profound, formless absorption for vast eons.

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Saṅgīti [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (DN 33) – Venerable Sāriputta categorizes the universe into these three realms.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter VII) – Details the lifespan and qualities of beings inhabiting these various planes.
