### Overview
The twenty-first triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā classifies states by whether the object they take is internal, external, or both. The Aṭṭhakathā: this triplet is stated by taking those same three kinds of states as the object (As 871:1).

### The List
1. **Ajjhattārammaṇā dhammā** - States with internal objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking internal states as object (Dhs 1821:2). In the later exposition the answer is the base of infinite consciousness and the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (Dhs 2236:2).
2. **Bahiddhārammaṇā dhammā** - States with external objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking external states as object (Dhs 1822:2).
3. **Ajjhattabahiddhārammaṇā dhammā** - States with internal and external objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking internal-and-external states as object (Dhs 1823:2).

### Quote
> **(Ka) ajjhattārammaṇā dhammā. (Kha) bahiddhārammaṇā dhammā. (Ga) ajjhattabahiddhārammaṇā dhammā.**  
> 21.(Ka) Mental phenomena having an internal object. (Kha) Mental phenomena having an external object. (Ga) Mental phenomena having an internal and external object.  
>
