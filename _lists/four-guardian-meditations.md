### Overview
The Four Guardian Meditations (*Caturārakkhā*) are a traditional group of four meditation subjects named together in the later manual *Khuddasikkhā* ("49. Caturārakkhaniddeso", Khuddas 1563:1). The term is not found in the Mūla (0 sentences); it occurs in the Aṭṭhakathā only in Sp-iv 345:2 (Samantapāsādikā), where monks are told to dwell "without neglecting the four-fold protection" (*caturārakkhaṃ ahāpentā*).

### The List
1. **[Buddhānussati](/glossary/buddhanussati/)** - Recollection of the Buddha.
2. **[Mettā](/glossary/metta/)** - Loving-kindness.
3. **Asubha** - Contemplation of foulness (the Khuddasikkhā's TPR translation: "ugliness").
4. **Maraṇassati** - Mindfulness of death (the Khuddasikkhā spells it *maraṇassati*).

### Quote
> **Buddhānussati mettā ca, asubhaṃ maraṇassati;**  
> 461. Recollection of the Buddha, loving-kindness, ugliness, and mindfulness of death;  
>

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *Samantapāsādikā* (Sp-iv 345:2) – the only Aṭṭhakathā use of the word *caturārakkha* (also VinSaṅg-a 683:1); it does not spell out the four.
* **Later manual**: *Khuddasikkhā* (Khuddas 1566:1, verse 461) – names the four; its ṭīkā (Khuddas-nt 3030:1) says "these are called the four protections".
