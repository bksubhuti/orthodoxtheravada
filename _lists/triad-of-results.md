### Overview
The third triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā categorizes all phenomena strictly by their relationship to the law of [kamma](/glossary/kamma/)—specifically, whether they are the result of past actions, able to produce results, or entirely outside the system of kammic causality.

### The List
1. **Vipākā dhammā** - Resultant states: These are the states of consciousness and mental factors that are the direct ripening (*vipāka*) of past wholesome or unwholesome kamma. They are the *effects* (e.g., eye-consciousness seeing a beautiful or ugly object).
2. **Vipākadhammadhammā** - States possessing the property of result: These are the wholesome and unwholesome states (of the sensuous, form and formless planes and the unincluded) that are able to produce results (Dhs 992). They are the *causes* of resultant states.
3. **Nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammā** - States neither resultant nor possessing the property of result: These states are neither causes nor effects of kamma. This includes functional consciousness (*kiriya*), physical matter (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*), and [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).

### Quote
> **(Ka) vipākā dhammā. (Kha) vipākadhammadhammā. (Ga) nevavipākanavipākadhammadhammā.**  
> 3.(Ka) Resultant mental phenomena. (Kha) Mental phenomena producing resultant states. (Ga) Mental phenomena which are neither resultant nor producing resultant states.  
>
