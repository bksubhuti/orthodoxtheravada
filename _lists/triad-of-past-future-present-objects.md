### Overview
The nineteenth triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā classifies states by the time of the object they take: a past, a future or a present object (Dhs 60:1-62:1). The Aṭṭhakathā: those for which the past is the object are *atītārammaṇā*, and so on (As 869:1-2).

### The List
1. **Atītārammaṇā dhammā** - States with past objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking past states as object (Dhs 1815:2). In the later exposition, the base of infinite consciousness and the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception are given as *atītārammaṇā* (Dhs 2232:2).
2. **Anāgatārammaṇā dhammā** - States with future objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking future states as object (Dhs 1816:2). The later exposition says that by rule there is no state with an exclusively future object ("Niyogā anāgatārammaṇā natthi", Dhs 2233:1).
3. **Paccuppannārammaṇā dhammā** - States with present objects: the mind and mental factors that arise taking present states as object (Dhs 1817:2). In the later exposition the answer is the five pairs of sense-consciousness (*dvepañcaviññāṇāni*) and the three mind-elements (Dhs 2234:2).

### Quote
> **(Ka) atītārammaṇā dhammā. (Kha) anāgatārammaṇā dhammā. (Ga) paccuppannārammaṇā dhammā.**  
> 19.(Ka) Mental phenomena having a past object. (Kha) Mental phenomena having a future object. (Ga) Mental phenomena having a present object.  
>
