### Overview
Energy (*[viriya](/glossary/viriya/)*) is essential for traversing the Buddhist path. In the Padhāna Sutta (AN 2.2) the Buddha speaks of two kinds of striving (*padhāna*) that are difficult to endure in the world: that of householders and that of those who have gone forth. He then says the foremost of the two is the striving for the relinquishment of all attachments.

### The List
1. **Gihīnaṃ padhānaṃ** - The householder's striving: the striving of those dwelling in a home, for the sake of providing robes, almsfood, lodgings and medicinal requisites for the sick (*cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānapaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānuppadānatthaṃ*).
2. **Pabbajitānaṃ padhānaṃ** - The striving of those gone forth: the striving of those who have gone forth from the home life into homelessness, for the sake of renouncing all attachments (*sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggatthaṃ*). The Aṭṭhakathā explains this as the energy arisen together with insight and the path, for the sake of Nibbāna (Mp-ii 19:2).

### Quote
> **“Dvemāni, bhikkhave, padhānāni durabhisambhavāni lokasmiṃ. Katamāni dve? Yañca gihīnaṃ agāraṃ ajjhāvasataṃ cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānapaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānuppadānatthaṃ padhānaṃ, yañca agārasmā anagāriyaṃ pabbajitānaṃ sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggatthaṃ padhānaṃ. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, dve padhānāni durabhisambhavāni lokasmiṃ.**  
> “Monks, there are these two kinds of striving that are difficult to endure in the world. What are the two? The striving for householders dwelling in a home to provide robes, almsfood, lodgings, and medicinal requisites for the sick; and the striving for those gone forth from the home life into homelessness for the sake of renouncing all attachments. These, monks, are the two kinds of striving that are difficult to endure in the world.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Padhāna [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 2.2, A-ii 11-13) – The two kinds of striving; the Buddha calls the striving for the relinquishment of all attachments the foremost (A-ii 13:1).
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-ii 19) – Explains the two strivings.
* **Related**: *Āhāra Sutta* (SN 46.51, S-v 632) – a different teaching: the nutriment for the energy awakening factor is the three elements, *ārambhadhātu*, *nikkamadhātu* and *parakkamadhātu*, given careful attention.
