### Overview
Prior to his renunciation, Prince Siddhattha lived a life of ultimate sensory indulgence. King Suddhodana, when the Bodhisatta was sixteen, had three palaces built for his son, one each for the winter, the summer and the rainy season. 

### The List
1. **Ramma:** The first of the three palaces (nine stories, according to the Buddhavaṃsa commentary).
2. **Suramma:** The second palace (seven stories, according to the same commentary).
3. **Subha (Subhaka in the verse):** The third palace (five stories, according to the same commentary).

The Buddha says (AN 3.39) that he had three palaces, "one for the winter, one for the summer, one for the rainy season", and that in the rains palace he stayed four months surrounded by female musicians. The texts read do not say which of the three names belongs to which season.

### Quote
> **Ekūnatiṃsavassāni, agāraṃ ajjhahaṃ vasiṃ; Rammo surammo subhako, tayo pāsādamuttamā.**  
> 14. For twenty-nine years, I dwelled at home (in the palace); Ramma, Suramma, and Subhaka were the three supreme palaces.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sukhumāla [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 3.39) – The Buddha describes his extreme luxury as a young prince inside these three palaces, explaining how reflection on aging, sickness, and death caused his intoxication with life to vanish. It does not give the palaces' names.
* **Commentary**: *Buddhavaṃsa-aṭṭhakathā* (Bv-a 4077:1) – "rammasurammasubhanāmakā tayo pāsādā navabhūmikasattabhūmikapañcabhūmikā" (three palaces named Ramma, Suramma and Subha, of nine, seven and five stories); *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-iii 537:1) – King Suddhodana had the three palaces built when the Bodhisatta was sixteen.
