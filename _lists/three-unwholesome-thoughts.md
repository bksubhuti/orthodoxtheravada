### Overview
Before he attained enlightenment, the [Bodhisatta](/glossary/bodhisatta/) realized that the mind is shaped by what it frequently ponders. He divided all thoughts into two categories. These three unwholesome thoughts (*[vitakka](/glossary/vitakka/)*) lead to one's own affliction, another's affliction and the affliction of both, obstruct wisdom and lead away from Nibbāna (M-i 691:4). In the Mūla the same three, as *saṅkappa*, are called wrong intention (M-iii 427:2).

### The List
1. **Kāmavitakka** - Thoughts of sensuality: Thought associated with sensuality (It-a 1298:1); it arises regarding dear and agreeable beings or formations (It-a 1298:6).
2. **Byāpādavitakka** - Thoughts of ill-will: Thought associated with ill will (It-a 1298:3); it arises from the moment of looking with anger upon disagreeable beings or formations (It-a 1298:6).
3. **Vihiṃsāvitakka** - Thoughts of harming: Thought associated with harming (It-a 1298:4); it does not arise regarding formations but only regarding beings, as in thinking "may these beings be beaten, bound, cut off" (It-a 1298:6).

### Quote
> **“Pubbeva me, bhikkhave, sambodhā anabhisambuddhassa bodhisattasseva sato etadahosi – ‘ yaṃnūnāhaṃ dvidhā katvā dvidhā katvā vitakke vihareyyan ’ ti. So kho ahaṃ, bhikkhave, yo cāyaṃ kāmavitakko yo ca byāpādavitakko yo ca vihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ ekaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ; yo cāyaṃ nekkhammavitakko yo ca abyāpādavitakko yo ca avihiṃsāvitakko – imaṃ dutiyaṃ bhāgamakāsiṃ.**  
> “Monks, before my enlightenment, while I was still only an unenlightened Bodhisatta, this thought occurred to me: ‘What if I were to divide my thoughts into two classes?’ So, monks, I made these—sensual thought, ill-will thought, and harmful thought—one division. And I made these—renunciation thought, non-ill-will thought, and non-harmful thought—the second division.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Dvedhāvitakka [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 19) – The Buddha recounts that, before his awakening, he divided thoughts into two classes (M-i 690:3-4), and that whenever sensual or harming thoughts arose he abandoned them (M-i 691:9, 692:9).
* **Canonical**: *Andhakaraṇa Sutta* (Iti 87; It 840:1 "8. Andhakaraṇasuttaṃ", It 842:1-6) – the three unwholesome thoughts, each making one blind, ignorant and leading away from Nibbāna. Also S-iii 476:1 and Vibh 2704:1.
* **Commentary**: Papañcasūdanī on MN 19 (Ps-i 1786:1 "9. Dvedhāvitakkasuttavaṇṇanā", Ps-i 1787:5); Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā (It-a 1298:1-6).
