### Overview
In contrast to the fool, the wise person (*paṇḍita*) is defined by their moral clarity and their interpersonal grace. Wisdom in Theravāda is highly practical; it involves recognizing one's own flaws and fostering harmony within the community.

### The List
1. **One who sees their transgression as a transgression:** Having offended, he sees his own offense and asks for forgiveness by making amends (Mp-ii 122:1-2).
2. **One who rightfully accepts the confession of another:** When another says “I will not do this again, please forgive me,” he accepts the transgression according to the Dhamma and says “I forgive you” (compare Mp-ii 122:3-4).

### Quote
> **‘ Dveme, bhikkhave, paṇḍitā. Katame dve? Yo ca accayaṃ accayato passati, yo ca accayaṃ desentassa yathādhammaṃ paṭiggaṇhāti. Ime kho, bhikkhave, dve paṇḍitā”’ ti.**  
> “Monks, these two are wise people. Which two? The one who sees a transgression as a transgression, and the one who rightfully pardons another who has confessed a transgression. These two are wise people.”  
>
