### Overview
While the 31 planes of existence detail the specific locations in [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/), the Nine Abodes of Beings (*Sattāvāsa*) categorize living beings based on their cognitive and physical structures—specifically, how their physical bodies (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*) and their mental perceptions (*[saññā](/glossary/sanna/)*) differ or align. 

### The List
1. **Nānattakāyā nānattasaññino** - Diverse in body and diverse in perception, such as humans, some devas and some beings in the lower realms (*vinipātikā*) (A-ix 196).
2. **Nānattakāyā ekattasaññino** - Diverse in body and unified in perception, such as the gods of the Brahma-order who are first reborn (*devā brahmakāyikā paṭhamābhinibbattā*) (A-ix 197).
3. **Ekattakāyā nānattasaññino** - Unified in body and diverse in perception, such as the Ābhassara devas (A-ix 198).
4. **Ekattakāyā ekattasaññino** - Unified in body and unified in perception, such as the Subhakiṇha devas (A-ix 199).
5. **Asaññino appaṭisaṃvedino** - Without perception and not experiencing, such as the Asaññasatta devas (A-ix 200).
6. **Ākāsānañcāyatanūpagā** - Those who have gone to the base of infinite space (A-ix 201).
7. **Viññāṇañcāyatanūpagā** - Those who have gone to the base of infinite consciousness (A-ix 202).
8. **Ākiñcaññāyatanūpagā** - Those who have gone to the base of nothingness (A-ix 203).
9. **Nevasaññānāsaññāyatanūpagā** - Those who have gone to the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception (A-ix 204).

### Quote
> **“Navayime, bhikkhave, sattāvāsā. Katame nava? Santi, bhikkhave, sattā nānattakāyā nānattasaññino, seyyathāpi manussā, ekacce ca devā, ekacce ca vinipātikā. Ayaṃ paṭhamo sattāvāso. “Santi, bhikkhave, sattā nānattakāyā ekattasaññino, seyyathāpi devā brahmakāyikā paṭhamābhinibbattā. Ayaṃ dutiyo sattāvāso.**  
> “Monks, there are nine stations of beings. What nine? There are, monks, beings diverse in body and diverse in perception, such as humans, some devas, and some beings in the lower realms. This is the first station of beings. There are, monks, beings diverse in body and unified in perception, such as the gods of the Brahma-order who are first reborn. This is the second station of beings.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sattāvāsa [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 9.24, A-ix 195–204) – The nine abodes of beings. The same set is in the Saṅgīti Sutta (DN 33, D-iii 1611, "Nava sattāvāsā"). The Aṭṭhakathā (Mp-ix 349) says the Pure Abodes are also abodes of beings but are not included because they are not present at all times (asabbakālikattā).
