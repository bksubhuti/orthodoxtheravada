### Overview
The Samantapāsādikā, describing the First Council, says that the elder Mahākassapa questioned and the elder Upāli answered regarding the entire Vinaya Piṭaka, "with both Vibhaṅgas, the Khandhakas and the Parivāra" (*saubhatovibhaṅgakhandhakaparivāraṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ*, Sp-i 111:9). These are the three divisions: the Vibhaṅga (in two parts), the Khandhaka and the Parivāra. The same commentary and the Aṭṭhasālinī give the numbers: "both Pātimokkhas, two Vibhaṅgas, twenty-two Khandhakas, sixteen Parivāras: this is called the Vinaya Piṭaka" (*ubhayāni pātimokkhāni, dve vibhaṅgāni, dvāvīsati khandhakāni, soḷasa parivārā*, Sp-i 131:3; As 151:4).

### The List
1. **[Suttavibhaṅga](/glossary/suttavibhanga/)** (*ubhatovibhaṅga*) - The two Vibhaṅgas, the Bhikkhu-vibhaṅga and the Bhikkhunī-vibhaṅga, built on the two Pātimokkhas, the rule-codes of monks and nuns (Sv-nt-i 297:1-2). The Aṭṭhakathā describes how the rules of the Bhikkhunī-vibhaṅga were fixed at the Council in groups: eight in the Pārājika section, seventeen, thirty, one hundred and sixty-six, eight, seventy-five, and seven adjudication-settlements (Sp-i 111:1-7).
2. **[Khandhaka](/glossary/khandhaka/)** - The twenty-two Khandhakas found in the *[Mahāvagga](/glossary/mahavagga/)* and the *[Cūḷavagga](/glossary/culavagga/)* (Sv-nt-i 297:3).
3. **[Parivāra](/glossary/parivara/)** - The sixteen Parivāras: the Ṭīkā explains that they are called sixteen because each is marked by sixteen sections (*vāra*), sixteen in the Mahāvibhaṅga and sixteen in the Bhikkhunī-vibhaṅga, thirty-two in all (Sv-nt-i 297:4-5). The Ṭīkā adds that the Parivāra was drawn out from the two Vibhaṅgas and Khandhakas and made into a separate text (Kkh-pt 42:11).


### Quote
> **Evaṃ bhikkhunīvibhaṅgaṃ saṅgahaṃ āropetvā eteneva upāyena khandhakaparivārepi āropesuṃ. Evametaṃ saubhatovibhaṅgakhandhakaparivāraṃ vinayapiṭakaṃ saṅgahamārūḷhaṃ sabbaṃ mahākassapatthero pucchi, upālitthero vissajjesi.**  
> Having thus redacted the Bhikkhunīvibhaṅga, they also redacted the Khandhakas and the Parivāra in this same manner. In this way, the Elder Mahākassapa questioned and the Elder Upāli answered regarding the entire Vinaya Pitaka, which includes both Vibhaṅgas, the Khandhakas, and the Parivāra (saubhatovibhaṅgakhandhakaparivāraṃ), all of which was included in the collection.  
>
