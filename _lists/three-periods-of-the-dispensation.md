### Overview
Directly connected to the mechanics of decline, the [Mahāvihāra](/glossary/maha-vihara/) commentarial system tracks the lifespan of the current Sasana by dividing its historical trajectory into three broad epochs (*yuga*). This framework guides practitioners to realize which spiritual attainments are contextually accessible to them based on the current age of the world.

### The List
1. **Vimutti-yuga** - The Age of Liberation: The initial pristine epoch (historically spanning the first millennium after [Parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/)) characterized by an abundance of holy disciples who achieve full Arahatship and the analytical knowledges through direct insight.
2. **Samādhi-yuga** - The Age of Concentration: Also called *Paṭipatti-yuga*. The intermediate era where practitioners, though still highly disciplined and capable of attaining deep meditative absorptions (*jhānas*), find the flash of supramundane liberation exceptionally rare and difficult to trigger.
3. **Sīla-yuga** - The Age of Outer Virtue: Also called *Pariyatti-yuga*. The final broad era (the current epoch) where the deep meditative absorptions have largely decayed. The collective effort of the community shifts to preserving the text of the [Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/) intellectually and maintaining basic moral discipline (*[sīla](/glossary/sila/)*) before total disappearance.

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (Introduction to the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/)) – Buddhaghosa explains that despite the macro-decline, as long as the legal procedures of the Vinaya are kept pure, the outer shell of the Sasana remains intact.
