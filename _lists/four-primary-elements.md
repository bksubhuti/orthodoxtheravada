### Overview
The material world (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*) is analysed in the suttas as the four great elements (*cattāri mahābhūtāni*) and the form derived from them (*upādāya rūpaṃ*); this is what the "form constituent that is fuel for attachment" is (M-i 1035:2). In the Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta Venerable Sāriputta explains each element as internal or external, and teaches that both are simply that element, to be seen with right wisdom as "not mine, I am not this, this is not my self" (M-i 1037:6-7). The Aṭṭhakathā adds that the inanimate nature of the external element is obvious while that of the internal one is not, so the meditator who sees the two as the same finds the internal one easy to grasp (Ps-i 2250:2). The practice of reviewing the body by way of its elements (*dhātuso paccavekkhati*) is taught in the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (D-ii 1363:1).

### The List
1. **Pathavīdhātu** - The Earth Element. Internally: hairs of the head and body, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones, bone-marrow, kidneys, heart, liver, pleura, spleen, lungs, intestines, mesentery, undigested food, excrement (M-i 1037:4). The Abhidhamma commentary gives its characteristic as hardness (*kakkhaḷattalakkhaṇā*), its function as acting as a foundation (*patiṭṭhānarasā*) (As 2135:7).
2. **Āpodhātu** - The Water Element. Internally: bile, phlegm, pus, blood, sweat, fat, tears, grease, spittle, snot, oil of the joints, urine (M-i 1042:4). Its characteristic is trickling or flowing (*paggharaṇalakkhaṇā*, Ud-a 1499:4).
3. **Tejodhātu** - The Fire Element. Internally: that by which one is warmed, ages and is consumed, and by which what is eaten, drunk and tasted is properly digested (M-i 1044:4). Its characteristic is heat (*uṇhattalakkhaṇā*), its function is maturing (*paripācanarasā*) (As 2135:8).
4. **Vāyodhātu** - The Air Element. Internally: up-going winds, down-going winds, winds in the belly, winds in the bowels, winds coursing through the limbs, in-breath and out-breath (M-i 1046:4). Its characteristic is distension or support (*vitthambhanalakkhaṇā*), its function is moving (*samudīraṇarasā*) (As 2135:9).


### Quote
> **“Katamo cāvuso, rūpupādānakkhandho? Cattāri ca mahābhūtāni, catunnañca mahābhūtānaṃ upādāya rūpaṃ. “Katamā cāvuso, cattāro mahābhūtā? Pathavīdhātu, āpodhātu, tejodhātu, vāyodhātu.**  
> “And what, friends, is the form constituent that is fuel for attachment? It is the four great elements and the material form derived from the four great elements. “And what, friends, are the four great elements? The earth element, the water element, the fire element, and the wind element.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Mahāhatthipadopama [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 28, M-i 1032:1 "8. Mahāhatthipadopamasuttaṃ", paras 300-302 ff.): the four great elements (M-i 1036:2) and the internal and external earth, water, fire and air elements (M-i 1037:1-1047). The elements also appear as a body-contemplation in the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (DN 22, D-ii 1363:1).
* **Commentary**: *Majjhima-aṭṭhakathā* (Ps-i 2250:1-2, 2251:1-3) on MN 28; *Aṭṭhasālinī* (As 2135:7-9) for the characteristics of earth, fire and air.
