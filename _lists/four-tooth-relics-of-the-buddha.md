### Overview
After the Buddha's [Parinibbāna](/glossary/parinibbana/) and cremation, his bodily relics (*[dhātu](/glossary/dhatu/)*) were divided. The verses at the end of the [Mahāparinibbāna](/glossary/mahaparinibbana/) [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/) (DN 16) say that there were eight measures (*doṇa*) of relics, and that four tooth relics (*dāṭhā*) were honoured in four places. The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī says that these closing verses were spoken by the elders in the island of Tambapaṇṇi (*tambapaṇṇidīpe therehi vuttā*).

The commentary adds that seven relics were not broken up: the four canine teeth, the two collarbones and the *uṇhīsa*; the rest were scattered (*catasso dāṭhā, dve akkhakā, uṇhīsanti imā satta dhātuyo na vippakiriṃsu*). It also tells how the brahmin Doṇa hid the right canine tooth in his turban while dividing the relics, and how Sakka took it to the world of the [devas](/glossary/deva/) and enshrined it in the Cūḷāmaṇi [Cetiya](/glossary/cetiya/).

The forty teeth (*cattālīsadanto*) are not relics. Having forty teeth is the twenty-third of the thirty-two marks of a great man in the Lakkhaṇa Sutta (DN 30), where it is the result of having abandoned divisive speech in former lives.

### The List
1. **Tidivehi pūjitā** - One tooth is worshipped in the heavens (*tidiva*). The Sumaṅgalavilāsinī tells that Sakka took the right canine tooth to the world of the devas.
2. **Gandhārapure mahīyati** - One is honoured in the city of Gandhāra.
3. **Kāliṅgarañño vijite** - One is in the realm of the king of Kaliṅga.
4. **Nāgarājā maheti** - One is honoured by the king of the nāgas.

### Quote
> **Ekāhi dāṭhā tidivehi pūjitā, ekā pana gandhārapure mahīyati; Kāliṅgarañño vijite punekaṃ, ekaṃ pana nāgarājā maheti.**  
> One tooth (dāṭhā) is worshipped in the Heavens, and one is honoured in the city of Gandhāra; A further one in the realm of the Kaliṅga King, and the King of the Dragons honours one more.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* (DN 16, D-ii 648–651) – The closing verses on the eight measures of relics and the four tooth relics.
* **Canonical**: *Lakkhaṇa Sutta* (DN 30, D-iii 476, 807) – Forty teeth (*cattālīsadanto*) as a mark of a great man, not a relic.
* **Commentary**: *[Sumaṅgalavilāsinī](/glossary/sumangalavilasini/)* (Sv-ii 780, 795, 814) – The seven relics that were not scattered, the story of the right canine tooth, and the note that the closing verses were spoken by the elders of Tambapaṇṇi.
* **Later chronicle**: *Dāṭhāvaṃsa* – A later Pāḷi chronicle of the tooth relic (not in the Tipiṭaka or its Aṭṭhakathā).
