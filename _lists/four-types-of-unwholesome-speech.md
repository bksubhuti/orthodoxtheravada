### Overview
Verbal action carries immense kammic weight in Theravāda. The Buddha lists four kinds of verbal misconduct (*vacīduccarita*) in AN 4.148. The Sāleyyaka Sutta (MN 41) describes the four kinds of verbal righteous conduct, which is abstaining from each of them.

### The List
1. **Musāvāda** - False speech: its opposite, abstaining from false speech, is described at M-i 1446: when questioned as a witness he does not, knowing or not knowing, seeing or not seeing, speak falsehood in full awareness for his own ends, another's ends or some trifling worldly end.
2. **Pisuṇā vācā** - Malicious (divisive) speech: repeating elsewhere what was heard here to divide those people from these, or repeating to these what was heard elsewhere to divide these from those (M-i 1447).
3. **Pharusā vācā** - Harsh speech: the opposite of words that are gentle, pleasing to the ear, loveable, going to the heart, courteous, and agreeable to many (M-i 1448).
4. **Samphappalāpa** - Gossip (frivolous talk): the opposite of speaking at the right time, speaking what is fact, what is good, the Dhamma and the Discipline, words worth recording, reasonable, moderate and beneficial (M-i 1449).

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, vacīduccaritāni. Katamāni cattāri? Musāvādo, pisuṇā vācā, pharusā vācā, samphappalāpo – imāni kho, bhikkhave, cattāri vacīduccaritānī” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these four kinds of verbal misconduct. What are the four? Telling lies, evil speaking, harsh speech, and frivolous talk—these indeed, monks, are the four kinds of verbal misconduct.”  
>
