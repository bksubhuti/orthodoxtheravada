### Overview
The Analytical Knowledges (*Paṭisambhidā*) are four kinds of knowledge that have reached specific distinctions (*pabhedagatāni cattāri ñāṇāni*) regarding meaning and the other three fields (Vism-ii 301:1). The Vibhaṅga defines them as knowledge of meaning, of Dhamma, of the expression of that Dhamma in language, and of the knowledges themselves (Vibh 718). In the Itivuttaka commentary, the Buddha attained these four knowledges by himself, and they are true and unfailing because they do not fail to correspond to their subject matter (It-a 616:3).

### The List
1. **Atthapaṭisambhidā** - Analytical Knowledge of Meaning (*atthe ñāṇaṃ*): knowledge of the result of a cause (*hetuphale ñāṇaṃ*, Vibh 720). The Aṭṭhakathā counts five things as "meaning": whatever has arisen from conditions, Nibbāna, what is spoken, result (*vipāka*) and functional states (*kiriyā*) (It-a 611:2).
2. **Dhammapaṭisambhidā** - Analytical Knowledge of Dhamma (*dhamme ñāṇaṃ*): knowledge of the cause (*hetumhi ñāṇaṃ*, Vibh 720). The Aṭṭhakathā counts five things as "dhamma": a cause that produces a result, the noble path, what is stated, the wholesome and the unwholesome (It-a 611:6).
3. **Niruttipaṭisambhidā** - Analytical Knowledge of Language (*tatra dhammaniruttābhilāpe ñāṇaṃ*): the knowledge of the divisions of the *sabhāvanirutti*, the unerring usage in the Māgadhī language, the root language of all beings: "this is the true expression, this is not" (It-a 616:1).
4. **Paṭibhānapaṭisambhidā** - Analytical Knowledge of Perspicuity (*ñāṇesu ñāṇaṃ*): knowledge of the knowledges; it takes as its object all the knowledge that has occurred in the other three and knows its divisions (It-a 616:2).


### Quote
> **Catasso paṭisambhidā – atthapaṭisambhidā, dhammapaṭisambhidā, niruttipaṭisambhidā, paṭibhānapaṭisambhidā. Atthe ñāṇaṃ atthapaṭisambhidā, dhamme ñāṇaṃ dhammapaṭisambhidā, tatra dhammaniruttābhilāpe ñāṇaṃ niruttipaṭisambhidā, ñāṇesu ñāṇaṃ paṭibhānapaṭisambhidā.**  
> 718. The four analytical modes are: discrimination of meaning, discrimination of mental phenomena, discrimination of language, and discrimination of perspicuity. Knowledge about a meaning is discrimination of meaning. Knowledge about an idea is discrimination of mental phenomena. Knowledge in the expression of ideas and language is discrimination of language. Knowledge of knowledges is discrimination of perspicuity.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Vibhaṅga](/glossary/vibhanga/)* (Vibh 718-720, Paṭisambhidāvibhaṅga), which defines the four in the sections on the Truths and on Causes. The *[Paṭisambhidāmagga](/glossary/patisambhidamagga/)* is a book of the Khuddaka Nikāya (Sutta Piṭaka), not of the Abhidhamma.
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Vism-ii 300:1-301:3, para 428): "In the second tetrad, the four analytical modes are four kinds of knowledge that have reached specific distinctions regarding meaning and so on" (*Dutiyacatukke catasso paṭisambhidā nāma atthādīsu pabhedagatāni cattāri ñāṇāni*). *Itivuttaka-aṭṭhakathā* (It-a 611:1-612:1, 616:1-3) and *Aṭṭhasālinī* (As 188:1-6).
