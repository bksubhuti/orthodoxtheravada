### Overview
The Noble Eightfold Path is the Fourth Noble Truth, the middle way awakened to by the Tathāgata, which makes the eye and makes knowledge, and leads to peace, direct knowledge, awakening and Nibbāna (S-v 2476:1-3). The Path is comprised in three aggregates: right speech, right action and right livelihood in the aggregate of virtue; right effort, right mindfulness and right concentration in the aggregate of concentration; right view and right intention in the aggregate of wisdom (M-i 1583:2-5). In the Aṭṭhasālinī, the Noble Path is eight-factored, but in mundane consciousness the three abstinences are not found at the same moment, so the mundane path is called five-factored; at the moment of the supramundane path the abstinence of right speech arises in a single consciousness (As 1352:3, 1661:7-10).

### The List
1. **Sammādiṭṭhi** - Right View: knowledge of suffering, of its origin, of its cessation, and of the way leading to its cessation (S-v 50:2). (Wisdom group, M-i 1583:5).
2. **Sammāsaṅkappa** - Right Intention: the intention of renunciation, of non-ill-will and of harmlessness (S-v 51:2). (Wisdom group).
3. **Sammāvācā** - Right Speech: abstaining from false speech, malicious speech, harsh speech and frivolous chatter (S-v 52:2). (Virtue group, M-i 1583:3).
4. **Sammākammanta** - Right Action: abstaining from killing, taking what is not given and unchastity (*abrahmacariyā*) (S-v 53:2). (Virtue group).
5. **Sammāājīva** - Right Livelihood: the noble disciple gives up wrong livelihood and keeps his life going by right livelihood (S-v 54:2). Among the wrong trades for a lay follower are trade in weapons, in living beings, in meat, in intoxicants and in poison (A-v 1194:3). (Virtue group).
6. **Sammāvāyāma** - Right Effort: the monk generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts his mind and strives to prevent unarisen evil states, to abandon arisen evil states, to arouse unarisen wholesome states, and to maintain, increase and perfect arisen wholesome states (S-v 55:2). (Concentration group, M-i 1583:4).
7. **Sammāsati** - Right Mindfulness: the monk abides contemplating the body in the body, feelings in feelings, mind in mind and phenomena in phenomena, ardent, clearly comprehending and mindful, having put away covetousness and grief for the world (S-v 56:2-5). (Concentration group).
8. **Sammāsamādhi** - Right Concentration: the four jhānas, from the first, "secluded from sensual pleasures, secluded from unwholesome states, with applied and sustained thought, with the rapture and pleasure born of seclusion", to the fourth, with purity of mindfulness due to equanimity (S-v 57:2-6). (Concentration group).


### Quote
> **“Katamā ca sā, bhikkhave, majjhimā paṭipadā tathāgatena abhisambuddhā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati? Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṃ – sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo sammāvācā sammākammanto sammāājīvo sammāvāyāmo sammāsati sammāsamādhi. Ayaṃ kho sā, bhikkhave, majjhimā paṭipadā tathāgatena abhisambuddhā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṃvattati.**  
> “And what, monks, is that middle way awakened to by the Tathagata, which creates an eye … which leads to Nibbāna? It is this Noble Eightfold Path; that is, right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration. This, monks, is that middle way awakened to by the Tathagata, which creates an eye, which creates knowledge, which leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to Nibbāna.  
>
