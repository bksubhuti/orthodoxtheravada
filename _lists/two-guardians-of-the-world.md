### Overview
In Theravāda psychology, these two wholesome mental factors (*cetasikas*) are considered the foundation of all civilized human behavior. Without them, the Buddha warned, human society would collapse into animalistic chaos, with no respect for family, morality, or social order. 

### The List
1. **[Hiri](/glossary/hiri/)** - Moral Shame: An internal sense of conscience and self-respect that causes one to recoil from doing evil. It is motivated by a sense of one's own dignity and principles.
2. **[Ottappa](/glossary/ottappa/)** - Moral Dread: An external fear of the consequences of wrongdoing, such as fear of blame, legal punishment, or rebirth in hell. It causes one to avoid evil out of respect for external forces.

### Quote
> **“Dveme, bhikkhave, dhammā sukkā lokaṃ pālenti. Katame dve? Hirī ca ottappañca.**  
> “Monks, these two bright qualities protect the world. What are the two? Shame and moral dread.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 2.9, Cariya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_02&page_number=53&name=dukanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=OS7igJjigJggRHZlbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgZGhhbW3EgSBzdWtrxIEgbG9rYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Cariya [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 2.9) – The Buddha declares these as the two bright qualities that protect the world; without them no one would recognize a mother, aunt or teacher's wife.
* **Canonical**: *Sukkadhamma Sutta* (Iti 42) – The same teaching in the Itivuttaka.
