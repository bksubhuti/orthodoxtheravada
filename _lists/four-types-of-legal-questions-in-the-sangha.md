### Overview
The [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/) functions under a rigorous constitutional framework. Every dispute, crime, or administrative issue that arises within a monastery must be processed formally as a legal case, known as an *Adhikaraṇa*. The [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) categorizes all possible community problems into four distinct types, each requiring specific legal remedies to resolve.

### The List
1. **Vivādādhikaraṇa** - Disputes over Doctrine: Monks dispute whether something is Dhamma or not Dhamma, Vinaya or not Vinaya, stated and uttered by the Tathāgata or not, practised by the Tathāgata or not, and so on (Vin-iv 1068).
2. **Anuvādādhikaraṇa** - Accusations of Misconduct: Monks criticize a bhikkhu on account of a falling away from virtue, from right habits, from right views or from right livelihood (Vin-iv 1068).
3. **Āpattādhikaraṇa** - Rule Violations: The five classes of offences and the seven classes of offences are the litigation arising from offences (Vin-iv 1068).
4. **Kiccādhikaraṇa** - Administrative Obligations: The Saṅgha's obligations and duties: the formal act by announcement (*apalokanakamma*), the formal act with a motion (*ñattikamma*), the formal act with a motion and one resolution, and the formal act with a motion and three resolutions (Vin-iv 1072).

### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, ānanda, adhikaraṇāni. Katamāni cattāri? Vivādādhikaraṇaṃ, anuvādādhikaraṇaṃ, āpattādhikaraṇaṃ, kiccādhikaraṇaṃ – imāni kho, ānanda, cattāri adhikaraṇāni.**  
> “Ānanda, there are these four kinds of litigation. What four? Litigation because of a dispute, litigation because of an accusation, litigation because of an offence, and litigation concerning proceedings—these are the four kinds of litigation, Ānanda.  
>  
> — [Majjhima Nikāya](/glossary/majjhima-nikaya/) (MN 104, Sāmagāma Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ma_03&page_number=35&name=uparipa%E1%B9%87%E1%B9%87%C4%81sap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=NDYu4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJpbcSBbmksIMSBbmFuZGEsIGFkaGlrYXJh4bmHxIFuaS4%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sāmagāma [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 104) – After the Jain leader Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta dies and his followers quarrel, the novice Cunda tells Ānanda, and Ānanda takes it to the Buddha at Sāmagāma (M-iii 83–86). The four adhikaraṇa are given at M-iii 90, followed by the seven *adhikaraṇasamatha*. The Vinaya definitions are in the Cūḷavagga, Samathakkhandhaka (Vin-iv 1068–1072).
