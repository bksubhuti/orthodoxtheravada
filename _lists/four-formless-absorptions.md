### Overview
Beyond the fine-material jhānas lie the four Formless Absorptions (*[Arūpajhāna](/glossary/arupajhana/)*). To enter these, a meditator must completely transcend all perceptions of physical form (*[rūpa](/glossary/rupa/)*). These are the most refined states of peace possible in [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/), though they still fall short of [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).

### The List
1. **[Ākāsānañcāyatana](/glossary/akasanancayatana/)** - The Base of Infinite Space: Attained by transcending the perception of form and focusing entirely on the concept of endless, boundless space.
2. **[Viññāṇañcāyatana](/glossary/vinnanancayatana/)** - The Base of Infinite Consciousness: Attained by letting go of infinite space and turning the mind's focus onto the boundless consciousness that was aware of that space.
3. **[Ākiñcaññāyatana](/glossary/akincannayatana/)** - The Base of Nothingness: Attained by letting go of infinite consciousness and focusing on the absolute absence or "nothingness" that remains.
4. **[Nevasaññānāsaññāyatana](/glossary/nevasannanasannayatana/)** - The Base of Neither-Perception-Nor-Non-Perception: The absolute limit of worldly concentration. Perception is so exquisitely subtle that it can neither be said to exist nor not exist.

### Quote
> **sabbaso rūpasaññānaṃ samatikkamā paṭighasaññānaṃ atthaṅgamā nānattasaññānaṃ amanasikārā “ananto ākāso” ti ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso ākāsānañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma “anantaṃ viññāṇan” ti viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso viññāṇañcāyatanaṃ samatikkamma “natthi kiñcī” ti ākiñcāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati; sabbaso ākiñcaññāyatanaṃ samatikkamma nevasaññānāsaññāyatanaṃ upasampajja viharati.**  
> with the complete surmounting of perceptions of form, with the disappearance of perceptions of sensory impact, by non-attention to perceptions of diversity, he enters and dwells in the dimension of infinite space, thinking, “space is infinite”; by completely surmounting the dimension of infinite space, he enters and dwells in the dimension of infinite consciousness, thinking, “consciousness is infinite”; by completely surmounting the dimension of infinite consciousness, he enters and dwells in the dimension of nothingness, thinking, “there is nothing”; by completely surmounting the dimension of nothingness, he enters and dwells in the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Anupada [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 111) – The Buddha details Ven. Sāriputta's progression through all material and formless attainments before realizing awakening.
* **Canonical**: *Saṅgīti Sutta* (DN 33) – Lists "Cattāro āruppā" (D-iii 1416:1).
* **Commentary**: *[Visuddhimagga](/glossary/visuddhimagga/)* (Chapter X) – Details the precise mental operations required to transition from one formless sphere to the next.
