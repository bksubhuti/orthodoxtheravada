### Overview
The twelfth triad of the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) Mātikā classifies states as limited, exalted or immeasurable (Dhs 39:1-41:1). The Aṭṭhakathā explains the three terms at As 862:1-7.

### The List
1. **Parittā dhammā** - Limited states: all the wholesome, unwholesome and indeterminate states of the sense sphere, and the aggregates of form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness (Dhs 1794:2-3). Aṭṭhakathā: a trifling amount, cut off all around, is called *paritta*; these are like small things for having little power; a designation for sense-sphere states (As 862:1-4).
2. **Mahaggatā dhammā** - Exalted states: the wholesome and indeterminate states of the fine-material ([rūpāvacara](/glossary/rupa/)) and immaterial (arūpāvacara) spheres, with the four mental aggregates (Dhs 1795:2-3). Aṭṭhakathā: they have attained greatness through the ability to suppress defilements, abundant fruit and long continuity, or are practised by great ones of exalted desire, effort, mind and wisdom (As 862:5).
3. **Appamāṇā dhammā** - Immeasurable states: the supramundane paths, their fruits, and the unconditioned element ([Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/)) (Dhs 1796:2). Aṭṭhakathā: they have no measure by way of object or association, and are opposed to the measure-makers such as lust (As 862:6-7).

### Quote
> **(Ka) parittā dhammā. (Kha) mahaggatā dhammā. (Ga) appamāṇā dhammā.**  
> 12.(Ka) Limited mental phenomena. (Kha) Exalted mental phenomena. (Ga) Immeasurable mental phenomena.  
>
