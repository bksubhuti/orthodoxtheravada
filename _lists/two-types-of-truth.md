### Overview
Orthodox Theravāda hermeneutics relies heavily on the distinction between two types of truth. This framework ensures that the Buddha's teachings are interpreted correctly without falling into contradictions. Without this distinction, one might confuse everyday conventional language with the ultimate reality of elements (dhammā).

### The List
1. **[Sammuti-sacca](/glossary/sammuti-sacca/)** - Conventional Truth: Truth that relies on conceptual designations (*paññatti*) such as "being," "person," "god" or "Brahmā" (Kv-a 703:2). It is completely valid in everyday discourse and used by the Buddha to explain morality and worldly functions.
2. **[Paramattha-sacca](/glossary/paramattha-sacca/)** - Ultimate Truth: Truth pertaining to the irreducible realities of existence (mentality and materiality) which exist independently of conceptual labels. At this level, there are no "beings" or "persons," only aggregates, elements and sense bases (Kv-a 703:3).

### Quote
> **Tattha sammutisaccaṃ paramatthasaccanti dve saccāni. Ye pana evaṃ vibhāgaṃ akatvā saccanti vacanasāmaññena sammutiñāṇampi “saccārammaṇamevā” ti vadanti, seyyathāpi andhakā;**  
> There, there are two truths: conventional truth and ultimate truth. But some, like the Andhakas, without making such a distinction, say, by the generality of the word 'truth', that even conventional knowledge “has only truth as its object.”  
>  
> — [Kathāvatthu-aṭṭhakathā](/glossary/pancappakaranatthakatha/) (Kv-a, Sammutiñāṇakathāvaṇṇanā, commentary on Kv 434-435) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_03&page_number=187&name=pa%C3%B1capakara%E1%B9%87aa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIHNhbW11dGlzYWNjYeG5gyBwYXJhbWF0dGhhc2FjY2FudGkgZHZlIHNhY2PEgW5pLg%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Neyyattha passage* (AN 2.25, A-ii 64:1-4, CST para 25) – Whoever declares a sutta of neyyattha meaning to be nītattha, or the reverse, misrepresents the Tathāgata. Mp-ii 124:3-4 gives “ekapuggalo, bhikkhave” as an example of neyyattha, since in the ultimate sense (*paramatthato*) there is no person.
* **Commentary**: *[Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/)* (Mp-i 369:3-4) – “Puggaloti sammutikathā, na paramatthakathā. Buddhassa hi bhagavato duvidhā desanā – sammutidesanā, paramatthadesanā cāti.” Kv-a 703:1-3 and Spk-ii 292:7-9 likewise give the Buddhas' two kinds of talk, conventional and ultimate.
