### Overview
The Hindrances (*[Nīvaraṇa](/glossary/nivarana/)*) are the primary obstacles to deep concentration and insight. This Dyad isolates the precise mental factors that act as these blinders, separating the corrupted elements from the rest of the mind.

### The List
1. **Nīvaraṇā dhammā** - States that are hindrances: The Suttas teach five hindrances; the Dhammasaṅgaṇī lists six (the five with ignorance), with sloth-torpor and restlessness-worry each counted as one. In terms of ultimate mental factors, the [Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/) identifies eight specific mental factors that create these hindrances: Greed (*[lobha](/glossary/lobha/)*), Hatred (*[dosa](/glossary/dosa/)*), Sloth (*thīna*), Torpor (*middha*), Restlessness (*[uddhacca](/glossary/uddhacca/)*), Worry (*[kukkucca](/glossary/kukkucca/)*), Doubt (*[vicikicchā](/glossary/vicikiccha/)*), and Ignorance (*[avijjā](/glossary/avijja/)*).
2. **No nīvaraṇā dhammā** - States that are not hindrances: All other mental factors, all consciousnesses, matter, and [Nibbāna](/glossary/nibbana/).

### Quote
> **Katame dhammā nīvaraṇā? Cha nīvaraṇā– kāmacchandanīvaraṇaṃ, byāpādanīvaraṇaṃ, thinamiddhanīvaraṇaṃ, uddhaccakukkuccanīvaraṇaṃ, vicikicchānīvaraṇaṃ, avijjānīvaraṇaṃ.**  
> 1158. What mental phenomena are hindrances? There are six hindrances: the hindrance of sensual desire, ill will as a hindrance, sloth and drowsiness as a hindrance, restlessness and anxiety as a hindrance, uncertainty as a hindrance, and the hindrance of ignorance.  
>
