### Overview
The *Ganthā* are four "bodily knots" (*kāyagantha*). The commentary explains that they tie the mental body (*nāmakāya*) and fasten a being into the round of rebirth by way of death and rebirth-linking (*cutipaṭisandhivasena vaṭṭasmiṃ ganthenti ghaṭentīti ganthā*, Nidd-a-i 875; also Paṭis-a 1463).

### The List
1. **Abhijjhā-kāyagantha** - The Bodily Tie of Covetousness: The knot of greed, desiring the property of others and pulling objects toward oneself.
2. **Byāpāda-kāyagantha** - The Bodily Tie of Ill-will: The knot of hatred and aversion, wishing harm upon others.
3. **Sīlabbataparāmāsa-kāyagantha** - The Bodily Tie of Clinging to Rules and Vows: The knot of wrongly believing that ritualistic behaviors and vows alone can purify the mind.
4. **Idaṃsaccābhinivesa-kāyagantha** - The Bodily Tie of Dogmatic Fanaticism: The knot of rigidly insisting "Only this is truth; the rest is worthless," the Dhammasaṅgaṇī (Dhs 1925) shows this as the claim "this alone is true, the rest is worthless" (*idameva saccaṃ moghamaññanti*) about such views as whether the world is eternal or finite.

### Quote
> **“Cattārome, bhikkhave, ganthā. Katame cattāro? Abhijjhā kāyagantho, byāpādo kāyagantho, sīlabbataparāmāso kāyagantho, idaṃsaccābhiniveso kāyagantho – ime kho, bhikkhave, cattāro ganthā.**  
> “Bhikkhus, there are these four knots. What four? The bodily knot of covetousness, the bodily knot of ill will, the bodily knot of wrong grasp of behavior and observances, and the bodily knot of adherence to dogmatic assertion of truth—these, bhikkhus, are the four knots.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Gantha [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (Saṃyutta Nikāya, Mahāvagga, S-v 387–388) – The four bodily knots, with the Noble Eightfold Path to be developed for their abandonment. The same four are also in the Saṅgīti Sutta (DN 33, D-iii 1449) and the Niddesa.
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* (Dhs 1921, 1923–1925) – Lists the four *kāyagantha* and defines each, for example the bodily knot of dogmatic assertion of truth (Dhs 1925).
