### Overview
While the suttas primarily highlight the three core bases of merit (Giving, Virtue, Meditation), the later tradition (for example the *Abhidhammatthasaṅgaha*, §65) sets out a list of ten. This framework covers all possible avenues through which a layperson or monastic can generate wholesome [kamma](/glossary/kamma/) (*puñña*) in daily life.

### The List
1. **[Dāna](/glossary/dana/)** - Giving: Offering material wealth, food, or resources.
2. **[Sīla](/glossary/sila/)** - Virtue: Observing the moral precepts.
3. **[Bhāvanā](/glossary/bhavana/)** - Mental Development: Practicing tranquility and insight meditation.
4. **Apacāyana** - Reverence: Showing proper respect to elders, monastics, and the wise.
5. **Veyyāvacca** - Service: Volunteering to help others in wholesome tasks, serving the [Saṅgha](/glossary/sangha/) or the sick.
6. **Pattidāna** - Sharing of Merit: Dedicating the good kamma one has acquired to departed relatives or all beings.
7. **Pattānumodanā** - Rejoicing in Merit: Feeling genuine joy (sympathetic joy) when someone else performs a good deed.
8. **Dhammassavana** - Hearing the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/): Listening to the Buddha's teachings with an attentive, faithful mind.
9. **Dhammadesanā** - Teaching the Dhamma: Sharing the teachings with others out of compassion, without expecting a reward.
10. **Diṭṭhijukamma** - Straightening one's views: Establishing Right View, clarifying one's understanding of kamma and the Four Noble Truths.

### Quote
> **Dānasīlabhāvanāpacāyanaveyyāvaccapattidānapattānumodanadhammassavanadhammadesanā diṭṭhijukammavasena dasavidhaṃ hoti.**  
> 65. It is ten-fold (dasavidhaṃ) by way of: giving (dāna), virtue (sīla), meditation (bhāvanā), reverence (apacāyana), service (veyyāvacca), transference of merit (pattidāna), rejoicing in (others’) merit (pattānumodana), hearing the Dhamma (dhammassavana), teaching the Dhamma (dhammadesanā), and straightening out one’s views (diṭṭhijukammavasena).
