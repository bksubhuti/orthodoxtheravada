### Overview
*[Mada](/glossary/mada/)* (pride, intoxication, or infatuation) is a dangerous mental state where a person becomes blindly obsessed with their current temporary advantages. In the Sukhumāla Sutta the Buddha, recalling his own delicate upbringing in his father's house, says that by reflecting on aging, sickness and death his youth-intoxication, health-intoxication and life-intoxication were each completely abandoned (A-iii 312:4, 313:3, 314:3).

### The List
1. **Yobbanamada** - Intoxication with youth: The intoxication with youth: one intoxicated by it misconducts in body, speech and mind (A-iii 315:4), and a monk intoxicated by it gives up the training and returns to the lower life (A-iii 316:1). The Buddha overcame it by reflecting that he too is subject to aging (A-iii 312:2-4).
2. **Ārogyamada** - Intoxication with health: The intoxication with health: it leads in the same way to misconduct (A-iii 315:6) and to giving up the training. Overcome by reflecting that he too is subject to sickness (A-iii 313:1-3).
3. **Jīvitamada** - Intoxication with life: The intoxication with life: it leads in the same way to misconduct (A-iii 315:6) and to giving up the training. Overcome by reflecting that he too is subject to death (A-iii 314:1-3).

### Quote
> **“Tayome, bhikkhave, madā. Katame tayo? Yobbanamado, ārogyamado, jīvitamado.**  
> “Monks, there are these three kinds of intoxication. What three? Intoxication with youth, intoxication with health, and intoxication with life.  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 3.39, Sukhumāla Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_03&page_number=145&name=tikanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=4oCY4oCYIFRheW9tZSwgYmhpa2toYXZlLCBtYWTEgS4%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sukhumāla [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 3.39) – The Buddha describes his delicate life in his father's house (A-iii 310-311) and says that, reflecting on aging, sickness and death, each of the three intoxications was abandoned (A-iii 312-314); then he names the three and their results (A-iii 315-316). The triad is also in the Saṅgīti Sutta (D-iii 1397:1, "Tayo madā").
