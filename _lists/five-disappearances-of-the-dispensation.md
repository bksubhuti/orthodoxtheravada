### Overview
Orthodox Theravāda maintains that the Buddha's Dispensation (*Sāsana*) is temporary and will eventually decay and vanish entirely from the earth due to human heedlessness. The commentarial tradition details a precise, tragic five-stage sequence of disappearance (*antaradhāna*), mapping the gradual spiritual blindness that will envelop humankind before the next Buddha arises.

### The List
1. **Adhigamaantaradhāna** - Disappearance of Attainment: The four paths, four fruits, four analytical knowledges, three knowledges and six direct knowledges. They decline step by step, beginning with the analytical knowledges (Mp-i 345:2-4).
2. **Paṭipattiantaradhāna** - Disappearance of Practice: Unable to produce jhāna, insight, path and fruit, monks keep only the fourfold purity of virtue, then fall into offences until only the pārājikas are kept. The practice has vanished when the last monk breaks his virtue or dies (Mp-i 346:1-6).
3. **Pariyattiantaradhāna** - Disappearance of Learning: The Tipiṭaka word of the Buddha with its commentary. It declines from the top: first the Abhidhamma (the Paṭṭhāna first), then the Suttanta (Aṅguttara first, then Saṃyutta, Majjhima, Dīgha), then the Jātaka, then the Vinaya (Parivāra first). It has vanished when not even a four-line verse of the Buddha is current among people (Mp-i 347:1-349:6).
4. **Liṅgaantaradhāna** - Disappearance of the Monastic Sign: Dress and deportment decline until monks tie scraps of ochre cloth on the hand, neck or hair, and finally cast even these away (Mp-i 350:1-14).
5. **Dhātuantaradhāna** - Disappearance of the Relics: When the dispensation declines the relics gather at the Mahācetiya, then the Rājāyatana shrine in Nāgadīpa, then the Bodhi-pallaṅka. There they show the form of a Buddha, a flame rises up to the Brahma world and the relics are consumed (Mp-i 351:1-18).

### Quote
> **Yaṃ panetaṃ sabbasuttānaṃ pariyosāne tecimaṃ saddhammaṃ antaradhāpentīti vuttaṃ, tattha pañca antaradhānāni nāma adhigamaantaradhānaṃ, paṭipattiantaradhānaṃ, pariyattiantaradhānaṃ, liṅgaantaradhānaṃ, dhātuantaradhānanti.**  
> Regarding what was stated at the end of all the suttas, “they cause this true Dhamma to disappear,” there are five disappearances: the disappearance of attainment, the disappearance of practice, the disappearance of learning, the disappearance of the sign, and the disappearance of the relics.  
>  
> — [Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/) (Aṅguttara Aṭṭhakathā, Mp-i para 345; not a Mūla passage) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_an_01&page_number=67&name=ekakanip%C4%81taa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=WWHhuYMgcGFuZXRh4bmDIHNhYmJhc3V0dMSBbmHhuYMgcGFyaXlvc8SBbmUgdGVjaW1h4bmDIHNhZGRoYW1tYeG5gw%3D%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: The Mūla Ekakanipāta says that monks who proclaim non-Dhamma as Dhamma "cause this true Dhamma to disappear" (*te cimaṃ saddhammaṃ antaradhāpenti*, A-i 136:1-2, AN 1.130). The Manorathapūraṇī comments on this at Mp-i 345.
* **Commentary**: *[Manorathapūraṇī](/glossary/manorathapurani/)* (Mp-i 345-352) – Names the five disappearances and describes each; Mp-i 352:1 says the disappearance of learning (*pariyatti*) is the root of the five.
