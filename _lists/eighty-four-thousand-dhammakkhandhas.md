### Overview
The *Dhammakkhandha* represents the smallest granular "unit of teaching" or doctrinal segment within the Pāḷi Canon. Venerable Ānanda stated (Th 1027) that he had learned 82,000 teachings from the Buddha and 2,000 from the monks, 84,000 in all. The Aṭṭhasālinī (As 223-226) explains how this total is counted, by the units listed below, across the [Tipiṭaka](/glossary/tipitaka/) and the [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/).

### The List
How the Aṭṭhasālinī (As 226:2-7) counts one *dhammakkhandha*:
1. **Sutta** - A discourse with a single sequence (*ekānusandhika*) is one *dhammakkhandha*; where there are several sequences, the count follows the sequences.
2. **Gāthā** - In verse compositions, the asking of a question is one *dhammakkhandha* and the answer is one.
3. **Abhidhamma** - Each analysis of a triplet or a dual (*tikadukabhājana*) and each analysis of a cycle of consciousness (*cittavārabhājana*) is one *dhammakkhandha*.
4. **Vinaya** - Each portion (*koṭṭhāsa*) of the story (*vatthu*), matrix (*mātikā*), word-analysis (*padabhājanīya*), offence (*āpatti*), non-offence (*anāpatti*), interim offence (*antarāpatti*) and division into triplets (*tikacchedo*) is one *dhammakkhandha*.

### Quote
> **“Dvāsīti buddhato gaṇhiṃ, dve sahassāni bhikkhuto; Caturāsītisahassāni, ye me dhammā pavattino.**  
> “82,000 from the Buddha, and 2,000 more from the monks; 84,000 teachings I’ve learned, and these are what I promulgate.”  
>  
> — [Theragāthā](/glossary/theragatha/) (Th 1027, Ānanda) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_ku_08&page_number=347&name=therag%C4%81th%C4%81p%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTAyNy4g4oCY4oCYIER2xIFzxKt0aSBidWRkaGF0byBnYeG5h2hp4bmDLCBkdmU%3D)

> **Tattha ekānusandhikaṃ suttaṃ eko dhammakkhandho. Yaṃ anekānusandhikaṃ tattha anusandhivasena dhammakkhandhagaṇanā. Gāthābandhesu pañhāpucchanaṃ eko dhammakkhandho, vissajjanaṃ eko. Abhidhamme ekamekaṃ tikadukabhājanaṃ ekamekañca cittavārabhājanaṃ eko dhammakkhandho. Vinaye atthi vatthu, atthi mātikā, atthi padabhājanīyaṃ, atthi āpatti, atthi anāpatti, atthi antarāpatti, atthi tikacchedo. Tattha ekameko koṭṭhāso ekameko dhammakkhandhoti veditabbo.**  
> Therein, a discourse with a single sequence is one mass of Dhamma. Where there are multiple sequences, the count of the masses of Dhamma is according to those sequences. In poetic compositions, the asking of a question is one mass of Dhamma, and the answer is one. In the Abhidhamma, each analysis of a triplet or a dual, and each analysis of a cycle of consciousness, is one mass of Dhamma. In the Vinaya, there is the story, the matrix, the word-analysis, the offense, the non-offense, the interim offense, and the division into triplets. Therein, each individual portion should be understood as one mass of Dhamma.  
>  
> — [Aṭṭhasālinī](/glossary/atthasalini/) (As 226:2-7) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=attha_bi_01&page_number=27&name=dhammasa%E1%B9%85ga%E1%B9%87%C4%ABa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADhakath%C4%81&note=&selected_text=VGF0dGhhIGVrxIFudXNhbmRoaWth4bmDIHN1dHRh4bmDIGVrbyBkaGFtbWFra2hhbmRoby4%3D)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Theragāthā](/glossary/theragatha/)* (Verse 1027) – Ven. Ānanda declares: "82,000 from the Buddha, and 2,000 more from the monks; 84,000 teachings I've learned, and these are what I promulgate."
* **Commentary**: *Aṭṭhasālinī* – Explains the exact textual criteria used to measure a single "unit" of teaching (As 226:2-7: a single-sequence discourse, a question, an answer, or a triplet/dual analysis constitutes one dhammakkhandha).
