### Overview
While the classical frameworks detail ten or more defilements (*kilesā*), the orthodox tradition continuously traces mental corruption back to three primary psychological stains. These three act as the core obscuring smoke that blinds ultimate vision, locks beings into cyclic wandering, and fuels the entirety of unwholesome [kamma](/glossary/kamma/).

### The List
1. **Rāga** - Lust / Attachment: The burning stain of sensory greed, infatuation, and possessiveness that causes the mind to glue itself to pleasant conditioned objects.
2. **[Dosa](/glossary/dosa/)** - Hatred / Aversion: The violent stain of anger, irritation, and ill-will that scorches the mind stream, causing it to react aggressively to unpleasant objects.
3. **[Moha](/glossary/moha/)** - Delusion / Ignorance: The foundational mental darkness that masks the Four Noble Truths, preventing the mind from seeing things as they truly arise and cease.

### Quote
> **Channamativassatīti tayo kilesā rāgo doso moho, te khandhesu saṅkhārakkhandho … pe … te purā yathā niddiṭṭhaṃ khandhadhātuāyatanesu, ayaṃ otaraṇo hāro.**  
> ‘Rain soddens what is covered up’: there are three defilements, namely greed, hatred, and delusion; in the case of the categories, these are the volitional formations category;; these are as demonstrated earlier in the cases of the categories, elements, and bases; this is the Mode of Conveying Ways of Entry.  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *[Kilesa](/glossary/kilesa/) Saṃyutta* – Desire and lust (*chandarāga*) at each of the six sense bases is called "a corruption of the mind" (*cittasseso upakkileso*).
* **[Abhidhamma](/glossary/abhidhamma/)**: *[Dhammasaṅgaṇī](/glossary/dhammasangani/)* – Identifies these three elements as the primary unwholesome roots (*[akusala-mūla](/glossary/akusala-mula/)*) governing all unwholesome consciousness.
