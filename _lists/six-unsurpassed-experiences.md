### Overview
Worldly people often consider seeing a famous person, hearing beautiful music, or gaining great wealth to be unsurpassed experiences. The Buddha redefined what is truly "unsurpassed" (*anuttariya*) by pointing to the six spiritual experiences that decisively turn a being away from [saṃsāra](/glossary/samsara/) and lead to ultimate liberation.

### The List
1. **Dassanānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Seeing: The supreme sight. Seeing the Fully Awakened Buddha or his Noble Disciples with a heart full of faith.
2. **Savanānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Hearing: The supreme sound. Hearing the true [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/) taught by the Buddha or his Noble Disciples.
3. **Lābhānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Gain: The supreme acquisition. Gaining faith (*[saddhā](/glossary/saddha/)*) in the Tathāgata or a disciple of the Tathāgata.
4. **Sikkhānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Training: The supreme education. Training in higher ethics, higher mind, and higher wisdom in the Dhamma and Vinaya proclaimed by the Tathāgata.
5. **Pāricariyānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Service: The supreme action. Serving the Buddha or his Noble Disciples with a pure heart.
6. **Anussatānuttariya** - Unsurpassed Recollection: The supreme memory. Recollecting the Tathāgata or a disciple of the Tathāgata.

### Quote
> **“Chayimāni, bhikkhave, anuttariyāni. Katamāni cha? Dassanānuttariyaṃ, savanānuttariyaṃ, lābhānuttariyaṃ, sikkhānuttariyaṃ, pāricariyānuttariyaṃ, anussatānuttariyanti.**  
> “Monks, these six are unsurpassable. What six? Unsurpassable seeing, unsurpassable hearing, unsurpassable acquisition, unsurpassable training, unsurpassable service, and unsurpassable recollection.”  
>

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Anuttariya [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 6.30) – The Buddha contrasts worldly seeing, hearing, and gaining with these six spiritual equivalents, declaring them supreme.
