### Overview
This is the direct opposite of the wholesome courses. These ten behaviors, driven by greed, hatred, and delusion, constitute the "unrighteous" way of living. Engaging in these ten unwholesome courses (*[Akusala](/glossary/akusala/) [Kammapatha](/glossary/kamma-patha/)*) leads, in the Buddha's words to the brahmin householders of Sālā, to rebirth after death in a plane of misery, a bad destination, perdition, even hell (MN 41).

### The List
**Bodily Action (Kāyakamma):**
1. **Killing living beings:** Being bloody-handed and merciless.
2. **Taking what is not given:** Stealing by force or fraud.
3. **Sexual misconduct:** Having intercourse with those protected by parents, [Dhamma](/glossary/dhamma/), or a husband.

**Verbal Action (Vacīkamma):**
4. **False speech:** Lying under oath or deceiving others for gain.
5. **Malicious speech:** Repeating things to cause rifts and break friendships.
6. **Harsh speech:** Using words that are angry, insulting, and painful to others.
7. **Frivolous chatter:** Speaking at the wrong time, what is untrue, irrelevant, and contrary to the Dhamma and the Discipline.

**Mental Action (Manokamma):**
8. **Covetousness (Abhijjhā):** Obsessively wanting what belongs to others.
9. **Ill-will ([Byāpāda](/glossary/byapada/)):** Wishing for others to be killed, destroyed, or to suffer.
10. **Wrong View ([Micchādiṭṭhi](/glossary/micchaditthi/)):** Holding that there is no fruit or result of good and bad deeds, no other world, and no good and virtuous recluses and brahmins who have realised it by direct knowledge ([kamma](/glossary/kamma/) denied).

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Sāleyyaka [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (MN 41) – The Buddha explains to the brahmin householders of Sālā the ten kinds of unrighteous conduct (three of body, four of speech, three of mind), and states that it is because of this conduct that some beings are reborn in a plane of misery, a bad destination, perdition, hell (M-i 1428:1, M-i 1430-1440).
