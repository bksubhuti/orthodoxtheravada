### Overview
The Latent Tendencies (*[Anusaya](/glossary/anusaya/)*) are defilements that lie latent because they have not been abandoned. The Yamaka commentary asks in what sense they are *anusaya* and answers: in the sense of lying latent (*anusayanaṭṭhena*), that is, in the sense of not being abandoned (*appahīnaṭṭho*); they lie latent in the mental continuum of each person, and "lying latent" means that they arise when they meet a fitting cause (Yam-a 1771:1-6). The Paramatthadīpanī adds that, before they reach manifestation (*pariyuṭṭhāna*), they lie latent in the continuum alongside the arisen wholesome, unwholesome and indeterminate states, compatible with them and not in conflict (PmtDīp 1226:1-2). The Buddha teaches the Noble Eightfold Path to be developed for the direct knowledge, full understanding, utter destruction and abandoning of the seven (S-v 390:4). All seven lie latent in the ordinary worldling; in the stream-enterer and once-returner only five do, since the latent tendencies of views and of doubt have been abandoned (Yam-a 1818:1-3).

### The List
1. **Kāmarāgānusaya** - The latent tendency of sensual passion.
2. **Paṭighānusaya** - The latent tendency of resistance (aversion).
3. **Diṭṭhānusaya** - The latent tendency of views.
4. **Vicikicchānusaya** - The latent tendency of uncertainty (doubt).
5. **Mānānusaya** - The latent tendency of conceit.
6. **Bhavarāgānusaya** - The latent tendency of passion for becoming.
7. **Avijjānusaya** - The latent tendency of ignorance.

The order is that of AN 7.11 (A-vii 78:3), the Anusaya Sutta of the Saṃyutta (S-v 390:3, para 176) and the Saṅgīti Sutta (D-iii 1778:2). The Paṭisambhidāmagga, the Vibhaṅga and the Yamaka give māna before diṭṭhi (Paṭis 637:2; Vibh 2478:2; Yam-ii 823:1).


### Quote
> **“Sattime, bhikkhave, anusayā. Katame satta? Kāmarāgānusayo, paṭighānusayo, diṭṭhānusayo, vicikicchānusayo, mānānusayo, bhavarāgānusayo, avijjānusayo. Ime kho, bhikkhave, satta anusayā” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these seven underlying tendencies. What seven? The underlying tendency of sensual passion, the underlying tendency of resistance, the underlying tendency of views, the underlying tendency of uncertainty, the underlying tendency of conceit, the underlying tendency of passion for becoming, the underlying tendency of ignorance. Indeed, monks, these are the seven underlying tendencies.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 7.11, Paṭhama Anusaya Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_07&page_number=401&name=sattakanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTEu4oCY4oCYIFNhdHRpbWUsIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgYW51c2F5xIEu)
