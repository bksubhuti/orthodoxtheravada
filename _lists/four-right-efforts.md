### Overview
Right Effort (*Sammā-vāyāma*) in the Noble Eightfold Path is the energy of the Four Right Efforts (*[Sammappadhāna](/glossary/sammappadhana/)*). The Aṭṭhakathā explains *sammappadhāna* as "beautiful exertions, supreme energies" (*sundarapadhānāni uttamavīriyāni*) and "complete energy" (Mp-iv 1049:1-2). In each of the four the monk generates desire, makes an effort, arouses energy, exerts his mind and strives (*chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati*, A-iv 162:3).

### The List
1. **Saṃvarappadhāna** - The effort of restraint: for the non-arising of evil unwholesome states that have not yet arisen (A-iv 162:3). The sutta's example is guarding the sense faculties: on seeing a form with the eye the monk does not grasp at its sign or details, and practises restraint of the eye faculty (A-iv 168:4-6).
2. **Pahānappadhāna** - The effort of abandoning: for the abandoning of evil unwholesome states that have already arisen (A-iv 162:4). The example: he does not tolerate an arisen thought of sensual desire, ill will or cruelty, but abandons, dispels and ends it (A-iv 169:2-3).
3. **Bhāvanāppadhāna** - The effort of development: for the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen (A-iv 162:5). The example: he develops the seven factors of awakening, beginning with mindfulness (A-iv 170:2).
4. **Anurakkhaṇāppadhāna** - The effort of protection: for the endurance, non-confusion, increase, expansion, development and fulfilment of wholesome states that have already arisen (A-iv 162:6). The example: he protects an arisen auspicious sign of concentration, such as the perception of a skeleton, a worm-infested corpse, a bluish corpse, a fissured corpse or a bloated corpse (A-iv 171:2).

The Aṭṭhakathā explains the four names as the energy that has arisen in one who restrains the senses, abandons sensual thoughts, develops the awakening factors and protects the sign of concentration, respectively (Mp-iv 1053:2-5).


### Quote
> **“Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, sammappadhānāni. Katamāni cattāri? Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu anuppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ anuppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṃ pāpakānaṃ akusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ pahānāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; anuppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ uppādāya chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati; uppannānaṃ kusalānaṃ dhammānaṃ ṭhitiyā asammosāya bhiyyobhāvāya vepullāya bhāvanāya pāripūriyā chandaṃ janeti vāyamati vīriyaṃ ārabhati cittaṃ paggaṇhāti padahati. Imāni kho, bhikkhave, cattāri sammappadhānānī” ti.**  
> “Monks, there are these four right strivings (sammappadhāna). What are the four? Here, monks, a bhikkhu generates desire for the non-arising of bad unwholesome states that have not yet arisen; he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. He generates desire for the abandonment of bad unwholesome states that have already arisen; he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. He generates desire for the arising of wholesome states that have not yet arisen; he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. He generates desire for the endurance, persistence, multiplication, extension, development, and fulfilment of wholesome states that have already arisen; he endeavours, instigates energy, exerts his mind, and makes an effort. These indeed, monks, are the four right strivings.”  
>  
> — [Aṅguttara Nikāya](/glossary/anguttara-nikaya/) (AN 4.13, Padhāna Sutta) · [TPR](https://tpr.pali.tools/open?book_id=mula_an_04&page_number=322&name=catukkanip%C4%81tap%C4%81%E1%B8%B7i&note=&selected_text=MTMu4oCY4oCYIENhdHTEgXJpbcSBbmksIGJoaWtraGF2ZSwgc2FtbWFwcGFkaMSBbsSBbmku)

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Padhāna [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/)* (AN 4.13, A-iv 161:1 "3. Padhānasuttaṃ", para 13): "Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, sammappadhānāni" and the four. The names saṃvara, pahāna, bhāvanā and anurakkhaṇā are given with their explanations in the *Saṃvara Sutta* (AN 4.14, A-iv 167:1 "4. Saṃvarasuttaṃ", para 14), which says *padhānāni*.
* **Commentary**: *Manorathapūraṇī* (Mp-iv 1049:1-2 on AN 4.13; Mp-iv 1053:1-5 on AN 4.14).
