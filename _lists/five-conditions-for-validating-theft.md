### Overview
The legal hermeneutics of the [Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) are intensely precise. For a monk to break the 2nd [Pārājika](/glossary/parajika/) rule (resulting in permanent expulsion) or for a layperson to fully break the 2nd precept, the act of stealing must pass a stringent five-part test. The Vinaya says the offense is complete only with these five (*na tato ūnehi*, Sp-i 1524:4).

### The List
1. **Parapariggahitaṃ** - Belonging to Another: The object must be owned or held by another.
2. **Parapariggahitasaññī** - Awareness of Ownership: The perpetrator perceives the object as owned by another.
3. **Garuko parikkhāro** - A Heavy Requisite: The article taken must be of weighty value.
4. **Pañcamāsako vā atirekapañcamāsako vā** - Worth Five Māsakas or More: The value is five māsaka or more than five māsaka.
5. **Theyyacittaṃ paccupaṭṭhitaṃ** - Larcenous Intent: A mind set on theft is present.

### Quote
> **Pañcahi ākārehi adinnaṃ ādiyantassa āpatti pārājikassa – parapariggahitañca hoti, parapariggahitasaññī ca, garuko ca hoti parikkhāro, pañcamāsako vā atirekapañcamāsako vā, theyyacittañca paccupaṭṭhitaṃ hoti.**  
> 122. There is an expulsion offense for one who takes what is not given in five ways – if it is owned by another, if he perceives it as owned by another, if it is a heavy requisite, worth five māsa coins or more, and if a mind set on theft is present.  
>

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (Sp-i 1524:2-5) – Explains *pañcahi ākārehi* as five factors and counts them differently: *parapariggahitaṃ, parapariggahitasaññitā, parikkhārassa garukabhāvo, theyyacittaṃ, ṭhānācāvanaṃ* (the last is the moving of the object from its place).
