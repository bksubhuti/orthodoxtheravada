### Overview
Dependent Origination (*Paṭiccasamuppāda*) is the absolute core of Buddhist philosophy. It is the complex, 12-linked causal chain explaining exactly how suffering and rebirth occur without the need for a creator god or a permanent soul. The entire goal of Buddhism is to cut this chain, causing the entire mass of suffering to collapse.

### The List
1. **Avijjā** - Ignorance (of the Four Noble Truths) conditions...
2. **Saṅkhārā** - Volitional Formations (Kammic actions of body, speech, mind), which condition...
3. **Viññāṇa** - Consciousness (The rebirth-linking consciousness), which conditions...
4. **Nāma-rūpa** - Mentality and Materiality (The mind-body organism), which conditions...
5. **Saḷāyatana** - The Six Sense Bases (Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind), which condition...
6. **Phassa** - Contact (The meeting of sense, object, and consciousness), which conditions...
7. **Vedanā** - Feeling (Pleasant, painful, or neutral tone), which conditions...
8. **Taṇhā** - Craving (Lust for the feeling), which conditions...
9. **Upādāna** - Clinging (Fierce grasping and attachment), which conditions...
10. **Bhava** - Existence/Becoming (The kammic process of forming a new life), which conditions...
11. **Jāti** - Birth (The descent into a new womb/realm), which conditions...
12. **Jarāmaraṇa, Soka, Parideva, Dukkha, Domanassa, Upāyāsā** - Aging, Death, Sorrow, Lamentation, Pain, Grief, and Despair. 

### Textual References
* **Canonical**: *Paṭiccasamuppāda Sutta* (SN 12.1) – The Buddha outlines both the forward (arising) and reverse (cessation) orders of this twelve-fold chain.
