### Overview
The [Sutta](/glossary/sutta/) Piṭaka contains the conventional teachings and sermons of the Buddha. At the First Buddhist Council in Rājagaha, the senior Arahats organized this vast collection of discourses into five distinct structural repositories, known as *Nikāyas* (Collections), primarily grouped according to the length and content style of the texts.

### The List
1. **Dīgha Nikāya** - The Long Collection: Consisting of 34 major, expansive discourses dealing with philosophy, cosmic origins, and debates with other religious leaders.
2. **Majjhima Nikāya** - The Middle-Length Collection: Consisting of 152 core discourses that provide comprehensive guidance on meditation, psychological deconstruction, and monastic training.
3. **Saṃyutta Nikāya** - The Connected Collection: Counted in the Samantapāsādikā as 7,762 suttas (Sp-i 132:1), organized topically into sections (*saṃyuttas*) based on subject matter (e.g., the aggregates, sense bases, or dependent origination).
4. **Aṅguttara Nikāya** - The Numerical Collection: Counted in the Samantapāsādikā as 9,557 suttas (Sp-i 132:1), organized into eleven progressive books (*nipātas*), from the Ones to the Elevens, matching the mathematical layout of the teachings.
5. **Khuddaka Nikāya** - The Minor Collection: A diverse anthology of 15 divisions, from the Khuddakapāṭha to the Cariyāpiṭaka (Sp-i 132:1).

### Quote
> **Pañcanikāyā nāma – dīghanikāyo, majjhimanikāyo, saṃyuttanikāyo, aṅguttaranikāyo, khuddakanikāyoti.**  
> The five collections (pañcanikāyā) are: the Long Collection (Dīgha Nikāya), the Middle-length Collection (Majjhima Nikāya), the Connected Collection (Saṃyutta Nikāya), the Numerical Collection (Aṅguttara Nikāya), and the Minor Collection (Khuddaka Nikāya).

### Textual References
* **Commentary**: *[Samantapāsādikā](/glossary/samantapasadika/)* (Sp-i 118:1, 132:1) – Names the five nikāyas in its account of the First Council and counts their suttas: 34, 152, 7,762, 9,557 and the 15 divisions of the Khuddaka. *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* (Sv-i 173:2) gives the same five. The Mūla itself has no such list: the word *dīghanikāyo* occurs in the Mūla only in the DN book titles (4 hits, 0 for "pañca nikāy").
* **Canonical**: The First Council is told in the Cūḷavagga ([Vinaya](/glossary/vinaya/) Piṭaka), but that account does not name the five nikāyas.
